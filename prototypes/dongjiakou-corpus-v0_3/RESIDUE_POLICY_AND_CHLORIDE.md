# Recover everything accessible; distinguish intermediate sludge from final residue

Atlas Lattice research • v0.3 • 24 October 2026

Dave Sheldon's clarified objective is aspirational recovery of everything technically accessible into qualified uses, with water recovered/reused and final unrecovered residue minimized. A compound-rich composite remains a recovery feedstock. A missing pathway, buyer, affordable process or permit is **unresolved**, not proof that its contents are unextractable.

The formal ledger distinguishes:

- Present/characterized material and uncertainty in its inventory.
- Technically demonstrated recovery, including specific feed and operating conditions.
- Economics, capacity and allocation conflicts.
- Qualified product or internal use, with owner and destination.
- Material remaining in each intermediate stream.
- Final unrecovered residue, with the technology boundary and reason for non-recovery documented.

**Sludge is a physical process term, not proof of resource exhaustion.** Intermediate sludge may contain recoverable compounds. No material in this model has been proven fundamentally unextractable. `resource_status.csv` lists the first-stage remaining ions, explicitly before optional chloride routes; it does not call those masses final waste.

Portfolio cross-subsidy is allowed. A necessary treatment module can lose money while the whole permitted system is worthwhile. Independent environmental vetoes remain binding. The no-routine-marine-process-discharge constraint includes off-site ultimate fate; relocation alone does not close it.

## Can chloride be recovered?

Yes. NaCl/KCl/MgCl2 recovery already moves chloride into useful compounds. It is unnecessary to isolate elemental chlorine simply to recover chloride value. However, a good NaCl feedstock and a low-chloride irrigation water or mortar have different acceptance criteria.

Additional alternatives include:

| Route | Main products | What still needs resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Salt separation/crystallization | Qualified NaCl or other salts | Purification, concentration energy, mother liquor, market size |
| Bipolar membrane electrodialysis | HCl and NaOH | Pretreatment, power, water, concentration, impurity purge, actual use |
| Chlor-alkali electrolysis | Cl2, NaOH and H2 | High-quality brine, power, product handling, matched buyers and residual closure |
| Construction incorporation | A tested durable material | Exact chemistry, loading, strength, leaching, applicable approval and end-of-life fate |

The commercial chlor-alkali industry produces chlorine, caustic soda and hydrogen from salt brine. OxyChem documents these products and industrial applications. This is commercial chemistry evidence, not confirmation that a Corpus desalination residual meets cell-feed specifications or has a buyer. [OXYCHLOR]

A2025 primary research paper describes BMED integrated with pretreated concentrated seawater in China's Bohai Sea setting, reporting reuse-quality acid/base and a5.3-year project payback. Full operating/financial verification was not available here; this is **author-reported engineering-case evidence**, not a Corpus guarantee or proof of complete liquid closure. [BOHAIBMED]

Separate pilot research tests acid/base production from synthetic brines; a later study reports a real-seawater-brine pilot. Keep those feed/evidence boundaries distinct. [BMEDPILOT; BMEDREAL]

## Chloride is conserved, not wished away

Simplified stage reactions:

- BMED salt splitting: NaCl + H2O → HCl + NaOH.
- Chlor-alkali: 2NaCl + 2H2O → Cl2 + 2NaOH + H2.

The model records stoichiometric pure-active equivalents and required water, but does not predict whole-process water removal, real solution volume, current efficiency or energy. Purification and concentration are separate operations. Electrolytic hydrogen is a coproduct manufactured using electricity; it is not evidence that mineral sludge is biofuel or provides free net energy.

If HCl and NaOH are neutralized together, NaCl and water return. Internal reagents can reduce external purchases without permanently removing chloride. Each internal-use saving needs a real displaced purchase, and the same product cannot also be counted as sold. The acid-return sensitivity restores chloride to the remaining stream; its counterion chemistry is unresolved. Gross coproduct production is not booked as simultaneous sale and internal use.

Other chloride ions, added salts, purification wastes and all non-chloride constituents remain obligations. A route may recover chloride successfully without solving bulk-water recovery or other contaminants. Actual product and construction acceptance stays unresolved until specifications and trials are satisfied.

## Extraction aspiration versus demonstrated completeness

Adding recovery paths expands the feasible search space. It does not establish complete resource recovery until input compounds, products, side streams, losses, reagent additions and destinations balance. The gap between assumed salinity and the representative ion list remains uncharacterized inventory, not a newly identified commodity. No final-residue percentage is claimed by this version.
