# Shreddernaut named commercial components — first evidence audit

October 23, 2026, America/Chicago. Proposed system integration, no supplier selection or purchase. Existing v0.3 numerical yields are unchanged.

The useful architectural shift is from inventing individual machines to integrating a **multi-vendor recovery and remediation system**. The mobile screening/washing front end can serve several campaigns while specialized treatment is purchased as a service, housed at a separate hub or considered for a later fixed installation. We have not established that the complete system is mobile, containerized, compatible or available at the Desen screening reference price.

[DeepSeek's supplied original](DEEPSEEK_ORIGINAL.txt) is preserved as an attributed research lead. It supplied no exact source URLs. The following checks retrieve primary documents independently, distinguish evidence levels and retain contradictions. This is not a blanket validation of its answer.

## Important corrections to DeepSeek's claims

### Tianying: genuine installed project, different numbers and residuals

[Operator's environmental acceptance publication](https://www.cnty.cn/category_463/1113.html) links a [2020 acceptance opinion](https://www.cnty.cn/uploads/files/202011/4dec761e369557b66bb8a1872238b3ae.pdf). It records **40 t/day / 13,320 t/year** design scale and **¥142 million actual total project investment**. It identifies iron ingots for smelting, specialist disposal of heavy-metal sludge, and glassy slag then awaiting third-party hazardous-status identification. It also describes extensive air and water controls. Thus “no metals,” “eliminates the residual,” and automatic building-product acceptance are unsupported. These are historical project disclosures, not independent current plant verification or a Hawaii quotation. The ¥26.4 million annual operating cost, destruction efficiency, temperature range and payback remain unverified here.

Even DeepSeek's 50 t/day would process at most 18,250 t/year at 365 nameplate days, far below the model's 57,578 t/year original-mass-equivalent ash remainder. The documented 13,320 t/year design corresponds arithmetically to about **4.3 such capacity equivalents** for that quantity. That is not a recommendation for five units: eligible ash classes, actual wet/dry feed, contaminants, uptime and potentially larger designs must be established first. This is a scale mismatch check only.

### AlfaFlash/Jiarong: correct technology and scope

[Alfa Laval's leachate case](https://www.alfalaval.com/media/stories/sustainability/raising-the-bar-to-protect-groundwater-in-china/) names **Xiamen Jiarong**, not “Jaron.” The reported 1,000/500 t/day quantities concern the **project**, not a single evaporator. It includes other treatment and specialist dry-solid disposal. Its table reports conductivity/COD/ammoniacal nitrogen, not a PFAS destruction balance. [AlfaFlash's product page](https://www.alfalaval.my/products/process-solutions/evaporation-systems/alfaflash/) describes **forced-circulation flash evaporation**, not falling-film evaporation. The page offers mobile pilot-system rental/testing; Hawaiian availability and terms need inquiry.

Do not size water treatment from the model's 7,630 t/year inherent-water liability alone. Gross washing circulation, makeup, ash-washing liquor, leachate, purge and concentration factor determine actual treatment duty. Desal brine and contaminated process water are separate streams unless compatibility is demonstrated.

### Sesotec: stronger end-to-end reference, feed still unproven

[Sesotec's Steinbeis Polyvert case](https://www.sesotec.com/en/blog/blog-detail/steinbeis-polyvert-recycling-plastics-at-their-best) describes implemented reject recovery, then grinding/washing, flake separation, quality analysis and extrusion. It is therefore more informative than a standalone dry-sorter reference. It still does not demonstrate the proposed Oahu feed, 35% polymer recovery from rejected combustibles, or 20% acceptance from unknown mixed residue. Preserve those parameters as hypotheses.

### Leer and Desen: source-to-configuration mapping remains open

[Leer's supplier site](https://haileer.com/) and [project article](https://www.haileer.com/newsinfo/7763165.html) were located, but detailed page text was not retrievable. Patents, news and different application trains must not be conflated into one commercial machine specification. Request the Chinese legal entity, exact project, dated process diagram, license/acceptance documents and full mass/cost balance for each route.

For Desen, retain v0.2's unit-specific pages. A new broad “10–200 t/h” claim must not overwrite the DSLXC/01 table's 15–20 t/h or its differing overview. Contaminant removal, reduction in hazardous residual, and accepted-product yield are different metrics. “70–95% reduction” cannot determine cake percentage until its denominator, moisture basis and measurement method are known.

## Integration work now made concrete

1. Create a feed-and-interface sheet for every connection: particle size, dry solids/moisture, chemistry, temperature, contaminants, flow variability and storage time.
2. Ask each supplier for a test on the **actual upstream module's output**, not a clean surrogate or original unsorted feed.
3. Quote mobile front-end recovery separately from centralized/offsite specialist treatment. Do not force plasma, evaporation or full polymer finishing into the initial mobile package.
4. Request one accountable integration scope covering connected loads, starting demand, utilities, interlocks, spill/fire controls, buffers, wear parts and acceptance testing.
5. Close every accepted-product, offgas, liquid and residual endpoint; preserve unknown costs and unchanged simulation yields until evidence supports updates.
6. Obtain DeepSeek's exact URLs for every numerical claim not validated above. The most urgent are Leer process/model/deployment data and Tianying current operating costs/outputs.

**The named machines strengthen the procurement map. They do not yet justify lowering the model's 38.69% residual figure or claiming a complete zero-residual plant.**
