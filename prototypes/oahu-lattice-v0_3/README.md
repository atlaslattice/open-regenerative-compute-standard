# Oʻahu v0.3 — route the remaining material

**October 23, 2026, America/Chicago. Conditional annual screening envelopes, not equipment performance, permitted cleanup, a forecast or ROI.**

This extends [v0.2's full Desen/remediation catalog](../oahu-lattice-v0_2/README.md) by decomposing the earlier 82.34% remainder, applying compatible candidate routes, and keeping every unqualified residue on the account. It preserves [v0.1](../oahu-lattice-v0_1/README.md).

**Result:** the illustrative staged case lowers original-waste mass still requiring management from **82.34% to 38.69%**. This requires assumed full incoming-feed capture, selective fines washing, accepted ash products and additional sorting. None is a verified Oʻahu yield. The model does not establish complete remediation or zero landfill need.

## What percentage means here

The fixed denominator is the previous model's **272,208.95 metric tonnes/year**, from city-reported **2024** WGSL other MSW and ash/residue. This is not all Oʻahu waste, separate C&D, the legacy stockpile, or a 2026 forecast. Eligibility of the other-MSW stream for the assumed recovery routes remains unverified.

The city's current [siting FAQ](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/new-landfill-siting/) gives a different approximate annual total. Preserve that separately until time periods and scope are reconciled. Do not splice a newer denominator into the old material mix. The [February 2026 report](https://files.hawaii.gov/luc/dockets/sp09-403/annual-reports/12.pdf) also describes earlier maintenance-related diversion to WGSL, showing why a historical annual mix need not represent normal future operation.

Accounting is in **original-waste mass equivalents**: every original tonne ends in accepted material, the non-solid portion of energy recovery, or a residual-management account. It does not include all new water, binders, reagents, atmospheric oxygen, total flue gas, or newly generated treatment residues. Therefore the remainder is **not a forecast of weighed wet disposal tonnage or occupied volume**. Some of it is water requiring treatment. Actual final wet residuals, contaminants, fresh water/purge, emissions and cubic metres remain unknown.

Later steps are annual envelopes and do not inherit v0.1's daily scheduling validation. The final containment step is an accounting example: assigning a permitted endpoint does not make matter disappear or establish environmental clearance.

A strict no-new-clearance case retains 100% of the source material as requiring management. That does not imply existing city recycling ceases; it is the counterfactual for this new project's unverified routes, within the specified historical landfill-bound feed.

## What changed at each step

### S1 — intake and buffering

The prior model disposed of current feed arriving on nonoperating days. The new envelope permits capture across the year. At an assumed sustained 200 t/h, 300 eight-hour shifts and 80% availability, annual nominal screen capacity exceeds the approximately 140,641 t/year other-MSW feed.

This does not prove full capture: permitted storage, fire control, hazard isolation, actual schedules, outage storage and downstream rates must still work. There is no legacy excavation in this version. All-source feeding does not establish that material is legally or technically eligible for screening/fuel/reuse.

### S2 — selectively wash unqualified mineral fines

Retain v0.1's assumed primary mineral product. Of the remaining unqualified fines, assume 65% dry solids and 35% inherent water. Assume 70% of those dry solids becomes accepted mineral product. The residual is explicitly separated into dry concentrate and inherent water liability.

The route references Desen DSLXC/01–04, not a screen-only machine. The complete washing train must be quoted and balanced. The assumed annual raw mineral-fines quantity is below the 28,800 t/year arithmetic capacity of a 15 t/h, 300-shift washer, but short-term buffers, actual feed basis and filter-press/water bottlenecks are unresolved.

Gross illustrative added contact water is calculated at two tonnes per dry tonne washed; this is not fresh makeup demand. Recycle, purge, contaminants and added treatment residues require full engineering. The inherent water is not credited as free diversion.

### S3 — ash is a separate, existing opportunity

The [official February 2026 semiannual report](https://files.hawaii.gov/luc/dockets/sp09-403/annual-reports/12.pdf), printed page 23/PDF page 24, describes the existing Reworld ash project, its potential to divert at least 60% when permitted and built, and an additional sampling/data requirement then reported. This is a **project target, not a measured yield**.

The [city FAQ](https://www.honolulu.gov/env/ref/new-landfill-siting/) separately discusses enhanced metal recovery and potential **ash-volume** reduction, plus sand/aggregate investigations. Volume reduction is not a mass-recovery percentage; the model does not convert or add these claims.

Here, **60% accepted original ash mass** is an analyst-selected target case applied to both incumbent ash and hypothetical incremental fuel ash. The bottom/fly/APC split, assays, product acceptance and compatibility of the incremental ash remain unknown. A single fraction is a simplification, not proof that Desen can treat all ash.

This work is an existing city initiative. We assign it **zero realized Atlas credit**. With the same assumed 60% ash target enabled on the incumbent ash alone, remaining mass is 193,268 t/year (71.00%). Comparing S4 against that counterfactual gives approximately 87,939 t/year additional modeled original-mass removal, not the whole 166,880 t/year combined system result. Commissioning timing and incremental project attribution are not established.

The city also identifies special wastes and earlier ASR boiler-compatibility concerns. The inherited combustible fraction/acceptance assumptions must therefore be tested, not treated as an assay of actual landfill-bound special waste.

### S4 — secondary recovery targets

Assume additional accepted product equal to:

- 20% of the residual “other” category, after characterization and sorting.
- 35% of rejected combustibles, hypothesized as a suitably separable polymer fraction.
- 50% of the remaining unrecovered metal fraction.

These are **synthetic feed/acceptance assumptions**, not manufacturer claims or local composition measurements. The “other” category does not itself prove the presence of clean organics, glass or reusable textiles. Without those assays and accepted outlets this step stays unresolved. Alternative recipes in v0.2 remain available without receiving invented yields.

See [residual_routes.json](residual_routes.json) for module IDs, next evidence and missing yields/costs for every final stream. Treatment may make a residual safer without producing a saleable material. It can also create a smaller, more concentrated hazardous stream or add binder mass.

[FRTR soil washing](https://frtr.gov/matrix/Soil-Washing/) explains contaminant transfer and further residual/water treatment. [FRTR stabilization](https://frtr.gov/matrix/Solidification-and-Stabilization/) distinguishes immobilization from removal. No “treat again until zero” recursion is allowed without new evidence: a process that separates a coarse product cannot be assumed to give the same yield on its already-concentrated residue.

## Sensitivity: how dependent is the result on assumptions?

The 36-case grid varies:

- Accepted dry-product washing yield: 40%, 70%, 85%.
- Accepted ash-product mass fraction: 0%, 30%, 60%, 80%.
- Secondary acceptance from “other” residue: 0%, 20%, 40%.

Other secondary recovery assumptions remain fixed. The calculated remainder spans **25.00%–74.32%**. This is not a confidence interval, a forecast, or an established feasible range; it simply exposes sensitivity to the chosen inputs. The 80% ash case is an ambitious analyst target, not the city's reported target or verified performance.

Even this aggressive arithmetic does not deliver zero residuals. Product and energy endpoints also require contaminant, environmental and market evaluation; mass removal alone cannot establish complete remediation.

## What does each added percentage cost?

Actual costs are **unknown**: no comparable complete supplier, site, offtake and residual-disposal quotations exist in this model. No financial ranking is asserted.

[frontier.csv](results/frontier.csv) calculates the net incremental budget compatible with hypothetical allowable costs of $25/$50/$100/$200 per additional tonne of original mass removed. This is a **policy/portfolio break-even budget**, not estimated processing cost or actual avoided disposal savings.

At a hypothetical $100 per additional original tonne, the corresponding annual incremental budgets are:

| Step | Hypothetical incremental net budget/year |
|---|---:|
| S1 full incoming capture | $1.041 million |
| S2 additional fines washing | $0.992 million |
| S3 ash recovery target | $8.637 million |
| S4 secondary recovery | $1.212 million |

These ceilings indicate what quotations and verified public value would need to justify. They do not predict expenditures. S3 includes an incumbent initiative and cannot be presented as new Atlas expenditure/benefit without the right counterfactual.

One percentage point of this fixed baseline is 2,722.09 t/year. An allowable net cost of $100/t corresponds to approximately $272,209/year per additional percentage point. Thus expensive final fractions can be exposed directly instead of hidden by an average recovery percentage.

Use [v0.2 portfolio accounting](../oahu-lattice-v0_2/model.py) to allow metal revenues, qualified treatment losses and shared costs to balance. Add only demonstrated avoidable/deferrable public infrastructure costs, with a common horizon and discounting. A positive public comparison may justify funding an operator cash gap, but does not generate that cash automatically. Environmental vetoes remain independent.

Complete quotations must include contact versus fresh water, purge, reagent salts, binder addition, wet cake, external treatment, shipping, testing, support equipment, shutdowns and residue management. Moving a residual offsite may clear a source cell while transferring regional liability; track both. No net airspace estimate is made without measured densities and permitted placement design.

## Run and verification

Python standard library. Keep the repository folders as siblings:

```bash
python prototypes/oahu-lattice-v0_3/model.py
python -m unittest discover -s prototypes/oahu-lattice-v0_3 -v
```

Local development spelling `oahu-lattice-v01` is also supported for the v0.1 dependency. v0.2 is the catalog/reference companion and is not imported by this engine.

Fifteen checks verify original-mass conservation, exact reproduction of the earlier remainder, veto/unresolved behavior, duplicate routing prevention, no product reconsumption, no free water disappearance, containment accounting and parameter extremes. This is software validation, not field proof. Earlier artifacts remain unchanged.
