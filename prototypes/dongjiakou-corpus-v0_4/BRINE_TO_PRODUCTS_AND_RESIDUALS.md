# Can mineral profits pay for a no-ocean residual system?

Atlas Lattice working analysis • v0.2 • 24 October 2026

**Yes, that is a legitimate portfolio hypothesis.** A profitable mineral lane may fund a necessary loss-making water/residual lane. A mineral's sale value, recoverable quantity and net contribution must be established separately. The portfolio may balance financially; each environmental obligation still needs a physical route.

## Why mineral extraction does not automatically produce only sludge

A brine contains water, dissolved ions and possibly suspended solids. Selective precipitation produces solid crystals or sludge **plus a remaining liquid**, called mother liquor. Filtration removes suspended crystals; dissolved sodium/chloride and other ions can pass through with the water. A filter press removes water from the cake but generates filtrate that must also go somewhere.

A simplified magnesium example is:

MgCl₂ + 2 NaOH → Mg(OH)₂(s) + 2 NaCl(aq).

The Mg becomes a recoverable solid, while the chloride remains in solution and the added sodium contributes to the mother liquor. Electrochemical production of the alkali could change purchased-input requirements, but consumes power and creates other streams. A real ionic/speciation/reagent balance must replace this schematic reaction before design.

If all useful dissolved material is crystallized, separated or otherwise incorporated into products, most of the original water still needs to be recovered, reused, evaporated or retained in a qualified material. Some mixtures may leave wet cake; others leave large liquid streams. **The endpoint is determined by treatment, not by naming the remaining material sludge.**

The base model inventories approximately 154,000m³/day of combined liquid residual and 9,350t/day of dissolved feed salt at online nameplate. This does not mean all the salt is hazardous; it means the physical quantities needing products or final management are large. Gross salt mass excludes added chemicals and suspended solids.

## Construction routes deserve a real trial

Two distinct candidates should be retained:

1. **Qualified recovered ingredients.** Purified Ca/Mg compounds can become cement/binder inputs with a defined recipe, curing method, purity and market. A 2025 study demonstrated tailored Mg(OH)₂ recovery and carbonation for a calcination-free binder. This is experimental material evidence, not approval for arbitrary mixed sludge. [MGCEMENT]
2. **Qualified residual incorporation.** A slag-based mortar study used simulated RO reject as batching water and evaluated strength and leaching, including crushed material. Its favorable results apply to the tested formulation, not all post-extraction brines. [MORTAR]

The second route may reduce evaporation duty for the fraction accepted by the construction process. It needs actual mother-liquor testing: mineral extraction and reagents change the feed composition. Require mechanical durability, salt leaching, steel compatibility, weathering, applicable approvals and end-of-life fate. A material should not return salts to coastal water when wetted or crushed.

The study's sand/binder/water mass proportions were 5.7/2.9/1.0. As a **recipe-scaling illustration only**, each tonne of batching liquid requires 8.6 tonnes of dry constituents in that recipe. Thus a technically good formulation can still have a very large binder/aggregate and market requirement. Alternative formulations require their own evidence. Do not presume the local construction sector can absorb the whole plant's output. [MORTAR]

Inorganic brine solids are **not biofuel**. Organic wastewater sludge is a different feedstock. Construction conversion, drying, vitrification and reagent regeneration generally require inputs; thermal treatment does not make inorganic salt an energy source.

## Evidence of potential value, with clear boundaries

A 2026 study reports a 50t/year pilot producing vaterite calcium carbonate using SWRO brine, cement kiln dust and CO₂; its larger commercial profitability was modeled. The kiln dust contributes calcium, so the product cannot all be counted as seawater-derived mineral recovery. [PCC26]

Another pilot-based study models Mg(OH)₂ production from ultra-concentrated saltworks bitterns at 5,000t/year, with modeled costs of €0.40–0.45/kg. Those results support testing integrated recovery; they are not a Corpus brine quotation, a spot margin or proof that the entire residual system is paid for. [MAREMAG]

These are useful precedents for the lattice's cross-domain hypothesis. They also show why feed concentration, co-inputs, scale and product specification determine whether a profitable lane exists.

## A transparent 30-year financing sensitivity

The existing illustrative 50%-capture allocation yields about 387t/online day Mg(OH)₂ equivalent, or 127,202t/year at 90% availability. Neither capture nor sale is guaranteed. **These are assumed margins and full-volume sales, not quotes, predicted profits or realized credit.** The equivalent budget must cover the shared final-residual capital and operating obligation. Additional product lanes could increase contribution; uncertain sales, low margins, ramp-up, downtime or costs outside this margin definition reduce it. There is no claim that the listed dollar/m³ budgets are sufficient for ZLD.

The model uses constant annual quantities and margins, no ramp-up or price escalation, 30years and a6% discount rate. An executable financing case also needs construction timing, working capital, contingencies, contract duration, maintenance outages and replacement schedules. It must not mix a 30-year gross sales headline with a one-time equipment price while ignoring 30years of operating obligations.

**Testable decision:** compare the portfolio's discounted net contribution with quoted capital, operating, replacement and final-fate costs of the no-ocean route. Positive results would support the user's hypothesis. Until those costs and buyers are established, the result remains unresolved rather than failed or proven.

## Residual alternatives retained

Compare engineered water recovery/crystallization; contained solar-assisted evaporation where land, meteorology and permits support it; qualified brine-as-batching-water or solids incorporation; and permitted non-marine residual treatment/containment. Plasma/vitrification remains a candidate for compatible solids, with its energy and off-gas residues included. None is assumed universal, free or unlimited.

A fallback is only confirmed when a named receiver accepts the exact chemistry and volume, with capacity, price, transport, permit and ultimate fate documented. No-ocean operation may require stopping feed when storage or receiving capacity is unavailable. In v0.2 every actual fallback remains unconfirmed.
