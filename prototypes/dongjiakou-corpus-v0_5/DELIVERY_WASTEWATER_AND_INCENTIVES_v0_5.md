# Corpus alternative: integrated recovery and delivery incentives

Version 0.5, 2026-10-24. Proposed screening design for a better public offering. Historical proposals remain comparators, not accusations of wrongdoing. Passing software tests validates bookkeeping, not treatment performance.

## What changes

The alternative provisionally eliminates an automatic management fee calculated from project expenditure. It replaces that mechanism with reasonable salaries, employer costs, verified reimbursable expenses, and separately capped outcome incentives. Salaries, staffing, duration, allowable expenses, rates, caps, acceptance windows and payment benchmarks are TBD. Unknown management cost remains null and blocks an all-in budget. It is not booked as zero or as a proven saving from historical costs.

The purpose is to align compensation with dependable public service, economical delivery and qualified resource recovery. Historical general conditions, engineering and inspections are not erased: they include real work whose replacement scope must be priced. No evidence reviewed establishes that Corpus used a prohibited cost-plus-percentage-of-cost arrangement. FAR16.102 and16.405-1 are federal reference mechanisms, not a determination of local contract eligibility. Local public procurement, funding restrictions and any revenue-sharing authority require review before use.

## Recoverable materials count in the benchmark

Retain the full mineral register, stoichiometric allocations, actual pilot/industrial literature and explicit candidate yields. A zero realized credit is not a claim that resource recovery lacks evidence. It means this site has not yet qualified output or demonstrated net delivery value. Evidence and milestones are tracked in `mineral_evidence_benchmarks.csv`, covering magnesium/cement, calcium products, bromine/salts, acid/base and wastewater nutrients.

Management may earn a TBD capped share for brokering legitimate sales, replacing purchased inputs, or establishing a qualified new application. The proposed basis is **incremental net value**, not tonnes present or gross sales:

`incremental_net_value = collected_sales + verified_avoided_input_purchase - attributable_lifecycle_cost - baseline_net_value`

`recovery_award = min(agreed_cap, agreed_share * max(0, incremental_net_value))`

Attributable costs include recovery capital charge, operations, qualification, transport, residual obligations and other selling fees. Award expense is then deducted once from public retained value. The model does not duplicate the earlier after-cost mineral-margin view by charging its capital again. The new normalized award scenarios are separate illustrations, not mineral prices or promised awards.

Internally consumed material is valued at an evidenced avoidable delivered purchase of a comparable input, capped at the real avoidable purchase cost. It produces an in-kind saving, not sales cash. The same tonne cannot be sold and consumed; the same avoided purchase cannot be credited in both manufacturing and mineral ledgers. No bonus for moving inventory to a stockpile or an unqualified related-party buyer. A novel magnesium-concrete application must qualify its actual recipe and performance, buyer demand, lifecycle cost and final fate. Environmental vetoes apply regardless of profit; independent verification and public approval determine whether a revenue share is permissible.

## Wastewater and desalination as one recovery network

The city'sJune2026announcement describes a107.5m Oso–Greenwood connection intended to add11MGD and bring its reuse program to as much as16MGD. These are announced program figures, not verified firm delivery or a new Atlas gain. Model the committed city program in the counterfactual before evaluating additions.

Compare a single recovery campus with a connected network. Share only compatible capacity: laboratories, maintenance, trained operators, power, process data, appropriate heat, reagent production and qualified residual services. Keep biological, saline, industrial and potable process trains isolated where contamination or salinity requires it; include bypass and redundancy. Co-location does not authorize dilution or combining incompatible waste streams.

Brine magnesium can be allocated to qualified wastewater nutrient recovery instead of sold, subject to actualN/P concentrations, yield, contaminants and product demand. Suitable organic solids may produce biogas; inorganic brine solids do not. Reclaimed water can serve qualified industry, cooling or agriculture and potentially change desal demand. Do not subtract announced16MGD automatically from30MGD: quality, seasonality, commitments, firm supply and return-flow/ecological effects must be resolved. New reuse-demand outputs are illustrative only.

Existing Corpus nutrient/biogas recovery has not been established by this source review; it must not be labeled absent. Additional scope, capacity and quotes remain null.

## Benchmark discipline

Generic modularization, prefabrication, route shortening and schedule savings overlap and are not summed. CII's decision research evaluates savings or cost increases project by project. California's conditional existing-intake cost reduction applies to intake scope, not the entire marine category. Wastewater co-siting does not itself supply a seawater intake. Any avoided outfall must be assessed alongside replacement residual closure; zero routine marine discharge remains the alternative's objective, not a verified achievement.

Contingency needs a project-specific risk basis. A total escalation allowance cannot be compared directly with an annual escalation rate. General-conditions percentages need matched duration, scope and denominator. Unverified generic ranges stay attributed in `benchmark_register.csv`; none is adopted as a saving guarantee. A quantity template requests piping lengths, structural tonnage, building area, productive hours and comparable site duties. It is not an engineering reference design.
