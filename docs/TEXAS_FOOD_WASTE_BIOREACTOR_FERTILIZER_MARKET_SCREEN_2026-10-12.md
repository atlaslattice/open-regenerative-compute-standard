# Texas food waste → fertilizer + biogas + biological reactors | 2026-10-12 screen

**Status: REPORTED / DERIVED / PROPOSED. Non-canon, no realized credits.** This screening note does not establish commercial viability, delivered gas, fertilizer substitution, or permissions to process waste.

## Source-grounded anchors

- Texas Commission on Environmental Quality reported **40.1 million US short tons of total municipal solid waste disposed in FY2023**, *not food waste alone*. https://www.tceq.texas.gov/publications/sfr/tceq-biennial-report/biennial-report-to-the-89th-legislature/agency-activities
- ReFED reports US **~70 million US tons surplus food in 2024** across the food system, including flows not all suitable for digestion. Its 2026 report: https://refed.org/food-waste/refed-us-food-waste-report-2026/
- Texas-specific **5–8 million US short tons/year food waste** is a **planning hypothesis, NOT a validated statewide mass inventory**. Reconcile ReFED's Texas-specific state data with TCEQ solid waste characterization before promotion. Avoid incorrectly multiplying the *national* per-capita generation estimate by state population while claiming official state total.
- Texas Dept. of Agriculture cites **$1.3B expenditure in 2022** on **fertilizer, lime and soil conditioners combined**, from the agricultural census. It is a historical annual observation, not 2026 spending and excludes some outside-farm channels. https://texasagriculture.gov/About/Texas-Ag-Stats
- Oct 7, 2026 DTN US retailer survey lists roughly **$683/US short ton urea**, **$934 DAP**, **$974 MAP**, **$499 MOP**, **$483 UAN32**. These are *US averages*, not verified delivered Texas prices, and chemical fertilizer market value is not equal to wet compost tonnage. https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/crops/article/2026/10/07/uan32-leads-fertilizer-prices-higher
- Texas Pure Products **Oct 1 2025** municipal compost list **$38–40/cubic yard** bulk and **$46–48/cubic yard Compost Ultra** depending on buyer class, before delivery/tax. Not a uniform wholesale farm-gate price. https://www.texaspureproducts.com/149/Products-and-Pricing
- EPA 2022–23 national food-waste AD survey reports **2023 $22.23 per wet US ton median, $32.27 average tipping fee among 18 respondents**; do not extrapolate as a guaranteed Texas gate price. https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/anaerobic-digestion-facilities-processing-food-waste-us-2022-2023
- EPA basic anaerobic digestion: AD converts carbon to gas, leaves digestate with substantial N, P, K. Subsequent separation, struvite and ammonium sulfate are possibilities, not automatic products. https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion

## Correct units, boundaries, priority

- Food waste first: edible rescue/donation and prevention; then safe animal feed where lawful and economically appropriate; processing/disposal of unavoidable inedible streams by digestion, compost, nutrient recovery and other methods.
- Gross example using **1 wet metric ton source-separated food waste**: illustrative methane **60–150 m3 CH4** (feedstock and BMP dependent), or **2.15–5.37 GJ fuel energy** at 35.8 MJ/m3. Actual methane sold is smaller after uptime, gas clean-up and methane recovery.
- Wet digestate **~0.7–1.0 tonnes/t wet incoming** is only a water-inclusive rough engineering band, and its true weight depends on water addition, solids separation, gas losses, packaging rejection and digestate recirculation.
- Alternative finished compost product **~0.2–0.4 tonnes/t wet incoming** is a highly variable screening band requiring input moisture/mass balance and process validation. **Do NOT add wet digestate plus finished compost tonnage** if compost was made from that digestate.
- Do not equate 1 tonne digestate to 1 tonne purchased chemical fertilizer. **Value nutrients in kilograms N, P2O5, K2O**, plant availability, spatial shipping cost, contaminants, and crop nutrient limits, and avoid double counting mineralization.
- Illustrative **unverified** *recovered, crop-accessible nutrients* per 1 wet tonne of incoming food waste: N 2–6 kg, P2O5 0.5–2 kg, K2O 1–3 kg. These are scenarios, not Texas feedstock assays and depend on ammonia volatilization, phosphorous partition and nutrient treatment.
- Theoretical nutrient replacement using DTN Oct 7 fertilizer inputs: $683 / (907.1847 kg*0.46) = $1.64/kg N as urea; $934 / (907.1847 kg*0.46) = $2.24/kg P2O5 as DAP **but DAP also brings N**; $499/(907.1847 kg*0.6) = $0.917/kg K2O as MOP. Sum of representative N/P/K *standalone marginal proxies* ~$5.1–$16.9 per wet metric tonne of feedstock (ignoring P-fertilizer N credit, processing and hauling). **NOT a product selling price or profit**.
- Compost marketing: $38–40/cu yd (Texas municipal *retail/pickup*, not converted to $/ton without bulk-density testing), no automatic comparability to commercial concentrated fertilizers.
- Bioreactors: covered dry or wet anaerobic continuous stirred tank digesters (CSTR), plug-flow manure co-digestion, high-solids batch AD, thermophilic wet AD, modular acidogenic/acetogenic staged AD for stability, microbial compost reactors (aerobic), and post-AD nutrient separation/precipitation. Biochar or insect/protein feed is a distinct regulatory pathway.
- Technical safeguards: moisture, dry and volatile solids, biochemical methane potential, C:N, pH/alkalinity, ammonia inhibition, H2S, digestate conductivity/Na, heavy metals, PFAS where relevant, pathogens, crop-use contracts and salt/water rights. Food packaging/polymer contamination and biomaterials compatibility are first-order limits.

## Statewide illustration, not forecast

If **5–8 million US short tons** are treated as a *synthetic generated-waste envelope*, multiply by 0.9071847 for **4.54–7.26 million wet metric tonnes**. Assume **25% collected for AD** in a hypothetical case, resulting in **1.13–1.81 million tonnes feedstock**. At **60–150 m3 CH4/tonne**, gross **68–272 million m3 CH4/year**, or **2.4–9.6 Bcf/year**, before uptime/upgrading loss. Assumed 25% diversion is not a measured Texas rate. Illustrative alternative composting at 0.2–0.4 tonne compost/tonne organic feedstock would be ~0.23–0.73 million tonnes finished product if all 25%-collected feed was composted rather than digested, again without lab mass balance. Do not sum this compost with wet digestate produced from that same feedstock.

At assumed 2–6 kg N / t, 0.5–2 kg P2O5 / t and 1–3 kg K2O / t crop accessible, the 25%-collected stream could replace nominal **2.27–10.89 million kg N**, **0.57–3.63 million kg P2O5**, and **1.13–5.44 million kg K2O**. Fertilizer market value is *not* one-for-one; maximum nutrient proxy in this screen is tens of millions of dollars, not the entire Texas $1.3B farm fertilizer bill.

EPA-reported tip fees could be economically consequential; at 1.13–1.81 million metric tonnes, a **hypothetical $20–30/US short ton** tipping tariff gives ~$25–60M per year *before packaging rejects, transport and operating costs*. Actual Texas contracts unknown.
