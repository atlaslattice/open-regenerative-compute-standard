## Abstract / conclusions

The U.S. has no demonstrated single-technology cure for energy, fertilizer and industrial-material shortages. It does have several complementary, near-term optimization opportunities: (a) secure and regenerate process-ready sulfuric acid and elemental sulfur from verified industrial streams; (b) reduce delivered-feedstock and logistical constraints at existing fertilizer capacity before crediting new plant nameplate; (c) deploy metered AI inference at actual process plants, water treatment, solar farms and grid/storage assets; (d) integrate renewables, storage, cooling and water recovery where genuine process synergies exist. The value proposition is a **Lattice of measured, locally accountable industrial optimizers**, not indiscriminate construction of training hyperscalers or a universal claim of free energy.

**Top conclusions (evidence → decision):**
1. **Supply shock confirmed:** Mosaic reports $522 per U.S. long ton sulfur cost in Q2 2026 and new Gulf Coast contracts at $705 per long ton, accompanied by reduced U.S. phosphate production. A sulfur ton must be delivered at required purity to a permitted acid plant; physical sulfate inventory elsewhere is NOT fungible feedstock. [S1–S3].
2. **Domestic base sizeable, but not spare:** USGS's 2026 sulfur summary reports approximately **8.1 Mt sulfur (all forms, sulfur content)** produced in the U.S. in 2025, of which about **7.6 Mt elemental**, and approximately **34% of domestic sulfur consumption supplied by imports of sulfur and sulfuric acid**. Existing output is committed, not a free pool available for diversion. [S4].
3. **Chemical sensitivity:** One tonne of commercial phosphate fertilizer containing 46% P2O5 made using domestically produced wet-process phosphoric acid requires theoretically **0.312–0.346 tonnes sulfur**, **0.954–1.059 tonnes pure H2SO4**, with **1.67–1.86 tonnes calcium sulfate dihydrate** coproduct depending rock stoichiometry, before process losses. These are conditional mass-balance factors, NOT recipes or independently verified Mosaic plant rates.
4. **Finishing vs feedstock:** CHS/OCP's proposed **>1 Mt/year** Louisiana phosphate fertilizer plant and **up to $450 million** investment would be domestically located manufacture, while **OCP supplies phosphoric acid**. Do not count that output as fully domestic acid/feedstock supply or add 0.3Mt/y sulfur requirement to Louisiana without confirming whether local wet-process acid is built. [S5].
5. **Real industrial AI precedents, not direct proof of Lattice ROI:** Google documented up to **40% cooling-system energy savings** from ML in one operating data center (not 40% total plant power); China MIIT documented **+22.3% productivity, −50.2% defects and −20.4% carbon emissions** in selected leading smart factories (not measured effects attributable to local LLM inference alone). A 2026 Lawrence Berkeley lab industrial cement study modeled **3–9% primary energy** savings conditional on assumptions, not field achieved. [S13–S15].
6. **Real solar energy, conditional biosolar uplift:** EIA measured/estimated **296TWh utility-scale + 93TWh small-scale solar in 2025**. Australian UTS measured **11.1% summer PV improvement** for a planted roof, but a multi-year European study found **0.95–1.14%** system improvement after correcting design bias over March–August. Do not forecast uniform +11.1% annual U.S. generation. [S16–S18].
7. **Energy grading matters:** Regasification LNG cold = theoretically around **830 kJ/kg (230.6 kWhth/metric tonne)**. U.S. 2025 LNG imports alone correspond to about **65 GWh thermal/year**, not 65GWh of firm electricity; domestic peak-shaving vaporization is a missing U.S. inventory field. PHMSA's 186 in-service LNG facility records are NOT 186 sites actually regasifying LNG year-round. [S19–S21].
8. **Capacity ≠ dispatch:** PJM's reported **6.8GW prospective capacity-auction shortfall** is not evidence that 13 states experienced rolling blackouts. DOE issued an Oct 12 emergency order; PJM's Oct 14 notice said it had not identified need for specified emergency generators on Oct 15–16. Solar annual TWh and peak MW with four-hour BESS are separately measured. [S22–S24].
9. **DPA opportunity is contingent:** Sept 8 Executive Order 14427 adjusts delegations; Oct 13 Delek Tyler refinery expansion under DPA was announced, but incremental bpd/funding and executed rated order not established. A DPA pathway for additional sulfur refining or other ORCS components requires lawful need identification, appropriations/program authority, contracts, permitting and metered production; none assumed. [S25–S27].

## 1. Accounting boundary and source quality

Use these labels in every node ledger: **MEASURED** (metered site receipt), **OFFICIAL ESTIMATE** (e.g., USGS/EIA published), **OPERATOR REPORTED** (contract, price or capacity declaration), **PUBLISHED EXPERIMENT** (specific pilot/lab data), **SIMULATED** (models and user-editable inputs), **PROPOSED** (not funded or permitted), and **QUARANTINED** (unresolved or inconsistent). Never credit prospective cash flow, recoveries, emissions or reserves as realized.

The central dataset is a mass-energy-flow ledger: incoming process input → applicable conversion → internally reused product → **net additional qualified output** → end user; transport, capex, parasitic energy, displacement of existing industries, safety compliance and residual liabilities are posted as costs. For energy, record kWh thermal at temperature, kWh electrical generation, and kWh of chiller electricity avoided on different fields. For solar, record conventional PV production vs *incremental* biosolar gain, thermal/HVAC savings and monetized non-energy benefits separately. For domestic-first reserves, record consumption and *accessible* inventory by grade/location rather than nominal gross material stock.

### Data quality / invalid inference vetoes
- An offsite sulfur-bearing waste stream is not supply unless a chemical regeneration path, tested product and delivered economics exist.
- Sulfuric acid content, elemental sulfur content, commercial DAP tonnage and P2O5 equivalents are not interchangeable.
- Existing U.S. refinery sulfur output, phosphogypsum inventory, export figures and estimated recovery cannot be added as new output unless incremental and mutually exclusive.
- A project at a refinery, liquefaction terminal, green roof or utility is not endorsed by its operator merely because theoretically co-sitable.
- All cost assumptions must specify year/currency, equipment lifecycle, applicable site scope and whether they include permitting, labor, maintenance and the disposal of rejected materials.

## 2. The fertilizer shock: why producers curtail during shortages

Mosaic's public Q2 2026 operating results and SEC 10-Q describe availability and affordability of sulfur as material constraints. Second-quarter U.S. phosphate sales were ~1.4Mt; the company's Faustina phosphate operation was idled and Bartow at ~40% target operation; Q2 sulfur price **$522/long ton**, newly settled Gulf Coast sulfur **$705/long ton** expected mainly in Q4 cost of goods. Mosaic forecast DAP prices **$820–840 per metric ton FOB**, but revenue per tonne must also cover ammonia, ore, power, logistics, wages, equipment uptime, maintenance, capital and environmental compliance. Some supply may be physically unavailable even at higher price. The SEC disclosure explicitly documents limited sulfur supply and further curtailment risk. [S1–S3]

The USGS 2025 domestic sulfur production estimate (~8.1Mt sulfur content, ~7.6Mt elemental) and the imported-share (~34% of apparent consumption) show why the issue can coexist with large national sulfur production. Competition between copper leaching and phosphate fertilizer, refineries' commercial sulfur export incentives, port exposure and geographic/delivered acid freight costs can make locally available material scarce. A refinery expansion may alter sulfur byproduct output but only as a function of crude sulfur specification and actual processed sulfur mass, not simply new barrels per day.

### Stoichiometric check — illustrative wet-process 18-46-0 / P2O5=46% finished fertilizer
The conventional wet process uses phosphoric-acid extraction from phosphate rock by sulfuric acid, creating gypsum/phosphogypsum. Grade-46 commercial product has 460kg of P2O5-equivalent per metric tonne; mineralogical bounds of 1.5 to 1.667 mol sulfuric acid per mol phosphoric-acid phosphorus give:
| Conditional input/output | Per 1 tonne 46%-P2O5 fertilizer | Per 250kt/yr | Per 1Mt/yr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sulfur, elemental equivalent | 0.312–0.346 t | 78–87 kt/y | 312–346 kt/y |
| Pure H2SO4 needed for wet-process acid | 0.954–1.059 t | 238–265 kt/y | 954–1,059 kt/y |
| Theoretical gypsum dihydrate generated | 1.67–1.86 t | 418–465 kt/y | 1.67–1.86 Mt/y |

These values depend strongly on input ore composition, acidulation and product grade. Practical acid overfeed, reaction losses, wet cake and impurities change plant requirements. Do not apply to fertilizer FINISHING that receives imported OCP phosphoric acid. Nor should the gypsum row be priced as immediately saleable byproduct (see Section 4).

The $183/long ton Mosaic sulfur contract change converts to approximately **$180/metric tonne of S**. For an illustrative 1Mt/year grade-46 producer producing its own wet-process acid, the sulfur-only incremental purchase-cost burden is **about $56–62 million/year** (or $56–62 per finished tonne), not including other variable cost changes. Sulfur cost at $705/long ton alone is roughly **$216–240 million/year** at this output. These are PRICE x STOICHIOMETRIC INPUT sensitivities, not an assertion of the company's actual hedged sulfur purchases or financing position. A long ton is 2,240lb ≈1.01605 metric tonnes; convert before calculations.

### Existing versus announced capacity
Mosaic's older SEC U.S. operational processed-phosphate nameplate ~9.9Mt/year and 2025 output ~6.3Mt/year span several product families, not 3.6Mt of empty, instantly restartable DAP capacity. Plants can be limited by ore, acid, sulfur, ammonia, maintenance, process permits and demand mix. CHS/OCP's announced Waggaman joint venture would produce **over one million tonnes a year** and OCP explicitly would supply its phosphoric acid feedstock; it may reduce imports of finished fertilizers but merely shift dependence upstream to imported acid. [S5–S6].

**Priority question for a sulfur recovery pilot:** How much *incremental delivered elemental S/qualified H2SO4 at Mosaic's viable price and specification* would move a named facility from current output toward its approved operating rate? A 100kt/year sulfur supply improvement could theoretically support ~289–321kt/year of grade-46 fertilizer on a wet-acid mass balance, if no other constraints bind. That is potential, not restart authorization.

## 3. Recovery pathway opportunity ranking — physically distinct sources

| Candidate stream | Technological maturity | What can become saleable / usable | Critical bottleneck | ORCS priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing refinery sulfur recovery / acid gas H2S | Commercial | Elemental sulfur, acid feed, potentially steam | H2S captured already? available incremental tonnes, permits, price, sulfur recovery unit capacity | **1A — most immediate** |
| Natural-gas sweetening / gas processing tail gas | Commercial | Recovered sulfur, acid | Feed H2S and solvent/Claus unit system, logistics | **1B** |
| Spent industrial H2SO4 regeneration | Commercial in appropriate facilities | Qualified reconstituted acid / SO2 conversion | Contaminants, acid grade, transport, energy, site contracts | **1C** |
| Nonferrous smelter SO2 capture | Commercial | Sulfuric acid from sulfur-bearing off-gas | Captured already? offtake, distance, tonnage, local emissions permit | **1D** |
| Industrial wastewater / biogas sulfide | Treatment established; sulfur recovery varies | Elemental S/H2S-derived products | Streams commonly dilute, variable, corrosion; likely small scale per site | **2A** |
| Pyrite/other sulfur-bearing sulfides | Long-established processing but site-specific | SO2 → acid / heat | Ore impurities, smelter/roaster emissions, capex, competing minerals | **2B** |
| Conventional FGD/synthetic gypsum | Commercial gypsum recovery; acid reversal hard | Tested gypsum products or, with extra reduction/conversion, potential sulfur species | Reduction energy, cost, byproduct solids/CO2, grade | **3A — do not treat as cheap sulfur** |
| Phosphogypsum stacks | Massive inventory, limited approved uses | Research on REEs, gypsum use and possibly sulfate chemical recovery | Radium/radon, metals, low value density, costly acid reversal, EPA approval | **3B — regulated research only** |
| Desal brine sulfate | Feed in solution, not concentrated elemental sulfur | Selective gypsum, magnesium, K, Br, salt; sulfate recovery after testing | Recovery energy, feed chemistry, concentrate disposal | **3C — mineral sidecar, not emergency acid** |

The EPA specifically identifies sulfuric acid production from **burned elemental sulfur, the decomposition of sulfuric-acid-containing waste, and nonferrous metallurgical byproduct gases**; spent-acid regulation/qualification is context-dependent. These are real established paths and should outrank speculative extraction of sulfur from already-oxidized sulfate. [S7–S8]

**Why gypsum isn't a free sulfur replacement:** CaSO4·2H2O has sulfur at ~18.6% by dry pure mineral mass, but the sulfur is oxidized sulfate. It is not elemental sulfur; to create sulfuric acid via alternative processes requires energy and reagents/equipment to break/re-route calcium sulfate bonds and control waste streams. Pure 1Mt gypsum contains ~186kt of sulfur atoms, an **absolute element-content ceiling**, NOT 186kt commercially recoverable sulfur or 570kt low-cost acid. Reusing clean existing synthetic gypsum as soil or building material may be easier and more immediately valuable than turning it back into acid; using a waste already entering a legitimate market is not new supply.

**Phosphogypsum warning:** USGS notes roughly **1 billion tonnes already accumulated in Florida stacks**, and around **30Mt new material per year** in its cited account, but EPA identifies naturally occurring radium/radon hazards and generally requires engineered stacks with specific approved use conditions. Stack inventory is not safe fertilizer, aggregate, housing fill or acid feed merely because a process flow diagram draws an arrow. Lab leach testing, radioactive material assays, permits and full lifecycle economics are mandatory. [S9–S10] A 2026 pilot research paper assessed rare earth recovery from phosphogypsum at a 5t/h design scale, but is not proof of commercial national deployment. [S11]

**Do not relocate environmental burdens:** Recovering sulfur or REEs cannot increase community radon, air SO2/mist, heavy metals in crops, brine contamination or unlicensed waste disposal. Avoid harmful shortcuts in the name of resource resilience.

## 4. Reserves and domestic-first material accounting

The U.S. objective should be resilient availability, not forced trade shutdowns. A proposed 20%-of-annual-consumption strategic inventory equals about **73 days** of average use. A **90-day** reserve equals approximately **24.7%** of annual consumption. These two thresholds must never be described as identical. Reserve inventories require grade and shelf-life qualification, verified location, working capital, legal ownership, disaster continuity and port/rail/interstate backup logistics.

For sulfur/acid/phosphate, track physical tonnes and phosphorus nutrient equivalent; distinguish sulfur stored as elemental raw sulfur, concentrated corrosive acid, imported phosphoric acid and finished DAP. Finished DAP may be the safest reserve for farmers; acid transport/storage can impose greater incident risk. The domestic-first / reserve-before-exports framework is a **proposed procurement, emergency inventory and incentive policy**: it cannot by itself establish federal legal authority to prohibit private exports or abrogate existing contracts. Model lawful alternatives first: commercial reserve contracts, voluntary producer offtake, domestic-first procurement premiums, and permitted inventory floors. Any export control requires an independent statutory and legal review.

## 5. DPA and policy status — not automatic project authorization

Executive Order 14427 of Sept 8 2026 adjusts DPA energy authority delegation to Energy and Interior secretaries. Reuters Oct 13 reports an administration plan for DPA-assisted expansion of **Delek Tyler** refinery (roughly 74k bpd existing refinery processing), intended for commercial and military fuel, with **incremental bpd, dollar amount, construction sequence and physical completion NOT established**. Separately, DOE in June announced up to $500M of DPA Title III support for specified coal power and export projects, showing that selected industrial assets may receive program aid, but those named awards do not finance ORCS. [S25–S28]

Three possible nonautomatic **screening** topics: verified process chemical capacity critical to defense supply chains; reliability systems for named critical defense manufacturing operations; and qualified energy or materials equipment needed to address documented national-security production bottlenecks. Eligibility, rated priority orders and funding are distinct. DPA neither creates free private grants nor suspends emissions and safety obligations as a blanket matter. Title III or other support requires appropriation, project selection and compliant contracting. The FY2027 NDAA (authorization) is separate from actual appropriations and project orders.

For Tyler specifically, heat-recovery, refinery wastewater reuse, reliable process operation, sulfur capture/offtake and incremental qualified fuel supply are reasonable due-diligence projects. There is no verified LNG IMPORT regasification cold stream on the Tyler oil refinery; don't sell a cryogenic retrofit there as cheap without a distinct actual cold source.

## 6. Desal brine and dual mineral/fertilizer value

The ORCS named register now has **91 facility/project records** across various historical and proposed operating states; this does NOT mean 91 operating desal brine streams with metered chemistry. Earlier standardized scenario isolates three named seawater RO plants with ~78MGD combined nameplate product capacity; applying 90% uptime and assumed 50% recovery yields ~97million m³/year of reject brine. The theoretical 50%-of-feed-ion capture sensitivities for that named seawater subset (e.g., MgO equivalent, K2O, gypsum, bromine) remain speculative physical recovery, **not tonnes of merchant grade product or dollar revenue**. Brackish water salinity and ion chemistry require their own assays. Full recoverable U.S. mineral inventory is UNKNOWN.

Current Chinese comparison:
- **Caofeidian Sanyou:** Chinese 2026 industrial-trial accounts show ultrafiltration/nanofiltration hardness removal and multi-stage reverse osmosis concentration. Roughly 3t upstream concentrated brine → 2t fresh water + 1t liquid refined brine at ~120g/L salinity is reported, not independently audited field throughput and not proof of salt crystallization. [S29]
- **Dongjiakou:** Real, separately reported RMB108M cold-use Rankine/HVAC demonstration projected **26GWh/year electricity and 8GWh/year avoided purchased cooling**, currently developmental/under construction in 2026; separate RMB230M LNG-assisted ASU agreement. The larger **LNG→ASU→freeze-desal→mineral recovery→compute cooling** integration is Atlas' **PROPOSED** system and no measured integrated facility is established. [S30]
- **US LNG exporters at Corpus Christi** consume refrigeration to liquefy pipeline gas. They are NOT equivalent to import regasification stations where cold is otherwise dumped in vaporization. An export terminal may still be an edge-AI/heat-recovery/biosolar/BESS candidate, but no free cold credit to operating liquefaction compressors.

Test four processes on identical assay: A (NF/RO alone), B (NF/RO+electric freeze), C (RO+LNG waste-cold freeze), D (**NF/RO+LNG cryogenic split for freezing + warmer branch for data-center chilled loop + server heat to melt washed ice**). A data-center return fluid heated by servers cannot afterward be assumed cold enough to crystallize ice; use temperature-ordered or independent parallel cold branches with freeze duties first. Run water-ion balance, kWh electricity, cryogenic exergy, cleaning, water quality, materials tests, capex/opex and thermal availability. No claim the four-way hybrid is installed in China or America.

## 7. U.S. LNG cold / domestic peak-shaver opportunity

PHMSA lists **186 U.S. LNG facility records in service in 2025**, a mixed population including peak-shavers, export liquefiers, holding sites and receiving terminals. This is a scope for a national database, NOT proof that 186 regasification streams discard heat-exchange-ready cold. EIA reported **13.786 Bcf 2025 LNG imports**, approximately 0.283Mt LNG at assumed conversion ~48.7Bcf/Mt. LNG specific cryogenic thermal potential ~830kJ/kg gives **~65GWhth/y import stream only**, before recovered share, existing cold use, hourly schedules or geography. [S19–S21]

**Illustrative domestic vaporization fleet**, not a historical PHMSA throughput estimate:

|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 25×25 | 144 | 14.4 | 11.7 | 26.1 |
| Mid | 75×50 | 865 | 86.5 | 70.2 | 156.7 |
| High | 150×100 | 3,458 | 345.8 | 281.0 | 626.8 |

These scenarios cannot be added to each other or claimed as actual US wasted energy. **The true annual domestic utility LNG volume vaporized is missing**; PHMSA storage inventory and nameplate alone are insufficient. The electricity conversion fractions are approximate sensitivity inputs, not measured net ORC turbine performance. Annual mean electric-equivalent 3.0/17.9/71.6MW in those cases is not firm at PJM peak hours; winter peaking may make output intermittent and potentially coincident with winter grid stress. The high case 15Mt/y domestic vaporization is extremely speculative absent utility records.

Best US site screen: actual vaporizing LNG terminals such as Everett MA, dual-mode terminals only when verified receiving LNG, then utility domestic peak-shavers by actual annual vaporizer tonnes and cold kW. Cold recovery may be practical for industrial process refrigeration and cooled **offsite** data halls with isolation; locating servers literally inside a hazardous LNG exclusion zone is not required.

## 8. Industrial inference: energy-positive Lattice and comparative economics

China MIIT documented >230 selected excellence smart factories with **production efficiency +22.3%**, defects −50.2%, and carbon emissions −20.4% on its reported programme statistics. Those are **factory-wide, selection-biased outcomes**, not causal LLM inference efficiency percentages. Google's live data-center ML reduced *cooling* consumption up to 40% in a specific deployment, not the entire plant's fuel or electricity. A 2026 Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory analytical cement case suggested **3–9% modeled net primary energy/emissions reduction**, not metered performance. These establish engineering motivation, not an automatic universal percentage for every factory. [S13–S15]

**Corrected compute bill:** A 100kW IT node operating at 80% utilization and PUE1.2 consumes **100×0.8×8760×1.2 = 840,960kWh = 0.841GWh annually**. Earlier v3.2 reported **1.051GWh** after inadvertently omitting the 80%-utilization factor (1.051 is the 100%-utilization figure); do not repeat.

Hypothetical site A: 100GWh/year metered process electricity and a 2% real process improvement →2GWh/year gross saved, **1.159GWh/year net electricity** after the 100kW node, worth ~$115,900/year at a hypothetical $0.10/kWh, **before** edge-node capital, cybersecurity, controls changes, instrument calibration and software costs. Such a small energy margin might NOT pay for a 100kW node. A 10kW inference appliance may fit better. Hypothetical 25Mt LNG export site using a **research-specific** 0.281kWh/kg electricity baseline produces 7,025GWh/year process electricity; a 2% improvement would save 140.5GWh gross less 0.841GWh local IT = **139.659GWh/y**, before costs. The plant baseline and marginal improvement are NOT actual Cheniere/Venture Global metered data.

**Deploy only read-only and human-approved control advice at first:** equipment health and predictive maintenance; permitted wastewater/brine quality forecasts; sulfur/acid logistics and refiner dispatch planning; commodity demand reserves; solar soiling, inverter faults, clipping/curtailment forecasting; price-aware BESS controls subject to ISO instructions. Safety instrumented systems and hazardous process interlocks must be independent and deterministic, with separate approval, audit and rollback.

**Jupiter vs Lattice tests:** Jupiter central training and general cloud inference benefits from model development economies, centralized staff and high utilization; Lattice distributed on-site inference benefits from shorter OT-control latency, local-data governance, intermittent backhaul support and potentially process-coupled waste heat/cooling. Deploying a model centrally vs locally with identical industrial tasks is the right A/B test; training research and factory control cannot be compared solely by FLOPs. A distributed network is energy-/cash-positive only if measured incremental productivity, conserved energy, avoided downtime and recovered products exceed amortized IT/BESS/solar/security/people/maintenance costs.

## 9. Biosolar: real generation, variable gain, complete multi-benefit ROI

EIA's **389TWh of solar electricity actually generated in 2025** includes 296TWh utility-scale, largely ground-mounted, plus 93TWh small-scale, not all suitable for vegetated roofs. NREL's 2016 **1,432TWh/year** of *all potential rooftop PV* is a reproducible engineering modeling study, **not** existing generation or automatically coverable green-roof area. [S16, S31].

A UTS Australian field study found **11.1% average PV yield uplift in four summer months**, up to 23.25% at extreme heat, plus stormwater and biological benefits. A 2026 multi-year European field analysis, correcting structural bias, found **0.95–1.14%** net PV gains over March–August; unmaintained vegetation reduced or reversed yield. Thus test **1%, 5%, and 11.1% annual uplift** independently; none is established U.S. fleet-wide. [S17–S18]

**Incremental retrofits of already-PV equipped sites:** model 93TWh EIA small-scale generation as a *screening universe only*, then an eligible share (e.g., 25%). This yields 23.25TWh annual baseline participating PV; extra gain **0.233/1.163/2.581TWh/year** at 1/5/11.1% hypothetical annual yield. The entire 93TWh is NOT roof eligibility confirmed. At a stronger but unlikely 100% of all 389TWh national PV exposure and 11.1% annual uplift, math gives +43.2TWh but mostly ground arrays are not conventional roof-biosolar retrofit candidates. At 10% of NREL rooftop *newbuild* technical potential, the main output is **143.2TWh/y new conventional PV**, plus hypothetical +15.9TWh if fully greened and +11.1% annual uplift; do not claim all new PV energy is vegetation yield.

**Cost per recurring additional TWh/y** assuming participating PV roof output 261kWh/m²-year (24.25kWh/ft²-year) and all participating area treated:
| Incremental vegetation installed cost / ft² | +11.1% annual uplift: capex per additional TWh/y | 25yr/7% levelized incremental PV $/MWh, excludes O&M | +1% annual uplift: capex per additional TWh/y |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $0.10 (ground-seeding-like, not verified rooftop install) | ~$37M | ~$3.2/MWh | ~$412M |
| $0.25 (low-cost scenario, not bid) | ~$93M | ~$8/MWh | ~$1.03B |
| $1 (lightweight roof installation scenario, not bid) | ~$372M | ~$31.9/MWh | ~$4.12B |
| $5 (engineered installation scenario) | ~$1.86B | ~$159/MWh | ~$20.6B |
| $15 (conventional full roof floor in EPA cited range) | ~$5.57B | ~$478/MWh | ~$61.9B |

Important: **$0.10–$0.25/ft² hydroseeding on prepared ground** is not a quote for putting saturated vegetation/substrate atop occupied commercial roofs. EPA cites full extensive green roofs often **$15–35/ft²** and typical ongoing maintenance after initial years **$0.10–$1.00/ft²-year**. Structural reinforcement, cranes, waterproofing, wind and fire setbacks, irrigation/drainage, membrane life and PV shade management all vary. A truly low-cost, modular, roof-rated living layer deserves a real trial, but standard yard planting prices are NOT automatically executable installation costs. [S32]

**Combined ROI** may improve because cooling, stormwater retention, flood reduction, worker comfort/beautification, biodiversity and roof membrane longevity are real separate effects. They must be monetized by measured site conditions, not added as arbitrary per-ft² benefits or counted twice as PV electricity. Ground-mounted solar farms deserve ground-cover agrivoltaics/pollinator habitat screening with a *different* shading/albedo/water model, rather than forcing a green-roof cost onto acreage.

## 10. Grid adequacy, peak storage, and on-site resilience

PJM's July 2026 resource capacity auction reportedly fell **6.8GW short** of its reliability resource target; this is an auction/prospective adequacy deficit, NOT observed rolling blackouts in 13 states. DOE issued Oct12 federal grid emergency authority but PJM said specified emergency backup resources were not needed for operating days Oct15–16. DOE's **>35GW nationwide unused backup generation** is not equivalent to permitted, fuel-backed, interconnected, immediately dispatchable PJM MW. [S22–S24]

A hypothetical sustained **6.8GW shortage for 4 hours** requires 27.2GWh **delivered**; 80%-usable storage requires ~34GWh BESS **nameplate** with >=6.8GW inverter grid delivery, plus inverter/dispatch/network margins. Four hours EVERY day requires 9.928TWh/year delivered, and ~11.68TWh/year input at an assumed 85% charge/discharge round-trip efficiency. Firm 6.8GW 24/7 would consume 59.568TWh/year before losses. Aggregate nationwide solar TWh cannot establish nighttime PJM emergency deliverability without actual same-zone hourly charging, extended cloudy winter weather, transmission and reserves.

At a local 1MW IT node, PUE1.2 →1.2MW facility rated load; 4h needs 4.8MWh discharge; at 80%-usable battery = **6MWh nameplate**, before pumps, degradation, emergencies or other site critical loads. Batteries STORE electrical energy, do not create more net kilowatt-hours. Cold thermal storage, liquid-air energy storage, and conventional BESS should be separately modeled with all external thermal/electrical inputs. Co-siting alone does not make an installation zero-grid-impact if dedicated gas generation competes for public pipelines/fuel.

Venture Global's Oct15 2026 announcement of **up to 16 gas-fired 720MW power islands** and potential **up to 10GW adjacent data centers by 2034** is commercial validation of industrial power+compute co-siting interest, **not** a commissioned 11.52GW plant, ORCS implementation or LNG import cold-energy scheme; future orders, fuels, emissions and customer commitments still matter. [S33]

## 11. Integrated demonstration design and ranked investment priorities

**Pilot 1: Gulf fertilizer sulfur-and-acid recovery corridor.** Select an actually constrained producer's process-unit boundary, obtain contractually available sulfur recovery, tail gas acid, existing spent-acid regeneration and dock/rail/barge delivery, independent product specifications. Baseline sulfur tonnes *delivered* and sulfur-contract cost. Deliver 5–25kt/year additional verified sulfur-equivalent as a **test scale**, not a claim this reserve exists. Quantify avoided phosphate curtailment and counterfactual price, do not mix as free incremental revenue. Start with known chemistry rather than gypsum reduction. Include ship/port resilience and phosphogypsum permitted disposal.

**Pilot 2: Gulf refinery/chemical industrial inference and energy.** Read-only, cyber-segmented edge node connected to sensors, historian mirror and advisory dashboards. Benchmark electricity, fuel and steam normalized by bbl/tonne production, operator override, avoided unplanned downtime, raw sensor WAN rates. Evaluate 10/25/100kW AI appliance sizes. Add behind-the-meter rooftop PV/BESS only if marginal installed cost and real-time grid peak benefits are competitive.

**Pilot 3: PV rooftop biosolar pilot across 3 U.S. climates.** Pair installed PV arrays of same azimuth, tilt, module, inverter and height, planted versus comparable nonvegetated cool roof and routine cleaning/control. Measure one full year at minimum (preferably 24 months), 1%/5%/11.1% comparison, irrigation/wet loading, habitat, stormwater and cooling reduction. Ordinary low-cost ground cover at ground-mounted sites is a separate control. Measure BESS use on peak reliability and solar forecasts; never claim battery net electric creation.

**Pilot 4: Authentic LNG IMPORT + cold value pilot.** Locate active regasification tonnage and hourly cryogenic cold schedule, test conventional vaporizer baseline, site cooling service and/or Rankine power, environmental and process permitting. Validate >95% purity claims only in their tested enriched-feed research setting, confirm no antiscalant-toxic agricultural use, discharges and water quality. ## 12. Economic decision rule and minimum evidence needed

For each node calculate:
- **Benefit:** verified additional net product yield × REAL offtake margin; verified electricity/fuel/purchased acid avoided; qualifying incremental cooling energy saved; avoided downtime and transport; lawful reserve security value; monetized green-roof stormwater or HVAC savings; optional measured quality-of-life/community indicators (reported separately if non-cash).
- **Cost:** annualized capex, all energy/fuel/parasitic refrigeration, chemicals/consumables, transport, labor, cybersecurity, instrument/OT safety, roof irrigation/wet-load/reinforcement, residuals/disposal, risk transfer and finance. Include baseline facility upgrades that would occur without AI anyway.
- **Decision:** require positive risk-adjusted value under low-case commodity and weather conditions, plus safe operating permit, proven local process conservation and independent audit. Use counterfactual controls and publish the provenance of every coefficient.

**Critical unknowns**: actual sulfur tonnes needed at individual Mosaic plants; sulfur recovered but currently exported vs otherwise discarded; 2025/2026 PHMSA domestic LNG annual vaporization, not just LNG tanks; true U.S. physically green-roof-eligible installed PV generation; 12-month biosolar uplift in U.S. climates; cost and life of a genuinely inexpensive low-load vegetation roof module; colocated data center value net of process IT draw, and grid-region actual peak dispatch. Without these, national technical potentials remain scenarios, not forecast production.
