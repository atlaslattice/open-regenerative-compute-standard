# ORCS v1.5 — Corrected U.S. gypsum material gap, 20% reserve, and real named desal facilities

**2026-10-14 | EVIDENCE-GATED QUANTITATIVE SCREEN | NOT A VERIFIED MINERAL RECOVERY PLAN**

## Critical USGS correction
USGS MCS2026 gypsum (2025 estimates, metric tonnes) states crude mined 20.0 Mt, *domestic synthetic* 17.0 Mt, imports crude/anhydrite 6.8 Mt, crude exports 0.038 Mt, apparent total gypsum use 44.0 Mt. The simplistic **44 − 20 = 24 Mt** is **NOT a 24 Mt net supply gap**, because it ignores already-used domestic synthetic gypsum and imported gypsum. True import exposure ~6.8 Mt/y (6.762 Mt net crude imports), USGS net import reliance 15%. The accounting rest of ~0.2 Mt is rounding/category differences. Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026.pdf (gypsum chapter).

Assume **strategic reserve stock target 20% of annual total consumption**, achieved within **one year** (a scenario, not established U.S. policy). 20%×44 = **8.8 Mt stock build**, plus 6.8 Mt annual imported supply replaced → **15.6 Mt qualified incremental domestic supply in first year**; yearly replacement ongoing ~6.8 Mt after reserve accumulated plus withdrawals/losses. Alternate net-crude trade quantity 6.8−0.038=6.762 Mt leads 15.562 Mt; use approximate due mixing product categories. Reserve stock is finite inventory, not a perpetual annual 20% extra production target. Existing industry inventories not measured; 8.8 Mt should be reduced by any usable verified preexisting reserve.

Harbor Island previously modeled 0.733 Mt/year *theoretical* gypsum-equivalent at 100 MGD finished water and ~191.2 MGD anticipated discharge; its federal permitting **completed September 2025**, but it is **NOT operating** and commercial gypsum recovery/assays unknown. Source: https://www.permits.performance.gov/permitting-project/fast-41-covered-projects/harbor-island-seawater-desalination-facility and earlier ORCS Harbor Island brief. This value is only a gross chemical ceiling, not delivered CaSO4·2H2O.

For **illustrative normalized capacity scaling only** from Harbor Island's equivalent 733,000 t/y / 100 MGD, gross potential ~7,330 tonnes gypsum-equivalent per MGD-year assuming *identical seawater feed chemistry, RO recovery and operating days*. Under that physically unsupported assumption, those four named seawater projects' listed capacity equals 178 MGD → 1.30474 Mt/y gross inventory; excluding proposed Harbor Island, existing operating three seawater plants 78 MGD → 0.57174 Mt/y. These are NOT real mineral yields and two actual plants have different process routing/chemistry.

Brackish plants CANNOT inherit seawater Ca/SO4 concentration ratios. For all sites, verified intake and reject flow, paired Ca and SO4 assays, contaminant/salt profile, solubility chemistry, purity, recovery, uptime, market, permit and isolation from seawater mixing must be obtained.

## Agricultural safety is achievable in principle, not automatically qualified
CaSO4·2H2O (gypsum) precipitated from brine is chemically plausible for agronomic use *after* filtration, washing, drying or conditioning, and sufficient testing to meet applicable product, contaminant, salinity and performance requirements. NaCl carryover can harm soil; sulfates, trace contaminants and crop/soil-specific dosing matter. Gypsum can improve sodic/Al-affected soils and supply Ca/S, but not automatically substitute N/P/K fertilizers. Competing sulfuric-acid feed, direct soil use and reserve cannot double-count tonnes.

## Model caveats
The named site list is **not a complete national census**; 100 plants were never established as 100 Harbor Island equivalents. Full-scale U.S. mix includes many brackish and small seawater systems whose concentrations differ. A real national feasibility answer requires each site's accessible brine and gypsum assays, not nameplate capacity alone. Strategic reserve locality and grade matter. This quantitative analysis is a **planning screen**, not estimated future production.
