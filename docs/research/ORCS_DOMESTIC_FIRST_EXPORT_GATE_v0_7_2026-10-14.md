# ORCS v0.7 — Domestic Sufficiency, Resilience and Affordability Before Exports

**2026-10-14 | PROPOSED POLICY INVARIANT | EMPIRICAL ONLY | NON-OPERATIONAL | ZERO REALIZED CREDIT**

## Explicit priority
Domestic supply, national and local resilience, and consumer affordability take priority over exports. Evaluate exports only when all three conditions have been independently verified. This is the U.S. ORCS scenario objective hierarchy, not merely a petroleum option.

1. Domestic sufficiency: measured grade-compatible and geographically deliverable supply serves households, essential services, agriculture and industry, with regional shortage vetoes. National surplus cannot mask local scarcity.
2. Resilience: measured, geographically accessible national, state, regional and local physical reserves; critical infrastructure continuity targets verified. Grid reserve margins and microgrid islandability are not interchangeable with excess generated MWh.
3. Affordability: observed regional prices for relevant commodities meet published targets. USD $3/US gallon gasoline is a proposed scenario input, not an existing national cap or a target for all commodities. Include equity and cost-to-serve checks.
4. Residual export candidacy: only remaining provably deliverable surplus may be evaluated for trade, subject to independent legal authority, safety, environmental and commercial agreements. The simulation never actually authorizes exports or controls private assets.

## Evidence states
PASS, FAIL, UNKNOWN. Incomplete, stale, synthetic or contradictory data never count as PASS. Report provenance/source receipts, geography, time interval, quality, and accessibility for every decision. An UNKNOWN prerequisite prevents export-candidate certification, while allowing human-authorized research and evaluation.

## Policy and economic constraints
Tariffs, import restrictions, export controls, voluntary preferential procurement, reserve policy, and lawful Jones Act waivers require separately researched legal authority and adverse-outcome sensitivity. Export restrictions can reduce domestic production investment or induce retaliation; affordability cannot be assessed solely from a fuel pump threshold, and economic turnover cannot be conflated with GDP or welfare. Emergency exceptions and existing contracts require review.
