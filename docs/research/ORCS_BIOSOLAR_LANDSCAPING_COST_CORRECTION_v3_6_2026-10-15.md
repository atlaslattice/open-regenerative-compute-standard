## Correction: the $1.86B / incremental TWh-year was not merely seed/plant cost
Earlier v3.5 calculated $1.86B upfront per **one additional TWh/year of annual solar production** for $5/sf hypothetical installed green roof. It is not $1.86B per TWh of EXISTING solar output, and scaling it by 1432 TWh/year technical POTENTIAL without reducing denominator to the biosolar increment is mathematically invalid. 1432 TWh/y rooftop PV NREL is all-suitable **technical PV potential** not installed output (https://www.nrel.gov/grid/news/press/2016/24662). At assumed annual 11.1% PV uplift, all-roof extra = 158.95TWh/y (if physically eligible) rather than 1432TWh/y. Scale all-roof upgrades on footprint, not added generation alone.

## Standard landscaping evidence versus on-roof engineered system
September 2026 US consumer contractor estimate hydroseeding **$0.10-$0.25/sf**, median $0.15/sf, prepared ground excludes soil prep ($0.30-$1.50/sf) (https://costhomebook.com/landscaping/hydroseeding-cost). Assumptions for specialized rooftop living cover should not equate to lawn hydroseeding because waterproofing, root barrier, drainage, media, saturated structural load, access and safety matter.
EPA engineering BMP notes green roof MATERIAL only ~$1-$3/sf, full installed extensive roof typically ~$15-$35/sf, with crane costs and high rooftop placement labor; after 5y maintenance ~$0.10-$1.00/sf/y (https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2021-11/bmp-green-roofs.pdf). EPA arid green roof guideline gives shallow extensive loose vegetation layer $2-$6/sf (historical not 2026 quotes) https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100Y9NB.txt . Full engineered rooftop ≠ cost of planting grass. User's low-cost hypothesis $0.10/$0.25/$0.50/$1/sf needs a genuine already-compatible roof, substrate and drainage, no cranes/structural changes and validated biological survival.

## Global NREL roof potential scaling requires matching numerator and denominator
At 1432TWh/year technical rooftop PV and hypothetical 11.1% annual gain: 158.952 TWh/year **extra generation**. 1432 TWh baseline alone is NOT 1432 additional TWh biosolar.
All eligible full national rooftop PV roof area is not directly the same as 2016 PV module area; if approximating with 261kWh/m2/year, area=5.4866billion m2 or about **59.06 billion square feet**. The historical 2016 PV roof suitability assessment estimated ~8.13 billion m2 suitable roof, and with different coverage assumptions these are NOT interchangeable. Cost of treating assumed PV footprint at $0.10/$0.25/$0.50/$1/$2/$5 per sf becomes **$5.91B/$14.77B/$29.53B/$59.06B/$118.12B/$295.3B**, versus incorrect $1.4T inference. This is an ALL-ROOF technical scenario, NOT rollout budget for existing roof PV fleet. Solar-related footprint has modules occupying far less than all roofs; a green roof may need more surface than module footprint. Physical roof structure and existing suitability can reduce possible deployment.
Use measured rooftop class and PV inventory to avoid imagining all technical roof potential physically suitable for living vegetation or retrofit.

## Important contradictory measured biosolar gain study
2026 multi-year field study **correcting layout bias** reported only +0.95–1.14% PV yield gains March–August, and unmanaged vegetation overgrowth reversed them: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378778826005724 . An 11.1% Australian summer study is not universal. This materially affects the energy-only cost: at 1.0% PV uplift, the incremental PV per sf falls by factor 11.1, so $0.25/sf capital per additional TWh/year goes from ~$92.9M to ~$1.03B and annualized capex ~$88.5/MWh (rather than $7.98/MWh) before maintenance. Need climate-controlled head-to-head measurement and non-electric cooling/roof/QOL ledgers.

## Valid pilots
Choose genuinely low-load vegetated roofs that already have compatible soil/drainage, or roof-ready newbuild prefab, and separately test no-substrate ground planting at PV array as agrivoltaics. Measure supply bids for sedum, moss, native flower/pollinator cover and grass, roofing integration, fire and safe access, seasonal water and maintenance, solar panel shading and yield, building cooling kWh, stormwater credits, and worker amenity/QOL. Avoid grass roots and wet soil directly on normal commercial membranes. Compare against reflective cool roof, planted ground around solar farms, artificial shade, evaporative cooling and active module cooling.
