# ORCS v1.7 — 100-real-facility brine recovery scale programme + industrial gypsum

**2026-10-14 · PROPOSED / EVIDENCE-GATED / NOT PHYSICAL DEPLOYMENT / ZERO REALIZED CREDIT**

## Purpose and correction
The U.S. already has **far more than 100 desalination facilities across all feedwater types**, so the ambitious national programme should inventory and screen at least 100 real, named facilities, rather than pretend only a handful exist. A November 21, 2025 Congressional Research Service report cites a 2018 municipal technology census identifying **13 seawater and almost 400 brackish desalination facilities** (including some nanofiltration), with an update anticipated to identify another 50–70. These numbers are historical census totals, NOT verified 2026 operating recoverable calcium-sulfate plants. https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-11-21_IN12378_f667edaa4ac4f641d01b49cdd6915adcc7ea39ad.pdf

TWDB separately quotes roughly 325 brackish groundwater facilities in older national studies; do not add this population to CRS's ~400 because of **overlap and differing dates/methods**. https://www.twdb.texas.gov/innovativewater/desal/faq.asp

## Scale rules
1. Assemble **>=100 uniquely named, genuine desal installations**, recorded by facility name/operator/county/state, operation year, status, source URL, source type (seawater, brackish groundwater, brackish surface, industrial reuse), rated finished-water MGD, measured *annual actual* water delivery, brine outlet location/flow and chemistry if published.
2. Deduplicate multiple RO skids at the same municipal plant, distinguish proposed projects from active sites, and record incomplete rows without invented capacities. A city name alone is not proof of a named active installation.
3. No seawater-model gypsum/MGD coefficient may be applied to inland brackish RO sources. Brackish source calcium/sulfate mineralogy may differ drastically. Harbor Island 0.733 Mt/y is a *proposed site's theoretical chemical inventory*, not a transferable actual yield.
4. Stage gates: documentary existence → capacity/flow confirmation → brine calcium/sulfate assay → technically separated calcium sulfate → salt/chloride/trace/radionuclide and agricultural qualification → downstream delivery economics → national/regional reserves → legal exports only beyond sufficiency, resilience and affordability.
5. Count 100 screened sites as programme coverage; **do not equate 100 facilities with 100 productive mineral-recovery plants**.

## Existing gypsum volume to leverage, without double-counting
USGS MCS 2026 gypsum (2025 estimated, million tonnes/year):
- mined/crude **20.0**; synthetic **17.0 already used**; imports **6.8**; exports **0.038**; total consumption **44.0**; net-import reliance about **15%**.
- reported gypsum manufacturing scrap recycled onsite **~0.700 Mt/y, already recycled**. It is not a new source unless incremental recovery above baseline is proven.
- Official stockpile **none** in MCS; existing private stocks not measured.
Sources https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-gypsum.pdf ; https://www.usgs.gov/centers/national-minerals-information-center/gypsum-statistics-and-information

### Priority industrial residuary workstreams
- **FGD gypsum**: existing coal plant flue gas desulfurization coproduct; identify unutilized/stockpiled *incremental* lots, not the ~17Mt/y synthetic gypsum already used. EPA's 2023 beneficial use evaluation and product constituent database distinguish conditional agricultural usability. https://www.epa.gov/coal-combustion-residuals/beneficial-use-evaluation-flue-gas-desulfurization-fgd-gypsum-agriculture
- **Phosphogypsum**: very large existing stacks and ongoing phosphate fertilizer byproduct, but radionuclide/impurity/environmental restrictions are material, and some EPA approvals have changed through time. This is a research/pilot opportunity only, not automatically releaseable ag/acid supply. Use current EPA rules and actual stacks/permit assessment. https://www.epa.gov/radiation/phosphogypsum
- **Recovered wallboard and construction/demolition gypsum**: user-specific processing, source sorting, painting/adhesives/paper contaminant quality, proven additional recoverability. USGS says many sources have unknown actual quantities. Do not assume all landfill gypsum is recoverable.
- **Desal concentrate precipitates**: measure site calcium/sulfate and reject flow, determine whether economically viable gypsum precipitation occurs, preserve NaCl/salt contaminant boundaries. Brackish and seawater columns separate.

## Proposed 20% reserve target
The earlier ORCS first-year screen assumed 2025 U.S. gypsum apparent consumption ~44Mt/y, import substitution 6.8Mt/y + one-time 20%-of-consumption reserve build 8.8Mt = **15.6Mt first-year incremental qualified supply**, ignoring any existing usable reserve. This is a user-specified POLICY SCENARIO, not a known government mandate or an actual deficit of 15.6Mt. Historical USGS *44-20 = 24Mt gap* is incorrect due to domestic synthetic gypsum already in supply. Verify grade-specific national need and reasonable inventory costs before purchasing or allocating reserve.

## The demonstration-to-scale argument
Prove **at a few real operating sites** that certified CaSO4 recovery is mechanically repeatable, financially competitive and ecologically better than baseline discharge. Then adapt by assayed chemistry and market to the larger existing plant network. New capital investment is not presumed a substitute for water service continuity; do not build plants solely for mineral output before robust life-cycle and opportunity cost analysis. Existing facility owner approval, environmental permits, port/road access, energy and process economics are binding gates.

## Deliverable after this record
Versioned CSV/JSON census with **>=100 named sites**, each a verifiable source citation, plus prioritized real FGD, phosphogypsum and recycled wallboard locations with incremental tonnes rather than existing-used supply. Then run the threshold model on known quantities; unknown quality/yield stay NULL rather than fictional. No operating plant has been commissioned or contacted in this programme.

## Practical program waves
- Wave A: Already named operating seawater facilities: Carlsbad, Tampa Bay and Santa Barbara. Add named brackish: Kay Bailey Hutchison, H2Oaks, Southmost, Sherman, Horizon Regional MUD, Antioch, etc., subject to accurate chemistry classification.
- Wave B: Enumerate all Texas TWDB named facilities, California State Water Board seawater and municipal brackish references, and Florida DEP potable RO installations; prioritize flow/assay completeness, not geography guessing.
- Wave C: Add FGD stacks and power-plant byproducts with site-specific recoverable, currently unused CaSO4, EPA classification and custody records, then phosphogypsum only with current legal and radiation permitting.
- Wave D: Published comparisons of cost/ton qualified gypsum delivered, resilience/import reductions, crop-soil benefits, acid feed alternative and outfall ecology.
