### Fit-for-purpose irrigation & soil control panel
Every potential farm-bound water lot needs conventional microbial and chemical treatment and continuous/verified monitoring: bacteria/viruses/protozoa, electrical conductivity/TDS, sodium adsorption ratio and sodicity, chloride/boron, metals, PFAS, pesticides/herbicides/pharmaceuticals, endocrine-active molecules, micro/nano plastics quantified by polymer and size distribution, and residual nutrient N/P/K adjusted for crop need and water flux. A PolyPod is only a **microplastic capture sidecar**. It cannot be counted as pathogen control, RO desalination, PFAS neutralization, source chemistry remediation, or potable certification; repeated reuse could raise salinity and damage soil structure. Agricultural runoff and agricultural biosolids are distinct exposure routes. [W25–W28]

**Disaster-specific performance** includes usable full-cycle water m³ stored, pumping stations and distribution backup, generator fuel, BESS power AND energy, metered 1/3/14-day safe output, discharge permit emergency modes, tanker/mobile temporary water, field irrigation priority, water rights, raw intake after hurricanes, cyber isolation, contaminated influent shutoff and clean residual disposal. Being adequate during routine demand is not the same as surviving a three-day blackout plus damaged distribution.

### B. Microplastics: effluent AND sludge two separate lanes

Research at wastewater treatment plants finds that treatment often transfers plastics out of water and INTO solids. An Australian EPA/CSIRO 2026 study observed ~79% removal in a primary STP, >98% in tertiary, with the likely majority ultimately retained in biosolids. A 2025 review records large variability by particle size and treatment, with 95% median tertiary microplastic-removal rate in published studies. Neither proves 'microplastic-free' irrigation or safe sludge. More serious, nanoparticles below standard detection limits may remain; the technology's claimed nanoscale capture is **not** independent validation of such removal at commercial flow. [W20–W23]

EPA 2024 biosolids reports from covered facilities: ~**4.0M dry tonnes sludge generated**, ~**2.39M tonnes land applied**, and remainder incinerated, disposed or other uses. EPA explicitly says the report is incomplete, excluding certain authorized-state reporting and small facilities; **do not call 4Mt U.S. national total**. [W24]

**Mandatory soil-protection sequence:**
1. Screen household textile fibers and industrial plastic sheds at source. Prevent fragmentation in sewers/treatment.
2. Treat reclaimed liquid through verified filtration/PolyPod **plus** risk-adjusted disinfection/chemical control. QA microplastics by polymer/particle size per liter, including effluent after storms and filter bypass.
3. Keep all captured filter particles away from agricultural compost/digestate/biosolids. Monitor captured plastic mass and contamination; disposal/upcycling must be qualified as environmentally sound.
4. Characterize biosolids separately; remove or safely segregate plastics via validated solid-focused technologies or withhold unsafe lots from farms. **Do not assume PolyPod effluent module remediates sludge**.
5. Only apply verified compliant biosolids to cropland. Audit crop soil micro/nanoplastics baseline, changes, N/P/K plant availability, heavy metals, salt, PFAS and pathogen outcomes.
6. Deploy watershed + soil sample geospatial log and risk-gated automated recommendation system, with human and regulatory approvals and public transparent data.

**Soil health cannot be declared restored purely by microplastic reduction.** Soil structure, microbial ecology, carbon, nutrient balance, water infiltration, crop and groundwater outcomes require long-duration soil/field trials; reducing microplastics is an important prevention hypothesis with known contamination pathways, not proof of a prescribed numerical productivity boost.

## 7. Domestic import substitution — revised NATIONAL benefit ledger

For each qualified recovered lot, classify: NaCl (industrial/food/spec by purity), MgO or Mg(OH)2 (different markets and stoichiometry), K2O / KCl fertilizer basis, gypsum/anhydrite for agricultural/building use, bromine/brine derivatives, certified secondary CaCO3; industrial-grade lithium/strontium/boron only if qualified. Compare each **2025–26 U.S. imports at same chemical grade and units**, country concentration and supply disruption risk, domestic reserve floor and local delivery.

Define:
- Recovered mass R of **independently qualified new product**, not theoretical ion content.
- Genuine import substitutable demand I by identical product grade, location and period, net of current domestic supply already committed.
- **Qualified import displacement Q=min(R, I)** after proof of competitive delivered quality, shipping, and supply contract. Extra tonnes may build an approved one-time domestic reserve or displace higher-cost production, but cannot simultaneously be sold as import displacement AND strategic-reserve accumulation.
- **Gross import invoice avoided = Q × baseline imported delivered unit price.**
- Domestic producer sales = Q × domestic delivered product price.
- Industrial buyer savings = Q×(imported delivered price - domestic delivered price). If price differential negative, immediate buyer savings can be negative while security/transport still have value.
- National resource gain should NOT be calculated by adding gross invoice avoided + domestic sales + buyer savings: those are distributional, trade and transfer metrics referring to overlapping output. Compute net foreign-exchange change after imported reagents/capex/services and export inputs, productivity benefit and consumer welfare appropriately.
- Strategic resilience option value = expected avoided losses under **specified** port closure, export controls, cyber/rail failure, drought or hurricane probabilities; report separately until a defendable probability/damage model exists.
- Water/aquifer relief value = avoided pumping energy, depleted groundwater, land subsidence, saltwater intrusion, drought allocation losses and relevant qualified ecological flow, subtract irrigation-reuse infrastructure and contaminant risk.
- Soil health avoided contamination has genuine societal relevance, but without controlled long-term data should be reported as pollution mass/particle flow reduction and risk index, not invented health dollar savings.

**Relevant official import baselines:** 2025 U.S. gross gypsum imports ~6.8Mt; magnesium compounds ~640kt as MgO-equivalent; potash imports ~5.6Mt as K2O-equivalent with net import reliance ~92%; USGS 2025 imported bromine price benchmark ~$3,000/t Br-content (not a local brine product contract). Under the already published **three operating seawater site / 78MGD** scenario at hypothetical 50% capture, gypsum ~171kt (~2.5% gross gypsum imports), MgO~217kt (~33.9% MgO equivalent imports), K2O~45.6kt (~0.81% potash imports). These are **not achieved import reductions** or pure same-grade drop-in without product tests. Exposing the national import reliance and resilience benefit is essential even when private plant profit is not yet proven. [W31–W32]

### Conceptual annual import displacement dollars example
A *hypothetical* 100kt/y **qualified** MgO-equivalent lot with imports of equivalent grade and landed invoice $500/t would displace **$50M/y foreign purchases**. If domestic producer charges $350/t delivered, buyers save **$15M/y** and domestic producer books **$35M/y gross revenues**, while imported consumables, plant capex and recovery OPEX still subtract from national net wealth. Do **not add $50M+$35M+$15M = $100M benefit**: the three figures represent one goods transaction from different viewpoints. Real prices and USGS import grade need verification.

## 8. Deuterium and heavy-water opportunity — included, but correctly valued

Natural seawater D/H atomic ratio ~0.000153–0.000156, or ~**0.0153–0.0156%** H atoms deuterium. Deuterium is stable, **not inherently radioactive**; heavy water becomes hazardous when contaminated with tritium or other radionuclides. At natural ratio, D mass fraction of bulk water is approximately **34.6 parts per million**. Gross isotope equivalents:
| Water throughput | Theoretical D atomic content/y | Theoretical D2O equivalent/y |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100MGD freshwater-equivalent flow, all water passing entire year | ~4,780t D content | ~23,800t D2O |
| Harbor Island original ~291.2MGD total seawater-feed assumption | ~13,900t D content | ~69,300t D2O |
| 34BGD U.S. municipal treated throughput (theoretical nationwide) | ~1.63Mt D content | ~8.1Mt D2O equivalent |

**ALL THREE ROWS ARE ELEMENTAL CONTENT ONLY, NOT PHYSICALLY RECOVERED DEUTERIUM/HEAVY WATER; 0% monetized, no isotope separation by RO or PolyPod.** These inventories are unsurprising because trace D atoms occur in enormous bulk water volumes. The value is gated by enrichment from natural abundance to commercial grade, industrial hazardous process controls, imported inputs, isotopic-fractionation energy and huge unit economics, law/licensing and actual national heavy-water/deuterated-compound off-take demand. Do not extrapolate a $/gram laboratory catalogue price to millions of tonnes.

**Critical 2026 strategic isotope news:** DOE's July 21 2026 Federal Register RFI sought collaboration to repurpose **about 530,000 gallons of already enriched Savannah River Site heavy water**, some tritium-contaminated, potentially avoiding new raw-water heavy-water production. It sought input on purifying/detritiating material, domestic uses in scientific, medical and industrial applications, and downstream contracts. GAO July 2026 separately highlighted U.S. critical isotope dependence and supply-chain vulnerabilities. No ORCS offtake, supply contract or eligibility inferred. [W17–W19]

**Practical priority:** assess whether existing high-enrichment domestic inventory meets U.S. strategic demand first. Study trace isotope harvesting as a strictly separate long-horizon licensed specialty lane. Natural D stock in water is a real physical resource but without proven cost/offtake, accounting realized value should be **zero/undefined** and reserve cannot be constructed from unextracted atoms.

## 9. Disaster resilience: treated water, aquifer security and soil

A water-resource recovery plant cannot be considered 'resilient' because 365-day nameplate water capacity exceeds demand. Evaluate **water accessibility at the exact hour** and event:
- Hurricanes and floodwaters: water intake salinity/turbidity, physical flood protection of switchgear, pipe/fuel outages, biological contamination, filtration debris surge, coastal outfall failure.
- Extreme heat/drought: irrigation supply, reservoir and aquifer minimum safe levels, water temperature algae, electricity demand, crop thresholds, seasonal scarcity.
- Cyber compromise: isolated OT supervisory logic, immutable audit logs, offline/manual fallback, instrumentation independent of AI suggestion, secure chemical dosing and safe shutdown.
- Interstate supply closure: local treatment chemical stock, membrane/media/PolyPod regeneration stock, salt delivery, ferric/chlorine disinfection, fertilizer import ports and spare pumping motors.
- Environmental disaster: contaminants in biosolids, PFAS, salinity, microplastic effluent, degraded crop soils, filtration fouling and groundwater infiltration.
- Infrastructure ramp: backup generator, 4–72h electrical BESS (firm energy and inverter power distinct), on-site water storage with pumping/pipe resilience, portable purification, solar/waste-heat coupling only if qualified and safe.

**Storage equations** per geographic node: emergency potable+farm water m³/day × service days; normal treatment kWh/m³ × emergency required m³/day + pumps/disinfection × backup days; BESS nameplate = critical MWh/usable fraction minus independently firm generator contribution (with outage derating). Batteries don't generate water or kWh. Keep contingency discharge standards, permit authority and untreated bypass risk explicit.

A 100MGD facility serving **15% emergency critical throughput** would supply ~15MGD. If incremental treatment uses **3.5kWh/m³** (assumed, broad advanced-treatment proxy), electrical treatment draw ≈**8.28MW** while running, and a **72h uninterrupted island with no fuel-backed generation** would need ~596MWh delivered and **~745MWh nameplate BESS** at 80% usable—not 6MWh of a 1MW inference rack. This shows why normal wastewater secondary effluent reuse, industrial emergency treatment and desal/ZLD cannot all be assigned the same power budget. Emergency re-use could require different treatment energy and substantial local generation/storage/pipe systems. The 3.5 kWh/m³ number is NOT typical verified municipal secondary treatment energy or actual PolyPod power.

### Soil impact measurements and KPI milestones
1. Effluent MP counts (polymer-resolved, per m³) including smallest quantifiable size and fibers.
2. Plastics retained in digestate/biosolids (particles/kg dry), actual sludge disposal path and mass reconciliation.
3. Farm soil plastic particles/kg dry, infiltration capacity, aggregation, microbial function and organic carbon over time; no arbitrary 'microplastic-free' claim below sampling detection limit.
4. Reuse irrigation delivered m³ by crop, salinity conductivity and Na adsorption balance; farm pumping kWh and pumped aquifer levels.
5. Qualified struvite/N/P nutrient tonnes delivered replacing external procurement at real grade and price; avoid PFAS/metals/contaminated residual land application.
6. Disaster water uninterrupted hours, critical irrigation liters delivered, back-up pumps uptime, electrical kWh supplied versus parasitic consumption.
7. Avoided untreated environmental discharge, acid mine/drilling waste and CO2e only with measured life-cycle controls.
8. Auditable labor and quality-of-life measurements alongside capital savings without assuming a monetary value.

### New research cost calibration, not one-size-fits-all
2026 NAWI brackish membrane/mineral network shows net LCOW possible ranging from slightly negative to ~$0.80/m³. A separate 2026 near-ZLD seawater paper modeled ~97.6–98.4% fresh-water recovery in optimized flowsheet and found thermal-only crystallization **not** economically attractive, with strong value from heat recovery and water/mineral combinations. Do not transfer those results to every municipal WWTP or assume near-total extraction is better than targeted recovery. [W13, W33]

A normalized scenario all-U.S.-municipal flow * 1 cent per 1,000 gallons vs $1/m³ are wildly different bases, so compare US$/m³ consistently: 34BGD≈**46.98B m³/y**; **$0.10/m³ net additional cost or benefit** would be **$4.7B/y** if implemented across that hypothetical entire municipal flow, and **$1/m³** would be ~$47B/y. These are scale factors, not realistic uniform national savings: some municipal flow is not treated with membranes/mineral harvest and may require huge new conveyance.

### Three phase-gated pilot types
**A. Municipal water-to-farm soil:**
- 10/25/50MGD actual effluent plants with proximity to irrigated farms and stressed aquifers.
- Paired inlet/effluent/shallow groundwater and biosolids sampling; 10MGD PolyPod if and only if sustained performance verified under actual storm bypass/peak flows.
- Test nutrient recovery from separate sidestreams, safe water polishing, irrigation distribution/pumps, groundwater quality and soil changes with controls.
- Independent multi-season M&V of microparticle capture size-spectrum; captured matter goes to properly regulated residual management.
- Measure savings of pumping, water imports, water delivery costs and direct avoided wastewater outfall losses.

**B. Brackish mineral-water recovery:**
- Representative inland feeds across ≥4 chemistry archetypes: carbonate-dominant, sulfate-rich, chloride-rich and brackish geothermal influence.
- Test preconcentration + selective Ca/Mg/N/P recovery, existing disposal cost and actual local products.
- Compare net LCOW against NAWI 2026 eight-cluster benchmark; record all brine disposal and unusable residue.

**C. Harbor Island / coastal mineral and emergency supply:**
- Keep proposed Harbor Island scale and reported ~191.2MGD discharge as an independent early project-design case, reconcile actual RO reject vs other wastewater before multiplying.
- Pilot staged magnesium/gypsum/bromine/potash/salt markets, chemical-specific import offsets and environmental marine risk. Do not pretend permitted proposed plant already producing.
- Parallel but separate isotope-prequalification research with U.S. DOE, environmental and dual-use experts; compare with existing enriched heavy-water inventory before any natural isotope extraction economics.

### Future national buildout (3–10-year scenario, not forecast)
**0–6 months**: Ingest EPA 2022 CWNS individual POTW IDs, nameplate/actual MGD, reuse type and permit. Collect PolyGone vendor official 10MGD operating curve and written supply pricing; prioritize urban-near-agriculture sewersheds. Assay five representative brackish regions and 3 municipal sludge baselines. Update current reuse baseline. Identify source-water conservation constraints.
**6–18 months**: 3+ wastewater-site independently audited PolyPod liquid pilots and paired sludge-lane controls, 3 inland brine mine pilots with offtake commitments, one candidate coastal brine pilot by operators; emergency water power and mechanical readiness tests.
**18–36 months**: Commission only site-fit systems with safe discharge, stable supply pricing, proven water availability, agronomic protection and positive risk-adjusted cash or explicitly funded social benefit; validate 12 months drought/soil data.
**36–120 months**: Scale to entire eligible sewershed network, audited nutrient/mineral reserve lots, and emergency 1/3/14-day capability. Require transparent legal authority for DPA support; private operator participation and regulatory approvals not assumed. New editions preserved as add-only GitHub commits.

## Appendix: reference model and units

- EPA 34BGD × 365 ×0.003785411784 m³/gallon ×1e9gallons/BGD ≈ **46.98 billion m³/y**.
- Network PolyPod theoretical aggregate units 34,000MGD ÷10MGD=**3,400**; one/site 17,544; at $110k/site **$1,929,840,000**. Neither accounts site size, 80% availability, two-unit redundancy or peak flows.
- Irrigation groundwater withdrawal historic USGS 57.2BGD; incremental scenario GW relief = max(0,34BGD × total reuse fraction - 2.2BGD historical already-reused) × assumed groundwater substitution share.
- Annual nutrient flow inventory tonnes=**water L/y × solute mg/L ÷1e9 mg/tonne**.
- Harbor ratio 291.2MGD projected total design intake / (200MGD assumed RO feed ×90% uptime)=**1.618**; 0.733/0.438 ≈**1.674**, remainder chemistry/process differences.
- Deuterium fraction natural D/H≈0.000156 atoms; atomic D fraction by mass water ~34.6ppm. Multiply by all water-throughput mass yields **gross isotope present, not output**. D2O equivalent=mass D ×20.027/4.028≈4.97 if fully collected and recombined in water, not a direct separation method.
- Import bill avoided = qualified import displaced × price of previously purchased foreign product, not free money plus domestic producer sales. A greenfield domestic mine can have negative cash and still have emergency-resilience value, which must be funded and explained explicitly.
