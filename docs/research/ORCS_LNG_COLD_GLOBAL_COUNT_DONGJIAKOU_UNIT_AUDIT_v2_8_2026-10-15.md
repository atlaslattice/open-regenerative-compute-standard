**2026-10-15 · source audit / non-canon where unmeasured / append-only**

## Population
- International Gas Union World LNG Report 2025 recorded **194 LNG regasification terminals operating at end-2024**. 2025 brought 13 new LNG receiving terminals and expansions; strictly summing without closures/refurbishments is unsound. 2026 count approx **200+**, not a verified terminal-by-terminal 2026 tally. IGU reported ~1,113.5 Mtpa nominal LNG regas capacity across ~50 importing markets at end-2025, whereas GIIGNL's 2026 report displayed **1,247 Mtpa** by its own reporting convention (likely counting different capacity base); do not conflate these denominators. Public sources: https://www.giignl.org/annual-report/2026 , https://giignl.webflow.io/annual-report , https://lnginsights.com/article/global-regasification-capacity-1113-mtpa-utilisation-39-percent-2025 .
- Share with genuinely commissioned reusable LNG cold energy and data-center + ice-desal specifically: **no sourced comparable global project count available**. Review in Energy Sept30 2026 says cold potential use only ~18–25% globally, but that percentage is cold-energy utilization, **not percentage of terminals retrofitted**. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360544226016269
- Existing Japan examples cold-powered electricity/freezer/cryogenic separation, Korea port cold stores and China industrial ASU are real precedents, not equivalent to entire Atlas cascade. Review https://doi.org/10.3390/en10122152 .
- 2025 reported utilization ~39.2% annual nameplate regas. Cold is only available proportional to LNG *actually regasified*, not terminal full nameplate, another significant gating factor.

## Dongjiakou measured/projected status
Chinese public official government June3 2026 and Qingdao Daily June2 2026 report Sinopec Qingdao receiving terminal cold-energy cascade demonstration began detailed design/construction after May19 tender award. Cost approx **RMB 108 million**; future design **26 million kWh/year electricity generated** (=26 GWh/year), **8 million kWh/year purchased grid electricity avoided by cold HVAC** (=8 GWh/year), total = **34 GWh/year gross generation+avoided-demand benefit**, without conflating generation and savings into a single meter. Equivalent 26/8.760=2.97 MW annual average generated, HVAC avoided 8/8.760=0.913 MW annual average, total ~3.88 MW averaged over 8760h. This is NOT 34 GW or even 34 MW continuous, nor certified achieved output.

## Why cascade can be physically more useful
LNG at -162°C needs controlled warming/vaporization. Exergy is temperature-sensitive. Cold-grade matching can apply low-temp streams where ice crystallization/air liquefaction is useful, warmer return streams for data-center chiller cooling, and server thermal heat for washed ice melting. Single stage electricity recovery gives low electricity conversion vs total cooling potential; cascade can reduce separate electrical refrigeration loads, but thermal uses, avoided electricity, actual power outputs are distinct and need metered balances. Added exchangers, cold-carrying pipes, geographic separation, intermittency, LNG cargo gas throughput, capital, insulation and pinch cost mean cascading isn't universally economically superior.

## Comparison and decision
A truly LNG import/regas site with adjacent brine/desal+AI facility is materially stronger candidate than an LNG export/liquefaction site where cold is a purchased process input. Need global site inventory of ~200 import terminals, actual annual throughputs, nearby desal/concentrate and available cold MWth by temperature, compatible demand, geographic distance, electricity tariffs, realistic retrofit CAPEX, alternate cold buyers, and plant owner permissions. Merely counting all ~200 as viable would be erroneous.
