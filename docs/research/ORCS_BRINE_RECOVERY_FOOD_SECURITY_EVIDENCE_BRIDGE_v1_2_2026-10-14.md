## Verified brine resource opportunity is not proven fungibility
A 2026 open-review article systematically evaluated 30+ brine-valorization technologies across salinity and technology readiness, identifying challenges in energy intensity, material performance and scaling (Morgante et al., *Desalination*, 2026): https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0011916425011956 . 2025/26 reviews identify magnesium, calcium, potassium, sulfate and trace ions as potential targets, not all easy or economic to extract: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344925001879 .

### Existing Atlas model reuse
- Harbor Island proposed 100MGD freshwater / ~191.2MGD discharge, theoretical gypsum equivalent 0.733 Mt/y, MgO equivalent 0.93 Mt/y, K2O equivalent 0.196 Mt/y, bromine content 27.9 kt/y; ZERO proven recovered saleable output.
- Existing Dongjiakou node gypsum equivalent **62.7–76.9 kt/y modeled potential**, not recovered calcium sulfate sold or converted. Chinese gypsum-to-acid project **design ratio 0.4 t acid / t feed** must not be used as actual conversion. 100 identical Harbor Island equivalents ~73.3 Mt/y gypsum inventory (pure hypothetical scale), not a known 100-plant U.S. desalination fleet.
- Gypsum contains sulfate; extracting CaSO4 as gypsum does not directly deliver merchant sulfuric acid. Converting it is nontrivial and may consume external chemicals/heat. Soil gypsum is a Ca/S amendment, **not a replacement for N/P/K fertilizers**. Agricultural and acid routes **compete for the same recovered gypsum mass**.
