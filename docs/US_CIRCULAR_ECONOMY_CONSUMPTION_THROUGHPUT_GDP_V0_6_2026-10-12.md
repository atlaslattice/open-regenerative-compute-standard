# US circular economy, domestic supply security, throughput and GDP — v0.6

## Result
The domestic circular-economy model can produce meaningful income/output benefits if qualified recovery actually substitutes for imports, processing is competitive and domestic labor/capital are available. This release broadens evidence to **43 archived original USGS mineral chapters**, **23 physical consumption rows**, and a **23-family $478.512B annual import screening basket** drawn from revised2025 Census data. It adds directly downloaded BEA macro data and a reproducible GDP sensitivity model.

Illustrative steady-state annual GDP level effects range from **+$8.5B** in the limited case to **+$186.0B** in the expanded case; middle case **+$61.0B (~0.20% of 2025GDP)**. An adverse case is **−$13.4B**. These are assumption-driven national sensitivity calculations, **not forecasts, calibrated BEA input-output results or proven physically feasible deployment**. Do not claim a causal national GDP impact from these numbers.

Earlier versions remain preserved. Imports remain a backstop for verified production/grade/delivery gaps; export eligibility follows domestic consumption and stocked20%-of-annual-consumption reserves. Private profit margins alone do not define required import quantities.

The user's ~$1.6 quadrillion recollection is consistent in order of magnitude with payment/financial-settlement aggregates. A precise current $1.6Q all-payments total has **not** been verified. The official figures above are different scopes and sometimes different years; **do not add payment rails as unique transactions** or apply a circular resource multiplier to the resulting sum. Financial asset transfers, refinancing and repeated settlement can move the same money many times.

GDP = gross output − intermediate inputs (~$30.861295T, matching published roundedGDP). Gross output/GDP ~1.71 is an accounting ratio, not a policy multiplier. Fedwire fund value/GDP ~37.2 is also not an economic spending multiplier or monetary velocity of a defined money stock. Physical material throughput requires tonnes/energy/water by stream, not transaction dollars.

## What domestic retention changes
Replacing qualified imported feedstocks with competitive domestic virgin/secondary/recovered production can increase wages, processing activity, supplier demand and domestic operating surplus. Spending by workers can support further activity where capacity is available. A domestic-spending channel is therefore plausible.

The GDP effect is the **incremental value added**, not the sum of import savings, product sales, supplier invoices, wages and all subsequent transactions. BEA value added consists of compensation, production taxes less subsidies, and operating surplus; supplier intermediates are subtracted at each stage. Imports are subtracted in GDP expenditure accounting to remove foreign output already included in spending; imports do not mechanically destroyGDP. Cutting imports without replacement can reduce output.

Foreign suppliers may buy US exports or hold US financial assets; offshore payment does not mean every dollar disappears from the economy. Conversely, US-based production contributes to USGDP even if owners repatriate profits. Domestic ownership and local retention affect income distribution/GNI and subsequent spending; they are not a second GDP credit.

## How waste recovery enters
Qualified supply = compatible domestic virgin production + existing domestic secondary production + **incremental** eligible recovered material + verified service/material replacement.
Subtract existing reuse, existing recycling, imported feedstock content, metallurgical losses, incompatible grades and undeliverable output. Existing refinery sulfur and lead-battery recycling are already production, not new GDP.

Import requirement = max(consumption + planned reserve build + verified losses − qualified domestic supply − permissible non-reserve stock draw,0). Reserve draw cannot be counted as sustainable recurring supply. Actual reserve eligibility requires stock target physically met and deliverable; the20%target is roughly73days average consumption, not peak-event proof.

## GDP model: transparent sensitivity
Census basket = **$478.512B imports/$541.717B exports** across23 selected distinct SITC leaf families from v0.4, excluding electric-current comparator and mixed chemical-materials/products. It contains upstream intermediates as well as raw materials. This is a custom screening basket, **not all US raw-only imports**. Broad food/agriculture groups are excluded from the additive GDP denominator because they contain processed products and overlap selected oilseed/oil rows. No whole-US customs census claim is made.

Let:
- M = basket imports.
- q = fraction technically/economically eligible for incremental domestic circular/renewable replacement.
- a = deployed fraction of eligible replacement.
- B = M×q×a = imports actually assumed replaced, at2025 import-value-equivalent prices.
- v = incremental direct domestic value-added fraction of replacement output.
- k = hypothetical total value-added multiplier, including supplier/household rounds.
- D = displaced domestic value added, foregone alternative/export activity and transition/crowding effects.
- Annual GDP level equivalent = B×v×k − D.

v excludes already-existing domestic collection/scrap production and imported input value from direct incrementalVA. k is a **scenario multiplier**, not derived from the gross-output/GDP ratio, payment throughput or a purchased BEA RIMSII table. D is a GDP displacement deduction, not an accounting subtraction of domestic OPEX or depreciation. Costs/profits and national welfare require a separate lifecycle economic assessment.

| Assumption | Limited | Middle | Expanded | Adverse |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Eligible basket fraction q | 20% | 40% | 60% | 40% |
| Deployed eligible fraction a | 25% | 50% | 75% | 50% |
| Direct incrementalVA fraction v | 35% | 50% | 60% | 30% |
| Assumed VA multiplier k | 1.2 | 1.5 | 1.8 | 1.1 |
| DisplacedVA deduction | 15% of gross modeledVA | 15% | 20% | $45B/year |

The q/a fractions are unverified replacement/deployment assumptions, **not measured waste-capture yields** and not engineering forecasts. Named scenarios are illustrative and have no probabilities.

The middle case replaces20% of this basket's gross imports,95.7024B/year; direct incrementalVA47.8512B; multiplier grossVA71.7768B; assumed displacement10.76652B; net61.01028B. This is a level difference once fully deployed, **not0.198 percentage points of extra growth every year**. An illustrative10-year linear ramp10→100% gives335.557B cumulative additionalGDP-equivalent in2025 prices; expanded case gives~1.023T cumulatively. Those totals are not profits, NPV or funding proceeds; they assume no changing commodity prices, baseline GDP, engineering availability or other feedback.

Adverse case demonstrates the arithmetic need not be positive:95.7024B import replacement,30%incremental directVA and1.1multiplier produce31.581792B before45B foregone/displacedVA, net−13.418208B.

## Exports, reserves, renewables and further effects
- Redirecting existing exports to domestic consumption or reserve stock is not new physical production. In expenditureGDP, an export decline and domestic inventory/demand increase can offset. Do not add retained export value as newGDP on top of its replacement effect.
- Purchasing existing stocks transfers ownership. Newly produced domestic reserve inventory may add production during build, but the stock purchase is not recurring annualGDP. Imports of reserve material remain foreign output.
- Import reduction can be achieved through lower service demand from efficiency/electrification as well as material substitution. Renewable electricity/hydrogen supply processing but do not create missing metal/K/P atoms. Storage, water, capital and imported equipment costs matter.
- A project CAPEX program can add construction/equipment value added during deployment. Without a verified engineering buildout, national CAPEX and its GDP effect remain UNKNOWN and are not invented here. Imported machinery content and displaced other investment must be accounted for.
- Resilience has value through avoided outages/production disruption. Example formula: expected avoided loss = event probability×affectedGDP×mitigation fraction. Those inputs remain UNKNOWN; no avoided disasterGDP is added.
- Existing recycling is not a new credit: EPA's2020REI report uses2012data and attributes681000jobs/$37.8B wages/$5.5B taxes to existing recycling/reuse. These are historical existing activity, not incremental deployment, not additiveGDP, and not2026job forecasts.
- Employment/tax projections are UNKNOWN until industry/value-added/worker productivity and fiscal assumptions are calibrated. Wages/taxes must not be added to value added again.
- RealGDP, price effects, trade retaliation/exchange rates, labor/capital scarcity and global feedback require sectoral national input-output or general-equilibrium modeling. Regional multipliers cannot be blindly stacked across a nationwide deployment.
