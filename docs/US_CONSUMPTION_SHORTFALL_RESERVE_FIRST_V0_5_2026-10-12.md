## Governing model
User clarification: take the national domestic-first principle previously developed for China and apply it to the US. **Consumption first → 20% consumption-based reserve stocked → verified surplus → lawful human-reviewed export eligibility.** Qualified incremental waste-stream extraction is part of domestic supply. This refers to the project's proposed China framework; no claim of an enacted Chinese or US policy is made.

v0.4's 20%-of-imports allowance remains a distinct exposure comparator. The governing reserve in this version is **20% of annual domestic consumption**, about 73 days at average demand, qualified by grade, season and deliverability.

For a build horizon T years:
- Qualified supply S = domestic virgin output + existing secondary output + **additional** qualified waste recovery + verified material-equivalent replacement.
- Reserve target = 0.20 × annual consumption C.
- Reserve gap G = max(target − certified accessible reserve R, 0).
- Planned annual reserve build B = G/T, with storage losses and replenishment added separately.
- Aggregate export candidate = max(S − C − B − required adjustments, 0).
- Domestic sufficiency shortfall = max(C − S, 0).
- No-export reserve-build shortfall = max(C + B − S, 0).

A positive annual candidate is a screen, not permission. **Latest user clarification supersedes the earlier blanket zero-import condition:** imports remain permitted for actual domestic production/quality/delivery gaps. Required import volume is max(consumption + planned reserve build + verified losses − qualified domestic virgin/secondary output − additional qualified waste recovery − verified demand replacement − permissible non-reserve inventory draw, 0). Once reserves are stocked, ordinary imports cover the remaining recurring production gap. A lower foreign price or a private profit-margin preference alone does not define national import need. Affordability and costs remain feasibility checks, not substitutes for the physical gap calculation. Reserve holdings must actually meet the target before export eligibility; domestic consumption delivered, grade/location compatibility and lawful human authority verified. Imports must not create exportable surplus by being reclassified as domestic production; any cooperative grade exchange needs explicit separate accounting. During initial reserve filling, prospective surplus is allocated to stock before exports. With uneven seasonal supply, monthly inventory/demand simulation is necessary. Emergency stock draw must not be treated as sustainable annual production.

## Import policy delta: gaps determine needs
This v0.5 gate is the current user-authorized proposal. Earlier v0.2–v0.4 documents retain the historical zero-import rule for provenance but that requirement is superseded here. Imports are a backstop for measured domestic shortfalls; exports follow consumption and stocked reserves. Imported supply serving one region or grade cannot be netted against exports of another without a demonstrated lawful, physically deliverable exchange. Private margins may affect whether capacity gets built, but they do not define the nation's required material volume.

## Waste recovery included in the gap closure
Existing secondary recovery is already in the copper/aluminum output proxies. Only additional qualified domestic production is credited.

From v0.3, illustrative processing of25/50/75% of exported aluminum scrap at70–95% yields gives .385–.5225 / .770–1.045 /1.155–1.5675 Mt/year before assay/alloy qualification. Against the3.44 Mt annual domestic-output gap, those equal **11.19–15.19% /22.38–30.38% /33.58–45.57%**. Against the zero-reserve one-year requirement4.58 Mt, the75% case equals25.22–34.22%. These are potential mass ratios, not actual gap closure; diversion of existing recycling subtracts foregone export value and requires domestic capacity.

Prior food-waste75% envelope gives .04082–.12247 Mt K2O/year under unverified1–3 kg/wet-tonne yield. That is ~.76–2.27% of the5.40 Mt potash production gap. Manure/WWTP additions may expand qualified recovery but cannot be counted without inventories and existing-use deductions. Do not count crop nutrients already field-applied as newly recovered output.

Waste-derived methane can contribute to compatible gas replacement; renewable electrification reduces specific gas/coal/oil service demand, which must be translated into commodity consumption avoided. Renewable ammonia production can expand N supply; it does not generate K/P or metals. The goal is measured reduction of S-to-C gaps, not attribution of national totals to Atlas.
