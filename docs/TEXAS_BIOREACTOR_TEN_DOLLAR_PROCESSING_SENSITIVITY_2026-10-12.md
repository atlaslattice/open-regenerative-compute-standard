# Texas organics — $10/t processing-cost scenario correction

**2026-10-12 · USER-PROPOSED ENGINEERING TARGET / MODELED / UNVERIFIED, zero realized credit.** This is a new additive sensitivity; preserves earlier reports and their higher-cost cases for provenance.

## Key correction
Previous analysis assumed **$70 per wet metric ton bioreactor operations**, while a **$10 per wet metric ton process-cost target** dramatically improves whole-system economics. $10/t must be defined: whether it includes labor, energy, chemicals, sorting/depackaging, odor and leak controls, maintenance and waste residue. Separate reactor processing from hauling, annualized capital, residuals, gas upgrading and safe product finishing. No qualified vendor estimate or demonstrated commercial performance has been checked for $10/t; do not label as measured cost.

100 wet metric tons/day ×365 =36,500 tons/year; **$49.77/t ×36,500 = $1,816,605/year** of *incremental modeled system benefit*.

## Important accounting gates
Avoided existing disposal costs benefit the original payer, while reactor tipping fees are transfers to plant operators; do not sum the same tip payment and avoided landfill gate cost as separate societal value. New collection costs may be higher for segregated streams and may require processing rejects and curing. Reactor gas and crop nutrients only accrue if independently accepted and marketable. Operating cost target $10/t is not verified and must be tested with real input conditions, capital utilization, uptime and lawful residue finishing.
