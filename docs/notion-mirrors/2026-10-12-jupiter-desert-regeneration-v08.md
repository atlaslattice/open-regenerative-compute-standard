**Purpose:** Evaluate whether *partially constructed* Project Jupiter near Santa Teresa, Doña Ana County, New Mexico, should retain its compute buildout, add an ORCS regenerative jacket, pause future expansion, or be reprogrammed toward water, bioindustry, agriculture, and arid-land restoration. THIS IS A STUDY, NOT ANY CLAIM THAT THE DEVELOPERS, OPENAI, OR LOCAL RESIDENTS APPROVE IT.
## Executive verdict
**Default test first:** Preserve economically committed and safe construction, retain tenant service and community obligations where feasible, build an ORCS regenerative *regional* network outside the compute fence. **An agriculture-first downsize is a high-cost counterfactual** that could strand partly constructed capital, trigger financing/offtake renegotiations, reduce original construction and operational employment, and underdeliver relative to climate-water constraints. Its merits need a legal/physical break-even analysis, not assumed superiority. Corpus Christi's existing coastal port/refinery, industrial wastewater and possible desal inputs make it a separate, likely stronger INDUSTRIAL-symbiosis comparator; Jupiter may be more attractive for ecology/agronomy, watershed restoration and workforce-oriented *distributed regional* production.

## Arid ecosystem hard gate: don't turn existing healthy Chihuahuan desert into lawn
The desert is an ecological community, not vacant terraforming substrate. [NMSU Bulletin 807](https://pubs.nmsu.edu/research/water/BL807/index.html): 4-yr local land-application field study found beneficial nutrient/plant gains BUT increased salinity, sodicity, chlorides and potential losses of native plant diversity. Therefore restrict restoration to degraded/disturbed industrial corridors and locally suitable native vegetation; soil and groundwater assay; salt exclusion/management; severe ET and heat exposure; no invasive/non-native irrigation expansion by default. Restoration KPI: native habitat, groundwater depletion, biological diversity, soil stability, dust reduction and storm infiltration—not simply satellite greenness.
[State of New Mexico water dashboard](https://water-dashboard.nm.gov/water-conservation/) prioritizes agriculture water reduction and measurable water security. [City 2025 aquifer recharge study](https://lascruces.gov/city-irrigation-district-partner-in-groundwater-recharge-study/) is a real planning precedent; don't assume Jupiter can claim replenishment independent of water-law permits.
## Three realistic Jupiter cases and one extreme counterfactual
**J0 — Current compute-led baseline:** complete planned permitted phases, closed-loop cooling, dedicated fuel cell microgrid if approved; separate water/community commitments; no Atlas credits. Model carbon/fuel/emissions, full lifecycle risk.
**J1 — Additive ORCS/regenerative jacket, preserve contracted compute:** add human-operated, firewalled wastewater pilot, anaerobic digester/compost and food/biomass processing offsite at existing civic treatment systems; native floodwash rehabilitation, dust control, SAF oilseed trial where agronomically justified, regional reclaimed-water service and local hiring; add transparent dedicated resident fund. This is preferred first pilot because avoids stranding compute work; incremental economics modeled separately.
**J2 — Phase 2+ hybrid:** hypothetical **500 MW to 1 GW total CAMPUS ELECTRICAL DEMAND**, NOT 500 MW or 1GW IT load and not current contracted program; scale future unbuilt phases and power generation to verified tenants and reserves; invest the demonstrated freed prospective capital in regional WWTP treatment/reuse, high-value greenhouse and mushroom/mycoprotein bioreactors (if water/feed/sterile and economics prove), regional food-waste anaerobic digestion, year-round value-added food process jobs and native-land restoration. Renegotiate leases, minimum payments, IRB PILOT, microgrid capacity, permits, workforce commitments and lender covenants; opportunity-cost and termination expenses explicitly included. Any funds 'freed' from unbuilt phases are NOT usable until developer confirms they are truly discretionary.
**J3 — Agriculture-heavy/250 MW campus electricity:** concept deliberately extreme; requires redevelopment of subleased parcel, permitting changes, plant/land tenure, water rights, actual net job and NPV superiority and approvals; likely not prudent for partly built campus. Consider if compute demand collapses or an uncommitted phase becomes stranded; regional farms and treatment are usually better located where WWTP and demand exist.
### Illustrative scale sanity checks — NOT SITE FACTS
- 500 MW electric average facility load at full year = **4.38 TWh/year**; if PUE 1.25 and fixed duty IT equivalent 400 MW, but do not equate with current Jupiter unknown duty. A 250 MW campus case = 2.19 TWh/year. 2.45GW is installed microgrid nameplate, not IT load, and a 500MW design is neither mechanically nor financially identical to 500/2450 share of old project commitments.
- **1 million gal/day reclaimed wastewater** for 365 d = **365M gal/y ≈1,120 acre-ft/y**. At an illustrative 2–4 acre-ft/acre-year applied irrigation duty, supports **280–560 acres** at water-balance ceiling, excluding distribution/treatment losses and required return/environmental flows. Strong net-blue-water availability requires measured diversion vs original fate, crop ET, groundwater impact and rights. A 1MGD transferable source near Jupiter is **unconfirmed**. The 1,400-acre campus *estimate* is not a farm field inventory.
- **100 wet tons/day regionally sourced food waste** × **180–280 kWh/ton** EPA historical digestion-to-electricity comparator = **18–28 MWh/day**, ≈**0.75–1.17 MW average electric potential**, before site system-specific net losses. This can support bioprocessing but is **less than 0.25% of a 500MW campus load**. Do not claim food waste powers a hyperscale data center. The Las Cruces 50-household pilot does NOT supply this hypothetical tonnage. EPA published anaerobic digestion study [https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-08/ebmud-final-report-anaerobic-digestion-of-food-waste-2008-03.pdf](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-08/ebmud-final-report-anaerobic-digestion-of-food-waste-2008-03.pdf) ; EPA narrative [https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/www3/region9/waste/archive/features/foodtoenergy/ebmud-study.html](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/www3/region9/waste/archive/features/foodtoenergy/ebmud-study.html) .
- Data center excess heat supply only after measuring flow temperature and seasons. In southern New Mexico, greenhouses may demand additional COOLING in warm seasons, so greenhouse heating should not be assigned perpetual sales revenue. Reuse actual waste heat for nocturnal/winter, digesters, hot washing and modest low-temp industrial use with backups.
- Agriculture may generate more DIRECT low/medium skill or technical jobs per invested dollar than compute, but not automatically exceed developer's asserted 1,500 ongoing project-supported jobs, which may include indirect roles; compare net FTE, salary, seasonality and family benefits at matched funding.
- Distinguish nutrient recovery/soil amendments versus potable water; no dirty water to food crops without proper reclaimed-water class and food safety testing. Residual concentrate, PFAS, pesticides, heavy metals and microbial loads require lawful separation and disposal.
## Candidate ORCS circular chain — regional, not all co-sited
Municipal food and green waste + agricultural byproducts -\> screened clean separation -\> food rescue first -\> compliant digesters/compost/bioconversion -\> measured methane/RNG, certified compost, optional animal/feed products after quality tests and legal approvals. Local WWTP -\> tertiary treatment/purple pipe -\> protected industrial uses / qualified crop irrigation, with concentrated residuals handled lawfully. Grid/fuel cells and optional solar -\> firm human-operated compute + municipal utilities -\> compute low-grade heat to independently metered matching greenhouse/digester/customer at appropriate temperatures, not a free cooling claim. Industrial water treatment / land stewardship -\> soils assay -\> soil salinity management + native revegetation, stormwater capture/washes, erosion/dust and local quality of life. SAF oilseed/permaculture from measured regional acreage only, not automatic tenant SAF plant. No desal in freshwater site without justified brackish groundwater feed and residual disposal assessment.
## Critical comparative economics
Compare J0–J3 and Corpus Christi C0–C3 at BOTH:
(i) same delivered compute service level where comparing compute-efficiency impacts (do not claim a downsize 'more efficient' simply because it consumes less),
(ii) same total INVESTMENT DOLLARS, independently reallocated only if not legally committed; and
(iii) independent location-specific QOL/resource accounts.
Compute net present value = retained contracted cloud rents and residual asset value + contracted merchant water/food/waste services + verified public environmental/fiscal benefit where monetization is defensible - actual new capex and decommissioning/stranded assets - cancellation fees/lease losses - maintenance/debt - power/fuel/microgrid - ecological liabilities - water-rights/return-flow constraints. Community-account separately measures protected utility affordability, employment net FTE/payroll, ecology, water quality, downstream users, drought security, ratepayer support and public transparency. Never add avoided flood loss and property appreciation if both represent same risk mitigation.
## Plan and sign-off
Gate 0: obtain current Oracle/STACK/Bloom executed tenancy and phased financing, actual paid construction invoices, \$165B IRB structure and site acreage; no \$65B 'burned' as fact.
Gate 1: inventory CRRUA and Las Cruces WWTP source water contracts, actual 2026 flow, treatment costs, downstream returns, water rights; quantify food and yard waste merchant feedstock and transport cost.
Gate 2: 10–50-acre native restoration + controlled environment agriculture proof plots (not a promised literal Jupiter site area), independently gated pilot digesters/offsite WRRF equipment; governance, local tribal/community/ecologist input. A small pilot need not cut existing compute.
Gate 3: compare the economics and emissions of full compute, ORCS jacket and downsizing with independent commercial auditor. A change requiring actual contracts/land rights is a new transaction, not a design tweak.
Gate 4: voluntary developer/community permitting with no presumption regulatory approval.
**Primary question to resolve:** can Jupiter remain compute-positive to its investors while measurably increasing regional food/water/soils and reducing environmental harms by adding regional infrastructure, or would altering its already-planned compute phases destroy more proven value than new activities create? J1 is the lowest-regret test; J2/J3 must earn an economic, water and community case.
