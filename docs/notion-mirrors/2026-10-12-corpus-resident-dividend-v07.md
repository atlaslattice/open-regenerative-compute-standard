## PolyGone microplastic interception at every relevant outlet
Original product: [PolyGone Systems technology](https://www.polygonesystems.com/technology), PolyPod advertised up to 10 million gallons/day nominal filtration capacity for open-channel municipal drinking-water/wastewater treatment, and Poly Cartridge for line-pressure industrial applications. These are vendor figures, not third-party guaranteed site treatment. PolyGone's ACUA 2024 pilot has reported 720 filter elements and microplastic interception; vendor said two commercial PolyPods under construction with a planned Oct 20, 2026 startup. Do NOT assume those units are fully operating by Oct 12.
**Unit price assumption: US\$110,000 per unit (user planning allowance, not supplier quote); prior internal estimate was US\$90,000.** Obtain written price, spare filter media/backwash/replacement/maintenance, guaranteed flow, hydraulic headloss, particle-size and polymer-resolved performance, wastewater matrix/salinity/antiscalant compatibility and field availability. No assumption a \$110k unit can be installed for that all-in cost.
Corpus Christi baseline: [City FY2026 proposed operational metrics](https://www.corpuschristitx.gov/media/lqxjv2h4/online-fy-2026-operating-proposed-budget-book.pdf) counts SIX WWTP sites, combined rated flow \~42.7 MGD, average effluent \~29–31 MGD at historical reporting. One 10-MGD pod at each outlet → SIX initial physical installations at US\$660k theoretical purchase allowance, with actual module count determined per site's design peak and multiple outfalls. At 30-MGD revised proposed desal water-output, three 10-MGD pods = US\$330k notional product-water configuration; but RO permeate may already be microplastic-free, whereas pretreatment backwash and brine outfalls may be higher-value intercept points. The 2021 predecessor 20-MGD desal application had \~30 MGD RO brine from a 50-MGD membrane feed (\~40% product yield); revised 2026 reject flow is UNKNOWN and must not be inferred. If all flow branches qualify, budget separate units for each treated outfall and RO concentrate; do not deploy PolyPods in an incompatible high-salinity or pressurized pipe. Nominal 6+3 = NINE units / US\$990k purchase allowance, NOT a full facility or procurement estimate; additional reject-flow units, redundancy, site work, civil/bypass and continuous monitoring extra.
Locations with microplastic need: WWTP discharge, refinery/industrial effluent as agreed, seawater intake/pretreatment residual flows and marine-concentrate outfall. Assess microplastic concentration/count, mass per day, particle size, efficiency, media backwash recovery, recovered solids disposal, sludge contamination; no claim of PFAS destruction or ocean-wide cleanup without receipts. The environmental goal is zero routine untreated discharges and net measured cleaner local waters; do not claim zero waste when a lawful desal brine outfall remains.

Texas [Government Code 1502.057](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/pdf/GV.1502.pdf) requires utilities with covered public securities to collect adequate equal/uniform service charges and generally prohibits free utility services except specified public uses; assess exact applicability with Texas counsel. An externally paid household bill CREDIT or separate cash dividend can potentially benefit residents without the municipal utility failing to collect charges; that precise mechanism still needs formal legal signoff.
Corpus Christi electricity consumers typically choose retail suppliers within the AEP Texas distribution territory. Cost-plus C01 onsite generation does NOT automatically provide energy to outside households; a licensed supplier/retailer, settlement/contract, grid-delivery tariffs and consumer protections must be modeled.
Municipal tax rebates/property tax structures, means-tested vs universal credits, renters, landlord pass-through and affordable housing need local counsel, city/county authority and state law review; avoid arbitrary citywide property tax refunds as though legally automatic. ## Proposed Resident Dividend and Beach/Ocean Restoration Fund
Illustrative annual funding at 91,000 eligible accounts/households (not a forecast):
- \$10 monthly water bill credit × 12 × 91,000 = \$10,920,000/year.
- \$20 monthly electricity retail billing credit × 12 × 91,000 = \$21,840,000/year.
- \$100/year resident dividend / potential tax-offset instrument × 91,000 = \$9,100,000/year (legality/eligibility UNKNOWN).
- Sum household credits = \$41,860,000/year (\$460/year per modeled account).
- Additional illustrative coastal/beach/ocean restoration + marine debris intercept and monitoring allocation = \$5,000,000/year.
- Total modeled recurring annual allocation = \$46,860,000/year BEFORE administration, fund taxes, reserve capitalization and inflation.
At a \$1.5B industrial/desal project capex denominator this is \~3.124% of capital PER YEAR, showing the seriousness of funding need; it does not mean a 3.1% realized net gain or shareholder ROI. Model optional stronger tiers (\$25 water, \$40 electricity, \$250 cash) only if independently verified excess cash supports them. If credits are less than legal rates, the city utility must still collect full charges from appropriate sponsor/fund/household as applicable.
Funding waterfall and annual true-up:
1. Gross incremental contracted industry offtake revenues, energy/heat/material sales and partner community contributions, explicitly net of transactions already in baseline. Separate municipal tax receipts attributable to real new projects, taking account of abatements and service costs. No Stargate/Jupiter allocated cash, expected rent, uncollected sales or unawarded government grants counted.
2. Subtract industrial operations, insurance, risk-adjusted CAPEX financing/debt-service costs, municipal seawater/desal and recycled water debt obligations, mandated environmental performance, collection/recovery/disposal, tax liabilities and project-level reserves.
3. Pre-fund annualized hurricane renewal, coastal nourishment, outage response and funded liabilities. No environmental netting to disguise permit vetoes.
4. Only legally distributable, **realized incremental unencumbered surpluses** (or contractually fixed developer contributions with creditworthy guarantee) fund the resident dividend. Neither taxes already budgeted for services nor utility bond proceeds are freely distributable.
5. Independent fiduciary and municipal audit, open dashboard, explicit sunset/renewal, correction mechanism; publish a minimum service guarantee, price-risk cap and escrow rules. Avoid placing new spending commitment on utility customers if project underperforms.
6. Residents see itemized cash/credit value at household level; renters and low-income customers should not lose benefits because of account ownership; legal design must work for multi-family, wholesale and seven-county service region.
Possible tiers based on legally verified after-reserve distributable funds: Tier 0 bill protection, no upward rate incidence; Tier 1 low-income assistance and essential-water/energy credits; Tier 2 equitable all-resident credits; Tier 3 optional dividend or independently lawful local tax reduction; Tier 4 habitat/beach investments protected from short-term corporate marketing cuts. Tier order is illustrative, not a resident referendum; local input needed.
