## Principal discovery — Dongjiakou has a public ENGINEERING process design
The 2020 peer-reviewed technical practice article **肖威、王楠、李丹, 膜法海水淡化工程中材质及设备的选型, 工业水处理 40(9):124–127, DOI 10.11894/iwt.2019-0799** is specifically about the Dongjiakou plant and includes its **Figure 1 process flow**, Tables 1–3 materials and corrosion engineering, RO pump specifications, operating design envelope and lifecycle energy attention: [full public engineering paper](https://www.iwt.cn/article/2020/1005-829X/20200926.shtml).
It is not an IFC drawing package, as-built BIM model, complete P&IDs, SCADA configuration, or a full proprietary EPC design. Availability of those has **not been established**. Distinguish Dongjiakou plant from the separately published 2009 *Engineering design of Qingdao seawater desalination plant* at another site (do not conflate plants).
**Dongjiakou process**: Yellow Sea feed intake → seawater pretreatment → UF → high-pressure seawater RO → product polishing/distribution; concentrated saline residual adjusted and discharged to nearby sea per original design; therefore ZERO LIQUID DISCHARGE of the entire original plant is not established and the published design explicitly discusses discharge. Product design chloride \<40 mg/L and pH 6.5–7.5. Initial design 100,000 m3/day (26.42 MGD), later design expansion 300,000 m3/day (79.25 MGD).
**Process equipment engineering from 2020 paper**: intake pipe DN1200, 3PE and cathodic protection; FRP/UPVC/HDPE low-pressure applications; high-pressure seawater/RO brine line super duplex 2507, pressures up to 7 MPa (PN100 design context); BWRO line SS316L; corrosion screening pitting resistance PRE\>40 for direct seawater metals; five RO high-pressure pumps of 2,100 kW motor power each, with \~920 m3/h flow per pump; selected pumping efficiency 86.1% or greater in some units, seasonal seawater 2–28°C and specified heads 400–630m; original designed whole-plant-ish specific energy 3.51 kWh/m3. 2020 paper's OPEX share at its stated boundary: energy 69%, chemicals 12%, membrane replacement 8%, maintenance 7%, management 4%. Treat as historical model/experience, not current independent audit.
**2025 Shandong Water Department report**: [12 Dec 2025 provincial water report](https://www.waterconserving.cn/shandong/localwater/detail/8088) identifies 100,000 m3/day, ¥450m (\~4.5亿) historical project figure and 40亩 site, \~2.2 kWh/m3 for UF+RO membrane systems, chemical input \<¥0.15/m3 and industrial water conservation savings. These are **officially reported** rather than independently metered. The 2.2 UF+RO figure has a **narrower energy boundary** than 3.51 historical design. Historical press sources report ¥450m/¥500m/¥650m/¥800m based on varying definitions or phases; investigate scopes, price year, land/energy/take-or-pay and distribution before making any US cost comparison.
**Dongjiakou observed utilization from user's dated Atlas source**: [W01 run 0.2 operating baseline](https://github.com/atlaslattice/manus-artifacts/blob/master/public_candidate_bundle_0001/nodes/dongjiakou_node_001/run0_operating_2025_v0_2.py) gives 17.93 million m3 produced in 2025, equivalent to 49,123 m3/day or about 49.1% of 100,000 m3/day nameplate. Reconcile the official statement of 36.5 million m3/year **water-saving capacity** against actual annual output. PPP's 70,000 m3/day take-or-pay threshold makes utilization a first-order economic issue, not a triviality. User's local 19-unit/23-stream ontology remains an external proposed expansion; do not call its 18 speculative cross-sector links physical installations.
## Corpus Christi — the more comparable REVISION has 30 MGD
**Source:** [Feb 24, 2026 public council presentation (21 pages)](https://corpuschristi.legistar.com/View.ashx?G=AE57B2E3-6490-43F9-A42F-FE60546ECF9E&GUID=4C308273-7546-4BF4-8960-1AC483DFDC0E&ID=15269350&M=F), agenda [26-0291](https://corpuschristi.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=7920187&GUID=A1189837-8989-4DE3-8FF6-658EB7A0D7E8): revised Corpus Christi Desal Partners/Acciona/MasTec design **30 MGD** (113,562 m3/day), not the 20 MGD 2021–2024 initial financing scope; previous projects have sometimes advertised 36 MGD maximum.
Proposal as of February 2026: preliminary GMP **\$978.77m**, 30 MGD, annual OPEX **\$32m** scenario, water cost **\$8.05 per 1,000 US gallons** under the original presentation assumptions (including capital recovery/rate structure — not OPEX alone). Development 1A/1B \$78.61m and Phase 2 \$900.16m. Phase 2: marine works \~\$130.99m, aggregate treatment equipment/construction \~\$542.96m including civil/buildings \~\$186.68m, piping/process \~\$253.55m, electrical/instrument \~\$102.74m; product pipeline/navigation pump improvements \~\$56.27m; subtotal \~\$730.22m plus escalation \~\$43.81m, contingency \~\$43.81m, bonds/insurance \~\$10.76m, general conditions \~\$71.56m. **DO NOT double count** the treatment plant subtotal and its subcategories. Compare old 10%-design cost \$1.315bn to the revised \$978.77m; note that reducing seven to six RO racks, pump station relocation and optimized buildings were proposed reductions, requiring proof in final design.
[September 1, 2026 agenda 26-0840](https://corpuschristi.m.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?GUID=63C2D0EE-6B8A-4EAE-9144-C93B0DCDF65B&ID=8038548&Options=&Search=) records the \$78.61m design/pilot contract **failed**, so the preliminary proposal is not an executed complete-plant contract.
**Older published 2021 application/permit technical process model**: [City Inner Harbor updated application PDF](https://www.corpuschristitx.gov/media/5xvapcgm/inner-harbor-desalination-plant-updated-application.pdf): 20 MGD product, 54.325 MGD raw water, 40% RO recovery, 50 MGD feed to RO, 30 MGD RO brine, 4.312 MGD thickener/centrifuge wastewater, 34.312 MGD total discharge, small solids landfill stream; stages include submerged screens, dissolved air flotation, microfiltration/ultrafiltration, seawater RO, remineralization and final chloramination. **2021 flow-sheet is NOT proven the final 2026 30-MGD design**; use as historical permitted reference and obtain change-order/revised design. The 2026 presentation names actiDAFF pretreatment, pressurized filters, RO building, product treatment, and intake reconfiguration.
**Funding and political**: [TWDB July 2024 source](https://www.twdb.texas.gov/board/2024/07/Board/Brd30.pdf) specifies earlier 20→30 MGD plant, multi-year financing, and a historical Hillcrest environmental-justice complaint. That alone rules out claims of uncontroversial permit fast-tracking. [City's Aug 2025 expenditure memo](https://www.corpuschristitx.gov/media/f1unrwbv/20250815_memo_water-supply-projects-update.pdf) documents spending \$34.278m to that date versus larger contractual encumbrances; earlier loan commitments or bond principal are NOT necessarily construction expenditures and not fungible. Request audited current financial and bond ledger.
## Side-by-side matrix — mixed-date, mixed-boundary, not a cost audit
- Physical output: Dongjiakou 100k m3/day / 26.42 MGD vs Corpus revised 113.56k m3/day / 30 MGD, i.e. Corpus nameplate 13.6% larger.
- Process: Dongjiakou UF+SWRO with RO energy recovery and industrial emphasis; Corpus earlier DAF + MF/UF + SWRO + potable remineralization/chloramination, revised actiDAFF/pressure filters/RO. Need membrane count, treated water class and influent quality.
- Land footprint: China 40亩 (2025 govt report), older report 60亩, needs ownership/scope reconciliation; US water footprint not established from preliminary summary.
- Plant energy: China 2020 design 3.51kWh/m3, 2025 UF+RO 2.2kWh/m3; Corpus 2026 site-specific SEC unknown. No claim of superior efficiency without common metered boundary.
- First-project capex: China ¥450m 2015–16 reported by provincial source, revised US 2026 \$978.77m. Currency, year, purchasing power, labor, marine works, potable spec, security, taxes, insurance, risk and site/civil scope prevent attribution of difference to profit extraction or incompetence.
- Residual: Dongjiakou original explicitly nearshore brine discharge; Corpus 2021 34.312 MGD of combined discharge at 20-MGD output; marine harm must be assessed on brine salinity, dispersion, ecology and permit limits, not assumed zero in either case.
- Offtake: China historical 70k m3/day take-or-pay (source Atlas archive), actual 2025 average 49.1k m3/day; US industrial municipal offtake to be contracted and independently validated.
- Labor, construction timeline, local politics and permits should be assessed with site-specific facts and not used as blanket national rankings.
## ARCHITECTURE — public engineering plus ORCS cascade are separate layers
Layer 1 **existing engineering**: water treatment/pumping/corrosion control, design capacities, intake/outfall/brine, water chemistry.
Layer 2 **locally possible optimization**: regional desal/wastewater reuse; selective brine minerals only with chemistry and separations evidence; industrial low-grade heat sinks, direct power, shared greenhouses/food processing where viable; shared port services.
Layer 3 **ORCS proposal**: 12+ sector subnodes; cloud compute as metered consumption and independently quantified advisory optimization only; storm defense beaches/dunes/engineered structures as distinct safeguards; community benefits; safe human-operated controls. A concept of LNG regas cold is NOT proven a real Dongjiakou desal-to-data center transfer or a Corpus Christi LNG export synergy; verify physical availability, temperature, transfer energy and simultaneous flows.
Layer 4 **audit ledger**: plant-alone balance, metered transfer edges, consolidated project NPV; never double count; unknown has zero credit; preserve component-level ecological and cyber vetoes.
## Jupiter as comparison, not established counterfactual
[New Mexico economic development report](https://www.edd.newmexico.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FY26-Q1-EDD-Performance-Report.pdf) describes \$165bn *project investment* across proposed Jupiter campus, NOT proof \$165bn contractually funded or \$65bn already spent. [Reuters Sept 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/oracles-18-billion-data-center-debt-under-pressure-ft-reports-2026-09-18/) reports \~\$18bn loans under market pressure. **\$65bn 'burned' remains unverified**; do not put into financial model. Oracle also publicly reports water-system commitments and closed-loop cooling/fuel-cell proposal; stress its genuine investments alongside permitting failures and local opposition. The useful policy test is whether verified cross-industry benefits reduce integrated net cost, environmental burden and schedule risk, not an assumption a poorly planned project wasted a known amount.
