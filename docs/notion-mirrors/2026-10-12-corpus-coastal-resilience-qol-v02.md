## Thesis and design boundary
Treat a Corpus Christi regenerative industrial park as a multi-industry system: desalination, wastewater reuse, AI/compute, thermal cascading, gas/LNG, refining, chemicals, material recovery, food/agriculture, power/storage, port logistics, habitat restoration, and QOL. An AI campus does not become intrinsically environmentally beneficial; it must demonstrate net incremental physical value through independently metered exchanges and protect community costs.
Include hurricane exposure, compound rainfall-surge flooding, shoreline erosion, drainage outage, saltwater intrusion, industrial spill consequences and long-term climate uncertainty from the first siting pass. Protect critical nodes without concentrating hazards in one infrastructure corridor. Coastal vegetation and beaches are complementary wave/erosion buffers, not a substitute for engineered protection where design storm surge would overtop them.

## Design scope and deliverables
1. **Storm-safe water service.** Compare siting, flood elevations, armoring, dry/wet-safe essential electrical systems, raw intake/offtake, membrane redundancy, brine dispersion, freshwater demand reduction and reuse; design for compound flooding and multiday black-start.
2. **Layered coastal defense.** Compare engineered floodwall/levee, dunes, beach nourishment, hybrid breakwaters, marsh and oyster reefs. Preserve navigation, public beach access, sediment transit and habitat. Quantify overtopping, annualized damages, habitat change, capex, lifetime maintenance and failure modes.
3. **Ocean cleanup integrated with production.** Monitor sources and mass of litter/oil/solid pollutants removed, stormwater interception, industrial pretreatment, marine discharge chemistry and biological outcomes. Cleanup operations create no permission for new damage or tradeoffs that violate permits.
4. **Agriculture and jobs.** Test heat-assisted greenhouses, food processing, organic-waste nutrient recovery, compost, salt-tolerant landscaping and wetlands/plant nurseries using treated nonpotable water where legally safe. Avoid asserting energy-intensive vertical farms or saline aquaculture are profitable without matched crop, demand, salinity and power economics. Separate temporary construction jobs from durable net local jobs; include wages, job training, local hiring and supplier contracts.
5. **Local QOL.** Meter residential water rates and affordability, noise (day/night dBA and low-frequency), air pollutants, grid costs/reliability, usable shoreline access, recreation, habitat, health safeguards, traffic, worker housing, and emergency access. Require consultation and tangible contracted community protections, not generic political messaging.
6. **Thermal and resource cascade.** Run LNG liquefaction and regasification as DISTINCT cases: cryogenic cold recovery only when physical flow supports it. Data-center low-grade heat transfer requires temperature matching and audited thermodynamics. Desal may be regional, not co-located. Include refinery, gas, water, energy and byproduct flows, hourly dispatch, distinct environmental/cyber safety vetoes.
7. **Funding and governance.** Corpus Christi water loans and municipal bonds are not an unconditional project grant; future private compute funding is not available cash. Check legal portability and non-duplication of state/federal commitments and enforceable industrial offtake. Maintain three ledgers: component standalone, metered inter-component exchanges, node-level consolidated. Explicit costs for restoration/resilience; no free assumed floodwall.
8. **Policy/political evaluation.** Compare documented state/federal priorities, fuel resilience, industrialization, disaster mitigation and community challenges in a neutral evidence-based analysis. Prior \~\$4B/year gasoline import/export example is *illustrative only* under optimistic assumptions, never an observed saving, election effect or financed cash flow.
## Proposed comparison experiments
A. Baseline standalone Inner Harbor desalination and separately built utilities, using the same water-output and risk-protection targets.
B. Integrated twelve-industry node WITHOUT coastal improvement (counterfactual, to isolate coordination synergies).
C. Integrated node WITH engineering-grade storm protection, public beach restoration, marine cleanup and agricultural jobs.
D. C with outage/storm stress: simultaneous surge, heavy rainfall, extended grid loss, communications outage, industrial release and water intake disruptions. Test black-start and independence from one shared thermal/power link.
**Hard gates:** valid site permits and public access; no unexplained adverse salinity/habitat effect; independently tested storm protection; bounded customer rate impacts; no uncompensated utility load; safe wastewater/irrigation quality; jobs evidenced by contracts; net financial benefits after capital, O&M, insurance and lifecycle restoration expense; component-level ORCS and ecology vetoes pass individually. Unknown status stays UNKNOWN.
## KPI register — all outputs must be measurable
- Water: potable and industrial m³/day, demand reliability, freshwater withdrawal, consumptive use, reused water, desal brine tonnage/salinity and receiving-water trends.
- Coastal: risk-weighted annual damage, overtopping probability by event, restored shoreline km, habitat ha, beach-access km, monitoring of sediment, litter tonnes removed, spill incidence.
- Energy: PUE, WUE, CUE, grid import, thermal MW/MWh delivered with temp, heat-pump input, industrial thermal fuel displacement.
- Jobs/QOL: permanent net local FTE, wages, local vendor spend, skills placements, residential water bill, day/night noise, air quality, 168-hour emergency service continuity target (PROPOSED).
- Financial: stakeholder NPV, incremental shared-network capex, user rates, regional fiscal effect, all O&M, insurance, labor, outage and maintenance costs.
