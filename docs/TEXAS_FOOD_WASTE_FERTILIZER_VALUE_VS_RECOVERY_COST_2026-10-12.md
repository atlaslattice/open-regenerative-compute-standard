All unverified field assumptions.
- October 7 2026 DTN US fertilizer retail survey: urea $683 per US short ton (46% N), DAP $934/t (46% P2O5 AND 18% N), MOP $499/t (60% K2O): https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/crops/article/2026/10/07/uan32-leads-fertilizer-prices-higher
- Screening nutrient standalone proxies: $1.637/kg N, $2.238/kg P2O5 and $0.917/kg K2O. **DAP also contains N**; these separate marginal reference proxies are not an exact minimum-cost nutrient blend, and summing them **can double credit fertilizer N**. Reprice via an NPK least-cost blend and agronomic actual plant availability before financial commitments.
- Food waste AD facility national historical EPA survey: 2023 median $22.23/US short ton gate fee from 18 facilities, national respondent statistic; is NOT a Texas price or a guaranteed waste-processing contract: https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/anaerobic-digestion-facilities-processing-food-waste-us-2022-2023
- A historical EPA-hosted technical feasibility review quotes European anaerobic operating costs $77–140/metric tonne (site/date/currency details require qualification): https://semspub.epa.gov/work/HQ/189814.pdf . We do **not** promote that international historical range to a Texas 2026 vendor estimate; the $40–140/t assumptions below are intentionally hypothetical sensitivity only, and exclude capital recovery and for some interpretations nutrient polishing.

## One-ton gross nutrient revenue ceiling versus costs
Input unit: one wet metric ton of collected food waste (not compost/digestate product weight):
- NPK replacement value: ~$5.31–17.05 per ton of incoming waste using current assumed nutrient yields/chemical fertilizer prices.
- Hypothetical gate fees $20–30 per US short ton equal **~$22.05–33.07 per metric tonne** received, if a processor actually charges and collects them.
- Hypothetical OPEX $40–140 per metric ton treated: before capital charges, feedstock travel, sales, upgrading, nutrient extraction and safe digestate handling.
- Potential gross methane range 60–150 m³ CH4/t collected: at an **illustrative $6/MMBtu LHV** and 95% upgrader methane recovery × 95% uptime, methane *commodity-only* equivalent $11.02–27.56 per wet metric ton of food waste. Grid spec and gas marketing differ (typically HHV); credit eligibility is not assured.
- These are **not additive net benefits until physical and contractual constraints are reconciled**; e.g., adding digestate sale and NPK fertilizer offset for the same nutrients double-counts.

### Illustrative combined commodity + tipping + nutrient values (no incentives)
Per treated metric ton: a very broad gross envelope of **$38–78/t** from ~$5–17 nutrient value, ~$22–33 tipping charges, and ~$11–28 methane commodity value, with no capex and no processing expense; at hypothetical $40–140/t OPEX, gross contribution would range **approximately −$102 to +$38 per tonne**, BEFORE capex and local delivery costs. This simplistic envelope is not a project-level profit forecast; it varies low/high cases independently, assumes all nutrients have buyers and cannot justify full plant buildout without site tests.

## Potential economic interpretation
Full standalone food-waste anaerobic digestion may be justified by *combined* gas, disposal, climate and regulatory benefits, not wet digestate fertilizer value by itself. Nutrient recovery can be a secondary benefit, or a locally attractive *avoidance* of nutrient disposal costs. Some NPK is already land-applied from conventional manure/compost; replacing this already-counted recycling with new digestion does not create additional Texas import substitution.
