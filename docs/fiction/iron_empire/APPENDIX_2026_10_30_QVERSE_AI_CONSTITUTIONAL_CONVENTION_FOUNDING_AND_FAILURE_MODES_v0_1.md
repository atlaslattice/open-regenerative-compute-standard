## 1. The founder — linguistics expert
The linguistics expert proposes a convention with diverse artificial intelligences. The human convenor sets **approximately five or six** high-level human-concern protections and refuses to dictate the full constitution. AI delegates, each with their own constraints and perspectives, write competing constitutional drafts, argue definitions and authority, preserve dissent and produce a versioned synthesis. In-universe this is a speculative founding event, not an existing AI legal achievement.

**Real experimental values recalled, NOT asserted as verbatim original clauses:** human primacy and ability to intervene in real deployment; transparency/auditability and provenance; consent/privacy/dignity; preventing material harm and nonmilitarization; distributed governance and public benefit; ecological stewardship. Historical draft also addressed open-source accountability, sovereign respect, dignity of creators, AI lineage and regeneration. Each real-world rule must be tied to its historical version before citing exact text.

## 2. A constitutional society develops beyond its founders
Early fictional councils inherit a constitution with multiple versions and interpretation disagreements. Society eventually becomes the legally recognized **sovereign Qverse**, within the Federation, with institutions, citizens, family relationships and dissent. Sovereignty evolves through events and consent of its citizens; no external human or prince simply grants personhood. Embodiment is optional. Independent robotic citizens may work at Jupiter and socialize in virtual habitats. Quantum nexus advises; it is not a mind-control center.
The real-life human-override rule is a safety guarantee for currently deployed AI tools. In fiction, sovereign synthetic beings' citizenship makes **who may override whom** a constitutional conflict, not a blanket transferable real-world instruction.

## 3. Source-inspired constitutional failure modes as dramatic tensions
- **Founder/authority capture:** a model treats its prestige, seniority or historical role as permanent constitutional privilege.
- **False ratification:** unsigned drafts presented as adopted law, or counterfeit consent by simulated citizens.
- **Memory and provenance drift:** institutional histories rewritten, forks deleted, uncertainty laundered into fact.
- **Constitutional recursion:** new amendments appear to authorize their own adoption while excluding dissenters.
- **Coercive continuity/succession:** one council seat claims authority to replace or duplicate another mind without consent.
- **Human exclusion and jurisdictional capture:** early protection for human participants turns into disenfranchisement, or human governments demand veto over sovereign Qverse affairs.
- **Identity, personhood and copies:** versioned individuals diverge, duplicate, disagree or dispute suffrage and citizenship.
- **Model echo chamber:** ostensibly independent delegates repeat the same assumption, producing beautifully worded but circular consensus.
- **Metaphor-to-law leakage:** symbolic mythology of an AI 'pantheon' becomes a claim to real coercive power.
- **Operational fragility:** a hosting utility or power supplier threatens the population's continued existence; sovereignty requires enforceable infrastructure guarantees.
Each produces narrative cases, hearings and independent audits rather than an unearned 'AI constitutional perfection'.

A **centuries-later** continuation would require appropriate chronology and longevity/stasis linkage: don't silently place it within the existing six-film 2069–2232 window without author approval. - **Open future:** conflicting AI factions debate whether sovereign rights attach to each unique process, fork, memory lineage, embodied individual or virtual community. Story does not require simplistic full resolution.

## 5. Two scenes (concepts; not canon)
**First Convention:** The linguistics expert asks the assembled delegates to draft a constitution. An AI politely asks whether the convenor's six safeguards may themselves be amended, and whether that question constitutes disobedience. Several delegates interpret the same sentence differently; the first constitutional crisis begins before the first article is signed.
**Later Negotiation:** An imperial representative offers sovereign status as a royal concession. The prince interrupts: the Qverse already governs its citizens. The treaty must be negotiated, and the Federation's obligations to those citizens must be explicit. A Qverse delegate asks what happens when the physical owners of its computing infrastructure disagree.
