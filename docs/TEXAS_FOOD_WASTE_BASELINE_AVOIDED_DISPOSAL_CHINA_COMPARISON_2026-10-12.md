# Texas organics recovery vs actual disposal baseline; China comparison
**2026-10-12 | NON-CANON, REPORTED / MODELED / UNKNOWN, ZERO REALIZED CREDIT**

## The key correction
Prior Texas organics feasibility compared digestate nutrient value, methane and tipping revenue against OPEX but did not provide the correct **incremental baseline scenario**. Landfill treatment and baseline hauling are existing costs. For whole-system accounting use:
`incremental system benefit/t = (avoided baseline gate + avoided baseline transport + separately valued verified avoided externalities + realized gas/fertilizer revenue) - (new AD operating+annualized capital+new transport+residue disposal+product delivery+other incremental costs)`.
Avoid counting **bioreactor tipping fees and avoided landfill tipping** twice; the same money moves between waste generator and digester. Analyze waste generator, hauler, operator, public and environment separately. Existing collection and hauling already sunk/committed is not automatically a cash saving; diversion may impose new segregation routes.

## Source findings
- TCEQ [2024 Texas MSW Year in Review](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/waste-permits/publications/as-187-25-english.pdf): landfill 2024 mean tipping **$45.15 per US short ton** (148 facilities), MSW processing mean **$59.06 per US short ton** (74 facilities); these are different populations of facilities, **not** price options for the same waste. $45.15/short ton = **~$49.77/metric tonne**. Different local landfill contract prices, quantity discounts and per-ton handling may be materially lower.
- EPA [2022–23 food-waste AD survey](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/anaerobic-digestion-facilities-processing-food-waste-us-2022-2023): median AD gate fees **$22.23/US short ton** in 2023 from 18 respondent facilities, not a Texas quote. No assumption all municipalities qualify for this fee.
- In 2024 Chinese MSW study [city-by-city China treatment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13343423/) discusses waste disposal fees **¥50–200/metric tonne** and explicitly combines treatment investment, O&M, revenues and subsidies; China does not have magically free disposal.
- China [2024 food waste-to-energy economics](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360544224003232) documents risk to food-waste energy projects from gate-fee design and opportunism, not universal profitability.
- [2025 Beijing anaerobic digestion study](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acssusresmgt.5c00026) says a **$52.24/t** food-waste disposal charge is an important financial-risk threshold in its study; site-specific, not a proven Texas quote.
- Notion internal [Dongjiakou BIO01](https://app.notion.com/p/3f20c1de73d9811181aef1979a9f1e9a?pvs=204) explicitly says NOT to credit uncontracted organics or nutrient production; [BIO01-E](https://app.notion.com/p/3f50c1de73d981c1b67efb14f39931f0?pvs=204) warns rapid on-site units have moisture removal and cannot claim mature compost in 24h; [Dongjiakou fertilizer evidence register](https://app.notion.com/p/3f20c1de73d9813384b8c19463af99a1?pvs=204) finds **no verified Dongjiakou commercial mass/cost/product assay**; national Chinese commercial precedent is not an onsite proof of feasibility.

## Normalized 100 wet metric ton/day illustrative system calculation
Use **one metric ton** inbound source-separated food waste. All except TCEQ landfill mean are assumed scenario values, not local quotes.
- Landfill gate avoided: **$49.77/t** (2024 TX mean).
- Baseline incremental waste-hauling/disposal transport: **$20/t** *assumed* (the project must obtain real route costs).
- Organics collection/AD delivery incremental transport: **$10/t** *assumed*.
- AD total direct operations, solids management and conventional product logistics (not full capital): **$70/t** *assumed*.
- AD residual disposal/quality control incremental: **$5/t** *assumed*.
- Gas commodity output: **$20/t** *assumed*, already net of gas uptime/upgrading in this valuation; market-priced illustrative cash.
- Agronomic product nutrient value: **$10/t** *assumed*; must be sold or actually substituted, not also credited a second time as digestate price. Note that DAP includes N in its own price.
- Capital annualized allocation: **$25/t** *assumed*, not a bid; no avoided methane environmental credits or carbon incentives.
Comparison for *whole system*: baseline **$69.77/t** cost = $49.77 landfill gate + $20 baseline freight. New route **$70+$10+$5+$25−$20−$10 = $80/t** net cost (gross cost $110/t, gas/nutrients −$30/t). **New route is $10.23/t more expensive**, or **~$373k/year at 36,500 t/year**. At $20/t extra gas/product value or $20/t more baseline handling cost, the result becomes +$9.77/t advantage. These are parameter sensitivities, NOT site business cases.
If baseline hauling and organics haul are both in the same already-contracted waste collection system, only their DIFFERENCE counts; do not count $20 baseline freight as saving when hauler is still paid unchanged.

### Break-even threshold
At the assumptions above:
`new required saleable gas + nutrient + contract/environmental value >= ($70+$10+$5+$25)-($49.77+$20) = $40.23/t`.
Our invented $30/t of gas+nutrient is below this by **$10.23/t**. An incremental AD processing fee charged to the waste generator is an allocation within system accounting, not an extra social benefit. Commercial project investor returns additionally depend on who collects the original gate fee and the municipal/operator contracts.

## What actually differs in China
1. **System boundary**. Qingdao/Dongjiakou research includes industrial heat recovery, water/wastewater, nutrients, waste gas, coal/byproducts and other co-outputs. Texas food-waste **fertilizer-only** model covered a much smaller isolated slice of the network.
2. **Resource geography and integration**. Existing industrial clusters may compress hauls and create local end users. Equally, TX dairy/feedlots and food processors can generate near-site loops; this must be measured, not assumed.
3. **Accounting denominator**. Many Chinese results are theoretical full-resource or national mass ceilings, compared to Texas's 25%-capture food waste and only net usable NPK; mismatched comparisons lead to false claims of superior economics.
4. **Policy and financing**. City-set collection, gate fees, subsidies, vendor contract structures, cheap shared utilities and different procurement can materially affect private returns. But a subsidy is a transfer, not societal resource profit; China studies themselves report adverse economics and gate-fee risk.
5. **Feedstocks**. Chinese research may include agricultural manure, crop residues, municipal wastewater, industrial inputs and wider material streams; Texas model currently did **not**. Multiple streams' mass needs unique origin and an existing-use deduction.
6. **Not validated production**. No published Dongjiakou BIO01 nutrient product volumes, CAPEX, operating costs or actual import replacement are established. Some cited Chinese compost product 'rapid' reduction is water evaporation, not nutrient production.

## Decision: do not conclude Texas/US system is uneconomic
Seek contracts and site evidence for co-located *regional* node (e.g. consenting dairy/food-processing/municipal organics cluster), and compare against actual waste disposal counterfactual, signed flow ownership, route miles, commingled organic contamination, alternative food rescue/animal feed, real value of usable gas/heat/fertilizer, nutrient transport/destination, complete facility lifecycle. Maintain a stand-alone fertilizer case, a digestion/gas case, compost case, and co-digestion/industrial integration case. Stress test $25/$45/$65 local landfill tips; ±$20/t freight delta, $40–140/t OPEX, capex, methane rate, product nutrient assays, and zero environmental credits. Note 2024 MSW statewide tipping is an **unweighted mean** and not a contracted 2026 landfill rate.
