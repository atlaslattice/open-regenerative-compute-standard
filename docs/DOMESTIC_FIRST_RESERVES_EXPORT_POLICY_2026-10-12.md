# Domestic Supply, Reserves and Exports: China 2.0, Proposed America-First Analog, Russia/India Comparison

Date: 2026-10-12. **PROPOSED POLICY COMPARISON / NON-CANON / ZERO REALIZED CREDIT. This is not US or Chinese national law, not a binding embargo, and not an instruction to evade export regulations.**

## Source concept (China 2.0 / Dongjiakou)

[National surplus, zero-import gate, and cooperative ledger v0.1](https://app.notion.com/p/3f40c1de73d9816183e6f284057ab613?pvs=204) defines three distinct proof obligations: (1) domestic usable supply meets *national*, not merely Atlas, commodity-grade demand plus required reserves; (2) actual commodity-grade import volume is verified zero for a defined period before marking an import-elimination objective achieved; and (3) any domestic production due to Atlas is measured separately from total national supply. Materials supplied internally to cooperating nodes are modeled at audited cost, with no guaranteed external sale revenue. An export sale gate additionally requires legally transferable, uncommitted reserve-adjusted surplus and all relevant lawful permits. This design is a voluntary cooperative planning rule unless an authorized government adopts it.

## America First translation (proposal, not existing policy)

An analogous *domestic-first resilience ledger* would track strategic critical-resource demand, secure domestic production, essential civilian and defense needs, regional/quality shortfalls, essential reserve minimum, and actual net trade before recommending export of an unencumbered surplus. An industrial co-op may choose lawful domestic-first supply and reserve contracts; **only governments exercising valid statutory authority can impose nationwide export restrictions**, and export controls are resource-/country-/end-use-specific. Proposals must preserve public-health, lawful commercial obligations, emergency imports, price and trade impacts, treaty constraints, anti-discrimination requirements and transparent appeal. Restricting exports can carry economic costs or provoke retaliation; competition with China does not by itself justify prohibition.

Illustrative accounting identity: AVAILABLE_SECURE_DOMESTIC_T = verified production + physically accessible inventories + compliant substitutes - losses - prior enforceable obligations; UNSERVED_NEED_T = max(0, forecast vital demand + validated emergency reserve requirement - available secure domestic supply). A positive surplus only exists after essential needs, seasonal bottlenecks, environmental caps and reserves are satisfied. Realized US resource surplus and export-policy changes = **NONE ESTABLISHED** by this study.

## Comparison with Russia / India oil procurement episode

The project archive notes March 2026 oil-trade disruption around Hormuz and a reported temporary US Treasury authorization for Indian purchases of some Russian-origin oil (see [Hormuz briefing](https://github.com/atlaslattice/atlas-lattice-foundation/blob/main/docs/Hormuz_Escort_Briefing.md) and [India impact analysis](https://github.com/atlaslattice/atlas-lattice-foundation/blob/main/docs/GRII_India_Impact_Analysis.md)). Treat that account as **REPORTED IN PRIOR INTERNAL RESEARCH, NOT independently verified here**. It illustrates why strategic reserves, source diversification, import dependence and *temporary sanctions exceptions* are separate policy levers. India's agreement to buy Russian oil is not evidence of a negotiated Russia–US domestic-reserve model, and is not evidence China, US or Russia has adopted this proposed gate.

Temporary sanctions authorizations for particular goods and counterparties are legally and causally different from a structural rule permitting exports only after complete domestic sufficiency. Don't call them interchangeable or assert a specific 'Russia deal' beyond the sourced transaction. The research comparison must be amended when the exact agreement, commodity, date and legal authority are established.
