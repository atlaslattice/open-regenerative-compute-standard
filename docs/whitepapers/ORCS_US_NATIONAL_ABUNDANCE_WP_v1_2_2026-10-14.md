# ORCS White Paper v1.2 — American Industrial Abundance, Fuel Logistics and Strategic Reserves

**2026-10-14 | PUBLIC RESEARCH · PROPOSED/NON-CANON · NO REALIZED NATIONAL CREDIT**

## Thesis
Build a nationally coordinated, state-resolved network for domestic industrial supply, transportation, strategic reserves, resource recovery and energy affordability. Measure physical throughput and public economic benefits first. Company margins and public subsidy costs are feasibility constraints. **Domestic needs → verified, grade-specific reserve target → lawfully exportable surplus** is a scenario, not adopted U.S. trade law.

## Empirical baseline
2025 BEA goods trade deficit **$1.241 trillion**, goods/services deficit **$901.5 billion**. [BEA official release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/us-international-trade-goods-and-services-december-and-annual-2025). Different denominators from China's 2026 merchandise surplus.
2025 EIA distillate, million barrels: net refinery/blender production **1,822.486**; domestic product supplied **1,426.504**; exports **456.168**; imports **57.989**; Gulf Coast exports **411.180**; East Coast imports **45.424**. [Matched EIA flows](../US_2025_MATCHED_TRADE_AND_RESERVE_GATE_PILOT_V0_2_2026-10-12.md).
At **hypothetical $100/bbl**, complete replacement of East Coast imports = **$4.5424B gross goods imports avoided**. If same barrels diverted from $100/bbl exports, direct goods trade balance change is **$0**, not $4.54B. If genuinely additional refinery production replaces imports while exports persist, first-order positive goods balance up to **$4.54B** before extra imported inputs. The $4.54B is product value, **NOT foreign ocean freight paid**.

## National economic accounting
Five separated and reconcilable accounts: (1) trade value and customs terms, (2) gross domestic transportation/manufacturing output, (3) U.S. GDP value-added and employment, (4) public cost/benefit, including real affordability, reserves and resilience, (5) operator free cash and lawful subsidy costs. Domestic freight spending may support American wages and upstream suppliers but should not be reported twice as total GDP or automatically multiplied by 1.5. Provide 1.0–2.0 optional sensitivity **with displacement and import-leakage adjustments**. Avoided foreign ocean freight must be priced and assigned to the actual cost-bearing party; otherwise UNKNOWN. Domestic pipelines, coastal vessels, rail and trucks are compared by actual achievable route and all-in cost, not assumed to be universally cheaper. Modal environmental and resilience costs included.

## Quantified initial reserve screen
90 days of **total distillate use** at the 2025 daily average = ~351.7M bbl; published year-end marketed distillate stocks ~100.2M bbl = ~25.6 days. Not certified emergency inventory, nor proof of actual strategic shortfall. The IEA minimum 90-day oil-stock rule refers to **net imports**, not days of gasoline/diesel domestic consumption. User's 90-day use target is a proposed policy.

## Architecture
51+ independent state/federal geographic accounting partitions, refineries and product slate; inventory by region and grade; transport edge graph with capacity, rate, transit, risk, owner, permissions and status; reserves; energy/water/circular outputs; product prices and downstream diesel pass-through. Data sources: EIA refining and PADD; BTS FAF for benchmark freight; USITC HS/HTS; BEA Input–Output; USGS mineral data; owner-authorized private operational sources. Provenance tags **MEASURED/DERIVED/MODELED/PROPOSED/UNKNOWN**. Regional and live capacities currently **UNKNOWN**; do not invent.

## Policy sensitivity
Business-as-usual; voluntary domestic routing; permissible shipping waivers; capacity restoration and refinery efficiency; grade-specific domestic-first/90-day-reserve gate; import restriction alternatives with shortage safeguards; comprehensive refinery–desalination energy integration. DPA powers support certain priority/capacity measures but do **not automatically authorize import/export bans or waive all permitting requirements**. Analyze legality and ocean/ecology non-negotiable constraints.
