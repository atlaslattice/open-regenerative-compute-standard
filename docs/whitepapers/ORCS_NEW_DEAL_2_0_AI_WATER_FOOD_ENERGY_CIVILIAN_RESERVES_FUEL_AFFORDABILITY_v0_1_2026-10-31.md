# New Deal 2.0 × ORCS — AI-Powered Water, Food, Energy, Civilian Reserves and National Resource Security

**Public research white paper v0.1 | 2026-10-31 | Atlas Lattice / Open Regenerative Compute Standard**

**Status: PROPOSED / SIMULATED / NON-CANON / ZERO REALIZED CREDIT. No legislative adoption, appropriated funding, signed government or corporate contracts, refinery agreement, price ceiling, physical deployment, verified national net saving, or regulatory authorization is claimed.** Preserve earlier source work and contradictions for audit. This is a policy and engineering hypothesis, not an enactment, investment-grade forecast, legal opinion or endorsement by OpenAI, other AI companies, oil companies or government agencies.

## Executive proposition

Treat AI infrastructure as an anchor customer and potential co-financier of **productive public-benefit infrastructure** rather than as an extractive project buying after-the-fact environmental offsets. Integrate clean residential drinking water, affordable community electricity, lower-cost domestically produced food, wastewater nutrient recovery, organics processing, circular construction, and community beautification. Humans and AI jointly monitor operations, but trained people and legally responsible entities retain consequential authority.

A federated national intelligence layer joins plant measurements with county, state and national commodity and freight ledgers. It identifies genuine shortfalls, verified surpluses, vulnerable import chains, competitive domestic substitutes and opportunities for cost-effective routing. Publish safe aggregates, preserve sensitive data rights and test the system through *Hormuz, natural disaster and armed-conflict civilian continuity* stress scenarios.

**Mission order:** (1) demonstrated public benefit and safety, (2) viable and fair full-lifecycle financing, (3) broader trade, resilience and productive-capacity gains. The goal is beneficial infrastructure that can sustain itself commercially where practicable; universal-service and resilience obligations receive explicit funding rather than concealed profit claims.

## 1. Citizen-facing targets, without pretending they have been achieved

1. **Free, certified clean residential water — explicit long-term target.** Aim for a zero-dollar basic residential potable-water bill or fully funded bill credit for participating households, subject to legal utility rules, independently verified water-quality performance, dependable service, full infrastructure and O&M costs, equitable inclusion of renters and multifamily users, reasonable use/anti-waste safeguards and secure separate funding. **This target is retained, not replaced by the weaker promise of cheap water or nominal recycling.** Free refers to household financial incidence, not to zero resource or treatment cost. Do not underfund operator safety, source protection, replacement reserves or wastewater duties to make tariffs appear zero. Service scope beyond essential residential use requires detailed tariff design.
2. **Affordable household electricity.** Test an average 50%-of-comparable-retail-rate target for delivered residential kWh, including transmission, distribution, firming, time-of-use, taxes and reliability. An annual renewable-MWh match is not proof of 24/7 supply. The same kWh cannot be credited as both commercial export and free community delivery.
3. **Grocery price stability via water, nutrient and logistics cost reduction.** Dramatically reduce eligible agricultural freshwater withdrawals where legally and physically feasible through qualified wastewater reuse, leak control and crop-specific irrigation efficiency; lower purchased fertilizer expense through safely certified recovered N/P/K and agronomic precision; add affordable energy, cold-chain capacity, food rescue, regional production and transparent procurement. Measure actual **farm margin, delivered wholesale cost and retail grocery basket** separately. Savings at the farm gate do not automatically pass through to supermarkets: retail food prices also reflect processing, refrigeration, freight, wages, property and competitive market conditions.
4. **Affordable gasoline as a separate policy lane.** Retain the user's **$3.00 per U.S. gallon domestic regular-gasoline retail ceiling objective** as a legislative and contractual research target, **not** a currently feasible guarantee, enacted ceiling or a result already derived from the ORCS energy modules. Test whether lower physical demand, refinery modernization, reliable reserves, lower delivery costs, market competition and targeted consumer support could achieve the target without queues, shortages, hidden taxpayer transfers or disproportionate regional costs.
5. **Good jobs, durable local benefits.** Report construction job-years, permanent FTE jobs, retention, wages, apprenticeships, local procurement and net additional output. Wages paid are company costs and household earnings, not extra consolidated corporate profit.
6. **Verified local environmental improvement.** Measure aquifer withdrawals actually displaced, water quality, nutrients not discharged, landfill reduction, emissions, native habitat and sustained maintenance costs. No claims of net-positive watershed impact based only on nominal treatment volume.

Protect essential household services during financing shortfalls, outages and natural disasters. Set public benefit covenants before a project earns ORCS compliance status.

## 2. Seven physical modules: commercial service with public-first outputs

| Module | Public good | Creditable enterprise economics |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Community power** | Safe, reliable, lower-cost household electricity; microgrid resilience | Contracted sales, approved utility service, verified avoided purchases, thermal reuse, solar, storage, qualified geothermal, biogas |
| **2. Clean drinking water + agricultural reuse** | Free certified residential potable water as funding target, reduced aquifer depletion, secure firefighting and irrigation supply | Treatment and reclaimed-water service contracts, industrial offtake, avoided legacy cost, separately financed utility assets |
| **3. Wastewater resource mining** | Lower nutrient pollution and recovered domestic fertilizer ingredients | Assayed, sold struvite, N/P/K, eligible industrial reagents, distinct avoided disposal and O&M savings |
| **4. Food waste + bioreactors** | Food rescue first; safer organics diversion, fewer harmful discharges | Verified processing fees, gas/heat, compliant compost, certified fertilizer and qualified material sales |
| **5. Agriculture + food processing** | Lower food-cost pressure, farmers' net margins and local jobs | Contracted crops/greenhouse output, packaging, cold chain, institutional procurement, avoided spoilage |
| **6. Circular facility construction + Desen-compatible material recovery** | Reduce virgin material demand, landfill burden and construction impacts | Lower net construction input costs, recovered materials, local manufacturing, legitimate sales to unrelated projects |
| **7. Beautification (TBD)** | Attractive, healthy, accessible landscapes and habitats surrounding infrastructure | Maintained as separately costed public good; optional genuine commercial revenue only if verified |

**Construction invariant:** Design the facilities themselves *from* certified reclaimed components and qualified recycled construction-and-demolition material wherever technically, legally and economically justified, not merely retrofit a recycling station after construction. Prefer reuse of existing structures and components. Desen screening/washing/classification is a candidate part of recovery; compliant structural products require independent processing, testing and construction approvals. Target **at least 75% verified construction-waste diversion** for eligible projects, with separately measured recycled-input content, grade suitability, recovered material reused at another build, sold to independent buyers, or safely disposed. Preserve chain of custody and end-of-life design. A ton used onsite is not also a ton sold.

The seven modules can be co-located **or connected as a regional network** where transport, water rights, ecological protection and economics favor distributed sites. Each module must have both its own full-cost ledger and an integrated, intercompany-eliminated national ledger. Do not presume all modules or all geographies have positive cash margins.

## 3. Local human/AI operations and national Atlas Lattice intelligence

Provide instrumented local inference and digital-twin support for the seven modules: operational anomaly detection, equipment maintenance, water quality, nutrient recovery, farming conditions, construction-grade sorting, energy and freight forecasts. Qualified people retain operational authority; validated PLC/SCADA protection remains independent and offline-capable. No autonomous change to drinking-water disinfection, safety interlocks, emergency resource access or export permissions.

An **eighth cross-cutting information layer**, *Atlas Lattice HSN / National Resource Intelligence Hub*, can use House–Sphere–Node semantic labels as an **illustrative** complementary index, not replace FIPS, NAICS, HS/HTS, USGS/EIA/USDA classifications, actual addresses, grades or regulatory identifiers. China's 1+M+N national/regional/industry computing-resource interconnection offers a structural comparator, not proof that physical American commodity coordination has been demonstrated by that system.

Minimum fields: material/commodity identity and grade, units, owner and rights, locality, dated baseline, measured production, imports, exports, consumption, stocks, planned additions, current and forecast essential demand, permitted substitute, buyer and price basis, freight mode/capacity/delivered cost, quality certificate, reserve eligibility, operator authority, uncertainty, source and receipt.

Join public datasets (EIA, USDA, USGS, Census, DOT, FEMA, EPA, BEA, BLS) to authorized local industry measurements. Give the public aggregated audited outcomes; preserve commercial confidentiality and sensitive infrastructure security. Human reviewers must approve any high-stakes resource allocation. Training-data and AI-services revenues remain **zero in the base case** until independently contracted and netted against cost; verify actual model improvement against holdout regions, seasons and operators.

**Freight is a first-class constraint**: pipelines, coastwise marine capacity, inland marine, truck, rail, cold storage, terminals, Jones Act legal requirements, grade compatibility, trucker/rail scheduling, last-mile costs, available transport and time-to-delivery. Simulated excess material with no economically viable freight path is not a supply solution.

## 4. National trade, price stability and resilience mandates

Track **national and local trade deficits** by exact products, not by broad monetary aggregates alone. Goals: qualified domestic import displacement, diversified international sourcing, real downstream manufacturing, domestic-processing capacity, competitively priced regional transfers and adequate essential inventories. Export receipts forgone, supply substitutions and quality differences belong in the same calculation. Self-sufficiency is a strategic resilience metric, not a categorical claim that all imports are harmful.

Publish public aggregate price/volume ledgers and affordable consumer baskets. Distinguish an unexplained margin from proof of illegal gouging. Identify unusual regional freight and supplier spreads, publish methodology and refer genuinely suspicious conduct to authorized competition/consumer protection officials; do not turn a shared trading network into a hub for firms to coordinate prices.

Stress-test:
- **2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption**: petroleum, LNG, refined-fuel prices, fertilizer, sulfur, aluminum, insurance and maritime freight cascades, with crop-season impacts.
- Hurricanes, drought, wildfires, earthquake, flooding, severe winter storms, coastal port closures, wildfire smoke, grid failures, cyber incidents and pandemic supply disruptions.
- Armed-conflict disruption to import routes and manufacturing, while safeguarding civilian priorities, rights and private ownership.
- **7 / 30 / 90 day civilian continuity** by essential commodity and place, based on locally accessible *usable* inventories, replenishment lead times and legally available resources rather than theoretical national stock mass.

Every outcome separates observed service protection, forecast prevented losses, financial value, ecological benefit and uncertainty.

## 5. National Civilian Strategic Resource Reserve: new integrated overlay

**Design objective:** Establish a **nationwide, civilian-priority, multi-commodity strategic reserve and surge-capacity coordination system** alongside existing statutory stockpiles and ordinary commerce. It is **not** the first federal stockpile: the U.S. already has the Strategic Petroleum Reserve, National Defense Stockpile, Strategic National Stockpile for medical countermeasures, and dedicated regional products such as the Northeast Home Heating Oil Reserve. The novel design is integrated *civilian continuity* across water, energy, food, fertilizer, treatment chemicals, critical replacement parts, material processing capacity, transport and geographically dispersed delivery.

Layers:
- **Physical stores:** purchased or contractually reserved eligible inventories, quality-tested and distributed to avoid a single chokepoint.
- **Rotating commercial inventories:** lawful agreements to rotate perishables and keep minimum availability without waste; payment and title explicit.
- **Production-capacity reserves:** contracted surge capacity, maintenance, skilled crews, regional plants and supplier diversity (not misreported as immediately deliverable stock).
- **Emergency logistics reserve:** terminals, cold-chain capacity, rail/ship/truck contracts and backup utility service.
- **Public oversight:** safe aggregated reserve coverage, dated gap indicators, audited withdrawals, release priorities, appeal and replenishment rules; no public disclosure of vulnerable specific-site critical stocks.

**User-proposed 20% reserve-before-export principle:** First satisfy verified current and forecast essential domestic demand; then satisfy a legally established strategic reserve or replenishment obligation; only *verified uncommitted qualified surplus* is eligible for export review. For transparent modeling, **20% can mean different things and must not be conflated**:
(A) reserve contribution of 20% of otherwise eligible *new surplus output* before exports; (B) 20% of *annual essential domestic demand* held as a reserve inventory; or (C) earlier ORCS research's **20% of annual gross matching imports** as an import-exposure reserve allowance. These have radically different physical stocks and economic costs. Preserve the user's 20% target, test A/B/C explicitly by grade and region, and **do not silently certify one as enacted, equivalent or universally appropriate**. For perishables, fuels, water and critical components, choose science-based coverage days and replenishment rather than universal ratios.

**Legal boundary:** The existing U.S. crude-oil export framework generally bars federal officials from imposing export restrictions except under specified statutory exceptions (42 U.S.C. §6212a). A durable nationwide domestic-first/20% reserve-before-export rule would require detailed congressional legislation or another demonstrably applicable legal basis, scrutiny of trade commitments, existing sales contracts, consumer price impacts, allied exposure, compensation and due process. A model cannot suspend exports, create reserves or commandeer privately owned goods. Use voluntary contractual domestic-priority offtake and authorized procurement as an independently modeled alternative.

## 6. Separate $3/gallon gasoline and oil modernization proposal — preserve without merging budgets

**User-reported research concept, original detailed oil-company financial model not located in reviewed ORCS GitHub files:** Approximately **$100B one-time modernization support to oil-sector companies** combined with an independently modeled **approximately $100B/year affordability or value proposition**, with a domestic regular-gasoline **$3/gallon maximum-price objective**, domestic priority, and 20% strategic-reserve-before-export conditions. This paragraph **records the user's proposal**, not a verified oil-company offer, secured annual savings, specific legal instrument or enacted retail ceiling.

**Crucial finance separation:** The one-time $100B is **not automatically included** in the original $1.5T civilian loan pool, the separate proposed $180B liquidity/risk window, the proposed $20B standards program, or the AI campus regenerative capital. Treat it as an **additional candidate capital line until allocation, financing and overlaps are explicitly reconciled**. An annual $100B consumer expenditure reduction is not the same thing as $100B public revenue, project cash or petroleum-industry net margin.

**Transparent magnitude check (DERIVED from EIA 2025 gasoline volume):** EIA estimated U.S. finished motor gasoline supplied domestically at about 8.91 million barrels/day in 2025. Using 42 gallons/barrel and 365 days gives about **136.6B gallons/year**. On unchanged volume, a **$0.73/gallon** fall in national average retail price corresponds to roughly **$100B/year in gross consumer expenditures**. This is a comparison of spending, **not** proof of an equivalent net welfare gain, federal revenue, or a consequence of modernization. A **$100B up-front amount financed over 30 years at 5%** would require approximately **$6.51B/year** of level debt service *before* operations and maintenance; on that gasoline volume, debt payment alone is approximately **4.8 cents/gallon**. These are entirely different $100B concepts.

The retail target must distinguish **national average** vs a legally enforceable **every-station cap**, taxes by state, grade/formulation, geography, crude-oil world prices, refinery margins, blending, distribution, demand, and shortages. EIA's August 2026 national average of $4.058/gallon illustrates possible spread to the target but not attainability or the duration of savings. A binding below-market price ceiling without sufficient supply or funded compensation can cause shortages, rationing, lower incentives for production or cost shifted to taxpayers. An oil-company modernization payment is not proof that third-party retail stations will comply.

**Candidate contract design for independent examination only:**
1. Competitive, open solicitations rather than unconditional transfers; qualified refinery flexibility, pipeline/terminal bottleneck relief, storage, reliability and emissions/water modernization.
2. Clearly defined pricing mechanism: voluntary, enforceable wholesale-offtake with pass-through auditing, regionally differentiated household support, or explicit future legislation—not a hidden assumption of control over all retail prices.
3. Milestone-based loans, cost sharing, recoverable advances or performance payments, verified incremental capacity, public milestones, clawbacks for documented breach, antitrust safeguards, audit and repayment covenants.
4. Measure demand reduction independently: higher fuel economy, electrification and improved freight routing may reduce motor-fuel consumption; cheap electricity at water/AI campuses by itself does not directly lower gasoline demand. Lower domestic demand does not automatically translate into lower pump prices while world oil markets and refinery logistics remain binding.
5. Stress-test supply against **Hormuz closure, refinery outages, Gulf-to-East freight restrictions, winter diesel/jet grade differences**, and international spillovers.
6. Publish side-by-side no-intervention, contracted-modernization, targeted-subsidy, voluntary domestic-priority, and newly legislated export/reserve cases; report shortage probability, average pump prices, consumer and taxpayer incidence, oil-sector investment and producer margins, actual imports/exports and price volatility.

**Do not count gasoline savings twice** as lower household expenses and simultaneous government procurement savings, unless independent transactions warrant the distinction.

## 7. Original New Deal 2.0 fiscal framework and three comparisons

Preserve the **October 17, 2026 New Deal 2.0 v0.2** as original civilian-research baseline:
- **Up to $1.5T distinct financed civilian productive infrastructure**, illustratively borrowed full upfront at **5% over 30 years**, with about **$97.58B/year level debt service** and approximately **$2.927T total 30-year nominal payments**. A realistic staggered construction schedule requires multiple amortization cohorts and extends the final loan tail.
- **Separate proposed $200B federal authority**, comprised of **$180B** liquidity/bridges/contingent credit risk and **$20B** standards, permitting, independent labs, workforce, oversight and data interoperability. These are **proposed, not enacted**. The $180B is not all both spent and unspent reserve capacity. Taxpayer exposure, guarantees and credit subsidy require public scoring.
- **1.50× discounted lifecycle benefit/cost** as a project-screening aspiration, **not** proof of profit or debt coverage; project capital, financing, operations, ongoing replacements, publicly funded benefits and contingent guarantees require independent analysis.
- **Year 1 national mobilization; 3-year standardized modular, 5-year complex asset completion targets**, with independent exceptions and enforceable public-benefit receipts.
- The additional **$100B fuel modernization** is a distinct proposed instrument unless an explicit financed-capital reallocation is voted/contracted.

Model three comparison envelopes: **A commercial-only** (seven modules with private and utility project finance), **B AI-integrated** (A + demonstrated facility optimization/AI developer contributions + national intelligence service contracts), and **C New Deal 2.0 financing** (B + authorized public risk/standards finance and transparently purchased noncommercial public services). Fuel-price and civilian-reserve policy should be explicit sub-scenarios across A/B/C rather than automatic savings.

Test hourly firm electricity, quality-assured water, agriculture by crop and season, mineral/nutrient recovery yield and safe end use, certified construction material supply, real freight capacity, 30-year equipment replacements, salaries, public utility laws, water rights, debt coverage and downside scenarios. No AI speculative distributable cash, tax credit, carbon credit, AI training-data license, avoided AI campus delay or public service value enters the base cash ledger without the appropriate signed/verified evidence.

## 8. Evaluation, safeguards and independent evidence gates

Required parallel ledgers:
1. **Consumer affordability:** residential safe-water bill, delivered electricity, regional grocery basket, gasoline price, household service outages, actual beneficiaries.
2. **Business cash:** contracted revenue, avoided real spend, input/transport costs, debt, capital replacement, reserves, risk and net operating margin.
3. **Government finance:** separate authorized spending, grants, debt/guarantee exposure, tax receipts actually realized, purchased service and avoided budget outlays.
4. **Domestic supply and trade:** commodity-grade output, consumption, imports/exports, foregone export revenue, qualified reserve draws/replenishment, physically delivered domestic substitutions.
5. **Civilian security:** time-to-service-restoration, 7/30/90-day coverage, safe supply access, county equity, regional redundancy and freighting constraints.
6. **Environmental outcomes:** freshwater withdrawals truly avoided, independently tested drinking water and farm products, recovered materials safely used, soil and habitat performance, emissions and managed residues.
7. **AI and governance:** verified human approvals, instrument provenance, simulator/real-world disagreement, held-out model evaluation, independent safety controls, public data rights, logging and appeals.

**Evidence levels:** MEASURED / OFFICIAL-REPORTED / DERIVED / MODELED / PROPOSED / UNKNOWN / QUARANTINED. Keep dated source URLs for all claims, preserve the original simulations, publish negative cases and pass independent technical, legal and financial challenge review before scaling.

**Implementation order:** (0) legal, environmental and source-data pre-read; (1) select 2–3 contrasting pilot regions with existing operator participation; (2) baseline regional water/power/food/fuel cost and supply, reservoir/soil/waste chemistry, construction bills of materials and actual freight; (3) signed offtakes, financing offers and qualified vendor quotes; (4) run historical Hormuz and hurricane disruptions against no-intervention baselines; (5) pilot operating/data collection with human control; (6) publish audited before/after consumer benefits, project margins, deficit and reserve outcomes; (7) gate expansion to 50-state and every-county obligations.
