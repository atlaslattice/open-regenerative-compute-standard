# ORCS White Paper v1.0 — Recovered Paper Export Redirection, Idle Mill Capacity and National Fiber Reserves
**National Supply Chain Resource Recovery Ledger | 30 October 2026**
**Evidence grade:** Public-data screening model; not a mill-by-mill engineering study or investment offering.
**Scope:** The India-bound export tranche as a near-term candidate; all U.S. recovered-paper exports as a separately modeled longer-horizon scenario.

## Executive summary
The United States exported ~12.4 million **metric tonnes** of recovered paper and fiber in 2024 (approximately $2.6B customs export value). India accounted for ~17% of this volume, implying **~2.108 million metric tonnes** (2.324 million U.S. **short tons**) annually. American containerboard output in 2025 was 36.1 million **short tons** at 91.9% operating rate, corresponding to 39.28 million short tons of capacity and 3.18 million short tons of *theoretical* unused finished-product capacity. All units and different reference years must be preserved.

If 100% of the India-bound material were a compatible recovered-fiber grade, usable domestically at 90% finished-product yield, annual output would be **2.092 million short tons**, occupying ~66% of the 2025 theoretical containerboard headroom and leaving ~1.09 million short tons of annual finished-production headroom. **This is a numerical upper-bound screen, not confirmed existing recyclable-fiber-compatible spare capacity.** Containerboard mills may use virgin fiber, be idled for lack of orders, lack suitable preparation and pulping equipment, or have closed in 2026.

A directional **$840/short-ton** recycled-linerboard benchmark implies **~$1.757B/year gross finished-product sales** or **~$52.7B over 30 years** in constant nominal-price illustrative arithmetic (without discounting or inflation). **Not national net value added or profit**: do not subtract or add headline export customs value as a like-for-like mill profit margin. Some output displaces existing domestic/imported board, and supply/demand pricing responds to added volume.

**Recommendation:** Screen existing compatible mills by location, grade, confirmed 2026 available capacity and order backlog; redirect only contracted flows using regional reserves (e.g. 7–14 days) rather than building 25 new mills or holding all exports in one location. Estimate incremental domestic value added, not gross sales.

## 2. Reproducible capacity arithmetic
Inputs: E=12.4e6 metric t total exports; i=0.17 India fraction; c=1.10231131 short t/metric t; y=0.90 illustrative recovered-fiber to finished-board yield; P=36.1e6 short t (2025); u=0.919.
- India recovered-fiber feedstock: F=E*i*c=**2,323,793 short t/year** (rounded).
- Maximum illustrative India-route finished board: B=F*y=**2,091,414 short t/year**.
- 2025 installed theoretical containerboard capacity: C=P/u=**39,281,828 short t/year**.
- Notional idle finished output: H=C-P=**3,181,828 short t/year**.
- Headroom after India-route diversion (numerical *only*): H-B=**1,090,414 short t/year**.
- Hypothetical total operating rate: (P+B)/C=**97.22%** (does NOT imply technical or commercial feasibility).
- Full 2024 worldwide export stream: E*c=**13.6687M short t feedstock**, y*E*c=**12.302M finished short tons**. This **greatly exceeds** 3.18M 2025 theoretical containerboard headroom; full diversion requires additional capacity, other paper-grade mill capacity, demand substitution, or markets beyond domestic containerboard. Do NOT conflate India-only and entire worldwide stream.

## 3. Gross revenue sensitivity; do not call this profit
At India-route illustrative output B=2.0914M short t:
- $650/t -> ~$1.36B/yr; $750/t -> ~$1.57B/yr; $840/t -> **~$1.76B/yr**; $900/t -> ~$1.88B/yr; $1,000/t -> ~$2.09B/yr.
- 30-year gross sales at constant $840/t, full annual throughput, no ramp-up or downtime: **~$52.7B** (undiscounted, not NPV).
- At $840/t finished and 90% yield, gross sales per feedstock short ton ~$756 before recovered-fiber expense, labor, power, water, chemicals, capital recovery, collection, transport, waste management, distribution, taxes, losses, and sales displacement.
- 2024 global average recovered-paper export value $2.6B/12.4M metric t = ~$210/metric t, **not** a grade-specific India price, transport-adjusted receipt or comparable invoice.
- True national value add = domestic output value minus intermediate inputs and displacement, compared with counterfactual export receipts, resource use, external impacts and alternate use of mills; report incremental EBITDA and net present value separately. Avoid double-counting domestic gross sales, export revenues, imports displaced, and consumer spending.

## 4. National logistics and regional reserve planning
Base logistics inputs (user-adjustable screening assumptions): 25 **metric t** per loaded truck, 150 loaded miles to mill/transfer terminal, $2.50 per loaded mile (scenario assumption, not a verified quoted contract); 365 days/y uniform arrivals.
- India-route inbound movements: 2.108M metric t / 25 metric t/load = **84,320 loads/y**, ~231/day nationwide.
- Loaded miles ~12.65M/y; directional inbound transport = **$31.62M/y** at 150 miles and $2.50/mile (about $15/metric t). 
- Sensitivity: 75 miles -> $15.81M; 300 miles -> $63.24M. *Excludes empty miles, truck access, handling, rail/port opportunity costs, urban congestion, regional constraints, outbound finished board, brokerage, fuel changes, and transloading.* Recalculate with actual origins and routes.
- 7-day national inbound fiber reserve ~40,427 metric t (~44,563 short t); 14-day ~80,855 metric t (~89,127 short t); 30-day ~173,260 metric t. Stockpile at qualified regional depots and mills with fire suppression, moisture control, contamination inspection and grade segregation.
- **1.09M short t annual remaining production capacity is not 1.09M short t of storage space**, nor evidence each mill can absorb reserve stock.

## 5. Allocation algorithm for national supply chain dashboard
For each origin county / material recovery facility o and mill m, obtain:
  annual baled flow f_o,g (metric t/y by grade g); collection contamination x_o,g; accepted recovered-fiber grades g_m; publicly obtainable mill annual *recoverable-fiber-compatible* spare capacity cap_m,g (short t finished/y or input t/y explicitly); committed offtake contract demand d_m (finished product), conversion yield y_m,g, transport route distance D_o,m and quoted handling + rail/trucking rates, warehouse permitted reserve volume, and 2026 operability flags.
Objective: maximize **incremental annual domestic contribution margin** versus verified export / existing domestic placement baseline, subject to grades, demand, facility loading, practical utilization, stock turns, safety and storage constraints. Model export route as valid baseline, not necessarily waste. Preserve current mill customers and avoid cannibalization double counts.
Flags:
  - No confirmed mill offtake -> no redirection commitment.
  - Idle because insufficient orders -> supply alone cannot restore production.
  - Virgin-fiber machine without conversion -> excluded from direct recovered-fiber-eligible headroom.
  - Plant announced closed in 2026 -> removed from active capacity until verified.
  - India-bound paper is a mixture of recovered paper grades, not all OCC.
  - Export value is not a public subsidy; material is generally sold.
  - No unilateral compulsory redirection or stockpiling is proposed; commercial contracts required.

## 6. Deployment lanes
**0–90 days (research and procurement discovery):** build detailed origin-grade/export flows by HS code and state; crosswalk capacity and closure announcements; identify mill grade compatibility and commercial contacts; collect RFQs for rail/truck and reserved storage.
**3–12 months (contract-backed pilot):** route 25k–100k metric t/y of specific compatible recovered paper through 2–5 existing mills; measure realized net contribution, production yield, downtime, residue and distribution costs; compare against export counterfactual.
**12–36 months (expand only on demonstrated margins):** allocate flows into idle verified recovered-fiber-compatible machines and existing depots; consider brownfield conversion versus greenfield only for residual proven demand.
**30-year view:** discounted cash flow and value added by state/county; preserve alternative baseline and sensitivity to fiber prices, end-market paper demand, water/energy and export freight.

## 7. Dashboard integration contract
Layer name: **Recovered Paper / Domestic Capacity Match**. Cards: (a) export leakage by grade and destination, (b) confirmed spare mill input capacity vs theoretical all-containerboard headroom, (c) contracted redirectable fiber, (d) per-ton delivered cost and incremental margin, (e) national 7/14/30-day reserve requirements, (f) 30-year discounted net economic impact. County drill-down and source date required.
Maturity: **screening hypothesis, not verified $50B gain**. Public headline can state: *Up to ~$1.76B/yr hypothetical finished-board sales for 2024 India-bound recovered fiber, subject to grade/market/capacity verification; ~$52.7B 30-year gross sales arithmetic, NOT incremental domestic wealth.*

## 8. Decision gates and risk register
G0 units/source refresh; G1 mill-by-mill active 2026 available recycled-fiber-compatible input tonnage; G2 feedstock quality/grades; G3 firm finished-product demand; G4 net delivered production vs export economics; G5 inventory fire/insurance/environmental permitting; G6 30-year value-added NPV and capacity conversion options.
**Conclusion:** A nationally coherent capacity-matching pilot is justified as a low-CAPEX discovery program. A claim that all India-bound exports can be processed in today's idle domestic containerboard mills remains **unproven**, despite adequate national arithmetic; redirecting all worldwide exports is **not** supported by the containerboard headroom alone.
