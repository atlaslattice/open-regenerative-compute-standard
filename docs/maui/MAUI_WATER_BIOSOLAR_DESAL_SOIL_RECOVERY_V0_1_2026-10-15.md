# Atlas Lattice / ORCS — Maui Water, Biosolar, Circular Materials & Island Resilience v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-15  
**Status:** PROPOSED · NON-CANON · ZERO REALIZED CREDIT  
**Geography:** Maui island primary; Maui County statistics explicitly marked where used  
**Type:** Public research and scenario model; NOT a permitted or financed facility, technical construction design, community endorsement, or representation of any Maui agency.

## Executive thesis

Rebuild water resilience **and** ecological beauty together: desalination where warranted, reclaimed-water delivery before unnecessary new withdrawals, building-integrated photovoltaics and productive agrivoltaics (termed **biosolar** here), regenerative landscape management, careful material recovery, and modest inference/data services sized to real grid/water constraints. Protect local residents, culturally significant sites, nearshore reefs, marine mammals, native habitat and natural stream flows. Prioritize community-led decision-making and Hawaiian rights and stewardship.

**Biosolar definition:** Living landscapes + conventional solar PV, with crop/pollinator/native-plant co-design and water-efficient, fire-conscious planting. Biological photovoltaics may be explored as R&D but are NOT assumed commercially ready or credited with grid energy.

No new Atlas Lattice installations, exports, revenue, avoided imports, soil rehabilitation, ecological benefit, or operational agreement is claimed.

## 2. Desal mass and energy scenario (NOT a Maui design)

Inputs: finished water P=5.0 million US gallons/day (MGD); 1 gal=0.003785411784 m³; sea feed salinity C=35 kg/m³; recovery R=0.45; product salinity assumed 0.5 kg/m³ upper nominal limit; plant-electricity intensity E=3.5 kWh/m³ product. These numbers represent a **hypothetical seawater** case, NOT necessarily Maui's recommended saline groundwater option. Source and membrane reject chemistry must be sampled.

- Product: **18,927 m³/day = 6.91 million m³/year**.
- Intake = P/R: **42,060 m³/day**.
- Liquid brine reject = intake − product: **23,133 m³/day**.
- Dissolved salt into feed: **1,472 tonnes/day**; dissolved salt into product <= **9.5 tonnes/day**; remaining dissolved salt in brine about **1,462 tonnes/day / 534,000 tonnes/year**. Salt in feed **537,000 tonnes/year**. **None are booked as sold salt.**
- Approximate brine dissolved-salt concentration: **63 kg/m³** using simplified conservation (composition-sensitive).
- Electric RO scenario: **66.2 MWh/day = 24.18 GWh/year; 2.76 MW average** before any distribution/site adders omitted from assumed intensity. All-in power boundaries must be recorded.
- Solar annual energy offset at illustrative 20% AC-equivalent capacity factor: **13.8 MW nameplate** for 24.18 GWh/year. This is not a 24/7 supply guarantee; model grid, curtailment, charge/discharge losses and storm-day storage. 12 hours of average plant power equals **33 MWh useful delivered storage** before battery reserve/degradation and power electronics margins.
- Electrically fueled cost-only sensitivity: **$1.93m/year at $0.08/kWh; $7.25m/year at $0.30/kWh**. Not the county plant cost; cannot be compared directly with county ~$10m/year OPEX if cost boundaries differ.

**Correction to earlier conversational baseline:** The 5-MGD example's seawater dissolved-salt intake is ~537,000 tonnes/year, NOT the previously stated ~196,000 tonnes/year. The feed-mass conservation equation and component inventories must agree before economic evaluation.

### Desal brine mineral screening

Only the feed inventory is knowable from representative seawater chemistry; these are **elemental raw-feed inventories**, not recoverable yields or marketable products.

| Component | Assumed seawater kg/m³ | Approximate feed t/year | Technical caution |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Magnesium (element) | 1.29 | 19,800 | Precipitation/reagent/energy/market needed |
| Calcium (element) | 0.41 | 6,290 | May scale membranes; not all recoverable as gypsum |
| Potassium (element) | 0.40 | 6,140 | Fertilizer-product cost/grades matter |
| Bromide (Br⁻, not Br₂) | 0.065 | 998 | Chemistry, reagent safety and buyers required |
| Lithium (element) | 0.00018 | 2.76 | Seawater concentration generally uneconomic |
| Deuterium (isotope) | ~0.000037 | ~0.57 | Very dilute; expensive isotope separation, no assumed energy generation |

The **deuterium line is a first-order exploratory estimate** derived from ~0.0156% atom fraction D/H in seawater, seawater hydrogen mass fraction ~0.108 and 2x mass factor. It requires a precise composition basis and independent validation before use in a business case. Its feed inventory is not equivalent to salable heavy water.

### Gypsum bound (critical)

If seawater-feed calcium ~6,290 t/year were **all** converted to pure CaSO₄·2H₂O, mass stoichiometry would imply ~27,000 t/year of gypsum (172.17/40.08 ratio). This is only a *physics/chemistry ceiling* and NOT a feasible or recommended yield: recovery, reagents, process energy, sulfate availability, membranes/antiscalants, impurities, and local demand constrain it. Study specifically concluded available brine reuse was not recommended at its conceptual sites. Never multiply this ceiling by a gypsum price and call it revenue.

A safer first gypsum recovery candidate may be **clean source-separated drywall** in C&D streams; do NOT use contaminated wildfire debris or mold/lead/asbestos containing material as agricultural gypsum. County C&D throughput is not measured gypsum waste. A scenario of 1%, 3%, or 5% gypsum-board share of a hypothetical **35,000 t/year C&D reference** implies 350 / 1,050 / 1,750 t/year incoming gypsum-bearing boards, not certified finished gypsum; require measured material audit, sorting, coatings, fibers, metals, landfill rules, clean gypsum assay and qualified agronomic use.

## 3. Reuse before desal expansion

Water reuse offers potentially lower energy-intensive freshwater substitution than desal where fit-for-purpose and near customers, subject to treatment, pumping, pipeline, storage, nutrient/contaminant control and disposal rules. County cited ~11.4 MGD **excess** reclaimed water is county-wide and requires a facility-by-facility update.

County-wide *not Maui-only* illustrative utilization of that excess: at 25%, 2.85 MGD (1.04B gal/year); 50%, 5.70 MGD (2.08B); 75%, 8.55 MGD (3.12B). **Do not count wastewater reuse gallons as drinkable or Maui island-specific.** For context 1 MGD sustained is ~1,120 acre-feet/year; agricultural acreage served depends on measured crop water duty, salinity and seasonal availability. Avoid dual claiming reused water as both freshwater created and aquifer recharge.

## 4. Biosolar landscape architecture: three site typologies

**A — Lahaina rebuilding and neighborhood energy.** Building-integrated / roof PV under architectural and historical review, shaded walkways, accessible tree-canopy spaces, native/appropriate firewise vegetation, vegetated stormwater capture, batteries at community resilience hubs. Design roof runoff and debris control; maintain roof access, fire setbacks, hurricane loading, lineworker safety and backup islanding approvals. Never assert **every** construction project can or should be required without community choice, building economics and legal review. Honor historic Lahaina's cultural landscape and community recovery priorities.

**B — Central Maui agricultural fields.** On suitable parcels, elevated PV structures + demonstrably productive crop rows or grazing + pollinator/native habitat + soil organic-matter and water stewardship. Plot high-value agricultural land restrictions first; Hawaii agricultural land rules and special-permit requirements apply. Avoid shading unsuitable crops and blocking farm machinery. Require real harvested yield and farm revenue alongside kWh, not ornamental greenwashing. Fuel breaks / managed low-flammability landscapes are essential: **a green site is not automatically wildfire safe**.

**C — Desal / wastewater / water reserves.** Visually screened, planted structures and low-noise equipment, water-wise native planting, solar carports/roofs, habitat buffers, non-potable reclaimed-water cultivation, flood-height electrical systems, monitoring. Do not place plants, brine operations or data centers on flood-sensitive sites without climate and environmental review. Particularly cautious near reefs and shoreline aquifers.

**Legal/land use:** [HRS §205-4.5](https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/title-13/chapter-205/section-205-4-5/) imposes agricultural-district and PV/special-use constraints, especially on top-rated soils. 2026 agrivoltaic bills do not automatically equal enacted law. Culturally sensitive and Indigenous stewardship/historical uses are first-order siting constraints.

## 5. Small AI inference co-siting

Potential useful services: pump/valve leak detection, wildfire/vegetation forecasting, solar dispatch, grid loads, water quality anomaly triage, irrigation, flood nowcasting and anonymized marine monitoring. **Always independent human review for high-stakes alerts.**

Reference 50–250 kW continuous **IT load** (not mandatory); 250 kW × 8,760 h = **2.19 GWh/year** IT electricity before PUE, storage and cooling. 250 kW is ~9% of illustrative 5-MGD RO electric demand. At hypothetical 20% PV capacity factor, ~1.25 MW additional solar would offset **IT energy only** in annual terms; PUE and operational reserves increase it. No assumption that hot GPU exhaust can cheaply drive RO. Evaluate free-air / closed-loop air cooling, noise, dust, corrosion, flood, fire hazards, water impacts, telecommunications, affordability and grid interconnection. Take a no-new-compute alternative as comparison.

## 6. Hāna stormwater is a separate workstream

Drainage reliability needs local rainfall/runoff datasets, stream and culvert surveys, road-flood depths, sediment transport, debris and landslide modeling, maintenance access, culvert upgrades, permeable surfaces and restoration of floodplain function. Preserve stream flow rights and biodiversity; do not divert wet East Maui water to justify unrelated dry-area development. Coordinate with HDOT, Maui County, community and Native Hawaiian practitioners. Land stewardship and careful vegetative design can reduce sediment; gypsum alone cannot fix drainage.

## 7. Soil restoration and gypsum go/no-go

Brown/yellow fields cannot diagnose nutrient deficiency, sodium excess or poor management from appearance alone. Some former sugarcane lands have ongoing farming and pasture restoration; others may contain invasive, fire-prone grasses. Start with plot-level soil assays: exchangeable sodium percentage (ESP), SAR, electrical conductivity, pH, calcium/magnesium, CEC, organic carbon, infiltration, nutrients, pesticide/metal screening, crop/rooting conditions, microbial/ecological context. **Gypsum targets verified sodic soils with leaching water and drainage**; nonsodic Hawaiian soils may benefit more from compost, ground cover, correct nutrient balances and erosion management. Do not add gypsum at scale without agronomist-approved treatment plans or water-quality runoff monitoring. Gypsum adds calcium/sulfate, **not organic matter**. For contaminated fields prioritize containment and safe remediation separate from soil amendment sales.

University of Hawaiʻi is actively researching former sugar plantation grassland wildfire-risk restoration (2026). Source: https://www.hawaii.edu/news/2026/03/10/bezos-gift-restore-grasslands-reduce-wildfire-risk/ . Agronomic gypsum reference: https://extension.usu.edu/yardandgarden/research/preparing-and-improving-garden-soil .

## 8. Import/export, resilient reserve and prevented off-island costs

Reconcile annual **inbound receipts, outbound shipments, internal transfers and re-exports** at Kahului Harbor, airport freight, road-to-harbor supply movements, fuels, food/feed, fertilizers, soil inputs, cement/gypsum/drywall, chemical reagents, PV modules, battery materials, water equipment, outbound recyclables, waste transport, visitor-related consumption. Historical port receipt volume is gross receipts, not imports: never book all tonnage as avoidable imports.

Per commodity ledger:
`external purchased tonnes = island use + change in inventory + outbound sales - verified local supply`; segment direct foreign imports vs mainland/interisland receipts vs local transfer. 
Track delivered **landed $/t**, avoided ocean/interisland freight, storage and disposal/tipping costs, equivalent quality and reliability, domestic/island value retention, local jobs, local environmental costs, maintenance and reserve requirements. Export only true surplus **after** island supply security and strategic reserve goals, consistent with ORCS national-surplus-first rule, while respecting trade law and lawful producer rights.

Candidate systems: clean C&D drywall → certified gypsum where site-specific safe; organics and food waste → compost / biogas; wastewater nutrients → fit-for-purpose recovered fertilizer; batteries / PV → collection and recycling (shipping may outperform island smelting); glass/aggregate → validated construction specs; desal brine → limited measured mineral recovery only if full lifecycle economics and reef-safe residual disposal pass.

Accounting: no double count of avoided disposal + total product price if same underlying stream is already credited as avoided import; separate independent cash flows, distinguish private vs social avoided costs. No hypothetical export earnings until off-takers and saleable grades verified.

## 9. Environmental and community gates

1. Resident/Native Hawaiian community stewardship, Lahaina reconstruction needs and transparency before facility selection; no claim of local approval.
2. Potable compliance is not coastal ecological quality. Treat groundwater, drinking water, storm runoff, coastal nutrients and reef ecosystem metrics separately.
3. Site salinity chemistry, nutrient loads, brine fate/hydrogeology and discharge permit; avoid unsupported claim of reef-safe injection; 2024 study outlines NPDES/UIC concerns.
4. Confirm erosion sediment controls; protect coral, reef fish, whales; passive acoustic monitoring only where permissions and ecological protocols permit.
5. Fuel load/defensible space, storm resilience, habitat invasive species and landscape water budget.
6. Verified microplastic removal tested for matrix, capture fraction and waste disposition; vendor claims are not deployable efficacy.
7. Energy carbon intensity and real 24/7 power availability, not annual-only offset; battery embodied impacts, hazardous material lifecycle.
8. Stop/go thresholds and transparent third-party environmental/audit results; maintain right to reject unsound project economics.

## 10. Candidate work packages / measurements

**M1 — baseline:** DWS per-facility potable/reclaimed flow, water outages, irrigation sales, coastal DOH samples, Maui island electrical billing and hourly load, sewer outfall data.

**M2 — source and mass:** candidate well analyses by season, RO feed/reject assay, treatment yields, calcium/sulfate/magnesium mass balance; full costed brine-disposal and recovery comparison.

**M3 — soil & agriculture:** soil mapped ESP/SAR/EC, land tenure, fire risk, crop yield, local irrigation duty, revenue baseline, approved restoration plans; C&D gypsum audit.

**M4 — biosolar:** roof/field land availability, irradiance, shading/crop response, view corridors, community design consultation, electrical interconnection, permitting and storage sizing.

**M5 — import/export:** 2024–2026 actual commodity-level Kahului shipping and fuel inventories, price landed, waste recovery dispositions and shipping costs.

**M6 — Hāna:** flood maps, rainfall, culverts, water quality and sediment measurements; engineering comparisons.

**M7 — optional inference:** 0, 50, 100, 250 kW IT comparative scenarios with full power/water/PUE plus benefits validated against no-new-compute alternative.
