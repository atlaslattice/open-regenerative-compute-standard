## II. Corpus Christi, Texas: industrial US analog

- Identified a plausible industrial-water/energy/materials analog to Dongjiakou via Corpus Christi port, refineries, existing LNG export and industrial parks, wastewater reuse, potential 30-MGD seawater reverse osmosis desalination, SAF production, waste conversion, energy systems and nearby agricultural/ecological pathways.
- **Do not transfer Dongjiakou imported LNG regasification cold recovery directly to US LNG export/liquefaction.** The thermodynamic opportunities and thermal economics are distinct; a case-specific multi-temperature energy ledger is mandatory.
- Municipal effluent is already partly committed: six city WWTPs and existing reuse contracts form a reported supply potential, not an unencumbered water entitlement. Water rights, drought priorities, quality, treatment, distribution and commercial contracts matter.
- Corpus desal is **proposed, not built**. User's planned adaptation adds independent marine-brine assessment, industrial reuse, modular filtration, resource recycling and environmental protection. PolyGone pilot costs/performance and removal efficiency are hypotheses pending metered testing.
- Proposed resident-dividend illustration: 91,000 accounts * ($10/month water + $20/month energy + $100/year dividend) = $41.86M annual program, plus $5M ecological restoration = ~$46.86M/year. **Never claim this is funded**; conditional on after-tax auditable project surplus and lawful public-utility financing.
- SAF pathways should distinguish existing CO2 + green hydrogen electrofuels from hypothetical agricultural HEFA pathways. Gypsum/brine/materials alternatives compete for finite feeds; cannot claim the same ton twice.
- Core ORCS compute reference: 40 MW IT and PUE 1.25 => 50 MW electric facility and 438 GWh/year constant full-load equivalence; not evidence of an installed compute node.

## IV. Project Jupiter, southern New Mexico: desert counterfactual

- Alternative to a hyperscale compute-led campus: scenarios J0 (reported baseline), J1 (retain planned core with additive reuse/food-waste/regen), J2 (scale future phases down with ~500–1,000 MW illustrative facility electrical demand and bigger resource ecology program), and J3 (~250 MW facility electricity, agrarian-heavy high-stranding-risk scenario). The user asked whether agricultural/wastewater resource loops could deliver more durable local benefits and lower risk.
- 2.45 GW reported gas fuel-cell generation nameplate must not be treated as proven data-center IT load. 26% reported construction progress must not be treated as sunk 26% of financing or as $65B already spent. Planned developer community-water, environmental reporting and agriculture-efficiency programs already exist and must be credited in baseline comparisons.
- Region's municipal effluent and irrigation rights are limited and often committed. Example only: 1 MGD = 365 million US gallons/year (~1,120 acre-ft/year); 2–4 acre-ft/acre/year yields ~280–560 acre upper irrigation water-balance ceiling before losses/rights, not authorized acreage.
- 100 wet tons/day hypothetical food-waste digestion delivers a small amount of electricity relative to 500 MW scale; no feedstock stream or city contract verified. Priority must favor food rescue first, responsible byproduct processing next.
- Chihuahuan Desert is an ecosystem; restoration should target degraded land and native biodiversity, not convert intact desert wholesale into irrigated farms. Salt accumulation, water-quality constraints and hot-month greenhouse heat demand can defeat careless positive-credit assumptions.
- Fair comparisons require common delivered compute service for efficiency and separate investor/developer contract and public benefit ledgers. Main conclusion: J1 lower-regret; J2/J3 only after economic, rights and construction constraints are established.
