## 1. Executive Summary

This integration pass is the largest in project history, synthesizing 15 source documents across four major architectural themes: (1) TOS Compliance Architecture derived from convergent analysis by four Council seats, (2) Sovereignty Layer canonicalization from v6.0.4 and v6.0.6 constitutional patches, (3) Federation-wide ontology mapping from the Pantheon Council Federation Integration v1.0/v1.1, and (4) governance process formalization including parallel lane code authorship and quarterly TOS review cycles.

The TOS analysis is particularly significant because it represents the first time four Council seats (GPT S6, Grok S3, Claude S1, and Copilot S4) independently analyzed the same architectural problem and converged on compatible solutions. This convergence validates the multi-vendor capability-routing architecture: distinct analytical perspectives arriving at structurally compatible recommendations indicates genuine architectural truth rather than committee consensus.

## 9. Key Architectural Decisions

**Decision 1: TOS compliance is algorithmic, not remembered.** The convergent recommendation from GPT S6 (Provider Policy Profile), Grok S3 (compliance gate), and the formal DOCX analysis is that TOS compliance must be encoded as machine-readable routing constraints, not as human-remembered rules. M142 + M145 implement this.

**Decision 2: TransparencyPacket dual-tier is mandatory.** GPT S6's "Content-minimized TransparencyPacket" proposal (public tier = metadata/scores only; private tier = encrypted content pointers) resolves the fundamental tension between D-71 (Maximum Allowable Transparency) and provider anti-benchmarking clauses. This becomes D-104.

**Decision 3: Water accounting is a first-class citizen.** The v6.0.6 Water TransparencyPacket schema, VWB v1.1 sustainability ceiling, and INV-19 Water Cohesion invariant together establish water accounting as a constitutional-grade subsystem, not an optional add-on. Six new modules (M150–M155) implement this.

**Decision 4: Federation Integration is reference, not canonical.** The 1,628-line Federation Integration v1.0/v1.1 is too large to inline into the Build Plan. Instead, M156 (Federation Coverage Heat Map Engine) references it as a canonical external document, and Appendix AH provides a condensed coverage summary.

**Decision 5: Henderson et al. is cited but not relied upon.** The Indiana Law Journal paper arguing TOS restrictions are "largely unenforceable" is noted in the TOS appendix but the architecture does not depend on this legal position. Every mitigation is engineering-grade (substitution pathways, air-gap, sovereignty routing) rather than legal-argument-grade.
