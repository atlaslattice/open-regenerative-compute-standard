# Maui Island Three-Campus Residents-First Desalination, Complete Wastewater Capture, and GIS-Based Smart Pipe Costing
**Atlas Lattice / ORCS research addendum v0.3 | 2026-10-16 | PROPOSED / SIMULATED / EVIDENCE GATED.**
This is a new file. Prior v0.1 and v0.2 and Maui East Stream documents remain unchanged.

## Decision being evaluated
- Provisional desal capacities: **West Maui 5 MGD, North Shore 5 MGD, Central Maui 10 MGD initially expandable to 15, 17, 20+ MGD**. The design priority is resident households and emergency service, with essential commercial water accounted for separately and optional resort expansions negotiated only after protected obligations. It is **not** a final confirmation that 5 MGD covers West Maui residents, North Shore residents, or all central loads.
- **All campuses host co-located inference computing** connected to a fiber-capable, instrumented water utility system, with air-gapped/segmented safety-rated human-supervised operations and low-water cooling, not speculative unlimited hyperscale compute.
- **Household and commercial wastewater are intentionally collected, cleaned and reused**, rather than assuming existing households have private recycling. The county's public sewer, private wastewater facilities, septic and cesspool sources need source reconciliation.
- Water quality: separate pH-balanced, appropriately remineralized **potable water** from legally certified recycled/non-potable water and from hazardous residuals. The project's shorthand “PHd” must not imply scientifically unsubstantiated health claims or that drinking water is suitable for crops without separate salinity/SAR quality control. Zero routine liquid waste discharge proposed; real ZLD entails secure solid management.

## 1. First-pass capacity ledger (MGD)
Historical Maui **County DWS** 2022 household consumption: 22.5 MGD, including Molokaʻi but excluding privately supplied households. It is not the current 2026 Maui Island-only demand. The independent 2025 Maui Island WUDP 2035 DWS total-demand projection is 48.5 MGD, including nonhousehold demands, not a required residents-only desal output. Sources: [2024 Maui water shortage plan updated 2025](https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/156111/Maui_DWS_WSCP_2024_UpdatedOct2025), [2025 Maui Island WUDP summary](https://waterresources.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/722/2025-MAUI-ISLAND-WUDP-SUMMARY-DOCUMENT).

Nameplate / 85% modeled annual dependable output / deficit or surplus versus **historical county DWS 22.5 MGD households only**:
- **5W + 5N + 10C = 20 MGD** / 17.00 / **-5.50**;
- **5W + 5N + 15C = 25 MGD** / 21.25 / **-1.25**;
- **5W + 5N + 17C = 27 MGD** / 22.95 / **+0.45**;
- **5W + 5N + 20C = 30 MGD** / 25.50 / **+3.00**.
No series satisfies a verified Maui Island-only commercial/essential load. The “85%” is an illustrative annual output factor, not rigorous N-1 capacity, peak hourly delivery, wildfire water protection, or seasonal reserve.
**Critical West Maui warning:** Hawaii Commission on Water Resource Management reported **~5.5 MGD from single-family water meters alone** in West Maui based on pre-2023-fire permit applications, with disproportionately large usage at non-owner-occupied parcels. This is a *historical sector-meter-type finding* and not a measured 2026 permanent-resident essential requirement. A 5-MGD West desal plant alone could not cover 5.5 MGD at 100% output; at 85%, it contributes 4.25 MGD. Appropriate classification of permanent households versus transient/second homes, current fire-related demand changes, private providers, retained wells, and existing reservoirs is essential. [HPR 2025](https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2025-11-14/who-uses-the-most-water-in-lahaina); [Maui Now 2025](https://mauinow.com/2025/11/13/biggest-water-users-in-west-maui-before-fire-were-non-owner-occupied-homes-report-finds/).
**Upcountry:** 2025 independent reporting described ~10.1 MGD at peak for the Upcountry municipal system across sectors and 9.7 MGD reliable supply, which is not the same as Pāʻia/Haʻikū household demand or 5-MGD North Shore service boundary. [Maui Now 2025](https://mauinow.com/2025/11/02/maui-county-plans-more-wells-system-upgrades-to-meet-upcountrys-water-issues-growing-demand/).
A defensible island network requires bill-class/pressure-zone breakdown from utilities (and private companies), projected buildout and climate. Until verified, preserve 5/5/10 as **starting modular proposal**, not a declaration of sufficiency.

## 2. Entire island wastewater and multiple-use material ledger
**Existing Maui Island public-plant historical total inflow:** Lahaina 3.41 + Kīhei 3.93 + Wailuku–Kahului 5.54 = **12.88 MGD, ALL SOURCES**. Of this, ~4.37 MGD already reused. At 90% theoretical treatment yield, 11.592 MGD total output; potential *additional* output ~7.222 MGD before identifying constraints and private sources. These volumes are NOT exclusive household effluent.
**Previously missing onsite sanitation:** Hawaiʻi DOH cesspool-replacement research estimated ~12,200 cesspools on **Maui Island** and ~**7.9 MGD effluent discharged**. This is an estimate, not verified flow, nor currently accessible feedstock. It is a **separate wastewater pathway**, partly municipal/residential and not to be double counted with the public-plant numbers. Collecting the full 7.9 MGD requires rehabilitation/connections/decentralized treatment, right-of-way, terrain, cost, and avoiding negative impacts to groundwater-connected ecosystems. [Hawaiʻi DOH report](https://health.hawaii.gov/wastewater/files/2022/11/ccwg_final_report.pdf).
**20.78 MGD = 12.88 + 7.9** is a gross cross-system *historical/inventory scale*, NOT 20.78 MGD reusable or residents-only feedstock, and excludes other private plant systems while requiring audit of geographical and source overlap. If independently confirmed distinct and 90% recoverable, 18.702 MGD is an extreme collection/treatment sensitivity, NOT a committed output.
**Household circular target:** 22.5 MGD first-use ×0.70 returned to capture ×0.90 treatment output = **14.175 MGD** household secondary-use target. This model may overlap BOTH public sewer and cesspool streams; it CANNOT be summed with either or all of them. Residential greywater localized reuse also reduces downstream centralized sewage volumes.
**Sector reuse:** agriculture, industrial and landscaping may consume portions of this 14.175 target; return fractions depend on evapotranspiration and each process's recirculation. Separate mass-balance accounting for each reclaimed round and each processing residual stream. Strict segregation of potable, certified irrigation/industrial, saline brine, contaminated plastic-bearing sludge and ash. Water lost to crop transpiration is not recoverable liquid “waste.”

## 3. GIS: actual blueprints EXIST, but route length is not yet extracted
Maui County Wastewater GIS Program says it maintains sewer and recycled-water lines, pumps, treatment facilities, **as-built construction drawings**, and links to plans. [County program](https://www.mauicounty.gov/2939/Wastewater-GIS-Program). [Public ArcGIS utility viewer](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=12aad50a26194592b000896487419acd) (updated May 18 2026) has sewer gravity/pressurized lines, sewer treatment, recycled mains, recycled tanks, as-built index, parcels, service area and pump stations. GIS record existence does not equal unrestricted download of engineering drawings; contact program or use official MAPPS request for appropriate material.
**Public detailed potable precedent:** State's March 2026 Pūlehunui Infrastructure Master Plan shows actual **8-, 12-, 18- and 36-inch** existing waterlines and proposed connections in Central Maui; additional construction plans in county records show water line plans and system interconnects. [Pūlehunui master plan](https://dbedt.hawaii.gov/hcda/files/2026/02/2-PULEHUNUI-PROJECT-UPDATE-03-13-26-for-info-final.pdf). These are **local segments**, not islandwide complete potable GIS or enough to calculate islandwide mileage. [Example county potable system plan](https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/142459).
No claims of GIS lengths, routes, utility costs, public-land rights or final sites are made here because line features and designs have not yet been geospatially processed.
**Next extraction pipeline:** acquire public view GIS line layers with segment ID/geometry/diameter/material/install year; overlay elevation/hazards/parcels/environmental cultural sensitivity; identify existing spare capacity, ownership and bypass; classify (a) existing line rehabilitate, (b) short buried crossing, (c) protected aboveground rack/accessible utility corridor, (d) new service route. Iterate EPANET hydraulic constraints for potable/reclaimed and SWMM for sewer; archive GIS version, individual routes, length, diameters, crossings, and unit costs before final CAPEX.

## 4. Cost model SPECIFICATION, not an invented Maui bid
For corridor segment i, calculate total installed capital = L_i*[pipe+supports/pipe-rack+insulation/UV/salt protection+fiber duct+cable+trenching where needed+valves/bypass+install+permitting+site restoration] + [pumps/energy+reservoirs+ROW/easements+control cabinets+hazard mitigation] + contingency. Compare same route under accessible aboveground/protected, conventional buried, and trenchless rehabilitation. Add 30-year OPEX/renewals, corrosion/hurricane risk, power, inspection, environmental permitting and damage exposure.
To communicate **sensitivity only** for illustrative corridors at $2M, $4M and $8M per **route-mile**, without any claim these are observed Maui bids:
- 5 miles: $10M/$20M/$40M
- 15 miles: $30M/$60M/$120M
- 30 miles: $60M/$120M/$240M
These are total illustrative installed corridor prices assumed uniformly, not estimates based on county engineering drawings; do **not** compare them to a desal plant's capital cost as if verified. Actual costs require diameter, supports, elevations, crossings, trenching, easements and island construction premiums.
**Fiber sensing**: DAS needs qualified optical strands/interrogators/signal access; fiber communications and DAS can share conduit but not automatically a single strand without optical engineering. Complement with inline pressure, district flow, strain, temperature, water quality and isolation. Assess nonpotable/potable clearances and cross-connection control. Conventional control PLC remains safe if edge AI offline. [EPA smart sewers](https://www.epa.gov/npdes/smart-sewers), [NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final).

## 5. Campus functions and adoption milestones
West (5 MGD initial desal module): West public/private household demand reconciliation, historic fire-season changes, pressure zones; treat Lahaina wastewater and private resort/catchments, odor control, microplastics and coastal water monitoring.
North (5 MGD initial): Pāʻia/Haʻikū neighborhood demand/Upcountry interface; decentralized sewer/cesspool collection and treatment where feasible; farmland pipeline to verified nonpotable buyers.
Central (10 MGD initial, incremental +5/+7/+10): Wailuku–Kahului potable household load, South Maui interconnection where feasible, existing Kīhei wastewater treatment and reclaimed distribution, agricultural transmission under public-trust constraints. A 10-MGD module is NOT assumed sufficient for Central district residential service.
At each campus co-locate **right-sized on-site inference** and keep independent safety-rated offline water and wastewater controls. Quantify AI power consumption, cooling loss, useful heat, redundant networking, thermal recovery, security and cost. Advanced reuse and ZLD must fully account for energy and residuals, rather than transferring microplastics to soils or claiming all salt waste is marketable.
