# Beyond Q — Topaz Geological He-3 vs D–D Coproduction: Isotope Material Balance and Abundance Economics
**2026-11-01 · v0.1 · SOURCE-TRACED SCENARIO, NOT VERIFIED COMMERCIAL OUTPUT / NOT FUSION PERFORMANCE VALIDATION**

## Bottom line
**Topaz is a Minnesota primary-HELIUM and CO2-rich geological gas discovery, NOT an LNG plant and not primarily a natural-gas electricity generator.** Its gas may contain a small methane percentage usable as onsite fuel, but extracting an inert gas does not itself produce electricity. It may sell He-4, CO2 and an He-3 coproduct, so do not claim that operations face a categorical LNG-for-He3 choice. Its **finite geological resource / processing inputs** differ fundamentally from a **hypothetical 1.10 GW D-D fusion reactor's simultaneous energy and D-D He-3/T isotope production**; however neither economic advantage of the fusion route nor achievement of its required reactor net electric output has been demonstrated. The point is abundance-conditioned *full system* price and supply comparison, not declaring Topaz a bad project or attributing market-manipulation motives.

## 2. Conditional normalization (illustrative, not forecast)

If (**strong and unjustified hypothetical**) the *entire 200 million standard-cubic-feet-per-year gaseous helium nameplate stream* comes from gas with isotope ratio above AND all recoverable He-3 is captured **without extraction losses or feed gas variability**, potential coexisting `He3_gas = 200,000,000 scf He4 × (1.40e-7 ... 1.55e-7) = 28 ... 31 scf He3` per year. At 28.3168 L/scf, this is **~793–878 standard liters/year gaseous He-3** (roughly **0.1 kg/year**, depending on stated standard temperature/pressure).

This is a **gross composition-coupled stoichiometric thought experiment, not the company's published He-3 output estimate**. It assumes helium nameplate is actual He-4 product mass and excludes gas availability, well variability, purification isotope losses, purity and production downtime. The projected 350,000 gaseous L/year Beyond Q He-3 target would be ~**400–440× this illustrative single-plant envelope**. **That fusion target is a scenario goal, not a measured deliverable or proof of an affordable power-producing D-D reactor**.

Crucial unit control: do **not** compare 7.5M liters **liquid He-4** directly to 350k liters **gaseous He-3** or 150 liters cryogenic fill without temperatures and gas vs liquid basis. Tag every number `species, gas_or_liquid, pressure, temperature, purity, year, facility_boundary`.

## 3. Economic comparison must include price-collapse cases

| Scenario | Geological Topaz | Beyond Q hypothetical D-D + D-T |
|---|---|---|
| **Low He-3 demand / high specialized price** | Separation may be attractive even at tiny He-3 flow, against He-4 and CO2 business base | Isotope revenue may appear high; cap assumed sales by addressable market and purified grades |
| **High quantum demand / high He-3 price** | Could provide niche supplementary supply with proven wells and He-4 plant *if operated* | The aspirational high-volume fusion isotope flow may have strategic value **only if net power, burn ledger, separation and CAPEX pass** |
| **High demand / falling He-3 price (abundance)** | He-3 economics may weaken, although He-4/CO2 plant could remain viable on its own | High outputs may *cause price decline*: demand curve, replacement of scarcity pricing, nonfusion competition, cash conversion essential; do NOT credit 350,000L×historic scarce $/L as durable revenue |
| **Topaz separation fails** | He-4/CO2 project and other suppliers continue independently | Does not prove fusion economics; separate validation still required |
| **Topaz separation succeeds / 10× capacity hypothetical** | Scale is limited by proven resource, helium flow and viable processing rather than isolated He-3 purity; **10× is speculative** | Compare physically realistic feedstock/fuel/neutron/blanket closure, not technology slogans |

**Required numeric inputs**: sustained total well flow; He-3 ratio by reservoir; mass-balance true isotope recovery; purity/grade; separation power, OPEX and CAPEX; co-product revenue split; output licensed sales, pricing vs volume; demand by quantum *modality*; dilution-fridge He-3 loop inventory vs initial charge vs replacement **(closed circuits: fill is NOT annual consumption)**; tritium supply account and plant power use. Field depletion/energy and fixed equipment costs are important but **no evidence of intentional scarcity manipulation** is established.

**Energy metric:** Topaz `net_power_generated≈0` *in the absence of separately installed generation*. An onsite methane-driven generator could offset **some processing power** but is not a 1-GW-scale energy output. Beyond Q `P_net_elec = gross_generation - all_internal_plasma_laser_cryo_separation_aux_loads`; cannot assume net-positive electricity from fusion power nameplate alone. Compare integrated enterprise income without misleading production-vs-separation conflation.

**Status:** TOPAZ confirmed trace He-3 in sampled reservoir, plant nameplate proposed/contracted, HE3 pilot unvalidated. Beyond Q owner goals: **D-D Reactor A 1.10 GW fusion power target, D-T Reactor B 1.13 GW reference**, 350,000 standard gas liters He-3 and 350,000 standard gas liters T/year production *targets*, subject to physical fuel-cycle/transport viability.
