# Research discovery and indexing metadata

**Canonical title:** Beyond Q v2.3: Global Quantum-First Infrastructure & Fusion-Isotope Integration — Google, Microsoft and China Compared

**Alternate search titles:** Quantum-first data center energy requirements; Fusion powered quantum computing; ARC D-D/D-T isotope refinery; Helium-3 for fault-tolerant quantum computing; Google Willow and neutral-atom compute infrastructure; Microsoft Majorana 2 and topological quantum computing power; China neutral-atom rack power; tritium breeding and lithium blanket power balance; quantum cryostat wall-plug demand; nuclear-to-electric full plant accounting.

**Short abstract (180 words):** Independent global pre-feasibility research comparing the physical power and cryogenic isotope needs of quantum-computing hardware under active development by Google, Microsoft, and Chinese institutions. It contrasts superconducting transmon, topological, neutral-atom and photonic approaches; distinguishes vendor whole-device power, millikelvin cold lift, classical decoder demand, facility overhead and helium-3 first-fill/replenishment. It uses the 2026 peer-reviewed Commonwealth Fusion Systems ARC D-T physics-basis design as a published energy reference while exploring a clearly hypothetical ARC-derived D-D isotope production reactor. An executable algebraic sensitivity model separates primary and secondary nuclear Q, lithium reaction Q, external RF heating, collection, heat-to-electric conversion, auxiliary loads, isotope stoichiometry, tritium decay and electrical opportunity cost. Four competing infrastructure designs are evaluated at research level: conventional dual-D-T generation, D-T/D-D isotope integration, grid-hybrid optimization, and quantum modules embedded into conventional data centers. No integrated ARC-DD plasma, neutron-transport, isotope-recovery, million-qubit electrical-load or market feasibility model is claimed verified. Research gates prioritize independently measurable physical inputs and publish negative results.
