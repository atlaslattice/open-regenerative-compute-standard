# STARS 2.0-RF v0.2 — Recirculating Proton–Boron Fusion, Acoustic Modulation and Whole-System Energy Recovery

**Atlas Lattice | Research white paper v0.2 | 27 October 2026**

**Status:** Exploratory / noncanonical / falsifiable

## Executive abstract

Stars 2.0-RF proposes that low-probability proton–boron-11 fusion may become more economical if unreacted ions retain useful kinetic energy across repeated encounters, while the system recovers or redirects otherwise lost plasma energy and fusion-produced alpha-particle energy. The governing question is not simply how many attempts fail, but how much irreversible exergy is lost per unit of successful fusion yield. The architecture is hypothetical; neither sustained net-electric operation nor its proposed integration has been demonstrated.

## 1. First principle: account for every joule

“There is no waste unless you waste it” is adopted as a design heuristic, not a claim that thermodynamic irreversibility can be eliminated. Distinguish retained energy, recovered electricity, recycled heating, low-grade waste heat, radiation escaping the system, and truly unavailable work (exergy destruction). A joule redirected to proton heating cannot also be counted as a joule of exported electricity. System boundaries must include the laser wall plug, RF amplifiers, magnets, pumps, target manufacture, diagnostics, cooling, and electricity conversion.

## 2. Nuclear basis and why repeated encounters matter

The principal reaction is p + 11B → 3 alpha + 8.68 MeV. The fusion probability depends on relative collision energy, cross section, density, path length, scattering, stopping, and confinement. Repeated encounters may increase cumulative reaction opportunity, but do not guarantee eventual fusion: ions can thermalize, escape, scatter into unfavorable trajectories, or strike structures. Once fusion occurs the original reactants are consumed. Fusion alpha particles are new products, not reusable proton–boron fuel. Proton–boron fusion has been observed experimentally; a net-electric plant has not been demonstrated.

## 3. Three energy-management layers

LAYER A — Particle retention: Keep unreacted protons circulating at useful energies, or extract/decelerate and reaccelerate them only if the full round trip is favorable. LAYER B — Whole-system energy recirculation: Investigate recovery of escaping particle heat, conduction losses, electromagnetic wave energy, and bremsstrahlung photons, with realistic conversion efficiencies and temperature/exergy limits. LAYER C — Fusion-product management: Remove helium ash; partition alpha energy between direct electricity conversion, wave-mediated proton heating (alpha channeling), and unavoidable thermal/radiative losses. Alpha channeling applies to alpha particles generated by successful fusion, not to unsuccessful Coulomb rebounds.

## 4. Acoustic and laser modulation: preserve three distinct hypotheses

TRACK A: Mechanical/acoustic solid-target preparation. Ultrasound, surface acoustic waves, picosecond strain pulses, or laser-launched elastic waves may change target density, porosity, morphology, ionization onset, or ablation uniformity. Test with matched targets and measured density/temperature. TRACK B: Ion-acoustic and other plasma waves. In a plasma, collective density/electric-field waves may redistribute ion energy or modify transport; their dispersion and damping depend on electrons, temperatures, magnetic field, and plasma composition. TRACK C: Structured laser drive. Spatially and temporally shaped laser pulses may control plasma formation, ion acceleration, and field geometry. A vacuum does not transmit ordinary mechanical sound; a shaped laser is not an acoustic wave by itself. None of these macroscopic fields is known to mold the femtometer-scale shape of a nucleus or directly change the strong force. The falsifiable claim is improved net fusion yield or reduced net losses, not “nuclear acoustic shaping.”

## 5. Competing configurations

A — Recirculating ion beam or electrostatic trap: model discrete or continuous proton trajectories, charge-neutralization requirements, beam–target stopping and energy recovery. B — Quasineutral magnetized plasma: model ion distributions, electron drag, bremsstrahlung, conduction, alpha ash and wave coupling. C — Pulsed laser–solid target: model target preparation, proton stopping, shot-to-shot target replacement, laser wall-plug cost and alpha collection. D — Hybrid: use shaped target preparation with recirculation and selective direct conversion only if compatible in geometry and timescale. No route is assumed superior before comparison.

## 6. Mathematical ledger and corrected probability model

For encounter i, define p_i = conditional fusion probability, L_i = irrecoverable loss of useful energy, A_i = incremental auxiliary input, and S_i = useful retained kinetic energy. The probability of surviving n encounters without fusion is product(i=1..n)(1-p_i), conditional on no escape; include an explicit escape probability to avoid assuming eventual success. Expected fusion yield per injected trajectory is E_f × sum over encounters [probability of reaching encounter i × p_i]. E_f = 8.68 MeV. At steady state: P_net,electric = P_fusion→electric + P_other recovered→electric − P_laser,wall − P_RF,wall − P_magnets − P_pumps − P_processing − P_other loads. Internal alpha-to-proton transfer reduces required heating input but is not exported electricity. Track energy and exergy separately, and prohibit double counting. A positive per-success event ledger is not evidence of stable net-electric power.

## 7. Threshold illustration, not a forecast

At assumed 70% useful recovery of 8.68 MeV, the allowance per successful event is 6.076 MeV before all non-failure costs. If exactly 100, 1,000, 10,000, or 100,000 failed encounters occur per success, the maximum average irrecoverable loss per failure is respectively 60.76 keV, 6.076 keV, 0.6076 keV, or 0.06076 keV, assuming all other costs are zero. These are strict upper bounds in an artificial scenario, not measured achievable thresholds. In a complete model, account for initial energy, beam stopping, power supply efficiency, escape, alpha collection, target replacement, and finite confinement.

## 8. DeepSeek review: claims to verify, not adopted as measured inputs

The supplied Chinese technical review proposes that 80–90% recovery of conduction losses might reduce required energy confinement time by 5–10×; additional bremsstrahlung recovery might reduce it by another 2–3×; some modeled regimes might approach ~2 s confinement; and a 2026 study might show 99.96% coherent alpha-to-proton energy transfer under specified conditions. These numerical claims have NOT been independently source-verified in this paper. They must be tagged “reported, unverified” until the exact articles, equations, plasma regimes, denominator definitions, and experimental/theoretical status are checked. In particular, conversion of bremsstrahlung X-rays to usable work is technically and thermodynamically difficult, and high local wave-transfer efficiency does not establish whole-reactor efficiency.

## 9. Priority research questions

Q1. Which p–11B cross-section dataset and collision-energy distribution should anchor the model? Q2. What are the actual per-pass proton stopping and escape rates in each geometry? Q3. Can circulating ions retain collision-relevant energy without prohibitive electron drag or collective instability? Q4. How do bremsstrahlung power and alpha ash accumulation vary with ion/electron temperature and composition? Q5. Can helium ash be removed rapidly without losing valuable fuel or consuming excessive power? Q6. What physically realizable RF or plasma-wave mode transfers alpha energy into the desired proton distribution? Q7. Is energy retention better than extraction-and-reinjection at the device level? Q8. Can mechanical target vibration or laser-driven strain measurably change reaction yield after normalizing for target density, laser energy, and diagnostics? Q9. What wall-plug efficiency and repetition rate are realistic for the laser source? Q10. How much radiative energy can be captured, at what spectrum and exergy, and by which conversion technology? Q11. What are the effects of secondary nuclear channels and neutron/radiation protection? Q12. Does any parameter region yield positive net-electric output under uncertainty and conservative losses?

## 10. Stage-gated modeling and experiments

PHASE 0 — Literature verification: retrieve primary publications and nuclear data; classify measured versus simulated claims. PHASE I — Conservation-checked no-fusion simulator: validate electrostatic rebound, confinement, recirculation, extraction and recovery without artificial gain. PHASE II — Cross-section and stopping model: introduce energy-dependent fusion probability, finite target geometry, scattering, escape and energy distributions. PHASE III — Acoustic/laser controlled comparisons: separate solid-target strain, plasma waves and structured laser pulses; measure any changes in normalized alpha yield and total wall-plug energy. PHASE IV — Whole-system energy ledger: compare no recovery, conduction recovery, radiation recovery, alpha-to-proton channeling and direct alpha conversion without double counting. PHASE V — Engineering and uncertainty analysis: optimize full steady-state net-electric power, not only fusion yield; report uncertainty intervals and failure modes.

## 11. Explicit falsification criteria

Reject an architecture if energy recovered in a no-fusion closed cycle exceeds external energy input without identifiable stored-energy depletion (model error); if recirculation loses more useful energy than extraction/reinjection under equivalent conditions; if acoustic or laser modulation raises alpha yield but consumes more incremental wall-plug energy than the useful additional output; if plasma radiation and electron drag overwhelm any plausible fusion gain; or if the only favorable cases require recovery efficiencies unsupported by component-level evidence. A positive result in an idealized submodel does not establish reactor feasibility.

## 12. Status and next deliverables

STATUS: Exploratory, noncanonical. Established: Coulomb scattering, charged-particle confinement, p–11B nuclear reaction, laser-driven p–B fusion observations, plasma waves, and the theoretical basis of alpha channeling. Unestablished: the integrated repeated-encounter low-loss architecture, reactor-scale alpha-channeling efficiency, large-scale bremsstrahlung energy recovery, acoustic enhancement of net electrical gain, and sustained net electricity. NEXT: build a versioned assumption register with references; create a reproducible parameter-sweep model with explicit energy conservation; publish plots of net-electric gain against per-pass loss, reaction probability, wave-driver power, ash-removal cost, and conversion efficiency.
