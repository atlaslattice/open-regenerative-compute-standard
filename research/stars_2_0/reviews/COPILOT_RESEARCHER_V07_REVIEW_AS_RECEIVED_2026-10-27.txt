Absolutely. v0.7 is substantially stronger. The biggest improvement is conceptual: it has stopped treating the project as one coupled "gain stack" and instead turned it into several experiments whose boundaries can fail independently.

After checking the current paper against the archived thickness sweep and published p–¹¹B literature, I have **one fairly important correction, several refinements, and a few new research directions worth adding to v0.8.**

## 1. Important correction: the 675 vs 612 keV "discrepancy" is probably largely a reference-frame issue

I would rewrite §2.1.

<Sikora/Weller's> 2016 evaluation explicitly defines its dataset in terms of **incident proton energy \(E_p\)** and covers \(E_p=0.15\)–3.8 MeV. It also identifies the narrow low-energy resonance at \(E_p=0.162\) MeV. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10894-016-0069-y.pdf)

More importantly, Stave et al., “Understanding the \(^{11}\)B(p,α)αα reaction at the 0.675 MeV resonance” explicitly calls the feature the **0.675 MeV resonance**. [2](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/735841) Mazzucconi et al. 2025 likewise describes agreement above the **0.675 MeV resonance peak** within an experiment whose independent variable is proton energy from 0.34–4.73 MeV using a Van de Graaff proton beam. [3](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1291367)[4](https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/9bc23d9e-1aa7-4584-8915-7f6baaf36c5e/Mazzucconi_EPJA25_pB11%20cross%20section.pdf)

That strongly supports treating **~675 keV as laboratory/incident proton energy in this literature**, rather than 675 keV CM.

For a stationary \(^{11}\)B target,

\[
E_{cm}\approx E_{lab}\frac{11}{12},
\]

so 675 keV lab corresponds to roughly **619 keV CM**, very close to the ~612 keV-CM description that triggered the apparent conflict.

### Suggested replacement language

> **Reference-frame reconciliation.** Much of the apparent “612 versus 675 keV resonance” discrepancy is consistent with mixing center-of-mass and incident-proton laboratory energies. Stave et al. and Mazzucconi et al. describe the prominent feature near 0.675 MeV in proton-beam energy, while conversion for stationary \(^{11}\)B places this near ~0.62 MeV CM. The simulator shall therefore store every nuclear-data energy with an explicit `energy_frame` field and never convert silently.

I would consequently **retire 675 keV CM → 736 keV lab as the default working premise**, unless you uncover a particular source that explicitly labels 675 keV as CM.

That is a useful concrete result from this review.

# 2. A bigger modeling insight: don't model "the alpha"

I think WP-B v0.3 should elevate the sequential reaction physics from a refinement into a core architectural object.

Sikora and Weller 2016 state that the reaction proceeds as a two-step process with a primary alpha followed by two secondary alphas from \(^{8}\)Be decay; their cross-section reconstruction relies on a calculated alpha spectrum rather than three equal-energy particles. [1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10894-016-0069-y.pdf)

Stave et al. goes further: their sequential model predicts **two high-energy alphas of nearly equal energy, one primary and one secondary**, with coincidence data supporting that picture. [2](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/735841)

This creates an interesting STARS-specific consequence:

> **The optimal catcher may differ depending on which alpha phase-space population you're trying to collect.**

Instead of:

`fusion → 3 alpha → stopping → escape`

I'd make the model conceptually:

`proton + B11`
→ `C12* resonance`
→ `decay channel`
→ `(alpha1, alpha2, alpha3)` **correlated event**
→ individual path lengths
→ separate exit states
→ collector acceptance.

That matters because the converter doesn't care about the mathematical mean energy per alpha. It cares about the actual joint distribution.

### New WP-B output I'd add

For every simulated reaction:

```text
reaction_id
Ep_lab
Ep_cm
reaction_depth
decay_channel
alpha_id
E_birth
theta_birth
phi_birth
exit_face
E_exit
theta_exit
charge_state_exit
accepted_by_collector
```

Keep all three alphas tied to a common `reaction_id`.

That structure will let WP-D later ask much richer questions without rerunning the nuclear model.

# 3. Your current 1–12 µm experiment is even more valuable than it looks

The preserved [stars_2_0_wp_b_thickness_sweep.csv](https://onedrive.live.com/personal/0a56f85358812930/_layouts/15/doc.aspx?resid=4df59e7e-6640-452e-865b-1b7664a54381&cid=0a56f85358812930&EntityRepresentationId=3517e7f1-7d68-4687-9c92-76bf2cc71ae6) shows something physically useful beyond "5 µm wins."

|---:|---:|
| 3 µm | 0.6747 µJ |
| 4 µm | 0.7294 µJ |
| **5 µm** | **0.7456 µJ** |
| 6 µm | 0.7375 µJ |
| 8 µm | 0.6876 µJ |
| 10 µm | 0.6324 µJ |
| 12 µm | 0.5847 µJ |

[5](https://onedrive.live.com/personal/0a56f85358812930/_layouts/15/doc.aspx?resid=4df59e7e-6640-452e-865b-1b7664a54381&cid=0a56f85358812930)

I'd therefore avoid asking experimentally:

> "Is 5 µm optimal?"

Ask instead:

> **"Does a finite few-micron escape-energy plateau exist at all?"**

That's much harder to overfit.

### Three preregistered hypotheses

**H1: Birth hypothesis**

\[
N_f(t)
\]

increases monotonically before eventually saturating.

**H2: Escape hypothesis**

\[
E_{\alpha,exit}(t)
\]

has an interior maximum due to increasing births competing against increasing alpha attenuation.

**H3: Face hypothesis**

\[
E_{\alpha,F}(t)\ne E_{\alpha,R}(t)
\]

and their maxima occur at measurably different thicknesses.

If an accelerator experiment confirms all three without using WP-B parameters to fit afterward, that would be a **real independent success** for the architecture even if the best thickness turns out to be 2, 8, or 11 µm.

That would be much more scientifically impressive than "5 µm confirmed."

# 5. Acoustics: v0.7's causal treatment is now basically right

I agree with the correction applied to my earlier suggestion.

If

\[
A\rightarrow M\rightarrow P\rightarrow \alpha
\]

where:

- \(A\) = acoustic intervention
- \(M\) = morphology
- \(P\) = proton phase space

then conditioning on \(P\) while estimating the *total* acoustic effect can intentionally remove the effect you're trying to measure.

So v0.7 should absolutely retain three estimands:

### Total effect

\[
E[Y\mid do(A=ON)]-E[Y\mid do(A=OFF)]
\]

This is the engineering question.

### First-stage effect

Does acoustics reproducibly modify morphology, thickness, pointing stability, or proton output?

### Residual/direct effect

Does anything remain after the measured mediator pathway is considered?

These answer different questions.

There is also external precedent for **laser-driven proton acceleration from water microdroplets**, which makes droplet-state control a legitimate enabling-engineering question even without assuming an acoustic fusion effect. Becker et al. 2019 experimentally characterized laser-driven proton acceleration from water microdroplets. [6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6868211/) Published acoustic-levitation literature independently demonstrates contactless droplet manipulation phenomena including transport, coalescence, mixing and evaporation. [7](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ijmsa/37/2/37_370201/_pdf/-char/en)[8](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjmf/37/1/37_2023.T004/_article/-char/en)

That's an interesting cross-domain bridge.

But the claim should remain:

> **Published literature supports manipulating droplets acoustically and using droplets as laser-proton targets separately. It does not yet demonstrate that combining them improves p–¹¹B fusion.**

That is exactly the kind of adjacent-field bridge STARS should test.

# 6. New idea: make target reproducibility a primary acoustic success metric

There is a subtle opportunity here.

Acoustics doesn't have to **increase the mean yield** to be valuable.

Suppose OFF gives:

\[
Y=100\pm40
\]

and ON gives:

\[
Y=100\pm10 .
\]

No fusion gain.

But the second system could be much more valuable experimentally and eventually operationally because downstream collection can be tuned to a repeatable phase-space distribution.

So I'd add an acoustic objective such as:

\[
J_A =
w_1\bar E_{\alpha,\rm exit}
-w_2 \operatorname{Var}(E_{\alpha,\rm exit})
-w_3E_{\rm acoustic,wall}.
\]

Not as a universal physical objective, just a declared engineering figure of merit.

That might turn out to be acoustics' strongest contribution: **variance reduction rather than mean enhancement.**

# 7. I would now split "collector efficiency" into three efficiencies

The integral in §6 is good, but I'd make its semantics even harder to abuse.

Define:

### Geometric/phase-space capture

\[
\eta_{\rm capture}
=
\frac{E_{\alpha,\ accepted}}{E_{\alpha,\ exiting}}
\]

### Particle-to-electrical conversion

\[
\eta_{\rm conversion}
=
\frac{E_{\rm load}}{E_{\alpha,\ accepted}}
\]

### Collector subsystem efficiency

\[
\eta_{\rm collector}
=
\frac{E_{\rm load}-E_{\rm collector\,aux}}
     {E_{\alpha,\ exiting}}.
\]

Then:

\[
\eta_{\rm collector}\neq\eta_{\rm conversion}.
\]

This prevents a future "82% converter" number from accidentally becoming "82% of fusion alpha energy reaches the bus."

Given v0.7's evidence philosophy, I think this distinction belongs very prominently in WP-D.

That's not evidence that it works for STARS, and the plasma topology is radically different.

But conceptually it highlights that STARS' recovery study doesn't necessarily have to remain:

1. electrostatic DEC;
2. IBW;
3. hybrid.

A useful **C4 research category** could be:

> **collective inductive recovery from a deliberately magnetized charged-product/plasma structure.**

I'd initially treat that only as a comparative theoretical branch. Its virtue is that it asks a different question from DEC:

**Can broad charged-product energy first be converted into an organized collective expansion and then electrically recovered?**

If the answer is no under pitcher–catcher boundary conditions, retire it cheaply in simulation.
