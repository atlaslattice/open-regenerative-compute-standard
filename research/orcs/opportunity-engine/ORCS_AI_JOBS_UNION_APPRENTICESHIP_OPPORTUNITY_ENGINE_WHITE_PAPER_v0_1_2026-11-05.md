# ORCS Opportunity Engine v0.1
## Demand-verified jobs first; paid hands-on training second; human hiring and sustained employment third

**Research and policy design white paper — November 5, 2026.** All numerical examples are illustrative, not signed hiring commitments, available vacancies, realized savings, or verified deployment. This is an independent ORCS proposal, not an OpenAI, union, employer, government or utility endorsement.

### Executive summary
Large AI/data-center developments can impose localized electricity, water, land and infrastructure costs while distributing benefits unevenly. A credible community-benefit agreement should disclose **specific, funded, independently auditable deliverables**: residential utility credits; net new electricity and water capacity; named infrastructure work; and verifiable pathways into paid jobs.

The ORCS Opportunity Engine is a **demand-led employment coordination layer**. Before creating a training cohort, multiple independent employers disclose verifiable vacancies, wages, required competencies, hiring windows and interview commitments. AI assists with matching, personalized preparation and scheduling; qualified human instructors and supervisors run practical training; employers interview **at least two qualified candidates where feasible** and make all final hiring decisions. Candidates not selected remain eligible for other openings and receive continued matching, without being labeled failures.

### 1. Design commitments
1. **Jobs before training:** no scaled cohort without independently verified, non-duplicated employer demand and funding.
2. **Multiple employers per occupation:** proposed pilot gate of at least three independent employers with transferable skill needs; no single-company training trap.
3. **Demand buffer:** test >=1.5 independently verified open positions per planned graduate, adjusted for real conversion rates, location and start dates; this is an ASSUMPTION, not a placement guarantee.
4. **Two-candidate interview target:** each employer interviews two or more qualified consenting candidates per vacancy when practical; candidates may interview at multiple employers. Never train two people exclusively for one vacancy.
5. **Human-led instruction and decisions:** AI suggests, tutors and coordinates; human trainers verify competence and employers decide whom to hire. Provide explanations, appeal, accessibility and nondiscrimination controls.
6. **Pay for supervised practical work:** illustrative starting budget of $15/hour, only where lawful; comply with minimum wages, prevailing wages, apprenticeship schedules and required compensation for mandatory training, exams and study. Independent voluntary study may be unpaid, but required job-related training cannot simply be designated unpaid.
7. **Accelerated entry, not accelerated licensure:** 2–12 week pre-apprenticeship/entry modules may prepare workers for supervised entry roles; electrical, water, safety and licensed trades retain their full legal training and credential requirements.
8. **Portable credentials:** demonstrated competencies belong to the trainee and should be recognized by multiple participating employers, subject to licensing and credential authorities.
9. **Open-entry access:** support applicants without prior union membership or personal connections; include union joint apprenticeship training centers, community colleges, qualified nonunion providers and employers under applicable labor law.
10. **No invented outcomes:** distinguish announced jobs, funded training seats, verified vacancies, interviews, offers, actual hires, job-years, permanent employment, and 12/24-month retention.

### 2. Union-integrated delivery
Union and joint labor-management apprenticeship programs bring experienced instructors, equipment, contractor relationships, safety standards and existing credential pathways. ORCS may fund incremental instructors, equipment, access/pre-apprenticeships, and recruitment **against independently verified new-entrant outcomes**. Union membership and hiring should not be conflated; report existing union workers separately from previously unemployed entrants, newly registered apprentices and sustained placements. Preserve lawful bargaining, worker choice and prevailing standards. Union participation is a delivery channel, not an exclusive gate.

### 3. Demand and matching workflow
**Employer consortium → independently validated vacancy registry → skill-cluster selection → candidate consent and eligibility → AI individualized preparation → supervised paid practical modules → independent competency assessment → human interviews (2+ qualified candidates per opening where feasible) → hiring → continued placement support for unselected applicants → retention audit.**

Employer record minimum fields: employer and worksite ID; contact and verification timestamp; occupation and skills taxonomy; number of unique funded vacancies; pay range; hours and benefits; planned start date; geographic and mobility constraints; license and safety prerequisites; interview commitment; posting expiration; hiring disposition; provenance; and privacy permissions.

Do not expose private applicant data to employers without appropriate consent. Do not automatically reject applicants based solely on AI scoring. Screen for adverse impact and offer accessible human review.

### 4. Candidate occupations and initial modules
| Entry pathway | Illustrative preparatory window | Credential caveat |
|---|---:|---|
| Manufacturing equipment operator | 2–4 weeks | Equipment-specific safety and supervised operation required |
| Data-center hardware technician | 4–8 weeks | Vendor/site qualification may be required |
| Industrial robotics assistant | 6–10 weeks | Supervision and machine safety required |
| Water-treatment technician assistant | 6–12 weeks | Operator licenses cannot be bypassed |
| Electrical apprenticeship entrant | 4–8 weeks | Multi-year apprenticeship/licensure still applies |
| Solar installation assistant | 2–6 weeks | Site, electrical and fall-protection rules apply |

These are **hypothetical time-to-entry ranges**, not validated occupation-wide training durations. Employers must verify actual required competencies and hiring schedules.

### 5. Transparent pilot budget (ASSUMED)
A pilot with 30 employers, 1,500 verified vacancies, and 1,000 entrants over an eight-week practical-training period would require:
- 30 paid practical hours/week × 8 weeks × $15/hour = **$3,600 wages per trainee**.
- $4,000 additional instructor/equipment/support allowance per trainee (assumed).
- **$7,600 per trainee; $7.6 million for 1,000 trainees** before benefits, payroll overhead, major capital expenditure, and continuing apprenticeship obligations.
- The 1,500 vacancies are **not** confirmed; the 1,000 hires are **not** promised. Employer verification and signed commitments are preconditions for launch.
- Sensitivity: for 100,000 trainees, the same narrow assumptions yield $760 million, not an all-in national workforce cost.

Measure wages paid separately from net economic cost; productive work value must be observed, not automatically deducted.

### 6. Community Benefit Ledger: integrated with statewide ORCS electricity and water
For each data-center/industrial campus publish separate, non-double-counted accounts:
- **Electricity:** campus hourly consumption; additional generation; firm capacity; storage and transmission; grid charges paid by developer; verified household bill credits and eligibility; subsidy funding/escrow; annual and 30-year costs. Annual solar energy parity does not imply 24/7 reliability.
- **Water:** withdrawals, consumptive use, reclaimed supply, treatment losses, basin-level net effects, discharge obligations and water-bill credits. Recycled gallons are not automatically net new water; water bills include fixed network and treatment costs.
- **Workforce:** independently verified job postings, signed hiring commitments, training capacity, paid hours, interviews, offers, hires, wages, 12/24-month retention, job-years and permanent jobs.
- **Community control:** zoning, environmental review, consent processes, public comment, enforceable remedies and independent audit. Benefits cannot substitute for legal approvals.

**Example only:** a $50/month electricity credit for 1 million eligible households costs $600M/year before administration; a separate recurring funding mechanism is required. An illustrative $500B development with 10% capital community allocation means $50B of capital, **not** $50B/year in household subsidies. Avoid charitable/tax deduction claims without legal review.

### 7. Ohio, Texas and Georgia pilot design
| State | Proposed demand clusters | Benefit accounting |
|---|---|---|
| Ohio | Industrial equipment, electrical trades, robotics | Brownfield reuse, grid upgrades, household bill impacts, wastewater reuse |
| Texas | Power systems, cooling, water treatment, automation | ERCOT reliability, reclaimed/brackish water, incremental grid costs |
| Georgia | Electrical construction, manufacturing, hardware operations | Utility rate effects, water balance, transmission, workforce outcomes |

These are **research hypotheses**, not evidence of specific current vacancies, policy enactments or signed partnerships. Verify occupational demand, union and provider capacity, state wage and licensing law, utility tariffs, watershed constraints and actual 2025+ EIA data before committing money.

### 8. Evidence gates and reproducibility
- **OBSERVED:** employer signed demand, payroll, actual bills, metered water/power, verified hire and retention records.
- **PUBLISHED:** dated official labor/energy/water datasets, utility filings, apprenticeship standards and peer-reviewed evidence.
- **DERIVED:** transparent arithmetic based on observed/published inputs.
- **ASSUMED:** $15/hour, eight weeks, 30 hours/week, $4,000 support, 1.5 openings per trainee, pilot cohort.
- **PROPOSED:** Opportunity Engine, statewide benefit contracts and matching protocols.

Before publishing outcome claims: deduplicate jobs and postings; compare against counterfactual hiring; audit displacement and hiring quality; report incomplete data; independently verify legal compliance and economic feasibility. No automatic conversion of course completions to jobs.

2. Map union/JATC and community-college capacity and applicable wage/credential rules.
