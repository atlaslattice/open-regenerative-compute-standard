# US Wastewater Resources, Energy and Resilience v0.3

## Wastewater as domestic resource infrastructure

National nutrients, sludge energy, costs and import-equivalent supply. Evidence reviewed October 16-17, 2026. Water reuse sales excluded. Analysis date follows America/Chicago, October 16, 2026.

**Finding:** municipal wastewater can provide a meaningful recurring nutrient supply and distributed energy. The strongest project candidates combine recoverable products with genuinely avoided treatment and disposal costs. Gross potential is not additional production or profit.

| 50% screening case | Annual output / value | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phosphorus / ammonium N / K2O | 300,000 / 610,700 / 424,413 metric tonnes | Gross nutrient scenario; existing reuse not fully subtracted |
| P/N/K replacement value | $2.38 billion | Dated retail-equivalent nutrient value; before costs |
| AD combined heat and power | 5.36 TWh net electricity + 3.61 TWh usable heat | $588M energy value at stated bill-offset proxies |
| AD upgraded gas alternative | 47.9 billion cubic feet | $147M Henry Hub gross value; before upgrading costs |
| HTL liquid fuel alternative | 262.5M gallons diesel + 87.5M other fuel | $1.28B diesel-only wholesale value; conversion assumed |

**Do not add the three energy alternatives.** They allocate the same underlying sludge carbon differently. The nutrient and energy values also use different price bases and counterfactuals; this report does not present a combined national profit.

What is established, and what is modeled

Commercial nutrient recovery and sludge electricity exist [S28-S29]. National inventories and technology studies provide starting points [S1-S9]. The 25/50/75% cases, costs, price realization, heat use and fuel-upgrading splits here are explicit screening assumptions. No measured 2026 fleet-output claim is made.

The most useful near-term sequence is to audit existing recovery, target concentrated sidestreams and flared gas, compare full treatment baselines, then match qualified products to actual buyers. A facility that is large by flow is not necessarily the best nutrient or financial opportunity.

The dashboard discovery queue contains 14,948 EFF/ADC municipal permit records, with 1,545 heuristic flow-review flags. Application flow, design flow and annual DMR flow must stay separate [S37]. Flags prioritize source review and do not prove an error. The dashboard register is not used as a summed national flow denominator here.

Input sensitivity is wide: EPA concentration endpoints imply about 0.56-2.30 Mt/year ammonium-N before recovery; the older K concentration range implies 0.57-1.70 Mt K2O equivalent. These are sensitivity bounds, not confidence intervals. A national assay-weighted model is needed to narrow them.

Four distinct economic accounts

**Replacement value:** what comparable nutrients or energy cost a buyer. **Receipts:** what the recovery facility can actually sell. **Avoided baseline:** expenditure that genuinely disappears after retrofit. **Net incremental benefit:** receipts plus disjoint avoided expenses, minus new capital, operations and residual costs. Policy credits are a fifth, separate sensitivity.

Null values in the data package mean unestablished. They do not mean a resource has no value. Older composition references are marked as content proxies and are excluded from qualified-output valuations.

DTN retail survey, October 5-9, published October 14: ammonia $977, DAP $934 and potash $502 per short ton [S6]. Convert 1 short ton = 0.90718474 metric tonnes. N price uses 82% N ammonia. Subtract the 18% N component from DAP before allocating its remaining value to 46% P2O5. Potash uses 60% K2O. This keeps each nutrient counted once.

Struvite is a product route, not an extra resource

If all 300,000 tonnes of recovered P in the midpoint were converted into pure struvite, product-equivalent mass would be 2.38 Mt/year, containing 135,664 tonnes N and requiring 235,409 tonnes Mg. The remaining standalone recovered N is 475,037 tonnes. Magnesium and other reagent purchases belong in process costs. P/N nutrient-equivalent value is about $574 per tonne of pure struvite; no premium or magnesium sales credit is assumed.

That conversion is a stoichiometric illustration. Total P may require release, purification or an alternative phosphate route. Magnesium from desal brine could be investigated as an input, but it is not automatically compatible or free.

A focused phosphorus target

The largest-plant study lists 67,000 tonnes P to landfill, 34,000 to incineration and 30,000 to other disposal: 131,000 tonnes/year [S4]. Recovery of 25/50/75% gives 32,750/65,500/98,250 tonnes P and about $129M/$259M/$388M P-only replacement value. This subset is already inside the gross P total; the listed categories are not a complete national flow balance.

Midpoint annual output is equivalent to about 54 days of phosphate-rock import P under the assumed grade. That is annual production equivalence, not a stored reserve. Existing land application and other recovery must be retained in the baseline.

Interstate gap filling

The study identifies urban P concentrations and agricultural shortfalls in parts of the Midwest [S4]. A real route needs a named producer, seasonal qualified output, a named buyer, crop nutrient demand, product-specific delivered cost and the foreign or domestic purchase actually replaced. No particular interstate route is verified by this national screen.

## Three alternatives, one carbon account

| Deployment share | CHP net power | CHP usable heat | RNG alternative | HTL diesel alternative |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25% | 2.68 TWh | 1.80 TWh | 24.0 Bcf | 131.2M gal |
| 50% | 5.36 TWh | 3.61 TWh | 47.9 Bcf | 262.5M gal |
| 75% | 8.05 TWh | 5.41 TWh | 71.9 Bcf | 393.7M gal |

**AD-CHP:** anaerobic microbes produce biogas; clean it and generate electricity plus useful heat. The model uses methane HHV 55.5 GJ/t, 35% electric efficiency, 40% recoverable heat, 15% electric parasitics and 50% of recovered heat available after internal loads. These are assumptions, not measured national averages. Availability affects required generator capacity.

**AD-RNG:** reserve 15% of gas energy for process heat and recover 95% of the remainder during upgrading. Sellable gas is an alternative to burning it in CHP. Compression, siloxanes/H2S removal, connections and methane slip remain material costs.

**HTL:** PNNL literature provides a rounded 1 billion gallon/year biocrude reference [S8]. Here, 70% finished-fuel volume yield and a 75% diesel share are assumptions. At 50% deployment, 500M biocrude gallons become 262.5M diesel and 87.5M other fuel gallons. Hydrogen demand, product density, energy content and actual refinery yields need a process model [S34].

| Midpoint energy pathway | Annual gross value / offset proxy | Import-volume comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CHP electricity + usable heat | $524M power + $64M heat = $588M | Net power equals 17.8% of gross electricity imports |
| RNG alternative | $147M at Henry Hub | 1.5% of gross gas imports |
| HTL diesel alternative | $1.283B diesel-only wholesale value | 10.8% of gross distillate imports |

A 2014 federal roadmap separately estimated 5.6 TWh/year full WRRF electricity potential [S39], below this NREL-based energy conversion. Half of that reference is 2.8 TWh before harmonizing net/gross boundaries. Different scopes and assumptions prevent treating either as a precise forecast.

Import ratios use 2025 gross volumes [S15-S18]. Actual displacement is unknown. Electricity must match time/location and may replace domestic generation; the U.S. is a net gas exporter. Distillate includes products beyond road diesel. Finished-fuel diesel-only value excludes other products and all upgrading expenses.

## Break-even tests and baseline savings

At full benchmark realization, maximum all-in product-funded costs are about $3.95/kg P, $1.31/kg N and $0.92/kg K2O. At an assumed 75% realization, those ceilings become $2.96/$0.99/$0.69. Those are component-only ceilings. Struvite N may add value to the P route if not credited elsewhere. Verified treatment/disposal savings can also raise the ceiling, without double credit.

| Illustrative cost stress | P route, $/kg P | Standalone N, $/kg N | K route, $/kg K2O |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lower | 2 | 1 | 0.50 |
| Middle | 4 | 2 | 1.50 |
| Higher | 8 | 4 | 3.00 |

These rates are **assumed sensitivities, not nationally estimated costs**. They must cover annualized capital, labor, reagents including Mg, energy, finishing, freight, testing, rejects and residual management per qualified output. The P route includes struvite N co-recovery; standalone N cost is applied only to the remaining N, avoiding a duplicate process charge.

| 50% output case | All-in cost | Assumed receipts | Before avoided costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lower cost stress | $1.29B | $1.78B | $497M |
| middle cost stress | $2.79B | $1.78B | -$1.00B |
| higher cost stress | $5.57B | $1.78B | -$3.79B |

The middle stress case needs about $1.00B/year of disjoint avoided expenses or other support to break even. This is a sensitivity result, not a claim that such savings exist. Existing product revenue and land-application benefits must not be credited again.

Sludge conversion cost evidence

PNNL 2024 HYPOWERs mature-plant case reports $3.00/gal biocrude minimum selling price with avoided incineration and fuel credits; pioneer-plant analysis is much less favorable [S19]. Prices and credit assumptions belong to that case and date. It does not establish a nationwide 2026 cost. First-plant engineering and aqueous-phase treatment studies [S20-S21] reinforce the need for site-specific budgets.

A prior national HTL model estimated $1.4B/year disposal savings at modeled full deployment [S33]. A purely linear half-deployment reference would be $700M/year in that older model. Do not append that to an economics case already crediting disposal, or assume savings scale linearly at every facility. A current costed national net-benefit estimate remains unestablished.

## Additional energy opportunities need a heat sink

Wastewater sensible heat is separate from sludge chemical energy. Sewage-source heat pumps have measured site examples [S30], but national accessible heat depends on collection, temperature, seasonal demand and treatment constraints.

| Thermal sensitivity assumption / result | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Temperature extraction / accessible share | 5 C / 10% of approximate flow |
| Heat-pump COP | 4 |
| Source heat recovered | 27.31 TWh/year |
| Electricity required | 9.10 TWh/year |
| Heat delivered | 36.42 TWh/year |
| Avoided gas-heat proxy / power expense | $646M / $889M per year |
| Operating balance before capex and O&M | -$244M/year |

This particular gas-displacement case does not pay for its electricity at the selected national proxies. Cheaper power, displaced oil/electric resistance heating, cooling demand or better COP can change the result [S38]. It is not evidence against all sewage heat pumps. Do not add heat credits where CHP already serves the same load, and preserve treatment/discharge temperature requirements.

Efficiency can be an earlier, cheaper resource

DOE describes low/no-cost packages with 5-7% facility-wide savings [S25]. Applying an illustrative 6% to its historical 30.2 TWh/year sector reference [S24] yields 1.81 TWh/year, or $177M at the electricity proxy before implementation costs. Reconcile with the same post-efficiency load baseline used for CHP; it is not automatically additive.

Raw biogas also contains CO2. An assumed 60/40 methane/CO2 mix implies about 2.14 Mt CO2/year in the midpoint gas stream. Purification, compression and a matched buyer are missing. Carbon already in the sludge must not be credited twice; no CO2 sales or carbon-credit value is counted.

## Contaminants, residuals and product gates

A physically recovered resource becomes resilience supply only when usable, dependable and deliverable. The model does not certify fertilizer, soil amendments, fuel specifications, emissions or permits.

| Stream / risk | Required accounting | Release gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PFAS | Track aqueous, solids, air and concentrate destinations; capture is not destruction. | Validated technology-specific treatment and residual destination [S26] |
| Microplastics | Capture to filters/sludge creates a residual burden. | Demonstrated destruction or controlled management; no automatic soil application |
| Metals | Recovery and concentration alter speciation and reject hazards. | Qualified product assays and controlled residue disposal |
| Pathogens / pharmaceuticals | Route-dependent treatment and byproduct behavior. | End-use testing and applicable process requirements |
| Salt / sodium / crop response | Nutrient mass does not establish agronomic equivalence. | Buyer/crop specification, availability and application timing [S35] |
| Fuel / biogas impurities | H2S, siloxanes, heteroatoms, catalyst poisons and methane loss. | Cleanup, emissions controls, product certification and measured net energy |

Thermal sludge processing is not automatically a PFAS-destruction guarantee. EPA 2026 guidance highlights process-specific performance, emissions and products of incomplete destruction [S26]. Old biosolids compliance or a process label cannot replace current product evidence.

Cost items that must stay in the final project boundary

Collection; dewatering and drying; acid/base/Mg and other reagents; heat/electricity/H2; cleanup and upgrading; product finishing; laboratory testing; storage; freight; labor; maintenance; backup equipment; financing; startup/ramp losses; off-gas and aqueous treatment; residual transport/disposal; monitoring and decommissioning. Avoided expenses must use the same baseline boundary.

Current evidence cannot close all these items nationally. The data package keeps the missing values explicit instead of assuming zero. No national construction recommendation or verified profit is claimed.

## What to prioritize and what to measure next

**First priority:** concentrated P/N streams, gas currently flared, and existing digesters needing efficient CHP or cleanup. Existing infrastructure can make a retrofit more attractive than building a new train. Protect existing recovery as part of the supply base; pursue additional output separately.

**Second priority:** regional HTL or other sludge-processing hubs where measured sludge supply, disposal costs, refinery access, heat and qualified products support a complete case. Combine routes only with an explicit carbon, nutrient and contaminant balance.

**Selective research:** potassium concentration/recovery; coagulant recycling; Mg exchange with brine facilities; ash phosphate; specialized industrial metal streams; isotope separation. Do not extrapolate municipal averages into industrial or mine streams.

| Evidence needed | Resilience decision enabled |
| --- | --- |
| Unique plant and process stream; monthly flow/assays | Reliable annual yield, uncertainty and no duplicate accounts |
| Existing output, flare, land use and disposal costs | Additional supply and incremental economics |
| Equipment, heat sinks, parasitics and downtime | Net energy and realistic capacity |
| Buyer product/grade, seasonal demand and freight | Deliverable supply and true domestic/foreign replacement |
| Gas/energy/fertilizer imports by destination and product | Route-level import substitution, not just national ratios |
| Black-start/islanding, fuel storage, spares and outage tests | Actual operational resilience and reserve duration |

The biggest plants concentrate much of the resource, but the optimal shortlist also includes smaller sites with concentrated streams, high disposal costs, nearby farms/industry or vulnerable power supply. A resilience score should expose these inputs rather than hide them in a single weighted number.

Separate register extensions: food/beverage, dairy/manure, fertilizer manufacturing, landfill leachate, mining/metallurgy, power-plant cooling, brackish/desal and decentralized sanitation. Confirm whether each stream already enters a municipal account before combining inventories. National all-wastewater totals remain incomplete.

## Model contract and limitations

The accompanying ZIP contains inputs.json, results.json, run_model.py, the report text and this PDF. Python 3 standard library is sufficient to reproduce the numerical results: run **python run_model.py** in the extracted folder. No account or network access is needed.

Automated checks cover finite values, nutrient allocation, energy conservation, fuel-product splits, and retention of unknown margins. Report tables are generated from the same result objects where practical. No facility assays or engineering claims are invented by the checks.

• 25/50/75 nutrient fractions are net qualified-output assumptions; energy fractions are deployment shares of dated gross literature potentials, not equivalent recovery technology efficiencies.

• No total annual profit is reported. Product replacement value, plant-gate receipts, avoided costs and policy credits are different accounts.

• AD-CHP, AD-RNG and full-sludge HTL are alternatives here. A hybrid requires a separate explicit carbon/mass balance.

• Struvite N is inside total recovered N; do not add it or charge separate nitrogen-recovery costs to it. Mg reagent costs belong in the P route.

• CHP parasitic electricity is deducted once; heat reserved for internal process loads is not externally sold. Do not value the methane again after converting it to heat and power.

• Heat pumps consume electricity; do not count delivered heat as recovered source energy alone.

• Existing land application, generation, heat, products and baseline costs must be subtracted for additionality. Avoided expense must not be duplicated across nutrient and sludge trains.

• Imported nutrient content, gross electricity/gas volumes and diesel volumes are physical comparisons, not verified monetary trade-deficit reductions. Net gas exports and electricity transmission constraints matter.

• Old sludge composition times partial reported dry mass is a context proxy. Unknown qualified recovery/value is null, never zero-filled.

• Water reuse volume and sales value remain excluded; thermal energy is a separate resource account.

• Contaminants capture, soil-use clearance and destruction are distinct. No autonomous approvals, soil safety or emissions clearance inferred.

• No speculative deuterium fuel sales, carbon-credit revenue or critical-metal revenue included.

Source retrieval took place October 16-17, 2026 UTC. The local analysis date is October 16, 2026 in America/Chicago. Market observations have their own stated dates; source publication date is not a common measurement year.
