# Maui Resident-First Circular Water Allocation and Hydraulic Catchment Model — v0.2
**Atlas Lattice / ORCS | 2026-10-16 | Proposed and simulated; not a government allocation, approved rate schedule, or commissioned engineering.**
Companion to `research/maui-smart-water-spine-residents-first-fiber-ai-inference-v0.1-2026-10-16.md`. New version; prior publications preserved.

## 1. Public-language operating principle
**Residents get dependable water first. Use it responsibly. Collect and clean it after use. Put that reclaimed water to work in agriculture, suitable industrial processes, landscaping and commercial non-potable operations. Collect and treat recoverable return flows again, with declining volume after each use. Protect natural streams and aquifers.** Resorts can enter separately negotiated investment and supply contracts for capacity above protected household and essential-service obligations, subject to law, utility tariff, approvals, and independently documented benefits. A high payment cannot displace emergency drinking-water needs or public-trust obligations.

## 2. Physical hydrologic ledger: do not count a gallon more than once
**Existing historic benchmark (requires update):** Maui *County* DWS residential customer demand 22.5 MGD (17.5 single-family, 5.0 multifamily); includes Molokaʻi and excludes private purveyors. Not a verified 2026 Maui Island-only value. Use as a screening *demand* benchmark, not final capacity.
**Model A household first pass:** 22.5 MGD potable first-use; 70% of household water makes it to separately collected recoverable effluent = 15.75 MGD; 90% treatment yield = **14.175 MGD R-1-or-better candidate reclaimed water**, assuming suitability. This potential cannot be added to existing public-plant influent as a new source.
**Reality gate:** Three cited Maui public plants (Lahaina 3.41, Kīhei 3.93, Wailuku–Kahului 5.54 MGD) handle 12.88 MGD across ALL customer classes. Therefore the 14.175 household-only recovered target requires independently verified additional sewer/private-system flows, source-level residential split, and capture upgrades. At 90% public-plant total yield, 11.592 MGD reclaimed; documented existing reuse ~4.37 MGD is part of it, not additional. Remaining potential new reusable output max ~7.222 MGD before losses and constraints.
**Repeated reuse:** Recovered second-use water (14.175 MGD) may be allocated to irrigation, industry, and resort landscaping where approved. A further reclaimed third-use stream is limited by each sector's **actual measured return-flow fraction** and second-pass treatment recovery. For a hypothetical 14.175 MGD all sent to industrial uses with 40% return and 90% recovery, an upper illustrative third-use flow is 5.103 MGD. If all goes to crops with 10% collectable return and 90% treatment, third-use 1.276 MGD, and collecting field drainage may be technically difficult or ecologically harmful. These are ALTERNATIVE allocations, not cumulative contributions; do not sum industrial and farm return from same water. Aquifer recharge and crop evapotranspiration must be measured and respected, not described as leakage to eradicate.
**Potable capacity:** 22.5/0.85=26.47 MGD nameplate if dedicated desalination replaces ALL historic county household-metered consumption, with 85% annual dependable output; actual required seawater desal nameplate depends on Maui Island private/DWS households, conservation, dual-plumbing, retained verified supplies, pressure-zone limits and genuine emergency backup.
**Operational priority order (proposed, needs legal review):** (1) households' safe essential water and affordability, (2) hospitals, schools, shelters, fire/emergency and public-health uses, (3) essential commercial potable operations and guaranteed existing obligations, (4) remaining commercial and visitor uses with explicit non-potable reuse requirements, (5) discretionary/incremental premium resort allocations after protected uses and public-trust requirements. Industrial/farm **suitable** demands preferentially met with reclaimed water, not blanket exclusion from potable water when it is legally or technically necessary.

## 3. Geographic design: service zones, not arbitrary 5/5/5 MGD
**West Maui / Lahaina–Kāʻanapali:** Map actual DWS + private household connections, existing reservoirs and groundwater sources, resort/service potable vs landscape demands, wastewater collection, isolated subdivisions, cross-island transmission paths; account for post-2023 changes; treatment of odors, overflows, injections/coastal pathways, and reclaimed landscape and agriculture commitments.
**Central / Wailuku–Kahului–South Maui:** Largest hydraulic and source-substitution challenge; Wailuku–Kahului treatment flow 5.54 MGD historical and Kīhei separate 3.93 MGD; high-demand central pressure zones and irrigation distribution must be hydraulically distinct from potable distribution. County 2025 WUDP summary projects **Central System demand 34.134 MGD in 2035 with planned conservation/source development**, but that includes customer classes beyond households and overlaps South Maui and Pāʻia; it is NOT a replacement demand for a new central desal plant. See https://waterresources.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/722/2025-MAUI-ISLAND-WUDP-SUMMARY-DOCUMENT
**North Shore / Pāʻia–Haʻikū–Makawao/Upcountry interfaces:** Separate local residential account demand and relief options; do not treat all Upcountry as North Shore pipe-reachable without elevation, pumping, storage and existing supply study. Hāna and remote catchments require separate decentralized/resilience assessment.
**Engineering fields per district:** DWS metered delivered and source produced by sector/season, private purveyor, source water type and aquifer, current groundwater and stream withdrawals, wastewater connection rate and influent, sewer collection density, existing R-1 reuse and rights, actual irrigation demand by parcel, elevations and hydraulic grades, hazards/easements, nonrevenue water broken down physical versus accounting, current pipe condition, alternative corridor CAPEX/OPEX, compute footprint, disposal residuals.
**Capacity scenarios to test:** islandwide new desal nameplate 15/25/30/40/50 MGD; vary West, Central and North independently, with P95 demand peaks, N-1 failure, backup supply, district head losses and minimum emergency storage. No project parcel selection without GIS/rights review.

## 4. Negotiated resort contributions and household affordability
Official Maui FY2027 water-use tariff is **$17.69 per 1,000 gallons for hotel/resort properties**, compared to graduated single-family tiers starting at $2.13 per kgal. Multi-family water is $5.09/kgal. Existing hotels already face differentiated rates; a NEW infrastructure investment contribution must not be characterized as their first premium. Source: https://www.mauicounty.gov/216/Water-Charges
Model separately: (A) dedicated contract-for-capacity and upfront capital contribution; (B) ongoing verified cost-of-service or demand charge for nonessential incremental potable entitlement; (C) finance-agreed reclaimed water dual-piping for irrigation/cooling/cleaning; (D) legally adopted visitor environmental financing; (E) resident affordability subsidies/credits. Customer classifications, ratepayer protections, existing rights, anti-discrimination and public utility regulation require independent legal review. **Do not promise or assume passage or specific household bill cuts.**
For illustration only: 1 MGD of delivered water for a year = 365 million gallons = 365,000 kgal. At the FY2027 published resort volumetric price $17.69/kgal, gross volumetric bill would be ~$6.45685M/year, NOT profit and not an incremental premium above existing tariff. Any negotiated contribution is evaluated above such baseline and against incremental lifecycle capacity cost.

## 5. Smart spine, fiber sensing, and co-located inference: independent safety
Compare new protected aboveground/aerial utilidors, rehabilitated buried water mains, and conventional trenching **segment-by-segment** including scenic/cultural/hazard constraints. Accessible corridors may include separate insulated potable, reclaimed and fiber conduits on a dedicated engineered pipe rack. Leaks, corrosion, UV, sea spray, hurricanes, wildfire, seismic and flood hazards can offset the maintenance advantages. Separate hydraulic/drinking-water cross-connection protection and contamination-containment procedures.
Use optical fiber **DAS for acoustic events**, complementary DTS/FBG where suitable, district metering and acoustic hydrophones; use dedicated dark-fiber or other engineered fibers rather than presume any telecom carrying cable supplies sensing and communications simultaneously.
Peer-reviewed 2026 research in *Applied Acoustics* confirms experimental DAS leak-localization benefit: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003682X25005286 ; additional 2026 *Sensors* research tests urban water leakage using existing dark fiber: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/7/2152 .
EPA records the South Bend smart-sewer precedent with >70% reduction in combined sewer overflow volume, but not as a transfer-ready Maui saving estimate: https://www.epa.gov/npdes/smart-sewers .
Each treatment campus has its **own on-site inference node** for hydraulic modeling, anomaly detection, treatment optimization, crop models, energy scheduling, closed-loop resource ledger and transparency. Inference workload, IT electricity, cooling WUE, compute waste heat, backup and CAPEX must be audited; no assumption of hyperscale compute or free cooling. Operational plant safety and utility controls remain segregated, conventionally validated, offline-capable and human-governed; independent alarms interlock risky valve/disinfection changes. Use NIST SP 800-82 Rev 3: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final

## 6. Agricultural/industrial cascade and resource quality
Agriculture: match R-1 certified effluent to crop, soil, salinity, sodium adsorption ratio, boron, pathogens, trace organics and nitrogen/phosphorus mass balance, with verified micro/nanoplastic capture *and fate*. Use weather-driven soil water balance, tensiometers, dielectric moisture, electrical-conductivity probes, valves, variable-rate irrigation, mulching, native hedges and soil carbon; separately test piezo-soak and plasma treatment before claiming savings.
Industry/resorts: recirculate suitable process wash, HVAC cooling and landscaping water under permitted health protections. Municipal source streams include commercial restaurants/hotels already connected to treatment plants; count private flows only with documented independent additions. Graywater local recirculation can **lower central wastewater feed**; don't claim both local and centralized recovery from the same first-use water.
Microplastic filters move contaminant into retentate/sludge; prevent unqualified use of plastic-bearing solids on agricultural land; characterize salts/ash; ZLD manages liquid residuum without pretending solid residues vanish.
**Net basin benefit formula:** avoided withdrawals = measured retired source pumping/diversion minus replacement-induced fresh withdrawals and lost beneficial return flows (tracked separately); monitor environment and water rights. An increase in reused gallons alone is not proof of aquifer/stream restoration.
Reuse approvals: https://mapps.mauicounty.gov/238/WWRD-Recycled-Water-Review---Regular ; https://www.epa.gov/waterreuse/summary-hawaiis-water-reuse-guideline-or-regulation-agriculture

## 7. Existing Hawaiʻi law aligns with some efficiency components
Maui County's **Water Conservation Ordinance 5929**, in force March 1, 2026, restricts potable daytime landscape irrigation and requires additional water-efficient systems in relevant developments; it includes hotel, laundry, car wash and other conservation and recycling provisions subject to details. This is not approval of ORCS' entire proposal. https://waterresources.mauicounty.gov/226/Water-Use-Conservation-Ordinance-5929-Bi
Maui reclaimed-water supply/use requires County and State Department of Health approval. https://mapps.mauicounty.gov/238/WWRD-Recycled-Water-Review---Regular

## 8. Study schedule and deliverables (proposed)
**Phase 0:** Retrieve island-only 2024–2026 customer usage by zone and billing class, DWS source production, private purveyor residential demand, residential/commercial sanitary sewage flow and septic/cesspool geography, full existing reuse commitments, regional pipe elevation and GIS, and Maui district-specific source retirement priorities. Site consultation and public trust rights evaluation.
**Phase 1:** Simulate hydraulic flow/pressure/peaks with EPANET-type potable and SWMM-type sewer networks, accounting for proposed 70% return and 90% recovery sensitivities against measured influent; dynamic reclaimed allocation with changing crop demand; identify West/Central/North required treatment AND freshwater production separately.
**Phase 2:** Field-pilot 1–3 km accessible sheath/fiber corridor versus a rehabilitated buried control, and safe advanced treatment on actual feedwater; measure leak rate, true treatment yield, microplastics/nutrients, energy, storm resilience and maintainability.
**Phase 3:** Negotiate resort and institutional financial participation under law, forecast resident bill reductions net of energy and financing costs; scale only after independent oversight and measured benefit.
**Publishable outputs:** zone ledger CSV schema; annual/seasonal water mass-balance model; detailed 20-year nominal and discounted infrastructure financing; stream and aquifer offset register; versioned pilot baseline with receipts.

## 9. Explicit nonclaims
No project approved or funded; 22.5 MGD is historical county DWS demand, not current Maui Island-only; household second-use target not yet measured; 3×5 MGD does not meet household or commercial demand islandwide; no existing commercial aquifer-offset entitlement is displaced unilaterally; zero liquid discharge is not zero waste; AI sensors do not by themselves repair leaks; and no confirmed 2029 commissioning date for this Maui proposal.
