**Research date:** 2026-10-16
**Classification:** REPORTED facts / REGULATORY figures / COUNTY PROJECTIONS / MODEL ASSUMPTIONS explicitly segregated. **NO acquired business, completed deal, built pipe, realized revenue, or financial surplus is claimed.**
**Continuity:** New document; preserves v0.1–v0.7. Accompanies `research/maui_emergency_water_wholesale_v08.py` (compares Central 22/25/28 MGD). This paper selects **Central 25 MGD as a stronger planning test** without claiming it is approved or sufficient under all failure conditions.

## Executive decision
**Treat all currently operated water-supply chains as part of the audit, not merely bottled-water imports.** County DWS's own source operating and renewal costs, suppliers that sell raw/treated bulk water TO DWS or other utilities, privately operated public water utilities selling to households and resorts, local bottlers, and finished off-island bottled imports comprise DIFFERENT businesses and sometimes **the same water resold in sequence**. Present separate ledgers rather than summing gross sales and claiming all as net county profit.

**Service objective:** safe, affordable, resilient household/essential-service water under appropriately authorized public stewardship; reinvest economic surplus, avoided current-source costs, and recovered imported-product margins in closed-loop wastewater, pipelines, microgrids, soil restoration, jobs and emergency reserves. Existing operators may be acquired by consensual negotiated and lawful process, integrated through regulated transfer or partnered. Do not assume compulsory displacement, cancellation of rights, or that privatized customer revenue transfers without acquisition costs.

**Plant screening capacities:** West 6 MGD, North 5 MGD, **Central 25 MGD**, total **36 MGD new proposed nameplate**. Model net normal sustained output = 85% × nameplate => 5.10 +4.25 +21.25 = **30.60 MGD**. Historical 22.5 MGD *county* DWS household demand is only a sizing comparator; excludes some privately served Maui Island households and essential non-household uses and includes other islands. Real Maui household, hospital, firefighting and licensed/resort commitments must be mapped by zone. The original Central 22 and larger Central 28 remain sensitivity alternatives.

## I. Source ledger: TRUE discovered financial evidence
### A. Maui DWS — ALREADY MUNICIPAL
**FY2025 audited:** water sales **$81,444,411**; other operating revenues **$1,029,144**; total operating revenues **$82,473,555**. Administrative/general $20,205,886; power/pumping **$18,323,791**; depreciation/amortization **$16,876,258**; transmission/distribution **$15,187,456**; purification **$8,929,971**; customer accounts/collections $2,238,156; source of supply **$1,057,735**; TOTAL operating expenses **$82,819,253**, and **operating LOSS $345,698**. Nonoperating net income $6,500,745, income before capital contributions/transfers $6,155,047, change in net position $19,080,480 includes capital contributions/transfers and is not operating profit. These are countywide actual audited figures; do not represent costs *avoidable* upon water replacement. Source [Maui DWS FY2025 audited financials](https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/157425/Fiscal-Year-2025-PDF-2464-KB).
**Right model:** identify actual MGD/wells/stream water and meter zone; which power costs, treatment and renewal are genuinely retired; depreciation only disappears in municipal accrual results if assets disposed and not replaced by new depreciable facilities. New seawater RO+ZLD+microgrid could be more expensive than existing wells and treatment! No headline savings without cost-of-service and audit. Existing DWS water customer revenues are ALREADY county revenue, so never add them to ORCS receipts as newly captured income.

**County-projected, NOT existing private financials:**
- Launiupoko: projected DWS-tariff **annual revenue $2,561,044**, **$879,664 OPEX**, pre-depreciation/pre-capital/pre-debt operating spread **$1,681,380**.
- Olowalu: **$734,363 revenue**, **$671,554 OPEX**, **$62,809** spread.
- Combined **$3,295,407 annual modeled public receipts**, **$1,551,218 OPEX**, **$1,744,189 revenue less forecast operating cost**.
This **$1.744M is NOT NET county profit or the seller's previous margin**; county forecasts may imply higher customer tariffs, capital maintenance, staffing, acquisition/transition expense and inherited defects. Important distinction: *a public operator can retain surplus rather than distributing private profit*, but it also must fund all liabilities, operating and renewal costs; water rates should reflect public-service affordability and legal tariff obligations.

### C. Maui Land & Pineapple (MLP) — wholesale/bulk intermediary under MOU
**Actual 2025 segment-reported potable+nonpotable WATER sales $2.9M**, up from $1.1M in 2024, contained within broader **leasing segment $12.799M revenue and $8.456M costs**. The segment's $4.343M operating income is **NOT a water-only profit figure** and cannot be attributed to water. County signed a **May 18, 2026 MOU** for its West Maui source/transmission assets; MOU ≠ purchase completed. Sources [SEC 2025 form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63330/000143774926010765/mlp20251231_10k.htm), [county MOU](https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/18839).
MLP supplies DWS and Hawaiʻi Water Service; 2025 MLP water receipts overlap downstream utility customer charges. If county purchases MLP supply infrastructure while keeping downstream distribution private, it might become **their wholesale supplier**, not their immediate retail replacement. Purchased wholesale input expense avoided by DWS and same value formerly MLP's sales are one transfer within the financial chain: choose consolidated cashflow method, avoid duplicate credit.

### E. Local bottled-water suppliers versus imports
Maui already has local producer **Tropic Water**, which says it bottles on Maui and serves residential/business customers. Published 5-gallon delivery price ~$9.45–9.95 or historical price ~$8.49; these are **retail/service prices**, not private margin or mass volume; other local producers and distributors require inventory. Sources https://order.tropicwater.com/ and https://tropicwater.com/service-pricing/ . Do NOT classify Maui-made bottles as imports or assume county takeover enhances local jobs if it just displaces existing residents' businesses. Consider (i) county procurement of locally manufactured bottles; (ii) public-benefit brand license and local copacking; (iii) voluntary acquisition/co-op partnership; (iv) independent new municipal plant if validated economies of scale. Prioritize resident jobs, nonexclusive competitive procurement and emergency inventory.
Prior v0.7 bottled model: **53.93M 500-mL equivalent units/year** projected single-serve PET demand from imperfect Maui County-resident + Maui Island visitor population proxy and US per capita use; import fraction **80% hypothetical**. 75% capture of assumed imported portion ×$0.75 blended wholesale implies **$24.27M gross potential county-manufacturer wholesale revenue** and 30% **$7.28M net target** if the entire commercial, capital and tax stack can be held to <=$0.525 fully loaded per equivalent. This is NOT private-supplier financials, and the wholesale $0.75 cannot apply to low-end cases retailing at $0.40/bottle. **Import substitution IS a market-sale replacement opportunity** but importer freight/retailer margins require transfer audits: displaced import wholesale value and county's replacement wholesale sales are a SINGLE market, not TWO benefits. A targeted county-run bottled producer could operate at break-even for jobs/resilience, with net social value separately.
Consumer-quality water must use safe licensed purified/remineralized municipal/RO water, with truthful origin and food-contact-qualified PET; no drinking water from raw sewage, no untested 'alkaline wellness' promises.
For disaster reserve keep 500ml, 1gal and 5gal emergency products/stock regardless of commercial premium lines.

## II. Unified accounting WITHOUT double counting
For each metered/delivered gallon and each dollar, define invoice-chain role:
1. SOURCE water abstraction/import/original water producer: legal rights + source OPEX + asset owner + wholesale price.
2. TREATMENT: existing DWS/private plant fixed and marginal expense + new RO/energy/ZLD.
3. DISTRIBUTION: source wholesale → regulated private utility → resident/resort or county DWS; exact interutility transactions explicitly tagged.
4. BOTTLED/packaging: qualified PET supplier → local bottled manufacturer → distributor/retailer/airport/resort → end customer; paid premiums where actually contracted.
5. END USER payment posted ONCE as consolidated chain revenue; intermediate revenue eliminated on consolidation. Value distributed as input goods, logistics, labor, municipal cash margin and legitimate third-party supplier earnings.
6. AVOIDED LEGACY COST separate per source and date with *genuinely terminated* spending and stranded asset/retained emergency standby offset. No automatic assumption a decommissioned well saves entire historic supply cost if retained as disaster backup; no autonomous closure of all East Maui local freshwater rights.

***Revenue transfer vs profit distinction***:
- New county public water-billing revenues from acquiring a private system CAN be incremental county gross receipts but replace customers' prior private payments, not new island customer spending.
- Retained private wholesale profit is UNKNOWN, not equal to 30% bottle net margin nor project post-acquisition annual revenue less projected OPEX.
- Replacing county DWS's own wells with public desal **does not bring the County new DWS sales revenue**; only avoided source spending, enhanced reliability and environmental outcomes plus potential new end customer loads.
- When residents get lower bills, enterprise revenue decreases. Resident purchasing power and welfare improve; label as separate social benefit, not added municipal profit.
- Regulated utility 'revenue requirement' generally includes cost recovery and permitted return on invested capital; it is not net profit.
- Bottled import replacement is independent of metered DWS billing but both water products cannot be counted as new freshwater supply twice.

Maui ordinance conservation stages may authorize reductions to overall actual system demand; applying a 20% or 30% haircut to historical household use here is **only an emergency capacity simulation**, not a recommendation to curtail domestic essential sanitation or a claim such household cuts are lawful/feasible. Real 2026 demands, tourism/nonresident metered uses, peak/fire demand, district interties, hydraulics and source mix are omitted. **No 100% N-1 Central failure guarantee.**
**Emergency governance:** independent fire/contamination detection, automated mechanical safety interlocks, resilient manual SCADA, human-authorized staged restriction first of optional landscaping, pools, discretionary industrial/resort uses then other legally permitted loads; households and hospitals prioritized; preserve pressure for firefighting and sewer health. AI is decision support, NOT unilateral shutoff of protected customer classes. Meter class accuracy and privacy, publicly auditable orders, appeal mechanisms and native water stewardship in governance.
**Power budget screening:** 1 MGD at 3.5 kWh/m³ seawater reverse osmosis ≈0.552 MW sustained RO *electric* at that delivery rate. Full simulated 30.6-MGD plant production alone ≈**16.9 MW continuous RO electricity** before feedwater intake, pretreatment, transmission, high-pressure topography, wastewater, brine ZLD, mineral treatment, storage, cooling/inference, black start and reserve. Central 21.25 MGD effective output ≈**11.7 MW RO-only**. Fully offline grid running on biosolar/solar+BESS requires **hour-by-hour firm electricity and dispatchable clean backup**, not annual PV nameplate equivalence. Biosolar/bioreactor electricity treated as experimental/limited until measured. Withholds source water from pipes after wildfire contamination until certified safe and isolated.
**New smart pipe:** 1,014 mi historical multi-island benchmark remains user-selected complete network costing denominator but not all pipe replaces; aboveground protected racks along feasible routes with piezoelectric acoustics, dark fiber DAS, condition modeling, wildfire-resistant insulation, isolated interties, protection against falling debris/wind and gravity sewer pumping energy where converted. Aboveground still needs some foundations/crossings; segment 30-year alternatives compare retain/reline vs racks. Private water acquisition includes ~19 miles of real mains to be integrated in actual GIS.

## IV. DCF and public-value outcomes to quantify
For EACH supplier/asset use the explicit financial structure:
- Gross existing revenue from real final customer invoices, intermediate wholesale revenues eliminated on consolidation;
- existing cost of service (source/treatment/distribution, energy, labor, capital maintenance, depreciation, interest and contingency);
- supplier allowable profits/regulated return measured from rate case and specific segment books, not unrelated corporate net ratios;
- asset transfer or buyout purchase price, debt obligations, customer rate harmonization, PUC transfer and legal/pipeline easements;
- actual new cost from coastal desal ZLD, pumping, fiber, protective pipes, smart metering, AI, BESS and emergency reserve;
- retired water abstraction cost and deferred renewal net of stranded asset costs, plus quantified stream/aquifer restoration not treated as automatic cash;
- bottle importer displacement accounting separate by SKU, price band, origin, freight and **final wholesale sales transferred to municipal cooperative manufacturer**;
- Social/ecological results as independent ledgers: resilient clean water and sanitation, food safety, local payroll/jobs, local upcycling and waste reduction, microplastic capture, indigenous rights and public trust, avoided inbound freight (net of feedstock/inputs), long-run soil health.
No projection of a **single total dollar saving** unless all the above are reconciled; county's $82.47M existing utility income, $7.5M Kaʻanapali revenue requirement and $2.9M MLP water sales contain substantial overlaps and cannot simply be added.
