# ORCS Geothermal Siting and Biosolar–ZLD Integration: Corpus Christi and Maui
**Version:** v1.0 | **Date:** 2026-10-15 | **Status:** RESEARCH / PROPOSED; non-canon where unverified; zero realized credit
**Companion:** Maui Water, Ocean Regeneration and East Maui Stream Restoration v1.1. Preserve originals; new appendix only.

## Executive findings
Geothermal is a candidate energy and process-heat source for regenerative desalination, wastewater reclamation, mineral extraction, and zero-liquid-discharge (ZLD) systems. Existing Texas oil/gas wells merit a screening analysis, but no specific well is presently demonstrated to supply the modeled facility. Maui's Haleakalā rift and Olowalu–Ukumehame prospects may offer locational benefits, but no commercial reservoir is confirmed by the cited historic surveys. Use independent geology, engineering, land-use, cultural and environmental gates.

## Source-verified geography and maturity
1. **Corpus Christi:** DoD Defense Innovation Unit reported that Sage Geosystems and UT Bureau of Economic Geology completed reservoir modeling and conceptual designs for geopressured geothermal systems at Naval Air Station Corpus Christi and Fort Bliss (August 2025). This is **feasibility work, not a commissioned power plant**, and Naval Air Station Corpus Christi is distinct from the proposed Harbor Island desalination site. [DIU 2025](https://www.diu.mil/latest/department-of-defense-expands-geothermal-initiative-to-support-mission).
2. **Texas existing wells:** USGS 2025 used >1.4 million historical well-bottomhole temperature records to estimate geothermal potential of documented orphan wells: low <90°C, moderate 90–150°C, high >150°C. Temperature is not sufficient: verify well integrity, fluid production, transmissivity, pressure, corrosion, legal access, plugging obligations and thermal depletion. [USGS 2025](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/geothermal-potential-orphan-oil-and-gas-wells). [USGS orphan database 2026](https://www.usgs.gov/data/united-states-documented-orphaned-well-database-2026).
3. **Texas regulatory evidence:** Texas Railroad Commission permitted Sage geopressured well in Atascosa County in February 2025 for an energy-storage project, and reported another Galveston County permit in July 2026. **Energy storage is not independently generated geothermal energy.** [RRC Feb 2025](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/news/022625-geothermal-press-release/); [RRC July 2026](https://www.rrc.texas.gov/news/geothermal-energy-gains-ground-in-texas/).
4. **Maui Haleakalā SW rift:** Historic field-survey estimates of 30–40% low/moderate-temperature and 15–25% high-temperature resource likelihood; geophysical anomalies do not prove a commercial field. [Geothermal resources assessment in Hawaii](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0375650586900143); [Hawai‘i DLNR drilling guide](https://dlnreng.hawaii.gov/geothermal/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2016/01/Circular_C-126_DLNR_DEC2014_sm.pdf).
5. **Maui Olowalu–Ukumehame:** Historic anomalous temperatures and groundwater chemistry; ~50–60% probability of >=50°C resource and <=10% of >=125°C in original assessment. Not a confirmed electricity resource. [Geothermal assessment](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0375650586900143).
6. **Updated Hawai‘i resource mapping:** 2020 Hawai‘i Play Fairway work incorporated surveys on Haleakalā SW rift and identified *Haleakalā east rift* for additional geophysical surveys, with Lāna‘i and southeast Mauna Kea as leading drilling targets. No Maui production claim. [WRRC/UH publication](https://www.wrrc.hawaii.edu/publication/play-fairway-analysis-of-geothermal-resources-across-the-state-of-hawaii-4-updates-with-new-groundwater-chemistry-subsurface-stress-analysis-and-focused-geophysical-surveys/); [Hawai‘i State Energy Office](https://energy.hawaii.gov/geothermal/).

## Common geothermal integration branches
A. **No geothermal:** PV + biogas + batteries + grid, with geothermal exploration future option.
B. **20 MW net geothermal electricity at 90% capacity factor:** 157.68 GWh/year before curtailment, transmission losses and maintenance beyond capacity-factor assumption.
C. **40 MW net geothermal electricity at 90% capacity factor:** 315.36 GWh/year.
D. **Direct heat:** determine credible geothermal fluid delivery temperatures and recoverable MWth separately, then model evaporator/crystallizer heat duty and exchanger corrosion/scale. Do not convert MWth to MWe at 100%.
E. **Subsurface pressure-storage:** represent as energy storage with round-trip efficiency, NOT as net energy production.
All cases include exploration, drilling/workover, wells, power block or heat exchangers, interconnection, insurance, water rights, monitoring, and restoration; no geothermal CAPEX is yet evidenced for these specific locations.

## Maui baseline: three 5-MGD water production campuses
Nominal seawater RO product = 15 MGD (56,781 m3/day), 45% recovery. Original illustrative RO electricity 3.5 kWh/m3 product -> 72.5 GWh/year; high-energy full-brine ZLD sensitivity ~253.3 additional GWh/year; **total 325.8 GWh/year** before waste treatment, pumping and thermal heat integration. Illustrative solar PV 10.8 MWdc x 1,600 kWh/kWdc-year = 17.28 GWh/year; wastewater biogas 2.8 GWh/year assumed, not measured.
20MW geothermal branch => 157.68 GWh/year, ~48.4% of RO+ZLD annual electricity. 40MW =>315.36 GWh/year, ~96.8%. With PV+biogas added, 40MW may exceed modeled **annual** energy, but hourly dispatch, parasitic loads and geothermal feasibility remain unresolved.
**Critical caveat:** Full-brine ZLD energy model is a stress test, not a design. It should be recalculated using exact concentrate chemistry, selective mineral precipitation, mechanical vapor recompression, thermal and electrical duties, waste-heat reuse, and physical residue disposition. Do not double count PV, biogas heat, geothermal heat, waste heat, and electricity.
**Siting:** Keep wastewater collection interfaces near existing utility service areas; investigate remote geothermal-to-grid links or transportable thermal energy only where physics and economics support them. Haleakalā SW rift and Olowalu–Ukumehame are **screening areas**, not approved or selected plant parcels. Avoid protected areas, sensitive aquifers and culturally significant sites.

## Corpus Christi / Harbor Island baseline
For a *hypothetical* 100-MGD finished-water RO system: 378,541 m3/day x 365 x 3.5 kWh/m3 = ~483.6 GWh/year RO electricity. 20MW @90% yields ~32.6% annual RO-only electricity; 40MW yields ~65.2%, before concentrate management. These do not establish that 20/40MW of wells exist or that ZLD is funded. Separate seawater desalination from wastewater treatment.
The historical Harbor Island 50-MGD planning cost and later 100-MGD proposal require consistent CAPEX and process-boundary reconciliation; **do not equate a 100,000-m3/day Dongjiakou installation with 100 MGD or treat any $67M number as verified full-build CAPEX.**

## Microplastic, ZLD and sludge safeguards
- Screen and capture microplastics in wastewater, stormwater and sludges with size-resolved measurement and nanoparticle analytic protocols. Treat Polygone as proposed hardware pending tests.
- Maintain separate sewage-sludge and saline-mineral pathways to avoid contaminating marketable products or making soil amendments saline.
- Recover water, nutrients and commercially demonstrable minerals; prevent routine effluent injection into coastal-connected groundwater and routine liquid discharge, within permitted and verified engineering definition.
- **ZLD does not mean zero solid waste or zero air emissions.** Incineration or other thermal treatment of screened organics needs emissions control, PFAS/destruction evaluation, ash characterization and safe residual disposal. Never count unrecovered contaminants as destroyed.
- Include measured coastal nutrient, microplastic, enterococci, beach-advisory, odor, and overflow baselines; ZLD alone does not prove ocean ecological recovery.

## Commercial screening / hold points
1. Geothermal well GIS inventory within realistic electrical-connection radius of Harbor Island, temperature-depth corrections, legal/operational status and plugging risks; exclude irreparable/leaking wells.
2. Independent reservoir logs, production tests, pressure/flow/chemistry, temperature decline and binary-cycle net efficiency; compare direct heat vs electricity.
3. Maui GIS overlay of geothermal prospect, existing West/Central/North Shore wastewater network, sensitive ecological/cultural zones, parcels, hazard exposure and substation routes. Community/Native Hawaiian consultation required before siting.
4. Produce unit-consistent $/kW, $/MWth, $/m3 product, $/ton solids, GWh/year, and 25-year NPV including drilling failures and backup capacity.
5. Compare environmental outcomes and visitor-funded finance options without assuming geothermal resource, tourist-tax enactment, or guaranteed product revenues.
