## Executive proposition

Reorient the Olowalu–Launiupoko *mauka-to-makai* region toward resident-first housing, Native Hawaiian-led landscape stewardship, locally productive regenerative farms and native dry forest, public botanical gardens, fire-resilient BioSolar neighborhoods, and a carefully screened, beautiful distributed desalination/mineral-recovery network. Extend the same **proposed mandatory** BioSolar + battery-backed critical-load + microgrid-readiness performance standard to **eligible new Lahaina Town buildings**, with funded hardship relief and code-compliant equivalencies.

**Priority order:** (1) resident security and Indigenous cultural rights; (2) watershed, reef, fire safety and biodiversity; (3) durable housing, agriculture, local employment and public parks; (4) water/energy reliability; (5) visitor experience, optional retail and revenue. A visitor resort is neither required nor preferred. No displacement, title presumption, or conditioning permanent tenancy on employment.

**Nonnegotiable methodological distinction:** the **613-acre / 26,702,280-square-foot** historical Olowalu Town planning footprint is a *research-area denominator only*. It is **not** a single surveyed, vacant, available parcel. The nearby **19.4-acre / 845,064-square-foot** former cinder quarry used for temporary wildfire debris storage is **separate state-managed land**, is inland, and is **not** beachfront or presumed developable as housing or desal infrastructure. Olowalu also contains extensive documented archaeological, cultural and productive agricultural legacies. Brown vegetation in leeward Maui is not itself evidence of environmental degradation.

## 3. Living landscape: ecology, beauty and actual water demand

### 3.1 Three landscapes, not one imported rainforest

- **Native Hawaiian dry forest and stream corridors** are restored in naturally dry areas (including wiliwili and site-specific companions selected by native practitioners); reduce invasive grass fire fuel and erosion; no arbitrary forest irrigation requirement.
- **Regenerative agroforestry/farms** mix locally appropriate productive systems with soil tests and agricultural land-use law; compost/biochar only when safe and agronomically justified, no unverified recycled polymer or contaminants added to soil.
- **Curated tropical botanical rainforest attraction** is a limited 5–10-acre enclosed/contained or tightly managed microclimate garden inside the 60-acre botanical allocation. Exotic flowers must pass Hawaiʻi weed-risk and pest assessment; strict containment and invasive-species eradication plans, no imported predators or wildlife releases. Native predation is not an ecological defect.

Design rain capture, smart irrigation, reclaimed-water quality segregation and rainwater-compatible planting. **Drinking water for residents, stream rights and productive agriculture outrank ornamental rainforest water.** Botanical water volumes remain TBD pending evapotranspiration modeling, soils, seasonal and peak-day climate and legally available tertiary reuse. Do not assume RO water is economical for tropical gardens.

Water quality and landscape permits: https://mapps.mauicounty.gov/238/WWRD-Recycled-Water-Review---Regular ; native wiliwili reference https://dlnr.hawaii.gov/forestry/plants/wiliwili/ .

### 3.2 Community and tenure

Target **500 long-term affordable homes** as the comparative 613-acre concept only; study **100/250/500** homes against actual legal land and service capacity. Native Hawaiian residents and displaced/local households help lead governance; any beneficiary prioritization must follow applicable program law and fair housing rules. No invented DHHL entitlement. Separate residential leases/ownership from nursery, farm or garden employment contracts. Native Hawaiian-controlled stewardship seats; public reports on rent, jobs, habitats, water, and project costs. Emphasize apprenticeships and adult horticulture trades. Provide disability access, cooling, shade, storm resilience, community childcare/meeting spaces and affordable local food.

### 3.3 Printed and upcycled construction: measured savings only

Screen conventional **$450k**, hybrid **$360k** and optimized printed **$300k** all-in home *construction scenario inputs*; actual homes vary drastically by floor plan, utilities, island mobilization, hazards and approved materials. At 500 units, housing-only scenarios cost **$225M / $180M / $150M**: the apparent **$45M / $75M** potential savings are hypothetical and **not land or campus savings**. A formwork savings percentage must not be applied indiscriminately to roofs, utilities, foundation or financing.

Prioritize independently qualified locally recovered mineral aggregates in code-approved printing mixes. Recycled EPS/SurfBlock could be a **separate, already local recycled-material building alternative**, not silently treated as 3D-print concrete feedstock; validate fire, hurricane, corrosion, moisture, structural and insulation performance with local licensed professionals. Hawaiʻi and Maui already have examples of recycled surfboard EPS housing and factory-built home policy pathways.

Sources: https://www.hawaiipublicradio.org/local-news/2025-04-18/homes-made-from-recycled-surfboard-scraps-maui-housing-solution ; https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/18836 .

### 3.4 Food-truck and public realm integration

Model **six** locally owned/trained food trucks with legitimate power/water/wastewater/service connections and accessible shaded seating adjacent to visitor garden entry or lawful village market frontage. Example: 6 × $1,000 daily gross sales × 300 trading days = **$1.8M annual SALES TO VENDORS**; hypothetical 5% common-area fee = $90,000 gross campus receipts **before site operating costs**. Never equate vendor sales with project profit. Do not assume automatic vendor permission in Launiupoko Beach Park or agricultural zoning. Preserve surfing and public beach parking. Add affordable resident meals, locally sourced produce and plastic-free refill points.

## 4. Attractive BioSolar water plant: 1-MGD regional pilot with true ZLD

**Siting hierarchy:** use previously disturbed, **appropriately zoned inland** site(s) only if parcels/rights and engineering permit. Keep process and hazardous areas well separated from homes; design sound/odor shielding, fire access, evacuation, secure and ventilated equipment buildings, green roofs and planted berms, educational public frontage, separate restricted utility entrance. Never sacrifice hazardous materials separation or emergency venting for greenery. Coastline access is a **screening advantage**, not permission for an ocean intake, well, outfall or pipeline.

### 4.1 Physical mass balance, not fictional products

**Illustrative at 1.0 MGD *nameplate finished reverse-osmosis water*:**
- 45% initial RO water recovery -> **2.222 MGD** seawater feed, **1.222 MGD** RO concentrate (**~8,412 m³/day** feed; **~4,627 m³/day** concentrate).
- At **35 kg dissolved salts/m³** feed -> **~294.4 tonnes/day** theoretical feed dissolved salts at nameplate. At 85% duty: **~91,340 tonnes/year** feed salts. These are not saleable salt tonnages. Adjust for salts retained in product, process additives, residual water and actual feed chemistry.
- ZLD concentrators/crystallizers potentially return further condensate with treatment; **do not add this twice** to the 1.0 MGD initial RO output without defining full final recovery. Every residual solid must have a lawful tested disposition. **No routine liquid brine outfall** in the proposed design. 
- Product ledger: potable water and nonpotable purified flows only by permit; sodium/chloride, magnesium, calcium+sulfate (gypsum *OR* carbonate), potassium, bromine, boron, strontium, lithium and trace ions each assayed, balanced, separated and certified individually. Purity, reagents, unit energy, stockpiling costs, residue contamination, qualified local purchase substitution, off-island logistics and binding offtake define whether revenue is zero or positive.
- **Deuterium** inventory is mainly in the **water molecules**, not recoverable from salt crystals as an ordinary element. A theoretical whole-feed elemental-D inventory is of order **0.3 tonne/day at 1-MGD RO nameplate**, before any real isotope recovery. A heavy-water product requires a distinct isotope enrichment technology, high-specificity QA and an evidenced market; no commercial D/D₂O revenue or energy-production credit in baseline. Theoretical elements in raw seawater are not realized product or avoided imports.
- REEF RULE: no routine concentrate disposal to Olowalu reef; ZLD chemistry + residual solids + source intake must still undergo rigorous life-cycle marine/ecotoxicology review. Non-routine/emergency discharge cannot be presumed authorized.

### 4.2 Energy exposes a major engineering constraint

Assume **85% plant service factor**, 3.5 kWh/m³ finished RO and 10 kWh/m³ RO concentrate for ZLD processing (stress-test assumption; NOT a Maui vendor performance guarantee):
- RO annual electricity **~4.11 GWh**.
- ZLD processing **~14.35 GWh**.
- **RO + ZLD = ~18.46 GWh/year**, excluding chemical/mineral processing, pumps, homes, community operations, and replacement reserves.
- At $0.25/kWh scenario avoided energy value, **~$4.62M/year** electricity cost alone before demand charges (not a tariff quote).
- An illustrative **8 MWp BioSolar** array at **1,600 kWh/kWp-year** gives **12.80 GWh/year**; target **1.5% gain** adds **0.192 GWh/year**, giving ~**12.99 GWh/year** before losses and hourly matching. **This still falls ~5.47 GWh/year short of JUST the illustrative RO + ZLD electricity demand** even on an annual bookkeeping basis. Any sustainable power strategy needs additional lawful firm supply, efficiency, onsite generation, demand shaping, potentially shared municipal storage and independently verified 8,760-hour dispatch.
- Biomass/cooling/plantings do **not** substitute for firm 24/7 electrical supply. Vegetation/PV gain sensitivity: **0%, 1%, 2%** in annual yield; temperature, shading, water, labor and incremental cost must be measured.

The county's 2024 study identified **Honokōwai and Honolua** as favorable West Maui treatment locations, not Olowalu as the selected site. Its early desal facility estimates include **$119M / 0.5 MGD brackish**, **$261M / 2 MGD expanded brackish**, and **$328M / 4 MGD saltwater-quality** concepts, with distinct scopes and contingencies; **these do not establish a cost for our ZLD/brine-mining botanical campus**. Compare cost and source quality against these county-screened sites rather than treating scenic Olowalu as the most efficient treatment location.

Primary study: https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/154964/Maui-Desalination-Feasibility-Study-ES-2024 ; prior ORCS mineral balance: [v0.8 full material balance](maui-kapalua-full-recovery-minerals-v0.8-2026-10-15.md).

### 4.3 Distributed West Maui supply versus centralized ZLD

Keep the prior West Maui **6 MGD** planning target as a **network-wide illustrative capacity**, not six MGD of local permitted demand. Compare:
A) one 6-MGD campus;
B) decentralized **1 + 2 + 3 MGD** (Olowalu/Launiupoko proposal, Lahaina and north West Maui study areas);
C) decentralized local water production with some **shared certified mineral-processing/ZLD logistics** without ocean concentrate discharge.

Simulate actual elevation/pumping/head losses, pipeline route length and rights, purchased power, source interference, water-quality variability, shared maintenance, skilled shifts, hazardous material trucking, availability of N-1 spare equipment, firm power and mineral offtake. Distributed production may lower transmission mains but duplicate small-plant CAPEX, thermal crystallizers and staff. **Do not count avoided pipeline CAPEX twice against the 1,014-mile county replacement benchmark or deduct from water rates without a finance ledger.**

## 5. Proposed **mandatory** Lahaina Living Roofs + Battery + Microgrid Standard

**Proposal, not currently adopted law:** every eligible **new** Lahaina Town building must demonstrate integrated solar generation, cooling/green landscape, safe critical-load backup and microgrid readiness at final building inspection. Make this a **performance-based ordinance** with project-type tables and a lawful, predictable public financing mechanism. The 1–2% BioSolar annual generation improvement is an **R&D measurement objective**, not an automatic pass/fail requirement or achieved efficiency gain.

| New-build component | Proposed mandatory performance requirement |
|---|---|
| Rooftop/on-site PV | Install site-appropriate solar, not merely reserve roof area. Allow equivalent canopy/community-connected solar where historic, wind, fire or structural constraints preclude rooftop hardware. |
| **True** BioSolar | Engineer plant/PV co-design for cooling, rain harvest and botanical cohesion; rooftop plantings only on safe, moisture-robust, **Class A fire-rated** assemblies. Alternative at-grade courtyard/solar canopy landscaping where appropriate. Exclude vines/debris touching wiring or obstructing ventilation. |
| Storage | Include listed, approved battery systems for critical circuits, emergency lighting, communications and required refrigeration/medical-power needs; chemistry/size based on measured critical loads, not a single unreviewed capacity mandate. |
| Microgrid-ready | Utility-compliant transfer/islanding, protection and segmentation, accessible disconnects, communications/cybersecurity and designated common interconnection pathways; connection to a shared microgrid is conditional on operator/PUC/utility contracts. |
| Water and heat | Rainwater design where allowed, passive shading, high-performance envelopes, storm drainage/erosion controls, water-use efficiency and nonpotable water routing if legal. |
| Heritage / affordability | Historic-preservation-compatible assemblies or equivalent off-roof site systems; fully funded or financeable paths for disaster-affected households, no displacement, emergency rebuild grandfathering/transition rules adopted transparently. |

**Suggested ordinance intent (draft discussion text, not legal advice):**

> Subject to adoption by the appropriate authorities and applicable State law, each eligible newly constructed building within the designated Lahaina rebuilding area shall meet the adopted BioSolar generation, vegetation-integrated cooling, fire-safe onsite critical-load storage, and microgrid-interconnection performance standards. Equivalent compliance pathways shall be provided where on-roof installation is unsafe, incompatible with protected historic fabric, or technically infeasible. Administration shall include grant-backed compliance for eligible rebuilding households and a transparent appeals process without interrupting essential home recovery.

**Why legal/exemption paths are essential even for a mandatory standard:** battery fire code and Class A roofing, hurricane wind loading, heritage view corridors, roof-access safety, tiny/covered roof geometry, affordability and existing lawful permits can conflict. Mandatory *outcome* need not mean universally identical plant-covered roofs. Subsidies must actually be funded, not assumed.

**Current grid rule constraint:** Hawaiian Electric's Phase 1 Rule 30 generally excludes Maui hybrid microgrids **over 1 MW peak demand** and nonemergency island mode; pursue approved small customer microgrids or other authorized arrangements. A district-scale 4-MWp PV installation does not directly equal 4 MW *peak demand*; separate load and interconnection studies.

Utility source: https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/about-us/our-vision-and-commitment/resilience/microgrid-services-tariff ; earlier ORCS microgrid [v0.4 paper](maui-circular-ocean-front-street-v0.4-2026-10-15.md) and [v0.4 program](maui_circular_ocean_microgrid_v04.py).

## 6. 72-hour resilience: preserve the previous v0.4 design rule

At **0.4 MW continuously critical load**, 72 hours without useful sun -> **28.8 MWh actually delivered**. With an illustrative **80% effective delivery factor**, require **36 MWh rated storage**, plus inverter power sizing, degradation reserve, thermal/fire protection, appropriate placement and a field-tested dispatch and black-start process. This is a campus/district reference case, **not** the storage prescription for a single residence or full-time desalination.

For the proposed 500 homes, a simple rooftop illustration **500 × 7 kW = 3.5 MWp**, 1,600 annual kWh/kWp -> **5.60 GWh/year** residential PV, with 1.5% BioSolar hypothesis -> **0.084 GWh/year incremental**. These values do **not** demonstrate backup, grid independence or self-consumption. Actual units may have different roof geometry and apartment/duplex shared systems. The illustrated **8-MWp campus PV** and **3.5-MWp housing PV** are separate generation assets, never counted twice.

Prior v0.4 72-hour outage analysis specifically establishes why annual surplus cannot replace 8,760-hour resilient dispatch. Firmware and interconnection must be safety-approved, with OT/SCADA human-verified controls and offline-capable operations.

## 7. Coastal restoration and Launiupoko public surf access

At Launiupoko Beach and along selected Olowalu catchments, prioritize **source interception** (runoff, storm drains, debris capture, wastewater and agricultural sediment controls) before attempting to filter microplastic from open surf. Small floating ORCS/Polygone-style *research* units may be evaluated in a controlled, authorized location only after ecological sampling, seasonal current/swell/rip assessment, marine-life entanglement screening, navigational/surf access risk, mooring and cultural rights/permit review, independent microplastic capture mass balance and removal/maintenance strategy. **No floating device deployment at the popular beach is approved or recommended without those tests.** Do not claim the devices remove dissolved contaminants or generate quantifiable reef savings until actually measured.

Track upstream sediment and nutrient mass, polymer particle counts and sizes, O&M recovered kg/yr, false catches and energy demand. Clean ocean food-web and surf habitats are more valuable than a visually dramatic machine with no independently verified effectiveness.

## 8. Comparative economics and financing (not a developer pro forma)

**Housing-only at 500 units:**
- Conventional $450k/unit -> **$225M**; hybrid $360k -> **$180M**; optimized printing $300k -> **$150M**. No savings should be called verified.
- At $900/month average gross rent and 500 fully occupied homes -> **$5.4M/year** gross, before turnover, management, maintenance, taxes, utilities, vacancies, debt service, insurance, reserves and public facilities. That does not finance a $150M–$225M housing program on its own.
- Botanical forest/garden establishment, seawater works/ZLD/refining, roads, housing utilities, power, storage, land/rights, public buildings and long-run OPEX require **separate non-overlapping** capital and cash ledgers. The County's $119M–$328M desal references are not applicable ready-made ZLD quotes.
- Mineral revenue **$0 base case** until actual feed assays, separation yields, process costs, purity specs, logistics, demand and binding purchase agreements.
- Do not treat community labor, carbon credits, tourism tax, emergency grants, financing, land availability or ecosystem benefits as realized project cash without documented legal agreements.
- **2026-era Hawaiʻi construction inflation and skilled-trades scarcity** merit contingency; seek independent quantity surveys and EPC/architectural bids before publishing a single total project capital estimate.
- Existing ORCS islandwide v0.9 enterprise analysis has very large modeled water-network financing gaps; **this new village is an additional scenario, not already financed by those water line items**.

Offer enforceable right-to-remain protections, tenant/ownership pathways, rent affordability covenants and local vendor access. ## 10. Implementation gates, 30-year performance and decision

**G0 — Land/right to propose (0–6 months theoretical):** GIS-to-TMK cadastral reconciliation, landowner invitations, community-design authority and cultural constraints; if no legally developable appropriately situated land, do not advance construction.

**G1 — Baseline evidence (6–18 months theoretical):** soils, reef, hydrology, shoreline setback, 100-year flood/tsunami, wildfire modeling, native flora and invasive status, source seawater/saline aquifer assays, landfill-adjacent site tests, legal water allocations, resident demographics and verified housing needs.

**G2 — Pilot (1–3 years only if approvals):** native dry-forest restoration, nursery, controlled solar-with-understory A/B trial measuring 0/1/2% uplift, legal food-truck pads, 3D-print nonstructural/qualified structural test coupons, small-scale ZLD/mineral process R&D in a suitable existing zoned facility. No automatic 1MGD civil construction or housing subdivision before approval.

**G3 — Comparative procurement:** stress-test one versus three West Maui plants, full ZLD and possible shared minerals, source intake/reef impacts, 8,760-hour microgrid dispatch, N-1 firm supply, 72-hour outage and 30-year PV/battery replacement, workforce capacity and local/off-island materials costs. Get actual quotes.

**G4 — Phased construction only after gates:** first residents/public services and ecological protection, then legally approved treatment/mineral/visitor facilities. Record annual realized credit only after audited metering/operational evidence.

**30-year metrics:** legally secure affordable households, monthly household total water/energy/housing burden, residents' job retention, community-controlled income, native canopy survival, acres high-quality habitat protected, measured sediment/nutrient/microplastic interception, kg of certified mineral product sold locally vs rejects, measured marine ecological indicators, islanded hours at minimum emergency-critical load, ZLD residue mass, energy kWh/m³, water source quality, and total public subsidy/lifecycle cash balance.

### Decision recommendation

**Recommend** a **community-led land and ecological opportunity assessment** plus a *non-binding* **613-acre planning-area distribution scenario**. Pursue distributed beautiful BioSolar + full ZLD as a **technically challenging alternative**, not an assumed utility efficiency improvement. Seek a **mandatory, performance-based, fully financed** new-construction Lahaina BioSolar/battery/microgrid standard via proper legislative process, with safe equivalencies. No investor/developer ownership or permit should be inferred. Preserve public beach access, archaeology, native ecological integrity and residents' water access over build-out.
