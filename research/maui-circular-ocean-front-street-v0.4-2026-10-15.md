## Executive decision brief

**Resident quality of life and ocean recovery are the outputs; local water, energy, materials, agriculture, housing and value-added island trade are the instruments.** Propose a community-governed, phased Maui network:

- **Front Street:** rebuilt historic-compatible commercial district using biosolar shade, neighborhood batteries, fire/flood-safe infrastructure, high-efficiency fixtures, reclaimed nonpotable service where lawful, district-level water and energy accounting, and appropriate building-scale renewable systems. Design for near-zero **annual purchased** electricity where feasible, not false promises of permanently zero utility bills or off-grid security.
- **West Maui water:** maximize safe, beneficial wastewater reuse; benchmark seawater/saline-well/brackish desalination; evaluate zero liquid discharge (ZLD) as a marine-protection requirement **subject to full lifecycle analysis and legal, ecological alternatives**. Add nutrient and microplastic interception before seawater receives runoff.
- **Central Maui circular manufacturing:** appropriately sized and landscaped sorting, vehicle depollution, aluminum/steel/plastic/glass/C&D reclamation, Surf Block (existing ICCF) evaluation, **code-approved** 3D-printed buildings and nonstructural products, and a limited inference node only with measured added value.
- **Agriculture and fertilizer:** capture N/P/K and suitable organics from wastewater, responsibly collected manure, agricultural residues, and recovered minerals; match agronomic needs and water rights. Trial gypsum only on soils that actually benefit.
- **Olowalu coast:** use independent water/soil sampling data to verify restoration and coastal health; no speculative reuse of already relocated, possibly hazardous wildfire ash.

**No unilateral mandate, entitlement to philanthropic funds, disposal permit, structural certification, contracted offtake or community endorsement is asserted.** Community-centered design includes Native Hawaiian cultural, water-rights, coastal and historic stewardship.

## 1. Important fresh corrections (October 2026)

1. **Olowalu TDS is NOT an active inventory of accessible Lahaina debris.** The state says the site received >380,000 U.S. short tons of fire ash/debris in 2024–25; transfer to the permanent Central Maui site ran June–November 2025; the County announced closure and site restoration January 6, 2026. Roughly 400,000 tons cleared is a historical handling statistic, **not** prospective recoverable building feedstock. The DOH reports its monitoring found no harmful offsite concentrations at levels of health concern in tests conducted through late 2025. Continue transparent long-term monitoring, particularly near Olowalu reef.
2. **West Maui recycling has a funded collection anchor:** EPA selected Maui County for a **$3,607,065** SWIFR grant, announced August 27, 2026 for a planned West Maui recycling collection center and estimated **1,794 tons/year** diversion. This is prospective capacity, not operational feedstock or permission to redirect grant money to manufacturing.
3. **County Tow & Scrap has a free qualifying path** for titled conventional residential cars, restricted by weight, paperwork and annual count. County instructions warn that nonqualifying vehicles may incur fees; hybrids/EVs are excluded as of February 2026. Local resident reports of **$500–1,000** private disposal costs merit dealer quote audits but are not verified universal charges.
4. **Recycling accessibility is constrained:** County curbside residential service handles refuse; official updates describe dropoff facilities, Aloha Recycling's temporary Kahului facility, and the June 30, 2026 closure of the UHMC dropoff center. Avoid saying there is no recycling or just one facility.
5. **Maui Strong Fund:** HCF displays **$212.42372M donations including interest as of December 26, 2025**, and **$187.43489M awarded or committed as of July 28, 2026**. Different dates and commitments preclude treating ~$25M as cash available; a historical **$50M interim-housing pledge** was an allocation, NOT a free $50M microgrid pool.
6. **Dongjiakou cost comparator is real but limited:** Shandong water agency reports original Qingdao Dongjiakou UF/RO plant at **¥450M** for **100,000 m³/day** and reported 2.2 kWh/m³ electricity intensity. At the approximate period exchange rate this is ~$67M, **2010s Chinese desalination CAPEX**, not contemporary Maui cost, ZLD, mineral, or compute proof. Other Chinese accounts report ¥650–800M for alternate phases/scopes; preserve scope and date uncertainty.
7. **Kahului Harbor:** Hawaii DOT's FY2024 cargo statistics reported **1.97M U.S. short tons total throughput**; this mixes inbound/outbound classes and is not a commodity import ledger.
8. **USGS West Maui data:** nutrient-enriched, low-pH groundwater at Kahekili reef amplifies coral stress; reef pH/alkalinity depends on groundwater, carbon chemistry, reef metabolism and time of day. Base any remediation on site-specific carbonate chemistry and reducing land-based pollution, **not indiscriminate ocean alkalinity dosing**.
9. **Maui microgrid tariff:** Hawaiian Electric's current Phase 1 Rule 30 does not cover hybrid island-of-Maui microgrids **over 1 MW peak** or routine islanding outside emergencies. Larger shared Front Street microgrids need formal Hawaiian Electric/PUC engineering and tariff route; a 4 MW DC solar array is **not by itself** proof that the hybrid microgrid's regulated peak exceeds 1 MW, but likely warrants a detailed interconnection review.

## 2. Ocean-first governance and measurable gates

The preferred policy for newly proposed water treatment is **no untreated discharge, documented full salt/contaminant accountability, strong preference for ZLD where technically and environmentally feasible**, and permit/monitoring requirements sufficient to safeguard reefs. The ideal is a **closed-loop materials process**, not shifting ocean pollution into uncontained inland salt piles. If ZLD's extra fossil energy, solid waste, or chemicals create greater harm, compare cleaner alternatives transparently rather than claiming absolute ecological benefit.

Before any plant: measure seawater and groundwater salinity, temperature, pH (on total scale where appropriate), total alkalinity, dissolved inorganic carbon, aragonite saturation, nutrients, pathogens, metals, microplastics, coral and benthic condition, runoff and subsurface connectivity. Use controls, seasonality, time-of-day replication and independent sampling.

**Priority output metrics:** net pollutants prevented reaching reefs (kg/year, by type and analytical limit); safe water delivered; wastewater nutrients removed/beneficially reused; resident affordability; days of emergency service; unpermitted dumping reduction; local housing rebuilt; locally retained material value; rural/riparian soil and habitat improvement. **No fabricated monetized valuation of whales, reefs or scenic beauty.**

A new plant, especially seawater intake and marine outfall, fails until demonstrated coastal compatibility. Active mineral-recovery process wastewater itself must also satisfy zero/low effluent requirements.

Scientific benchmarks: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/cmhrp/news/polluted-groundwater-threatens-hawaiian-coral-reefs and https://www.nature.com/articles/s44359-025-00036-2

## 3. Front Street: physically realistic 'self-sufficient' district

Design a restored **historic Lahaina** streetscape, not a generic tech park. Rooftop PV set back from visible edges, solar shade where appropriate, accessible native shade trees, nonflammable landscape buffers, passive ventilation/filtration, flood-smart switchgear, limited shared water infrastructure, local cooling efficiency, service corridors, battery resilience and islanding capability subject to permits and utility rights. Screen all inverter and storage elements.

**Example energy budget (scenario, no utility bills yet):**
- 100 mixed-use businesses × **40 MWh/year** = **4.00 GWh/year** (illustrative commercial mix, not metered demand).
- Local **0.20 MGD** treated-water plant × **3.0 kWh/m³** = **0.829 GWh/year** (excluding distribution; consider reclaimed irrigation water first).
- Optional 50 kW average IT compute at PUE 1.2 = **0.526 GWh/year**.
- Shared lighting/pumps etc = **0.300 GWh/year**.
- **Total modeled load ~5.655 GWh/year**.
- **4 MWp** properly sited biosolar × assumed **1,600 kWh/kWp/year** = **6.400 GWh/year** nominal solar energy before hour-by-hour curtailment, storage losses and grid import/export restrictions.
- Annual generation > annual usage is **NOT** proof of zero grid purchases, outage survival, zero bills, deliverable space or legal project feasibility. Need 8,760-hour dispatch, shade/roof/land area, tropical corrosion, storms and financing.
- A **0.4 MW emergency-critical load** for **72 sunless hours** needs **28.8 MWh usable** or **36 MWh battery nameplate** if effective delivery fraction is 80%; distributed topology, backup fuels and safety may change this.
- At illustrative solar CAPEX **$2–4/W DC**, 4 MWp would cost **$8–16M** before site-specific upgrades; at illustrative **$350–650/kWh installed**, 36 MWh battery nameplate costs **$12.6–23.4M**. These are **stress-test price inputs**, not Maui bids. Distribution engineering, community facilities, interconnection, water, undergrounding, land, financing and O&M extra.
- Annual delivered cost to businesses must include capital amortization, insurance, O&M, reserve replacement, subscribed load and utility fixed charges; ratepayers may get **lower net bills** without necessarily zero cash outflow.

**Design alternative:** many smaller certified building microgrids plus a district resilience hub may be faster than a single shared tariff-constrained system. Compare both with an explicit equity rule serving resident-owned businesses.

## 4. Central Maui biosolar materials campus — right-sized sizing

**The existing 1,794 tons/year West Maui *planned* collection center alone does not justify a 15,000-ton/year industrial campus.** Island sourcing requires signed private/recycler/municipal routing agreements and segregation receipts. Start with **modular throughput choices** 5,000 / 15,000 / 30,000 short tons/year, not an assumed one-size-fits-all facility.

**Default 15k scenario:** 300 operating days/year → **50 short tons/day** incoming; at 10-hour shifts **5 tons/hour average intake**; allow downtime, seasonal peaks and load margins. Feedstock slate **ILLUSTRATIVE only**: 3k polymers (clean PET, HDPE, EPS separately), 6k depolluted metals from documented recyclers/vehicles, 6k *non-wildfire, independently tested* construction/glass/mineral fraction. Actual collection contracts determine mix. Do **not** classify historical Olowalu debris as today's feedstock.

**Recovery is not identical to local manufacturing:** model 60% total recovered, i.e. **9,000 t/year** at 15k throughput as a SCENARIO. Separately assign part of that 9k for code-verified local use and the rest for optimized export to established recyclers. Include contamination, reject transport, sorting, washwater treatment, dryer heat, microplastic containment, metals remelting energy, alloy and print-ink qualification, free hazardous battery service, tire and end-of-life polymer pathways.

**Capital stress-test** for the **entire** hypothetical 15k facility: **$15M, $25M, $40M**, not vendor quotes or approved plans. Annual OPEX **$175 per incoming ton** → **$2.625M/year**; 20-year annuity at 7% gives CRF **0.094393** and annualized total before material sales/import substitution, permitting and environmental residuals:
- $15M → **$4.041M/year**, **$269/t intake**.
- $25M → **$4.985M/year**, **$332/t intake**.
- $40M → **$6.401M/year**, **$427/t intake**.
These are minimum required **net benefits per input ton** under assumptions and exclude some system boundary costs. A $25M campus with a verified **$250/t** net avoided disposal/import expense and product contribution receives $3.75M/year, **~$1.235M/year gap** before additional items. At **$400/t** it would yield $6M/year and about **$1.015M/year** illustrative surplus. Never double count (scrap export + avoided virgin purchase + recycled product sale) for the same material.

**Site amenities and biosolar:** native drought-tolerant landscaping, covered equipment, wind/dust and sound barriers, rooftop/canopy PV with battery, clean stormwater, closed-loop polymer washing, microplastic filters, coastal/road runoff capture, visually appealing public-facing community education and safe dropoff zones. Heavy processing should remain off the historic waterfront, away from sensitive coastlines, in appropriately zoned Central Maui.

**Materials product shortlist**: Surf Block manufacturer-supported ICCF forms; code-certified concrete printed panels or limited structural walls with professionally tested recycled fines; nonstructural weather-resistant printed shade/furniture; aluminum sheet/extrusions after alloy QA; salvaged building components; agricultural silicate/gypsum products only at required purity. Steel/metal reinforcement is a separate engineered component, not a reason to add random metal fragments to a polymer concrete.

## 6. Mineral and wastewater mass/energy portfolio — ocean-first

Baseline open-seawater **research comparator** from v0.3: **5.0 MGD** freshwater; 45% RO recovery; **42,060 m³/day** incoming seawater; **23,133 m³/day** concentrate; **~24.18 GWh/year RO electric energy** at 3.5 kWh/m³. **~534k metric tonnes/year** dissolved salt in concentrate; **no revenue or claim of recoverable clean minerals**.

Selectively investigate NaCl, Mg, Ca/gypsum, K, Br, B, Sr; trace lithium and deuterium only as research. Measure actual feed chemistry; use ion/charge/material closures and allocate every mole *once* among products, effluents and solids. Mineral products require purity, buyer, shipment weight, island offtake, competing imports and post-separation environmental treatment.

**ZLD scenario**: additional electricity at 5 / 10 / 20 kWh per m³ of the original 23,133 m³/day concentrate would be **42.2 / 84.4 / 168.9 GWh/year** respectively, on top of RO. These are **deliberate stress-test assumptions**, not equipment curves. Feed salinity, heat integration, evaporation/crystallization, scale and disposal determine true costs. Lowering brine volume through ion-selective processing can change this significantly. Model solid products/residues custody and no contaminated salt land dumping.

**Agricultural loop**: 4.2 MGD Lahaina wastewater processed / 1.7 MGD R1 reported in Nov 2025 → hypothetical additional **2.5 MGD R1 production capacity**, up to ~912.5 million gal/year **only if** treatment upgrades, demand, conveyance and seasonality allow. Some reclaimed water can be used for permissible agricultural irrigation; no unrestricted drinking-water classification. Recovered nitrogen/phosphorus from source-separated wastewater, livestock manure, food waste, green waste and ag runoff need sampling and achievable capture fractions, safe sanitation and nutrient-budget controls.

Fertilizer replacement compares:
**imported delivered equivalent nutrient cost** − **all-in recovered nutrient cost**; compare N, P2O5/P, K2O/K consistently; never add elemental nutrient and formulated fertilizer mass twice. Interisland trade goes through Kahului freight, stewardship and islands' own demand/reserves first, then surplus export.

**Specific requirement:** no assumed bulk gypsum recovery from Maui lands unless soil tests demonstrate sodicity/calcium/sulfate deficiency and the process makes chloride-safe gypsum. Potential >27k t/y calcium-limited theoretical gypsum from default seawater feed is an **upper bound** only.

## 7. Ocean chemistry and reef integrity

USGS studies off Kahekili measured low pH and enriched nitrate in groundwater-fed reef sites, sometimes ~50-fold normal nitrate. Monitor pH, total alkalinity, dissolved inorganic carbon, carbonate saturation, nitrates, phosphates, pollutants, microplastic polymer species and particle size, turbidity and coral biomass with upstream/downstream control sites. Decrease land-sourced pollutant loads before considering experimental engineered ocean alkalinity enhancement. Natural buffering is not evidence that adding untested alkaline solids near reefs is safe. No brine or 'mineral correction' discharge without ecological review and permits.

At Olowalu, use documented TDS restoration and DOH monitoring rather than asserting debris remains; assess long-term reef indicators and storm runoff sensitively because Olowalu coral habitat is valuable.

## 10. Scenario formulas, audit trail and research gates

See [v0.4 standalone calculator](maui_circular_ocean_microgrid_v04.py) for adjustable scenarios. Scope guards:

**Annualized processing break-even per incoming ton** =
(CAPEX × CRF + annual OPEX + additional environmentally required residual costs − verified net operating contributions) / actual weighted throughput.

**Annual net energy balance** = PV production − metered load; separately track 8,760-hour imports, exports, unserved energy and battery dispatch. A nonnegative annual energy balance is **not** 72-hour resilience.

**Marine-first liquid release** = measured post-treatment discharge by chemistry/species and location, not just claimed 0 m³; dry solid residuals must be monitored as diligently as liquid discharges.

**Land/water quality** = control-impact time series, groundwater, fish/coral, freshwater health; carbon/pH cannot be evaluated from one instantaneous number.

**Evidence states** = MEASURED / OFFICIAL_REPORTED / DERIVED / SCENARIO / PROPOSED / QUARANTINED.
