**Scope:** Maui Island first; Maui County resident count used as an imperfect, explicitly flagged demand proxy. This document distinguishes (a) measured local government/industry evidence, (b) assumptions, (c) simulated outputs, and (d) planned technical/commercial work.

## Executive synopsis
1. Three proposed coastal integrated potable desalination, sewage/reuse/zero-routine-liquid-discharge, useful-mineral recovery and AI-inference campuses: West **6 MGD**, North **5 MGD**, Central **17 MGD**, total **28 MGD nameplate**. Their separately modeled 85%-dependable output is **5.10/4.25/14.45=23.80 MGD**. The historic **22.5 MGD county DWS household** benchmark is not a verified current Maui Island residential demand or emergency N-1 service guarantee. Resident/essential water prioritized over resorts; wastewater capture, cesspool replacement and nutrient recovery linked to irrigation and manufacturing.
2. Existing national consumption extrapolation yields **~10.176 million gallons/year all-format bottled-water consumption**, with **~7.123 million gallons** single-serve PET and **~53.93 million 500-mL-equivalent units** (not actual SKU counts). This uses 2025 Maui County population **160,592**, 2025 Maui Island visitor spend **$5.97B**, average visitor spending **$304.98/person/day**, and preliminary 2025 US 47.5 gal/person-year, 70% single-serve PET share. It is NOT actual sales. It combines Maui County residents (including Molokaʻi/Lānaʻi) with Maui Island visitors and requires corrected Maui-Island-only census / point-of-sale data.
3. **Import fraction 80% is ASSUMED, NOT VERIFIED.** The resulting potential import-displacement volume is ~43.14 million half-liter equivalents/year, 21.57 million liters (5.699 million gallons) of water product, plus ~647 tonnes of PET at 15g/unit if same bottle weights. Full incoming/outgoing transport avoided depends on net counterflows of resin, reprocessing equipment, exports and returned waste; these masses are not equivalent to known freight invoices. All island mass-balance benefits subject to life-cycle assessment.
4. At a **hypothetical $0.75/bottle blended wholesale** delivered price and **target 30% net-of-all-expenses margin**, replacing 25/50/75/100% of this *assumed imported portion* yields gross sales ~$8.09/$16.18/$24.27/$32.36M annually and hypothetical net profits ~$2.43/$4.85/$7.28/$9.71M. The 30% is NOT historical industry average. At 30% net the **fully loaded per-bottle cost ceiling is $0.525** for $0.75 price and includes all operating, allocated capital depreciation/finance and taxes. Profit targets must be tested with supplier/distributor contracts, realistic local labor and rPET costs and capital financing; the program may intentionally operate at break-even or loss for independently valued resilience, jobs and waste outcomes.
5. **Primo Brands official 2025 consolidated results**, which cover a broad large company and merger costs, reported $6.664B sales, $2.020B gross profit (**30.3% gross**), $430.4M operating income (**6.5% operating**), and $80.4M after-tax continuing-operation net income (**1.2% net**), with adjusted EBITDA margin 21.7%. These are *one corporate comparator*, not an established sector-wide net mean and not Maui profit. https://ir.primobrands.com/press-releases/news-details/2026/Primo-Brands-Reports-2025-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Results/default.aspx ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2042694/000162828026012779/prmb-20251231.htm .
6. Disaster water security is *a separate deliverable* from sales revenue. Distributed generation, microgrids, reservoir storage, fire-resilient routes, sectionalized pipes, contamination isolation, interties and emergency bottled/bulk fill must preserve potable service when one plant/power feed/community corridor goes down. Aboveground lines can themselves be at heightened fire and wind risk and must be screened against protected buried crossing alternatives. Do not advertise island-wide N-1 resilience without verified hydraulic design and adequate alternative sourcing.

## II. Transparent demand and imported-water calculation
Maui County resident person-years 160,592; Maui Island visitor person-years ($5.97B/$304.98)/365 = 53,630.3. Combined model 214,222.3 person-years.
All bottled water = 214,222.3 ×47.5 gal/person-year = **10.176M gal/year** (ALL container types, not all imported).
Single-serve PET=70%=**7.123M gal/year**. Converting 500mL: 1 U.S. gallon=3.785411784L, thus **53.926M 500mL-equivalent units/year**. It is misleading to call these actual 500mL bottles if large bottles also sold.
Imported fraction scenarios **60%,80%,100%** of modeled PET demand (rather than a fabricated official import statistic) = 32.36, 43.14, 53.93M units per year respectively. Nonimported share includes Hawaiian producers and potentially island suppliers; actual Maui-island imported share unknown.
Baseline **80% imported** × 75% capture = **32.356M 500mL equivalents sold by Maui producer**, ~$24.267M annual sales at $0.75 wholesale, and **$7.280M annual net at 30%**. That displaces ~16.178 million liters / 4.274 million gallons of previously imported PET-bottle water-equivalent, plus ~485 metric tonnes PET bottle material at 15g/unit. If the county uses imported resin, do not count bottle resin mass as locally produced.
Annual POTABLE water demand for the *entire* 53.93M-equivalent single-serve market is 7.123M gallons/year /365 = **0.0195 MGD**, much less than the 28-MGD proposed desal nameplate; still require sanitation/process losses and priority override during shortage. Product bottling cannot use sewage-derived reclaim as direct potable feed without an independently licensed and demonstrated advanced potable reuse scheme.

## III. Wholesale margins and the two-price economy
**30% NET margin on SALES** means revenue minus fully allocated costs (opex, depreciation, interest, tax and prudent reserve accounting) = 0.30×sales. This is stricter than 30% gross margin or 30% markup on cost.
| $/500mL wholesale | Fully loaded maximum cost/unit for 30% net | 75% replacement sales under 80%-import scenario | Hypothetical 30% net |
| $0.45 | $0.315 | $14.56M | $4.37M |
| $0.75 | $0.525 | $24.27M | $7.28M |
| $1.00 | $0.700 | $32.36M | $9.71M |
$0.45 is already above the retail unit price of a Maui-displayed bulk case ($0.40). Therefore *it cannot be charged across all segments*. A program-wide $0.75 average requires genuine verified premium buyer mix, not an assumed across-the-board wholesale tariff.
ILLUSTRATIVE contract channel weighting (not market observation): 40% resident/bulk at $0.40 wholesale, 35% standard visitor/convenience at $0.75, 25% premium at $1.30 = **$0.7475 blended average**. Premium producer pricing requires buyer/retailer participation and actual sales volume; proposed price cannot be inferred from $6–$10 shelf anecdotes. Resident-discount delivery requires price-linked participating retailers or explicit community allocation, not a promise that wholesale discounts pass through to shoppers. Separate explicit potable relief stock sold at cost/subsidy to local emergency managers.
**Finance benchmark comparison** for 80% import and 75% capture at $0.75 wholesale = ~$24.27M annual sales:
- net 1.2% (Primo consolidated 2025 reference) = **$0.291M**;
- operating 6.5% (different profit definition!) = **$1.577M operating income before other net expenses**, not net;
- assumed 10% net = **$2.427M**;
- 30% desired net = **$7.280M**.
These are NOT independent streams to add. Do not multiply national Primo sales by local market size or claim Maui would capture its $4.6B cost of sales. Distinguish retail, bottler wholesale, gross, operating, EBITDA, cash flow and net.
**Tourist surtax:** If politically pursued, any new visitor excise/airport surcharge needs legislative and commerce/legal review, published legal authority and clear tax base; county cannot assume it can simply impose a selective import ban or unlegislated water surcharge. Current county GET surcharge 0.5% applies activities under Hawaii's 4.0% rate and does NOT apply to most wholesaling subject to 0.5%; importing from another state itself does not automatically incur a foreign-country customs duty. See https://tax.hawaii.gov/geninfo/countysurcharge/ . Fiscal transfers from enterprise require utility finance/municipal law review.

## IV. Multi-objective cost-benefit, beyond profit
Do NOT treat fiscal net income as the only value. Publish distinct annual ledgers:
1. **Municipal enterprise financial outcome:** earnings and cash after capital service, inventory costs and disaster reserve.
2. **Local value added and payroll:** locally sourced payroll/benefits, locally paid subcontractors and investments; do not treat an employee wage as county profit.
3. **Finished-product import substitution:** identifiable **outside-Maui** finished bottled water deliveries displaced. Exclude existing Maui producers. Dollars displaced do not equal cash profit to municipality or avoided cost if substituted by imported resin/supplies.
4. **Freight and waste**: net marine freight/port handling fewer heavy water shipments, minus freight on resin, equipment, chemicals and exports; separately quantify PET deposit collection, sort/reprocessing emissions and disposal costs; no assumed 'zero footprint' or 100% PET reuse.
5. **Resilience:** islandwide reserve capacity, 72h emergency inventory, delivered safe-gallons per resident during outages, restoration time after wildfire/tsunami/cyclone, proportion of distribution reachable under single-point failures, wildfire safe pressure. Value disaster avoided cost separately with scenario probability/insured exposures; do not add the same benefit more than once.
6. **Resident affordability:** actual retail pass-through of resident tiers and reduced municipal water utility rates, plus free disaster supply; avoid funding revenue with resident rate hikes.
7. **Resource supply**: priority municipal use, secondary wastewater R-1 agricultural and industrial off-take, retired stream diversions, ceased cesspool discharges and nutrient/struvite/gypsum recovery subject to quality.

## V. Disaster-resilient co-located facilities and piping
**Observed precedent:** 2023 Maui wildfires destroyed some water infrastructure and caused major pressure drops, contaminant ingress, loss of safe potable service and restrictions on showers; EPA documented VOC risk and isolation of hundreds of contaminated service laterals. https://www.epa.gov/maui-wildfires/water-infrastructure-repair-and-recovery ; https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-completes-drinking-water-and-wastewater-mission-lahaina .
**Safety design proposed:**
- Three independently switchable solar PV + batteries + resilient dispatchable backup / appropriate biogas microgrids (N-1 primary utility loads where designed) with black-start, islanding, spare RO membrane trains, emergency pumps, 72h to 14d critical energy/fuel plan; biosolar **pilot not bankable firm generation** absent independently measured yield.
- Protected aboveground/sheathed, high-temperature/fire-resistant and wind/seismic-rated potable and reclaimed pipes **only in safe corridors**; bury protective crossings and fire exposure hotspots where safer. Fiber DAS, piezo contact, flow, pressure and quality telemetry; certified independent safety controls and manual actuation continue if AI fails. Source-contamination isolation and backflow prevention across interties crucial.
- Cross-district interties and sectional isolation: when West module fails, other nominal 85%-factor campus output = Central 14.45+North4.25=**18.70 MGD**, a **3.80-MGD gap** relative to the imperfect 22.5-MGD county historical residential benchmark. If Central fails, West+North=9.35 MGD, **13.15-MGD gap**. Therefore 6+5+17 cannot claim N-1 household supply coverage absent verified legally reliable existing alternate sources, additional emergency production, designated storage and temporally managed demands. It may be physically impossible to deliver remaining water through an intertie without large cost/elevation limits; hydraulic simulation needed.
- Drought/wildfire allocation waterfall: 1 hospital/fire/essential potable, 2 local permanent resident drinking/sanitation, 3 food production/public health, 4 essential commerce and permitted agriculture (reclaimed wherever safe), 5 resort pools/turf/optional industrial uses restricted first; actual rights and public-trust constraints require policy review.
- Emergency inventory: CDC recommends >=1 gallon/person/day for >=3 days for emergency supply; 10,000 stranded people require **30,000 US gal** for 72h, or **~227,125 half-liter bottle equivalents**, or lower-packaging 1-5 gallon formats and mobile potable tanks. Crucially **bottled drinking-water provisions do not restore showers**. To provide an assumed additional 15 gal/person/day *for hygiene and minimal washing* to 10,000 people requires **0.150 MGD sustained**, or 450,000 gal for 72h, delivered via water mains/tankers; 15 gal is an illustrative restricted-service design not an accepted universal hygiene requirement. For all 160,592 county residents the 1gal/day x3day emergency minimum is 481,776 gal (~3.647M half-liter equivalents) but county total includes outer islands and full volume need not be stored as 500mL packages. Source https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/how-to-create-and-store-an-emergency-water-supply.html .
- Store emergency stock across fire-separated nodes, not only in bottling warehouse. Ensure protected tanks, safe fill lines and independently powered dispensing points. If source or network is contaminated, DO NOT send fresh plant output into unsafe mains until independently tested and isolated.
- FEMA explicitly treats safe water infrastructure and wastewater as an essential disaster Community Lifeline: https://www.epa.gov/waterresilience/femas-community-lifelines-construct .

## VI. Import replacement + packaging scale
Full modeled PET demand ~53.93M 500mL equivalents/year ~**809 tonnes PET** at 15g/unit plus caps/labels. At 80%-import and 75%-replacement modeled county plant makes ~32.36M units and requires ~**485 t PET/year** (before losses). Recovering bottles already circulating on Maui and diverting qualified #1 PET from outbound recycling could support the feed; **existing county recycling center 140.44 'tons' mixed PET+HDPE does not establish total island feedstock** or 2026 full recycling tonnage. More HI-5/retail/visitor and hauler mass audit required. Local PET loop needs food-contact qualified decontamination, loss controls, contamination exclusion (wastewater sludge plastic not suitable), actual production volumes and test campaigns.
Bulk 1gal/5gal, jugs and tanker refill are strategic emergency products with different packaging; reserve them independent of profitable premium small-bottle line.
Water product: 'Maui-made purified water, remineralized for taste and appropriate pH, independently safety-tested'; bottled water must satisfy Hawaiʻi HRS 328D and FDA good manufacturing standards, disinfection, honest labeling; no unsupported spring/natural mineral source or extra health benefit claims. https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/title-19/chapter-328d/section-328d-3/ ; https://law.justia.com/codes/hawaii/title-19/chapter-328d/section-328d-2/ .
Enforce packaging quality and recovery with four public streams: food/organics; HI-5 beverage bottles; other plastics; residual waste. Refund deposit or voucher, route to food-grade certified processing; count actual capture efficiency. Claims of 'plastic neutral', 'green' or superiority require product-level measured LCA and independent testing.

- Request 2024–2026 water-specific scanner volume by SKU and island from grocery + resorts/hotels, airport retail/boutique and F&B concessions; delivered wholesale prices, retailer markups, promotional rebates, annual contractual min purchases, manufacturing origin, inbound shipping and inventory levels; assume none presently observed.

- Obtain supplier proposals for food-grade recycled PET flake/pellets and preforms (local or qualified off-island), small-bottling filler lines, quality lab, staffing, premium SKU labeling and catastrophe inventory; compare contract-packaging versus fully county-owned factory.
- Cost power/heat, desalized water remineralization, fill disinfection, source water reserve, rPET processing, logistics, shipping tariffs and customer contracts, $/unit fully loaded net margin accounting for capex/financing/taxes.
- Physical hydraulics: actual potable system maps, available pressure heads, usable existing source storage, intertie feasible routes, protected fire-crossings and campus N-1 electricity and water yield; **no promise of full resilience before hydraulic proof**.
- Public co-design with Maui residents, Native Hawaiian water rights holders, environmental stewards and impacted businesses; county legal finance and state food/utility/environmental regulator review. Publicly publish validated clean water, jobs, recovery material, avoided imports and disaster readiness KPI dashboard.
