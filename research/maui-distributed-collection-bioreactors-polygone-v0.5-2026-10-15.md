## Executive finding

A voluntary, source-separated recycling and food-scrap collection subscription **may** fund an important portion of a decentralized island-scale processing network, **but** prices, local demand, collection density, vehicle costs, actual inputs and saleable outputs determine viability. Protect residents with lower fees and possible means-tested discounts; price large-volume business service based on actual weight, pickup volume, contamination and frequency rather than assumptions about tourist wealth alone.

Islandwide design links attractive **West Maui**, **Central Maui**, **North Shore**, and **Upcountry/Kula** collection and bioreactor nodes to specialized materials manufacturing, agricultural nutrient restoration, microplastic interception, and an ocean-science monitoring program. Not every region should house a costly full plastic/metal recycling line. Collect and sort locally where time saved exceeds added facility costs, with regionally permitted organics processing; concentrate industrial melters, battery depollution and specialized operations in appropriately zoned hubs.

**Binding objective:** resident quality of life, community consent, ocean health, clean food/water, attractive landscape and cost-of-living reductions outrank speculative mineral or credit revenue.

## 1. Grounded Maui context — note chronology and boundary

- Maui County curbside collection primarily provides refuse service, while recycling requires private curbside or public/private drop-offs. An **existing private provider, Maui Recycling Service**, publicly advertises curbside recycling starting at **$55.20/month**; its frequency and precise services require quote validation. New systems must cooperate/compete fairly with existing local businesses rather than claim no service exists.
- **West Maui Green Cycle** already offers food-scrap pickup to West Maui residences, restaurants, hotels and businesses from its Ku‘ia farm site. Its 2024–25 school food diversion reportedly reached **92,633 pounds**. An early partnership or contracted service should be evaluated.
- Maui County's draft solid waste plan cited in 2023 reporting estimated **82 million pounds (41,000 U.S. short tons) of food waste** landfilled **in 2019** across Maui County (includes Maui, Moloka‘i, Lāna‘i), not a current island feedstock guarantee.
- Maui's 2025 visitor inventory counted **21,911 accommodation units** and **26 hotel properties** on island. This does not mean 26 resorts would join, nor that all waste volumes are available to the model. 2025 visitor spending on Maui was reported near $5.97B, contextual only and not a contractual revenue stream.
- County of Maui was selected for **$3,607,065** EPA SWIFR award for **two West Maui collection centers**, estimated diversion **1,794 U.S. short tons/year** total. The county is the recipient. Funding is not granted to this private model and the same recovered tons cannot be counted twice.
- Hawaii DOH solid-waste permits cover waste handling and composting/co-composting. Facility siting, organics feed types, leachate management, vectors, sanitation, digestate qualification and storm resilience must pass local review.

## 2. Proposed optional subscription and collection economics

Every participating site receives presorting guidance and optional bins. Separate food scraps (temperature-control/odor management required), dry clean paper/glass/metals/plastics and special hazardous/e-waste collection. Resort back-of-house service requires measured weights, more containers, sanitation and staff—not the same operating cost as a household stop.

**User-proposed initial assumption:** **$20 direct collection cost per location per pickup** and **$20 one-time bin cost per location**, subject to actual route and equipment quotes. $20/stop weekly = **$86.67/month cash collection cost** (52 weeks /12) *before sorting, processing, central overhead, depreciation and margin*. Thus the proposed $20 is **not a cheap residential route** relative to the public $55.20/month incumbent starting price. This is a reason to optimize route density, not to conceal costs.

### 2.1 Explicit illustrative tiers (not Maui market contracts)

| Cohort | Fee per pickup | Direct cost per pickup | Visits/year/site | Revenue/month/site | Material load/visit | Evidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Resident | **$15** | $20 | 52 | **$65** | 9 kg | illustrative; $5/stop operating subsidy via cross-subsidy |
| Small business | **$75** | $45 | 104 | **$650** | 75 kg | illustrative; additional collection/handling cost |
| Resort / large property | **$500** | $180 | 156 | **$6,500** | 350 kg | **unverified willingness to pay**, actual fee must be contracted and priced per ton + pickup |

Once/twice weekly for residents can be selected; default is once weekly. Food-scrap service might need more frequent pickups due to heat, odor and pests. Businesses/resorts have flexible pickups and more bins. A **$20 one-time enrollment charge** offsets **$20 one-time bin cost** in the model, **not** multiple commercial food-safe carts, replacement/cleaning, or losses. An affordability voucher could reduce some resident bills further, but count only documented payments or grants as revenue.

Total material mass ≈ **6,818 U.S. short tons/year**, at the illustrative 9 kg/resident stop, 75 kg/business stop, 350 kg/resort stop. **Do not claim this volume has been collected or can be independently sourced; do not add it to the EPA program's 1,794 t/y without a distinct non-overlapping source inventory.** Contamination, seasonality and existing recycler agreements can reduce eligible input considerably.

### 2.3 Subscription margin, BEFORE processing and capital

- Residents: 4,200 ×52 × ($15−$20) = **−$1,092,000/year**.
- Small business: 380 ×104 × ($75−$45) = **+$1,185,600/year**.
- Resort: 23 ×156 × ($500−$180) = **+$1,148,160/year**.
- Net route contribution = **$1,241,760/year**.
- Illustrative shared admin, leasing, marketing, basic insurance and collection overhead beyond direct pickup costs = **$800,000/year**.
- **Annual collections operating contribution = +$441,760/year**, before processing and capital recovery. The bin charge cancels one-time bin cost under the simplifying assumption.

**Critically:** the modeled profits depend strongly on 23 resorts accepting $6,500/month and actual pickup cost staying below $180/visit; no such contracts have been observed. Municipal tipping and disposal costs, extra contaminated loads, bins, vehicle financing, truck downtime, regulatory bonds and sales taxes may require additional allocation. Incumbents and local organizations are stakeholders, not assumed customers.

## 3. Four-node logistics architecture

**No parcels selected; descriptions are district concepts only.**

- **West Maui (Lahaina/Kāʻanapali corridor):** complementary to the EPA-funded **two** collection centers and West Maui Green Cycle. Local sort/compost/transfer/microplastic and rainwater interception; compact clean-product workshop if feedstock and demand warrant. Avoid routing every load through the cross-island road.
- **Central Maui (Kahului/Wailuku industrial-compatible area):** primary weighed sorting/QA, high-volume code-compliant material manufacture, vehicle depollution/metal grading, specialist plastics/extrusion and 3D-print materials R&D. Any metal smelting contingent on grid/fuel and air-permit feasibility; export sorted aluminum if better.
- **North Shore (Pāʻia/Haʻikū):** neighborhood aggregation and sealed food-waste in-vessel processing, small clean-repair/Reuse operation; avoid truck overdevelopment in sensitive residential/farming areas.
- **Kula/Upcountry:** farm-adjacent, permitted organics and nutrient finishing / agronomic field trials, soil testing and compost retail, with no forced transportation of salty or unqualified residues onto agricultural land.

**Central-only vs network test:** additional land/permitting/maintenance/building CAPEX for satellite hubs must be compared against genuinely avoided vehicle-hours, missed pickups, fuel, road closures, spoilage, emissions and resident willingness to subscribe. Example stress test: avoiding 20 long transfer trips/week at 2 hours/trip and $120/truck-hour saves **$249,600/year**. If the satellite addition requires $6M capital, annuitized at 7% for 20 years the capital charge alone is **~$566,360/year**; travel avoidance **alone** does not justify those satellite investments. Local food waste sanitation, resilience, access and value-added output may change the result. No route map, travel time, or cost is claimed verified.

## 4. Bioreactor systems and organics mass balance

Design a **feedstock hierarchy**:
(1) prevent surplus edible food; (2) lawful safe food recovery; (3) edible-animal feed if legally appropriate; (4) in-vessel aerobic compost or anaerobic digestion (AD) depending on wet volumes and local energy/digestate demand; (5) conditioned fertilizer inputs where certified; (6) controlled disposal for contaminated residues.

**Default illustration:** 45% of 6,185.4 t collected is food/green organics = **2,783.4 metric t/year**, not a chemical assay. A purely illustrative **35% as-received wet-mass compost yield** would produce **974.2 t/year of compost**. Yield is not nutrient yield and cannot be turned into fertilizer N/P/K or dollar value without moisture, ash, contaminants, lab analysis, land capacity and buyer invoices. If AD is proposed, it requires a distinct volatile-solids and methane-potential test; **never count the same food waste as both full compost output and full digestate/biogas output**.

At 6,185.4 t/year, the **other 3,402 t** is a MIX of sorted dry recyclables and contamination and needs polymers/metals/glass/garbage reconciliation. Organic treatment should account for pathogen destruction, odors, leachate, rain protection, vectors, emissions, bioaerosols, microplastics in municipal food scraps, PFAS from disposable packaging, and real transport to farms. Compost output should carry QA grades and nutrient analyses.

Agronomic offtake: compare field-specific soil amendment needs and actual prices for imported fertilizer/compost; credit **nutrients displaced**, **soil organic carbon** (after monitoring), **avoided transport**, and **landfill/diversion**, each only once in the cash ledger and separately in ecological KPIs. Kula and Central Maui farm trials require soil, water-right and habitat vetos.

## 5. Five-to-ten-year capital payback: necessary conditions, not a forecast

**Capital stress test:** $15M all-in opening capital for a four-node basic network, fleet and selected mechanization, **not a procurement estimate**. Annual collection contribution under the $15 resident / $75 business / $500 resort example = **$441,760** before processor-specific net cash contribution and capital payback.

If actual annual feedstock reaching compliant processors is 6,818 **U.S. short tons**, and the **net incremental material contribution after all processing/residue OPEX** is:

| Net resource contribution per collected short ton | Annual total net cash before capital recovery | Simple $15M payback |
|---|---:|---:|
| $0/t | $441,760 | 34.0 years |
| $100/t | $1.124M | 13.3 years |
| $150/t | $1.464M | 10.2 years |
| **$200/t** | **$1.805M** | **8.3 years** |
| $300/t | $2.487M | 6.0 years |

**Thresholds:** To pay off hypothetical $15M in **10 years** requires **~$155.21/t** of verified net processor contribution; **5 years** requires **~$375.21/t**. At 7% cost of capital and 20 years, capital recovery factor = 0.09439, or **$1.416M annualized charge on $15M**, before borrowing structures, debt service, land taxes and other excluded risks.

A material stream cannot count both a $200/ton avoided import purchase and $200/ton product sale if it is the same local substitute. Price *net*: cash sales plus true avoided purchase/disposal, less all sorting, washing, conversion, maintenance, haul, rejects, certification, logistics and permitted final disposal; exclude ecological carbon/resilience credits unless independently verified and contracted. **$200/t is a high-performance hypothesis for mixed materials, not an evidenced island average**.

Capital sensitivity and startup working capital, ramp-up, debt financing, truck replacement, repairs, rates, payments defaults and household affordability are required before any 5–10-year repayment claim. Cross-subsidy is not assured until a signed resort/small-business contracting base exists.

## 6. PolyGone, floating microplastic interception and protected reefs

**What exists:** PolyGone reports 720 passive filters operating in a wastewater pilot since 2024 and a manufacturer-reported ~520M microplastic particles captured. It markets **PolyPod** for municipal channel/wastewater systems and says two commercial units are scheduled for October 20, 2026 at ACUA; its site lists **10 MGD capacity** for a PolyPod system. These are **supplier statements**, not independently proved Maui performance, price, or installed coastwide network. A 2026 supplier statement reports 98% removal, but extraction is highly particle-size, flow, medium and matrix dependent.

**Candidate architecture:**
- first priority **at source**: laundry/industrial filters, sewage treatment, storm drains, culverts, river/stream mouths **where permitted**; avoid obstructing runoff conveyance;
- then sheltered **harbor, marina and protected-channel pilot** of float-supported collection units, if navigation, aquatic habitat, marine wildlife entanglement and storm-release testing pass;
- **open-coast floating units** deferred pending wave survivability, bycatch, anchoring, maintenance and actual measured kg/year removal.
- Scientific study of 102 plastic clean-up technologies found poor standardization and limited independent ecological/performance testing; never advertise an unverified number of coastline units needed or inevitable aquatic impacts.
- Isolate captured plastic particles; polymer assays, additive analysis and toxicology **before** any manufacturing feedstock use. The tiny collected mass could cost more to process than it sells for; economic justification may be ocean-quality outcome, not feedstock revenue.
- DO NOT add high-pressure membrane flux figures to gravity-driven PolyPod capacity; certify vendor-provided hydraulic curve, bypass, differential head, flushing and lab particle size assays.

Sources: [PolyGone manufacturer](https://www.polygonesystems.com/) and [systems](https://www.polygonesystems.com/technology), [ACUA pilot](https://www.polygonesystems.com/acua-project-page); [2024 evidence review DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpolbul.2024.116978); [2026 field review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psep.2026.109366).

## 7. China OAE and Hawaiʻi pH/alkalinity — accurate translation of scale

**The Chinese research lane is real.** Institute of Oceanology, Chinese Academy of Sciences **June 30, 2026** announcement and a published journal paper describe calcium–alkali coupling with a **1,000 m³ southern Yellow Sea field experiment**, converting DIC to calcium carbonate and estimating **77.6 kg atmospheric CO₂** uptake in that test under specific conditions. This is a **bounded field demonstration, NOT a proven large-scale commercial ocean-pH remediation program**.

Source: [IOCAS/CAS official release](https://qdio.cas.cn/2019Ver/News/kyjz/202606/t20260630_8235835.html), [research paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301479726017548).

Maui reef monitoring needs **pH measured on a declared seawater scale, total alkalinity, dissolved inorganic carbon, aragonite saturation, pCO₂, nutrients, runoff, microplastics, thermal stress and coral biology**. Low pH near West Maui reefs can be influenced by nutrient-rich groundwater and local biogeochemistry. **First remove nutrient/runoff sources**, then assess whether an independent, culturally approved, laboratory/mesocosm-based alkalinity study warrants a *separately permitted* field trial. No unapproved alkalinity release, no assumptions that any island pH requires uniform upward correction. Track precipitation chemistry, ecological effects and possibly toxic trace metals. Mineral recovery and OAE may compete for same calcium/magnesium; no double-counting.

Relevant U.S. precedent: [WHOI 2026 LOC-NESS field trial preliminary report](https://www.whoi.edu/press-room/news-release/oae-prelim/) and [USGS Kahekili reef groundwater](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/cmhrp/news/polluted-groundwater-threatens-hawaiian-coral-reefs). Neither authorizes application at Maui.

## 8. Island manufacturing, mineral recovery and zero-liquid discharge

Continue v0.4: West Maui resilient, historic-compatible Front Street biosolar microgrids; Central Maui recycled-material 3D print technology validation and Surf Block ICCF partnership options; zero/low discharge desalination plant ecological design; all brine ion/mineral product allocations subject to chemistry, recovery rates and offtake.

Integrate plant nutrients from **separately assayed wastewater, manure, crop residues and saline mineral streams** with a single element-by-element N/P/K/Ca/Mg/S inventory. Specific crop soil tests and certification decide whether compost, gypsum, potassium salts or struvite have local demand. Sales to other Hawaiʻi islands require freight, buyer, local reserve and nutrient concentration vs shipping wet bulky product. Exports are unlocked after Maui's verified need/reserve allocations.

**No claim** that ZLD is inevitably greener than permitted alternative disposal; solids need a safe final destination and energy use can dominate. Model ZLD and mineral recovery jointly to minimize rejected liquid, lost recoverable salts, net ecological harm and operating expense.

## 9. Audit/next empirical steps

1. Obtain service quotes and minimum route stops/hr from actual Maui carriers, bins and resort back-of-house waste teams. Verify what $20/stop covers: fuel, labor, truck depreciation, insurance, sorting or disposal?
2. Survey 100 residents, 20 small businesses and 5 resorts per region for willingness to pay (not presumed); request actual kilograms and bins.
3. Secure permitted organics partner trials and agree on municipal feedstock boundaries; **do not appropriate existing West Maui Green Cycle customers without agreements**.
4. Price central-only vs four-node collection using observed route speeds, Honoapiʻilani Highway disruptions, bridge/flood detours and truck load factors.
5. Procure compost/AD pilot vendor assays and local agricultural purchase/offtake letters; compare commercial co-compost partner vs new capital.
6. Ask PolyGone for installed quotes, annual filters/maintenance, hydraulic curves, live sampling reports, particle-size distribution, wildlife/storm impact and residual fate.
7. Locate approved reef and water chemistry monitoring programs for noninvasive baseline; never authorize in-water alkalinity chemistry from a spreadsheet.
8. Model full collection-to-product gross cash, ratepayer fairness, pricing/impact of resort loads, worker wages, funding grants only if awarded, and 5/10-year CAPEX repayment under downside cases.

**No invented employment impact, product revenue, carbon removal, reduced ocean acidity, or deployment.** Default data grade PROPOSED or SCENARIO; support actual modeled results with traceable raw input and experimental outputs.
