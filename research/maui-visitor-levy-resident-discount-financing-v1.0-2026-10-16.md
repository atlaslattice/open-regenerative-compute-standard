# Maui ORCS v1.0 — Visitor-Funded Water Infrastructure and Verified-Resident Discounts, 20-Year Financing Addendum
**2026-10-16 | Simulation & policy options — NOT enacted tax, approved rate, issued debt or verified project cashflow.** New standalone version; prior v0.9 unchanged. Source code: [maui_visitor_funding_v10.py](maui_visitor_funding_v10.py).

## Findings
**5% of $3.9B = $195M/year, NOT $700M.** The alternative *maximum gross-spend base* from official 2025 Maui visitor expenditures was $5.97B; 5% = $298.5M before any exclusions, collection expenses, behavioral effects, obligations and resident credits. All tourism spending is not automatically eligible transactions; $3.9B is user-supplied assumption, not an independent Hawaiʻi government tax base.
The previous v0.9 full 1,014-mile replacement screening includes **$622.8M annual 20-year loan payments and ~$856.2M annual deficit AFTER existing DWS billings and projected third-party product revenues, including OPEX**. Do not represent $622.8M debt as the full fiscal gap or ADD annual preexisting water revenue twice. Existing operating deficit before borrowing ~$233.4M. The v0.9 steady-state capital is $7.762B, while multiple components, chiefly ZLD and $4M/mile smart corridors, are assumptions not bids. All amounts 2026 nominal USD. [v0.9](maui-consolidated-20yr-water-enterprise-financing-v0.9-2026-10-16.md).

## Proposed visitor charge and local affordability rules
User idea: 2%–5% visitor-facing charge on taxable purchases, and 3% discount for verified local residents on purchases. **Policy options only**: visitor-specific prices or tax can raise Commerce Clause / discrimination, administrative privacy, uniformity, political and tourism-demand issues. Do not assert a county can freely levy it. More legally administrable alternative to analyze is a legislated origin-neutral environmental fee on a defined transaction or expanded allocated portion of existing room/tourism receipts combined with *separately funded residency-verified refundable credits/participating merchant discounts*. Each variant needs independent Hawaiʻi tax and constitutional counsel, legislative authorization, public engagement, and verification of eligible bases. The user goal is resident affordability and locally captured visitor value, not presumed constitutional design.
Legal *existing* baseline, as of 2026:
- **Maui's 0.5% county surcharge on Hawaii GET** applies alongside state GET to activities subject to 4% GET; cap on existing authorized county GET surcharge is **0.5%**, in force Jan 2024-Dec 2030; it is NOT a tourist-specific tax, and may have earmarks. https://tax.hawaii.gov/geninfo/countysurcharge/
- **Maui County 3% TAT** applies to eligible transient lodging proceeds under county tax law, separately from statewide TAT (Hawaii raised state TAT to **11%** in Jan 2026). Existing TAT cannot simply be multiplied by total visitor spend nor presumed unappropriated; category-specific eligible taxable rental base. https://www.mauicounty.gov/2466/Transient-Accommodations-Tax ; https://files.hawaii.gov/tax/news/announce/ann25-03.pdf
- Actual Maui Island tourist spending in **calendar 2025 = $5.97B**. Spending includes accommodation, shopping, dining and other services, and multiple payments may fall outside a given tax base; this amount does NOT equal new levy taxable gross. https://dbedt.hawaii.gov/blog/26-04/ 
- ACS 2020–2024 **Maui County households = 55,841**; island breakdown needed. **$30,000 qualifying spending/household/year is UNVERIFIED ASSUMPTION**, not a county spend audit: https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/mauicountyhawaii/POP645224 .

## Core calculation (stand-alone and no effect on previous enterprise revenue)
Model resident cashback: 55,841 county households *$30,000 qualifying spending *3% = **$50.2569M/year**. This is a COUNTERFACTUAL maximum for that assumed base if ALL households participate. Merchants might fund discounts, but their funding cost is not free social surplus. Privacy-preserving residency-verification method essential; avoid intrusive real-time purchase monitoring.
Base rates at full collection with NO administration:
| Total eligible gross visitor spending assumed | 2% gross | 3% gross | 5% gross |
| $3.9B | $78.0M | $117.0M | $195.0M |
| $5.97B (optimistic ALL spending eligible) | $119.4M | $179.1M | $298.5M |
No evidence that all $5.97B is legally taxable under a single new proposed surcharge or that $3.9B precise base has been audited.
If 3% resident credits paid directly from visitor collections, **5% gross less $50.26M** yields $144.74M from $3.9B base or $248.24M from full $5.97B hypothetical base before administrative costs, new spending effects and disallowed categories.
**Additional screening**: assume 2% collection/administration cost on visitor gross collections; at 5%:
- $3.9B base: $195M gross -$3.9M administration -$50.2569M discounts = **$140.84M available**, reducing prior $856.2M annual gap to **$715.36M**.
- $5.97B: $298.5M gross -$5.97M administration -$50.2569M = **$242.27M available**, reducing prior annual gap to **$613.93M**.
The deductions assume full eligible participation and gross tax collections; no enforcement, tax incidence or demand model. These modeled additions are NOT current public funds and cannot be promised to lenders.
Relative to annual debt service $622.8M: $140.84M represents 22.6% of annual debt, $242.27M represents 38.9%; if directed wholly to debt they cannot additionally subsidize residents and remove the enterprise operating deficit. The model explicitly nets out the resident credits first.
**Threshold test**: even with ZERO resident discount and ZERO collection costs, to close prior $856.2M enterprise funding gap from visitor contributions ALONE needs **21.95% of $3.9B** or **14.34% of $5.97B**. With $50.26M resident credits and 2% administrative rate, gross rates required are ~$23.7% of $3.9B or ~$15.5% of $5.97B. These are mathematical counterfactuals, NOT tax recommendations, and behavioral/legal limitations make real collection lower.

## What financing might accomplish when correctly staged
**Existing allocation relevance**: Maui FY2026 County proposed budget included ~$63.7M water capital and $105.5M sewer capital in respective categories; those are **specific appropriations** under existing plans, not annual new cash available to service debt. Avoid identifying grants and financing as revenue simultaneously. [FY2026 program budget](https://www.mauicounty.gov/DocumentCenter/View/155200/000-FY-2026-Program-Budget-Combined).
**Visitor-funded public value**: new dollars could offset source-water OPEX or provide a dedicated bond revenue pledge ONLY if lawfully enacted, appropriated and demonstrably reliable, after allowing for reduced visitor volume, tax compliance, merchant discount costs, tourism industry effects, capital reserves and tax administration. Set 2/3/4/5% scenarios as legislative considerations, not forecast tax adoption.
**Resident benefit**: 3% discount on VERIFIED eligible local purchases, plus affordable metered basic household water. *Not* necessarily 3% on all services without exemptions or legal rules. Discount expected price pass-through must be externally validated. Lower water bills are a social benefit even if municipal enterprise accounting revenue falls.
**Funding mix**: current DWS and sewer enterprise rates, appropriate state/federal environmental and disaster grants, concessional SRFs, county debt subject to bond coverage covenants, commercial resource/mineral/reuse/retail profits after costs, voluntarily agreed premium resort infrastructure offtakes, targeted investor/contractual funds, verified avoided renewal expenditures and decommissioned legacy cost. Independently value jobs/avoided import value, ecological/stream benefit and disaster readiness without counting them as debt-service cash.
**Cost side**: v0.9 assumed $700M full-ZLD capital, $120M annual ZLD operating and $4.056B for full replacement of 1,014 historical miles; prioritize field-verified brine heat integration, islanded microgrids, selective pipes and viable remanufacturing to recalculate major cost drivers. Other-island mileage retained as requested **benchmark**, not guaranteed Maui Island construction. Full ZLD cannot be assumed energy-free due to useful salt revenues.
**Phasing**: test 10-, 20-, and 40-year financing, actual debt service under phased plant commissioning, rate collections by customer class, intertie N-1, maintenance reserve, realistic discounted cashflows and risk. v0.9 40y term still has debt outstanding after an initial 20-year reporting period.

## Reproduction
Run [maui_visitor_funding_v10.py](maui_visitor_funding_v10.py) in the same research directory; output `maui_visitor_funding_v10.csv`, eight scenarios 2/3/4/5% × two eligible spending bases, 3% resident benefit, 2% collection costs, annual funding gap after public revenues already modeled.
**Conclusions:** The visitor charge is a potentially substantial scenario revenue stream and could support some financing. **5% on $3.9B ($195M gross) does NOT cover a $622.8M annual debt payment, let alone $856.2M total operating-plus-debt gap.** Assess legality, eligibility and incidence first. A fiscally sustainable design requires substantial improved engineering, net new recoveries, capital grants, phased build or other funding. The 3% resident discount remains explicitly funded and is not ignored.
