## Abstract
The proposed pitcher–catcher p–¹¹B platform is a modular fusion-interaction and energy-management testbed, **not a demonstrated self-sustaining reactor**. Distinct research tracks include (1) acousto-optical target preparation; (2) energy-dependent fusion interaction; (3) alpha diagnostics and direct electrostatic conversion; (4) coherent wave-mediated energy transfer; and (5) a parallel polarized-fuel roadmap. The objective is to measure each module under randomized, matched controls, then integrate only positive, independently replicated results. Net electrical gain has not been demonstrated.

## 1. Physical premises and definitions
p + ¹¹B → 3 ⁴He (alpha particles) + approximately 8.68 MeV. Successful fusion consumes the original proton and boron. Energetic alpha particles are stable helium-4 nuclei; their *kinetic* energy is the intended energy source for post-fusion recovery. A ²⁸Si/¹³C metamaterial is not established as a means of preserving special alpha nuclear states, and mechanical acoustics do not directly modify nuclear forces.

For a stationary ¹¹B target, a 675 keV center-of-mass resonance corresponds approximately to a 736 keV laboratory proton energy, although actual beam–target yield depends on full energy spectra, angle, thickness and stopping. Laser-driven proton beams cannot be presumed monoenergetic. A magnetic field can steer charged particles but static magnetic fields do not directly extract kinetic work; electric fields and time-dependent coherent modes can exchange energy with them.

The power path is **target preparation → proton generation → fusion interaction → alpha phase-space collection/energy transfer → direct electrical conversion → conditioning/storage/reinvestment**. Electrochemical hydrogen fuel cells are only a loose systems analogy; alpha energy conversion is a charged-particle deceleration process, not an electrochemical reaction.

## 2. Evidence register and careful boundaries

### 2.1 2025 differential cross-section measurements — verified
D. Mazzucconi et al., *European Physical Journal A* 61, 114 (2025), DOI https://doi.org/10.1140/epja/s10050-025-01589-3 measured p–¹¹B reactions across 0.34–4.73 MeV using boron-coated targets and a two-stage silicon telescope, with differential cross-section and alpha-energy-spectrum analysis. The paper discusses behavior near the ~0.675 MeV resonance and discrepancies with earlier measurements. **Do not equate a differential cross section at 60° with an integrated total cross section**, or attribute a ~1.4-barn total peak to this work without tracing the particular dataset and energy-frame convention.

### 2.2 LFEX target geometry — verified with important qualification
*Hydrogen boron fusion in confined geometries*, *High Power Laser Science and Engineering*, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/high-power-laser-science-and-engineering/article/hydrogen-boron-fusion-in-confined-geometries/D273DDE00ABB65D29D2053845A22A887 reports greater detected alpha flux and a harder spectrum for spherical versus composition-matched planar targets in LFEX laser campaigns. The paper specifies **filter-dependent** comparisons: at least 3× through 10/20-μm filters, at least 10× through 40/50-μm filters, and approximately 100× through a 70-μm filter. The strongest ratio is **not automatically a 100× increase in total fusion events, all alpha particles, or recovered energy**. Target geometry, proton acceleration, self-generated fields and detection acceptance interact.

**Protocol consequence:** Initial G1 should compare planar and spherical targets, each with acoustics OFF, ON and a carefully matched acoustic sham if practical. Prespecify alpha energy-bin results, total calibrated yield, target geometry/areal density, proton spectrum and incident laser energy. Use randomized shot order, replicated shots and uncertainty intervals. Once interactions are measured, an optimized spherical target can become an engineering baseline.

### 2.3 Acoustic preparation — plausible cross-domain bridge, not demonstrated p–¹¹B benefit
Standing-wave surface-acoustic-wave nebulization and lithium-niobate acoustic devices are credible experimental *leads* for aerosol and droplet preparation, but the specific laser-plasma precedent noted by DeepSeek was **not independently source verified** in this v0.5 audit. Distinguish: (A) liquid/droplet/foil positioning and target fabrication; (B) vibrational control of target morphology and laser absorption; (C) plasma collective ion-acoustic modes after irradiation. These are different physical systems. Neither a p–¹¹B normalized-yield gain nor a change to the strong nuclear force has been shown via acoustics here.

### 2.4 ENN direct electrostatic conversion — verified with important qualification
H. Kong et al., *Plasma Science and Technology* 28(5), 054001 (2026), DOI https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-6272/ae445a reports **greater than 80% charged-particle kinetic-energy-to-electricity conversion efficiency** in a **low-parameter fundamental converter unit**. Authors state this was done *before observation of fusion alpha particles*, to verify converter principles. This is not proof of >80% full-spectrum fusion-alpha collection, full integrated converter efficiency, or net-electric fusion operation. The earlier ENN paper on EHL-2 discusses alpha extraction and the challenges posed by high voltages, power density and field geometry: https://pst.hfcas.ac.cn/en/article/id/77f44dd4-b160-48ff-b9f4-e3cbb84b8a65 .

**Protocol consequence:** Establish an independently characterized helium-ion beam and an electrostatic converter baseline. Measure energy arriving at the converter, charge-state distribution, angular acceptance, grid interception, secondary electrons, voltage/current/load energy, all auxiliary power and collection efficiency separately from conversion efficiency. Compare to a controlled plasma-wave-assisted variant; do not imply ENN hardware is directly available.

### 2.5 Alpha channeling and confinement — theoretical
Reports of 80–90% conductive-loss energy recovery, confinement-time reductions of 5–10×, or scenarios approaching 66 s to 2 s are **model outputs dependent on particular geometries and loss assumptions**, not measured energy recovery in the proposed laser target. A high reported coherent proton response or simulated alpha-to-wave transfer does not itself constitute recovered electricity. Electron stopping, alpha ash, bremsstrahlung, plasma confinement, driver consumption and exergy limits must enter the ledger.

### 2.6 Spin polarization — separate exploratory program
Theory suggests that spin-dependent nuclear reaction channels could alter p–¹¹B cross sections or product anisotropy. Figures such as 20% or up to 60% enhancement need a traced reaction-specific primary source and actual target-polarization measurement before numerical use. Optical pumping of atomic boron beams, ¹³C DNP, and ²⁸Si spin-zero nuclear hosts do **not** jointly establish polarization transfer into ¹¹B/¹H in a laser-shot solid target. The polarized fuel effort is therefore decoupled from G1 and DEC milestones.

## 3. Revised independent research lanes
**Lane T — Geometry and acoustic target engineering:** Measure planar/spherical targets × acoustic OFF/ON/SHAM, target shape, density and proton/alpha energy spectra. Endpoint: *normalized fusion birth yield*, not raw alpha counts alone.

**Lane D — Electrostatic direct converter:** Benchmark measured electrical work on a known charged-particle beam, with collection efficiency, stage conversion efficiency and complete wall-plug costs reported separately.

**Lane W — Coherent alpha energy transfer:** Establish whether a driven ion-acoustic/plasma mode extracts measured energy from a known alpha beam into a diagnosable mode or proton population, and whether the extracted fraction supplies incremental electrical work. A wave that simply heats electrons or receives externally injected laser energy is **not** proven alpha-energy recovery.

**Lane P — Polarized Fuel and Target Roadmap:** P0 primary literature; P1 produce and measure ¹¹B/¹H spin polarization; P2 measure lifetime and composition; P3 measure survival under representative pulse conditions; P4 test nuclear yield and emission anisotropy against matched controls; P5 consider integration only if benefit survives shot conditions.

**Lane S — Full-system simulation:** Quantify beam spectra, nuclear probability, stopping/escape, alpha birth, angular acceptance, converter stage efficiency, target repetition rate, auxiliary costs and uncertainty. Model interactions rather than multiplying assumed independent f_acoustic × f_spin × f_wave factors.

## 4. Revised gate descriptions
**G0 — Evidence and cross-section audit.** Record publications, DOI, beam versus CM conventions, total versus differential cross sections, filter transmission, alpha energy distributions and efficiency denominators.

**G1 — Geometry × acoustics experiment.** Randomized replicated factorial design. Include sham acoustic power, matched target alignment, geometry, incident proton spectrum and detector calibration. Predefine corrected alpha birth and proton-to-alpha yield before the test.

**G2/P — Independent spin roadmap.** Spin is no longer a prerequisite for acoustic or converter tests.

**G3 — Verified plasma waves.** Measure real wave frequency, wave number, amplitude, coherence and the incoming/outgoing energetic ion distributions.

**G3-A — Charged-particle energy recovery competition.** Compare (i) electrostatic conversion alone, (ii) electrostatic conversion plus resonant wave drive, (iii) same auxiliary drive with off-resonant/decoupled sham, at matched injected alpha-ion energy and target conditions. Measure *incremental* electrical load output minus incremental wall-plug driver energy; assess pulse-to-pulse repeatability and confidence bounds.

**G4 — Integration/interaction studies.** Combine only supported modules, using full factorial tests for three binary factors where practical (2³=8 conditions). Do not confuse higher escaping-alpha detector yield with higher fusion birth rate.

**G5 — Net electrical ledger.** Establish total electrical inputs and delivered outputs over enough pulses to quantify duty cycle, component replacement, downtime, energy reinvested in later shots and uncertainty. Positive module energy recovery is **not** total plant breakeven.

## 5. Non-negotiable ledgers
**Nuclear birth:** Y_f = N_p × spectrum-and-stopping-weighted reaction probability; alpha births ~3Y_f, excluding background/other reactions.

**Particle flow:** E_alpha,birth = E_alpha,escaped + E_alpha,deposited-in-electrons/ions + E_alpha,radiated + E_alpha,intercepted + E_unresolved, with external wave-drive exchanges identified.

**Electric boundary:** E_net = E_load,direct + E_load,thermal + E_other,delivered − E_main-laser,wall − E_wave-driver,wall − E_acoustic,wall − E_DNP,wall − E_magnets − E_vacuum − E_cooling − E_target-preparation − E_aux. Do not debit both optical laser output and wall-plug laser input. Do not credit the same alpha energy simultaneously as plasma heating, converted DC and wave transfer. Stored energy and electricity reinvested must be distinguished from exported electricity.

**Incremental wave test:** ΔE_net = (E_load,on − E_load,sham) − (E_wall,on − E_wall,sham), holding injected alpha beam energy and acceptance fixed.

## 6. Reviewer-facing outstanding questions

2. Which specific cross-section compilation supports the claimed ~1.4 barn resonance and in which reference frame?
3. Which original publication demonstrates LiNbO₃ standing-wave surface acoustic nebulization compatible with laser-plasma laboratories?
4. What exact beam species, distribution, voltage, load and efficiency denominator apply to ENN's >80% unit test?
5. What geometric and space-charge assumptions would limit scaling the ENN unit to broad MeV alpha populations?
6. Can a measured plasma mode transfer alpha-origin energy to a circuit rather than simply heating background electrons?
7. What experimentally grounded alpha-wave resonance condition is appropriate to a pulsed pitcher–catcher geometry?
8. What original research verifies polarization of ¹¹B and ¹H in the ²⁸Si/¹³C target concept and survival through laser irradiation?
9. Which stopping-power and radiation datasets are appropriate to the energy ledger?
10. What is the most decisive and affordable falsification experiment for each lane?

## 7. Conclusion
Evidence now strongly supports **target geometry as an experimental lever** and **direct electrostatic conversion as a demonstrated physical process at a small charged-particle-unit scale**. Acoustic preparation may offer incremental improvement; its p–¹¹B effect needs direct measurement. Spin-target fabrication and useful alpha-to-wave-to-electric conversion remain exploratory. These modules should be tested independently and compared using measured, compatible energy boundaries. This is a research architecture, not proof of an energy-producing reactor.
