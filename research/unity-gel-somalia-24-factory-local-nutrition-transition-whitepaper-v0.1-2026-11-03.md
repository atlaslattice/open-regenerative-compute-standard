> **Thesis:** Recurrent emergency food assistance can prevent famine and save lives without resolving the structural causes of chronic food insecurity. Somalia could investigate a locally owned, 24-facility *finished-food manufacturing* network, with upstream ingredient production added as feasible, and compare its long-run domestic capabilities to repeated humanitarian procurement. **The central objective is not to terminate emergency aid prematurely; it is to make long-term nutritional access less dependent on external finished-food donations.** No factory, funding commitment, agency endorsement, $2 ration, or humanitarian outcome has been demonstrated by this document.

### Executive decision points — what the figures actually establish

1. **Need:** The IPC's 23 September 2026 analysis projects **5.4 million people** in Somalia in IPC Phase 3 or worse for **October–December 2026**. This is a dated projection and not an immutable census or a single group with identical dietary needs. [IPC analysis](https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-country-analysis/details-map/en/c/1165451/?iso3=SOM).
2. **Five-year appeals:** UN-coordinated Somalia humanitarian plan requirements across **2022–2026 total approximately $8.77 billion** ($2.3B + $2.6B + $1.6B + $1.42B + $0.852B). **An appeal is not cash received or spent.** Annual plans cover health, protection, shelter, water, food, nutrition, logistics and other activities. Sources: [UN Somalia 2026 HNRP, p.18](https://somalia.un.org/sites/default/files/2026-01/Somalia_HNRP_2026.pdf), [2025 HNRP announcement](https://somalia.un.org/en/288199-humanitarian-partners-seek-us142-billion-assist-46-million-people-somalia-2025), [2023 HRP announcement](https://somalia.un.org/en/218500-humanitarian-partners-seek-us26-billion-assist-76-million-people-somalia).
3. **Reported historical funding, not expenditures:** A March 2025 inter-agency evaluation cites OCHA FTS data of approximately **$2.4B in 2022, $1.3B in 2023 and $0.9B in 2024**, approximately **$4.6B reported over these three years**, including funding *inside and outside* coordinated plans. The evaluators explicitly warn that data sources and coverage differ. **Do not add these receipts to appeal totals or assume they were all spent.** [IASC/IAHE Somalia evaluation, pp.29–30](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/inter-agency-humanitarian-evaluation-of-the-response-to-the-humanitarian-crisis-in-somalia.pdf).
4. **Documented operational expenditure:** WFP's June 2026 Somalia internal audit reports **$384M direct operational expenses** assisting 5.5M beneficiaries in 2024 and **$266M direct operational expenses** assisting 3.5M in 2025 (**$650M combined**). Audit scope runs **January 2024–June 2025**; clarify the exact 2025 accounting period before using this as a full-calendar-year rate. These are WFP expenses, **not all aid-agency spending** and not necessarily the total economic cost of all assistance. [WFP audit AR-26-04](https://www.wfp.org/audit-reports/internal-audit-wfp-operations-somalia-may-2026).
5. **Still not structurally resolved:** WFP's adopted Somalia Country Strategic Plan 2026–2030 says the country has received humanitarian assistance for **more than 30 years** and experienced IPC Phase 4 emergency food insecurity **each year since 2010**. **This is evidence of persistent need, not evidence that past aid produced no benefits.** [WFP CSP 2026–2030](https://www.wfp.org/operations/so03-somalia-country-strategic-plan-2026-2030).
6. **Future appeal:** Somalia's **2026 UN humanitarian appeal is $852M to target 2.4M people**, out of 4.8M assessed in need using narrower criteria. It is **not $852M to feed all 5.4M IPC Phase 3+ individuals a complete diet for a year**. [2026 HNRP announcement](https://somalia.un.org/en/308876-humanitarian-partners-seek-us852-million-assist-24-million-people-2026).
7. **Unity Gel target, not verified cost:** **$2.00 per 2,000-kcal-equivalent adult daily ration** is a deliberately aggressive **future production-cost target**, not a quote, costed bill of materials, or nutritionally qualified food. The pre-existing Unity Gel v0.1 model instead estimated **$2.88 per 1,000-kcal pouch, or $5.76 per two-pouch day**, itself only a *modeled variable-cost scenario*, with unvalidated formulation. See parent paper above.
8. **Scale:** 24 facilities, each with **132 metric tonnes/day nominal finished-food output**, yield approximately **2,709 tonnes/day delivered** after 90% operational availability and 5% processing/product loss, versus a target of 2,700 tonnes/day at an *assumed* **0.50 kg/person/day**.
9. **Cost:** At **$2 × 5.4M people × 180 days**, the six-month manufactured-ration target is **$1.944B**, excluding distribution, clinical nutrition, clean water, land/plant CAPEX and major upstream inputs infrastructure. Illustrative 24-site factory CAPEX at **$50M / $100M / $150M per site** is **$1.2B / $2.4B / $3.6B**, **not engineering bids**. A six-month food-plus-plant scenario thus runs **$3.144B / $4.344B / $5.544B before numerous additional costs**. **No first-year savings against current humanitarian appeals are established.**

## 1. The provocative question — where did the money go, and what remains?

**A substantial share of international funding has financed essential crisis response while recurring hunger persists.** Rather than declaring all assistance 'waste,' conduct an independent, transparent *expenditure-to-durable-asset* audit:

- How much funding was **appealed for, committed, disbursed, obligated, and actually expended** each year?
- What share directly purchased foods, vouchers, therapeutic supplies, water, clinical services, logistics, security, and staff?
- What share funded **durable, locally owned** facilities, irrigation, electricity, testing laboratories, storage, roads, agriculture, manufacturing and human training?
- What was the **duration and nutritional completeness** of assistance per person, disaggregated by population and age?
- How much spending **averted mortality, famine and livelihood collapse**? This counterfactual must be estimated, not assumed zero.
- After each program, how many households were able to procure enough food **without repeat emergency assistance**, and how many local businesses remained solvent?
- What barriers to self-reliance were **conflict/access, price shocks, drought, underfunding or governance-related** rather than a failure to build factories?

The 2025 independent inter-agency evaluation explicitly flags inconsistent funding data and discusses substantial humanitarian achievements and operational limits. [Primary evaluation](https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/inter-agency-humanitarian-evaluation-of-the-response-to-the-humanitarian-crisis-in-somalia.pdf). The 2024 UN HNRP explicitly says that structural drivers of need cannot be solved through emergency assistance alone, and calls for development investment. [UN HNRP 2024](https://somalia.un.org/en/260966-somalia-2024-humanitarian-needs-and-response-plan-hnrp).

But **$2/day full feeding for all 5.4M people costs more than the entire 2026 $852M multi-sector appeal in just three months** (~$972M for 90 days); this project cannot honestly claim to be an immediately cheaper substitute. The question is whether measured domestic capacity and resilience justify the additional industrial investment over **five to twenty years**.

## 2. Engineering basis: 24 regional finished-food facilities

**Target:** the calorie-equivalent daily needs of **5.4 million people** under a uniform adult *accounting unit*. This is a **capacity unit**, not an actual food prescription for every individual.

- (N = 5{,}400{,}000) illustrative daily ration equivalents.
- (m = 0.500 \\mathrm{kg/person/day}) **assumed dry/concentrated food mass** for a full ~2,000-kcal ration; meeting 2,000 kcal at this mass requires average **4 kcal/g**. The formulation and density **have not been validated**. Small children and vulnerable patients need separate products and protocols.
- (Q_{delivered} = Nm/1000 = 2{,}700\ \\mathrm{tonnes/day}).
- (F = 24) production facilities; availability (u = 0.90); saleable-yield factor (y = 0.95).
- (q_{nominal} = Q_{delivered}/(Fuy) = 131.58\ \\mathrm{tonnes/site/day}); round to **132 tonnes/day nominal**.
- (Q_{network} = 24 × 132 × 0.90 × 0.95 = 2{,}708.64\ \\mathrm{tonnes/day}).
- Finished food required for 180 days: **486,000 tonnes** at ideal fulfillment, before stockpiles and net distribution losses.
- Finished food for 365 days: **985,500 tonnes** at ideal fulfillment.

This is **not** a recommendation to build 24 identical fermentation bioreactors. Each initially represents a **blending/fortification/packaging plant** able to source quality-controlled ingredients. Microbial-protein fermentation, edible oils, starches, vitamins, minerals and food-grade packaging are separate upstream systems and cost centers. Ingredients may still be imported during initial commissioning, and true supply sovereignty requires eventually qualifying local or regional feedstocks economically. Where existing Somali farmers, herders and processors can supply nutritious foods, procurement should **strengthen rather than displace** them.

**Geography:** No addresses or exact sites are selected. First-stage siting requires Somali stakeholder consent and independently evaluated power, potable/process water, sanitation, road and port access, security, flood and drought risk, local labor, environmental permits, and distance to affected communities. A distributed network reduces single-site failure but increases QA, energy and logistics complexity.

## 3. The $2/day challenge: what it implies

At $2 per daily ration and 0.500 kg per daily ration, the cost ceiling is **$4.00 per kg of finished food**, for the specified included cost boundary.

| Population / duration | Number of daily rations | Manufactured-food cost @ $2 | Exclusions |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 100,000 people × 180 days | 18M | **$36M** | Delivery + CAPEX + health |
| 1.6M prioritized HNRP people × 180 days | 288M | **$576M** | Same |
| 2.4M 2026 HNRP target × 180 days | 432M | **$864M** | Same |
| **5.4M IPC acute-food-insecurity population × 180 days** | **972M** | **$1.944B** | Same |
| **5.4M × 365 days** | **1.971B** | **$3.942B** | Same |

All lines use the same illustrative $2 unit target and full daily-ration-equivalent assumption. They are **not donor funding requirements**, because not all 5.4M people need the same level of food assistance and many have access to food through markets. Medical nutrition, fortified complementary foods, RUTF and refeeding care are independently required for indicated people.

**Critical benchmark: existing Unity Gel v0.1.** The [2026-10-26 white paper](unity-gel-microbial-protein-global-nutrition-security-whitepaper-v0.1-2026-10-26.md) models **$2.88 variable cost per 1,000-kcal XL pouch** at 1M units; two modeled pouches cost **$5.76/day** for a claimed nominal 2,000 kcal. That 2026-10-26 v0.4 preliminary recipe **has not been built, safety-tested, micronutrient-qualified or production-quoted**. It is a ~288g moist emulsion per pouch, not the 500g dry complete daily ration modeled here. **Reaching $2/day implies cutting even that unverified modeled cost by 65.3%**, while changing format, sourcing, packaging and nutrition. Yeast supplier aspiration of <$1/kg cannot be substituted for edible food-grade delivered ingredient price.

**Necessary RFI fields:** digestible protein cost per kg; fats with essential fatty acid coverage; safe carbohydrates and fiber; micronutrient premix cost and upper limits; heavy-metal/mycotoxin and microbial screens; source water; processing heat; shelf life; thermal/aseptic or dry-product handling; packaging barrier; energy; working capital; lab assays; waste; QA; operator labor; in-country transport; customs and taxes; regulatory approvals and independent food safety process authority.

## 4. Facility CAPEX and five-year/20-year infrastructure comparisons

**Illustrative per-factory CAPEX** (not quotations): $50M / $100M / $150M for a 132-t/day nominal finished-food complex. These are sensitivity inputs, not findings. The lower case may be unrealistic for more advanced processing or difficult utilities. All three **exclude independently priced upstream fermentation factories and significant off-site upgrades**.

| Scenario | 24-plant CAPEX | + six months of full $2/day production | Still excluded |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Very optimistic | **$1.20B** | **$3.144B** | Logistics, clinical care, WASH, import inputs, finance |
| Intermediate | **$2.40B** | **$4.344B** | Same |
| Higher-cost sensitivity | **$3.60B** | **$5.544B** | Same |

At constant 5.4M ration-equivalent coverage, **five years of $2/day manufactured food cost $19.71B** (5 × 365 × 5.4M × $2); plant construction and operation overhead may increase total, and inflation, utilization, freight and nutrient composition will affect outcomes. A **20-year productive asset** has value only if competitive, maintained, financed, supplied and operated without crowding out existing producers. At the scenario's 90% uptime and 95% product yield, nominal 24 × 132-t/day facilities could make ~1.98B daily ration-equivalents/year, assuming sufficient demand and qualified inputs.

**Correct comparison:** a 5–20-year **total cost of ownership** for locally manufactured adequate food **versus** a realistic package of imported/commercially procured rations + cash transfers + existing local market purchases + resilience programs. Include donor discount rates, construction/commissioning time, emergency bridge, depreciation, energy and water, climate risks, governance and access. Do not compare one-time CAPEX to a full humanitarian appeal as if the latter only bought food.

## 5. Practical implementation — build the productive system first, preserve emergency feeding

**Phase A — Public feasibility and a Somali-led steering group (0–6 months):** Verify the food-security baseline by district and cohort, undertake security and siting analysis, secure supply and utility plans, conduct community consultation, establish a transparent cost ledger and independent nutrition/food safety review. Solicit real bids for **one** expandable plant before any 24-plant commitment. Publish sensitivity and negative findings.

**Phase B — One operating demonstration site:** Start with food-grade purchased ingredients in a licensed blending, fortification and packaging facility. Conduct laboratory assay, shelf-life validation, cultural taste studies, religious/dietary suitability checks, ingredient-origin audits, and independent harm analysis. Benchmark existing local food and voucher options. Define an explicit **stop/go** gate before distributing food as a sole-source daily diet.

**Phase C — Regional replication:** If demonstrated economics and health outcomes warrant, replicate to 4 sites, then 8, then up to 24, with separate QA laboratories, geographically diversified ingredient supply, water and power resilience and supply-chain emergency buffers. Avoid replacing commercial local agriculture with free external food.

**Phase D — Local upstream value chains:** Develop Somali-owned and regionally integrated agriculture, edible oils, quality grain processing, safe fermentation, micronutrient blending, packaging manufacture, spare parts, industrial maintenance and training. Fermentation is an option to validate, **not a precondition** for local food packaging.

**Phase E — Financial and local-ownership transition:** Public purchasing contracts tied to food-safety and nutritional performance, Somali SME participation, transparent cooperative or company ownership, apprentice programs, nonexclusive open technical standards, audited fiduciary controls, anti-corruption safeguards and cash/market assistance where it works better.

**Emergency exception:** Deliver existing validated food and therapeutic nutrition **immediately** wherever needed. Building a plant must not cause a six-month delay for starving people, nor condition humanitarian relief on participation in employment or production programs.

## 6. Questions for WFP, FAO, UNICEF, OCHA, Somali agencies, NGOs and manufacturers

This is an **open request for independent review and collaboration, not a claim of partnership, endorsement, or notification**.

1. Can each agency release or identify audited 2022–2026 **appeal / funding received / money spent** by country, sector and lasting asset class, avoiding interagency double counting?
2. What domestically owned **food, water, storage, power and production infrastructure** have previous projects already built, and what do survival/operating metrics say?
3. What share of the most affected population is better served by **cash/vouchers, locally sourced staples, fortified supplementary foods or individualized clinical treatment**, versus complete-ration substitution?
4. What is the independently certified lowest **per-day cost** for safe, adequate, acceptable adult nutrition produced locally, with verified bulk ingredient purchase prices?
5. Where would an initial Somali-owned **100–132-t/day finished-food plant** be commercially viable with existing power, water, road and food standards?
6. What impact does local production have on **existing pastoralists, farmers, millers, traders and women-led SMEs**, and which safeguards prevent displacement?
7. Can public procurement underwrite sustainable demand without creating new rent-seeking or replacing market-based household choice?
8. What measurable changes over 1, 3 and 5 years show **less recurring emergency need** rather than merely more meals distributed?
