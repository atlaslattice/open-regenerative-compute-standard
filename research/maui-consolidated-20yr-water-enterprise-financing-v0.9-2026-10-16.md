# Maui Atlas Lattice / ORCS — Consolidated 20-Year Water Enterprise and Full Acquisition Scenario v0.9
**Date: October 16, 2026. Classification: modeled screening / no consent, acquisition, construction, price commitment or realized profits.** Standalone new file, preserving all prior versions. Companion runnable script: [maui_20yr_enterprise_v09.py](maui_20yr_enterprise_v09.py).

## Design / policy objective
Bring all Maui Island POTABLE, RAW, IRRIGATION and WASTEWATER utility *provider assets* under one integrated **public-interest enterprise** over time by negotiated purchase, legally permitted transfers or county-controlled long-term service agreements, subject to independent appraisal, regulatory approval, public trust and Native Hawaiian rights, and avoidance of compromising residents' access or historic lawful entitlements. Not a claim that Maui County currently has legal authority to compel every sale. Electric utility acquisition is OUTSIDE this water-utility model; all-campus electric microgrids are IN SCOPE. The full 1,014mi historical cross-island County main inventory is intentionally retained as a cost BENCHMARK, per project direction, but does not imply 1,014mi should all be elevated/replaced.
Proposed campus capacities: **West 6 MGD, North 5 MGD, Central 25 MGD, 36 MGD nameplate** and household/essential service first. Expected 85% normal-duty output **30.60 MGD** is an assumed annual production factor, NOT instantaneous outage guarantee. Each campus links seawater desal, residual ZLD, municipally/private-sourced wastewater treatment, cesspool conversion/reuse, material extraction, on-site inference computing, and independent safely islandable microgrids. East Maui: replace nonlocal interbasin irrigation diversions, safeguard native community/kalo/domestic and ecological stream rights.

- **Maui Land & Pineapple 2025 water activity:** ~$2.9M sales, a WHOLESALE upstream line that may be recorded again in downstream Kāʻanapali DWS/customer bill; first phase county MOU only, not completed deal. [MLP 2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63330/000143774926010765/mlp20251231_10k.htm); [County May 2026 MOU](https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/18839).

- Hawaiʻi legislation cited average cesspool conversion ~$20k–$50k depending on technology; here **12,200** historical Maui Island candidate cesspools × assumed $35k = **$427M** BEFORE survey count change and costs of new off-site pipe/sewer catchments; avoid duplication with additional sewer capex. [Nayar et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0011916418324500). [Study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0255270124001119). Energy, salt purity and solid waste disposal remain critical.

## 20-year fully amortizing financing, full build at year 1
Annuity at **5%**, 20 equal annual payments, no grants or subsidized debt. A 40-year debt case is sensitivity but the displayed 20-year cashflow would end with 20 additional years of unpaid principal and payments (not 'paid off' in 2046).
| Construction scope | Total capex | Annual loan payment | Annual modeled revenues | Annual cash OPEX | **Funding deficit per year** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 25% of full historical pipe mileage | **$4.720B** | **$378.7M** | $142.7M | $376.1M | **$612.1M** |
| 50% | **$5.734B** | **$460.1M** | $142.7M | $376.1M | **$693.5M** |
| Full 1,014mi | **$7.762B** | **$622.8M** | $142.7M | $376.1M | **$856.2M** |
| Full 1,014mi, 40yr debt | $7.762B | $452.4M | $142.7M | $376.1M | $685.7M |

**Critical result:** under these assumptions even the 25% pipe scenario has an ~$233.4M annual **operating gap before financing** (OPEX $376.1M minus revenue $142.7M). No asset-leverage trick or zero-interest bond can make the *full stipulated service* fund itself unless the assumed costs fall, funding/revenues rise, or new social/environmental public grants support the operation. An apparent import spending 'margin' doesn't cover a $612–856M funding deficit.
A 20-year cash-flow CSV created by running the Python program uses stable nominal annual revenues/expenses for years **2027–2046** only as a full-build *steady-state counterfactual*, NOT a realistic construction ramp. Actual phasing requires separate year-by-year investment/commissioning, availability, changing customer base, interest during construction, reserves and scheduled reinvestment.
**Primo baseline:** Companywide 2025 net margin 1.2% is a comparator, NOT island manufacturer achievable net. Hypothetical 30% local NET margin would require annual bottling profit after proportionate $40M plant capital service, tax, depreciation and distribution; this scenario's 33.3% pre-capital contribution is insufficient by itself to certify a 30% net goal. $24.27M sales is not an extra $24.27M in cash *savings* from imports on top of enterprise sales: it is the same market transfer once.

## Consolidation plan: lawful, orderly and residents-first
1. **Public asset register:** DWS assets already public; register privately owned retail utility assets, private wastewater/sewer, wholesale water sources, irrigation cooperatives, easements, contracted operators, regulated tariffs, all customer counts and private pension/legacy liabilities; separate Molokaʻi/Lānaʻi contracts and full county benchmark.
2. **Acquisition sequencing:** pursue fully approved Launiupoko/Olowalu transaction if closing and independent diligence support; negotiate MLP source assets respecting HWS wholesale contracts; only then evaluate Kāʻanapali and Kapalua HWS and other retail distribution/wastewater services after PUC filing, independent engineering and discounted cashflow; community/Native Hawaiian traditional rights, public trust and existing residents protected. For nonpurchasable assets, pursue authorized interconnection or long-term offtake rather than claiming forced sale.
3. **Operations and redundancy:** staged assimilation, reserve existing supply wells/tanks for disaster standby until campus potable mains confirmed safe; prevent premature system retirement and stranded debt.
4. **Cesspools:** public finance and household assistance / cluster systems to meet 2050 legal requirements; treat recurring costs, ownership/resident consent and high-priority groundwater impacts transparently; do not demand at-cost household capital recovery with no subsidy.
5. **Food, materials and industry:** convert clean captured organics to useful fuels, soil amendments, struvite and gypsum subject to trace quality; sell recovered commodities only with purchase contracts; avoid claimed full salt-mine revenues before actual assay.
6. **Community tariffs:** protected low-price residential and emergency basic needs; optional hotel/resort premium and industrial reclaimed offtake, without artificial fees classified as private financial profit. Visitor financing requires legislated authority and independent projections. Public enterprise board requires water-rights and resident representation and transparent KPI/OPEX audits.
7. **Construction financing portfolio:** investigate clean-water and drinking-water SRFs, federal resilience/capital grants, disaster recovery and eligible environmental funds, tax-exempt bonds, real utility debt, confirmed resort capacity contracts, philanthropic capital and carefully structured leases. Each pot explicitly recorded **once**; debt-funded construction is not enterprise operating revenue. Grants reduce loan principal, not added income. Do not assume federal funds are allocated.
8. **Phased practicality:** first execute wastewater and cesspool pilot/stream offsets, limited fire-safe interties and district metering, then build only licensed capacity supported by verified island demand and permits; pursue ZLD pilot with actual energy and commercial brine-product testing before financing a $700M facility, and avoid 100% blanket pipe replacements unless life-cycle proof supports.

## Analytical limitations and near-term mandatory work
**The largest missing data are existing Maui Sewer Fund audits, comprehensive private utility purchase prices and debts, actual island district demand/pipe maps, RO/ZLD firm electrical power costs, verified customer water-use tariffs, operating expense benchmarks by real site, actual 2026 bottled market import share, and guaranteed capital grants.** Until those exist, results are a **stress-test showing scale**, not a bankable pro forma, legally authorized takeover, net welfare model or guaranteed public savings.
**Crucial distinctions:** financial deficit ≠ proof project socially worthless; hypothetical grants ≠ revenue already secured; displaced imports can be local economic value added but are NOT free fiscal cash; island resilience and restored instream flows are valuable separate benefit ledgers, not automatically budgeted cash to debt service. Retiring wells without rights and aquifer/stream monitoring may harm communities. ZLD solid hazardous residues remain disposal obligation.
