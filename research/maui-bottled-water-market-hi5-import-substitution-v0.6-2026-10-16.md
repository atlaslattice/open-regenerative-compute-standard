# ORCS Maui v0.6 — Bottled-Water Import Substitution, Qualified Recycled Packaging, and Public-Interest Revenue
**2026-10-16 | Research / modeled / unverified local market. New companion to v0.1–v0.5; no overwrites.**
**Headline:** No official Maui Island *bottled-water-only units or net profit* ledger has yet been located in accessible public records. We located regulated report forms, official statewide bottle counts, airport contracts, actual Maui displayed sales prices and a US industry consumption benchmark to construct an explicitly non-verified demand sensitivity.

## Sources and measured anchors
1. **Hawaiʻi DOH FY2024 HI-5:** ~538 million redeemed deposit containers at 54.82% redemption -> ~981.4 million eligible beverage containers SOLD **statewide across water, soda, juice, alcoholic beverages etc.** This is NOT the bottled-water market or Maui volume. State DOH 2025 OSWM report: https://health.hawaii.gov/shwb/files/2025/08/2025-OSWM-LEGISLATIVE-REPORT.pdf and Auditor 25-08 https://files.hawaii.gov/auditor/Reports/2025/25-08.pdf . The audit raised concerns about distributor self-report completeness and verification.
2. **Critical data granularity correction:** The official HI-5 Monthly Distributor Report Form enumerates containers sold by MATERIAL (aluminum, bi-metal, PET, HDPE, glass); it DOES NOT show water-only units nor an island sale field. Therefore simple aggregate HI-5 reports cannot answer Maui bottled-water demand. Product-list files identify brands/sizes but do NOT establish units sold by product. Source: https://health.hawaii.gov/hi5/files/2021/09/Monthly-Distributor-Report-Form.pdf . Program: https://health.hawaii.gov/hi5/distributors/ . Water-only and Maui island breakouts must be sought from sales/distributor POS inventory data or other sufficiently detailed independently collected records; don't imply DOH necessarily holds them.
3. **IBWA/Beverage Marketing Corporation preliminary 2025 U.S. benchmark:** 47.5 gallons bottled water per person/year, covering all package formats; roughly 70% of US packaged bottled-water volume single-serve PET. These are NATIONAL behavior ratios, **not observed Maui purchases**. https://bottledwater.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2025-Progress-Report_FINAL.pdf
4. **Maui tourism 2025:** $5.97 billion visitor expenditure; 2.52 million arrivals and $304.98 average visitor spend/day. Derived ~19.575 million visitor-days = expenditures/daily spend, approximate and subject to consistency of the population/scope. https://hta.hawaii.gov/what-we-do/plans-reports/dmaps/maui-dmap-2026-2028/
5. **Maui County 2025 residents:** 160,592 includes Molokaʻi and Lānaʻi; **NOT Maui Island alone**. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/release/tables?eid=163559&rid=119
6. **Kahului airport concession:** HDOT awarded International Shoppes a ten-year retail contract starting April 2026; food/beverage outlets and other retailers may hold separate concessions. No bottled-water-unit procurement totals were found. https://hidot.hawaii.gov/administration/hdot-welcomes-international-shoppes-as-new-concessionaire-at-honolulu-and-kahului-airports/ ; actual contract entry https://hidot.hawaii.gov/administration/lm/
7. **Maui retail pricing example—not wholesale:** Maui Grocery Service online lists Kirkland 40x500ml for $15.99 ($0.40/unit) and Aquafina 24x16.9oz for $13.99 (~$0.58/unit); local delivered retail/convenience channel prices include markups and are **NOT** proof retailer landed-buy prices. https://mauigroceryservice.com/products.aspx?aisle=23&shelf=173
8. **Maui County recycling reported Dec 2024:** 140.44 'tons' collected/shipped #1 and #2 plastic bottles combined by County Recycling Centers. Reporting period and whether US short tonnes need explicit verification; includes HDPE and does not cover all HI-5 PET channels; not evidence of 140.44 metric tonnes/year of usable food-grade PET. https://www.mauicounty.gov/2519/Where-does-Maui-County-recycling-go
9. FDA reclaimed plastic food contact guidance: https://www.fda.gov/food/packaging-food-contact-substances-fcs/recycled-plastics-food-packaging . Plastic from wastewater microplastic capture cannot simply be used in food-grade bottles.

## Top-down demand reconstruction (SIMULATED; not an observed Maui market)
Residence person-years ~160,592 (county, slight upper-bias for Maui Island). Visitor person-years ~19.575M visitor-days /365=53,630. Combined ≈214,222 person-years.
Applying **national** 47.5 gal/person-year => **10.18M gallons/year** of bottled water across all package forms (possible over/under depending on Maui consumption, climate, visitor purchasing and refill behavior).
At 70% SINGLE-SERVE PET VOLUME and 0.5 liter equivalent => ~7.12M gallons/yr PET and **53.93M 500-mL EQUIVALENTS**. This is NOT 53.93M physical bottles actually sold: PET package sizes differ. Model actual SKU mix before designing line speed.
This represents ~0.0195 MGD PET water volume, tiny compared with proposed 28 MGD freshwater nameplate.
No island-specific import fraction is known. Local producers (including Tropic Water and other existing Hawaiian operators) already serve some demand; replacing them is NOT mainland import substitution.

### Wholesale sales and contribution sensitivity
Assume an EXPLICITLY HYPOTHETICAL blended $0.45 per 500-mL equivalent wholesale, $0.27 in variable input/fill/pack/distribution costs and $2.0M/year fixed operating cost (excludes debt service, depreciation/capital recovery, regulatory setup, major local food-grade rPET reprocessing plant and tax/financing treatment).
| Market share of top-down modeled PET water equivalent | 500ml equivalents/year | Wholesale revenue | Initial operating cash after assumed costs |
|25%|13.48M|$6.07M|$0.43M|
|50%|26.96M|$12.13M|$2.85M|
|75%|40.44M|$18.20M|$5.28M|
|100%|53.93M|$24.27M|$7.71M|
THIS IS **not** verified available Maui market, an operating forecast or available county profit. Formula cash = share*53.93M*($0.45-$0.27)-$2.0M, no vertical integration CAPEX. Prices should be measured per island retail/distributor group; margin cannot be assumed to improve merely due to local origin. Brand marketing, retailer rebates, returns, airport concession terms, deposits/fees and supply-chain quality controls matter.
**Alternative stress:** At $0.30/unit wholesale and $0.27 variable, 50% capture contributes only $0.81M vs $2M fixed -> ~-$1.19M; at $0.55 and $0.30 variable, 50% capture yields $6.74M contribution and ~$4.74M before debt. Cost curves require actual quotes.
**Key financial separation:** (a) cash enterprise operating contribution, (b) local value added and jobs, (c) imported finished-goods displacement, (d) net freight saving, (e) PET collection avoided cost, and (f) county tax income. Never add gross imports replaced and manufacturer sales as independent profits.

## PET mass balance
At modeled 53.93M 500ml units * assumed 15 grams PET/unit -> **809 metric tons virgin-equivalent PET product demand annually** under 100% capture; 50% capture ~404t. Package size, actual PET mass, caps, labels, yields and quality certification alter real tonnage. Compare ONLY with full county/state PET collection tonnage across HI-5 and recycling centers using identical time period and material purity. Ingest pre-certified recycled PET preforms first; assess local high-grade decontamination/remelting CAPEX only after a verified feedstock and guaranteed outlet. County mixed #1/#2 shipment figure cannot establish enough feedstock. Bins marked (1) food/organics, (2) redeemable bottles, (3) other recyclable plastics, (4) true residuals; audit sorting and capture efficiency.

## Procurement and public records request schedule
- **Hawaiʻi DOH HI-5:** Request any existing item/category-level (noncarbonated water) sales volumes and container types, geography and reporting basis for calendar 2024, 2025, 2026; specifically ask whether product list is connected to volume, whether Maui Island breakdown exists, whether personal/company confidential redactions apply and acceptable aggregation. Monthly published form is material-only, so expect missing category/island data.
- **HDOT/Kahului:** OGG retail concessions gross sales by store/channel/product category if recorded, separately all airport food/beverage operators' annual bottled-water procurement/volumes where public; contract revenue terms and independent retailers. Clarify that HDOT might only hold airport-wide concession fees, not specific water SKU sales.
- **Resort/grocery/restaurant/distributor procurement audit:** 12–24 months Maui Island wholesale case invoices, current delivered price, SKU size, volume, place of bottling vs distributor office, freight and handling/returns, food-grade packaging sourcing. County-owned enterprise can use cooperative/request-for-information or willing buyers; private commercial invoices not presumed obtainable by public records law.
- **Kahului Harbor ocean freight:** Matson/Pasha/Young Brothers relevant cargo categories, port-level delivery origin; generic cargo tonnage doesn't isolate finished bottled water.
- **Retail demand:** commercial point-of-sale scanner data (Circana/NielsenIQ etc.) may require paid license; distinguish grocery vs airport vs resorts, and household bulk vs on-premise; 2025 visitor estimates separate.
- **PET:** DOH redemption claims by island, polymer and comparable time period; County MRF weigh tickets, #1 PET vs #2 HDPE and food-grade processing yields.
- **Enterprise law:** legal review county authority, procurement, competition with existing Maui local bottlers, bottling FDA/DOH quality/labeling, deposit and food-grade PET standards; sustainability and resident-priority drinking-water obligation must remain enforceable.

## ORCS interpretation
The data suggest a potentially **multi-million-dollar annual contribution** to Maui's water program at sufficient verified market share, not a one-year multi-billion-dollar capital windfall. Bottling may be phased after safe municipal drinking-water service and water-reuse investment; its freshwater volumetric demand is negligible relative to 28 MGD, while plastic manufacturing/feedstock and distribution are the binding dependencies. Do not assert all tourist buyers accept same prices, a bottled-water import ban is legally workable, or that one exported bottle re-entering the mainland generates net climate benefits.
