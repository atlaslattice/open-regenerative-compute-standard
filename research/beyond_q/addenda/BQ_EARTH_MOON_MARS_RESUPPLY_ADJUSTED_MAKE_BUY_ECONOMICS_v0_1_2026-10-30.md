## Critical reframing
The question **“why manufacture locally when it is cheaper to import on Earth?”** is incomplete. For a space habitat, compare **full delivered and risk-adjusted** cost of external procurement with full lifecycle cost of on-site manufacture, while valuing time, mass, power, spare capacity, personnel effort, reliability and mission continuity. Results must be calculated, not presumed. High terrestrial production cost does not imply poor space economics. Conversely, local printing may depend on imported powders, gases, parts, specialty tools, firmware and metrology equipment.

## Decision calculation (per component and campaign)
- **Imported strategy cost** = purchase price + Earth transit and launch allocation + transport/landing/surface logistics + handling/storage + inventory carrying + expected resupply delay loss + expected mission interruption and redundancy costs + decommissioning/disposal.
- **Local strategy cost** = amortized fabrication equipment and facility capital + shipped or locally extracted feedstock + processing energy/heat + labor and training + tooling/consumables + postprocessing + metrology and qualification + scrap/rework + spare parts for the manufacturing system + common-cause outages + environmental and safety controls.
- Both sides use comparable time horizons, mission scenarios, discounting, confidence intervals, mass budgets, hazard consequences and reliability targets. **Avoid counting the same avoided delivery or downtime twice.**
- **Break-even condition:** model local strategy as preferable under the chosen objective when its full risk-adjusted lifecycle cost is lower than the comparably scoped imported strategy; allow multicriteria/Pareto selection when the objective includes resilience and scientific learning rather than falsely monetizing every benefit.

## Three separate boundaries
**E — Earth commercial:** grid-connected production, viable road/air/sea procurement; external purchase may have a low delivered cost.
**L — Lunar habitat:** launch/landing/surface movement, harsh dust/vacuum, local gravity, available energy and feedstocks, resupply cadence and reliability.
**M — Mars habitat:** longer and less flexible resupply opportunities, communication delay, longer interruption consequences, variable local resource availability and possibly much higher value of local repairs, water/nutrients/gases.
**R — Earth-based habitat analog R&D:** Earth manufacturing is not billed imaginary launch cost. Report both actual terrestrial cash flows and *separate shadow-model* L/M projected delivery economics. Any outside R&D funding belongs in its own real funding ledger.

## Scenario tests
For every candidate pump, bearing, food input, membrane, soap ingredient, fertilizer stream, additive-manufactured spare or medical/other essential supply:
1. Map bill of materials, qualification and manufacturing chain; note every dependency imported into the supposed on-site factory.
2. Source current terrestrial delivered cost and assumed launch/surface logistics separately.
3. Model mission-specific lifetime, replacement distribution, downtime, stored backups and rare catastrophic/common-cause failures.
4. Solve imported, local and hybrid strategies under environmental and safety constraints.
5. Sweep transport price, cadence, payload mass, extraction yield, process energy, scrap, failure probability and manufacturing utilization without false precision.
6. Report comparative results as **VERIFIED**, **DERIVED WITH SOURCES**, or **HYPOTHETICAL/SCENARIO**. Do not assign a numerical price for Moon/Mars delivery without explicit dated source or labeled hypothetical values.

## Design policy
Aim for increasing autonomy and eventual surplus capability, but do **not** force local manufacture if a hybrid solution is safer, more affordable, or robust. The strong twenty-year self-sufficiency vision is an *aspirational systems research target* and possible outcome—not permission to suppress contrary results.
