## Mission
Over an illustrative **20-year** research and engineering horizon, seek the highest technically credible local self-sufficiency for all significant campus material and service needs. Investigate the strong aspirational target of **100% self-sufficiency** across energy, water, food, breathable atmosphere, nutrient supply, chemicals, maintainable equipment and selected manufactured goods, while quantifying which loops are truly closed, which still need imports, and which are thermodynamically or economically infeasible. Aim for *surplus exports* beyond resilient onsite demand, including valuable locally distributed goods, but never count gross production as surplus before reserves and internal uses.

The target is a **research challenge**, not a false assurance that a finite campus can make all elements, eliminate entropy, fabricate every semiconductor or avoid all imports forever. The Moon/Mars analog purpose may justify some otherwise expensive onsite capability when its added research value is independently funded and measured.

## Import elimination and circular loop hierarchy
Every project team repeatedly:
1. **Inventory:** quantify all imported materials and services per period, including spare parts, maintenance labor, catalysts, microbes, micronutrients, energy, electronic components and critical supplies.
2. **Avoid:** eliminate demand where safe and feasible.
3. **Recover:** treat wastewater, residual nutrients, heat, carbon, salts, metals and useful byproducts as candidate feedstocks; assess contamination and losses.
4. **Substitute:** use alternative materials, simplify designs, design parts for repair/reuse.
5. **Produce locally:** assess agriculture, microbial foods, water treatment, chemical processing, additive manufacturing and local energy.
6. **Store and protect:** preserve reserves for hazards, seasonal variability and prolonged resupply cutoff.
7. **Export only true surplus:** quantify quality-approved output after operational requirements, emergency reserves, ecosystem needs and research consumption; calculate incremental economics and recipient benefits.

## Sector modules and benchmark outcomes
| Domain | Possible on-site R&D | Required proof / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical power | Fusion/renewable/storage/grid dispatch; critical independent backup | Real net-electric and uptime ledger; fusion remains conceptual, not present capability |
| Water | Desalination and wastewater reuse, condensate collection, potable and process water | Feedwater availability, contaminants, energy, consumables, brine/mineral fate, water quality and verified losses |
| Food | Crops, controlled-environment agriculture, precision fermentation, algae and microbial nutrition | Full nutrient adequacy, bioavailability, palatability, food safety, microbial stability, crop yield and area/energy |
| Fresh crops and ornamental flowers | Hydroponics, horticulture, native plant propagation, beneficial microbiomes | Nutrient and water cycle, lighting energy, CO2/O2, pollination, ecological and human quality-of-life contributions |
| Atmosphere | CO2 removal, oxygen management, filters, humidity and contamination control | Occupant safety, gas mass balance, leaks and emergency isolation |
| Fertilizer | Compost, struvite/phosphate recovery, treated organic residues, controlled nutrient manufacture | Heavy metals, pathogens, PFAS/microplastics, trace-element imports and soil/hydroponic compatibility |
| Soap and hygiene | Selected safe surfactants/soap formulation from renewable oils and recovered chemicals where feasible | Alkali and chemical input source, purity, quality control, contamination, packaging and comparative cost |
| Manufacturing | 3D printing selected repair parts, structural elements, containers, tools and spare components | Feedstock purification, mechanical tests, certification, energy consumption, tolerances, material classes and missing imports |
| Material recovery | Waste sorting; plastics/metals recovery; bioreactor residues; mineral separation | Recovered mass by grade; throughput; rejected hazardous fractions; true cradle-to-gate cost |
| Safety and life support | Adversarial digital twins, training, emergency reserves, fault containment | Independently auditable proof, validated hazards, fail-safe rather than absolute prohibition of all venting/discharge |

## Scored metrics
- **Import dependency (by mass, energy, embodied value and criticality):** imports / total corresponding physical demand. Avoid a single misleading global percent; track all four denominators.
- **Self-supply ratio** per material/service over 12-month rolling windows and in a prolonged-isolation stress test.
- **Water/nutrient/carbon recovery fractions**, with documented inventories, external feeds, hazardous residues and quality compliance.
- **Renewable/recoverable energy and exergy utilization**, with unavoidable waste heat rejection separately disclosed.
- **Resilience time:** duration of essential services under loss of external supply, for different population, outage and damage scenarios.
- **True exportable surplus:** quality-qualified product minus own demand, reserves and reprocessing/rejection; compare regional opportunity costs.
- **Economic split:** terrestrial unit economics vs separately funded Moon/Mars R&D premiums and demonstrable knowledge transfer. Avoid double counting.
- **Closure exceptions:** imported isotope, Be, catalysts, filter media, electronics, trace nutrients and unavoidable consumables are visibly listed rather than silently assumed local.

## Nightmare Lab 'Full-Stack Challenge' process
An open, human-reviewed workstream invites interdisciplinary proposals for each dependency or waste stream. Teams submit (i) source/feedstock, (ii) mass-energy balances, (iii) expected performance and uncertainty, (iv) capital and operating costs, (v) safety constraints, (vi) resource-tradeoff and externalities, (vii) space-habitat transferable learning, and (viii) reproducible tests.
An independent adversarial reviewer asks: Why not purchase? Why not reuse? What fails on day 150 of isolation? What if a microbial culture crashes? Does making soap locally require more imports than buying it? Are local exports a real benefit or merely subsidized by unpriced research costs? No project receives a passing grade from its own proposing AI.
Uncontrolled routine process venting/discharge is disfavored; **required emergency pressure relief, life safety, hygienic isolation and physically unavoidable heat rejection remain permitted and designed**.

## Twenty-year cadence (illustrative)
**0–3 years:** baseline imports and wastes, life-cycle inventory, sector roadmaps, research instrumentation.
**3–7:** modular bioreactor/water/agriculture/manufacturing pilots, independent nutrient and quality validation.
**7–12:** integrated heat/gas/water/nutrient loop, spare parts manufacturing and multi-month isolation experiments.
**12–16:** progressively longer isolation and Moon/Mars transferability tests with independent physics, microbial and radiological validation.
**16–20:** demonstrated self-supply percentages for each domain, an audited remaining-imports register, local surplus where real, and a decision on next-generation habitats.
No promise of 100% success or externally funded program unless demonstrated.

## Science and mission exclusions
- Earth proof-of-concept is not proof of lunar or Martian deployment; gravity, radiation, feedstock chemistry and resupply differ.
- Energy is converted, not created from nothing. Local production cannot be 'closed loop' for every element without feedstock and unavoidable entropy/export of heat.
- Food-grade processes must remain physically separate from nuclear/radiological chemical handling.
- An impressive *percent recycled* is not automatically lower cost, lower hazard, improved public welfare, or meaningful long-duration autonomy.
- Reactor B remains 1.13 GW D-T fusion reference and Reactor A remains a hypothetical 20 MW primary D-D reference; no numerical model modified.

**Core design challenge:** Every imported dependency and every residual stream is an invitation to investigate—but no proposal earns a waiver from physical conservation, safety, evidence or honest economic accounting.
