Each hypothetical configuration assumes one adult per unshared room, exactly two per voluntary shared room; real allowed occupancy depends on **actual sleeping-room floor area**, exits, fire rating, bathrooms, lodging registration, insurance, disability access and local inspection. **No construction plan assumes all 80 beds are legally available.** Shared rooms should come with lower **per-person** charges, adequate storage, quiet-hours rules for incompatible shifts, private bathroom access and a right to decline without employment retaliation. Housekeeping and janitorial workers deserve the same respect and safe arrangements as engineers.

**Port Aransas legal diligence:** City code classifies stays of at least 30 consecutive days differently for hotel-tax purposes; the city website indicates 9% city hotel tax plus 6% state on many shorter stays, but the page contains a rate inconsistency in its explanatory text. Confirm exemptions, 30-day tenancy protections and proper rate administration in writing. Texas Property Code Chapter 92 and newer Local Government Code §§211.053–054 impose occupancy/tenancy and health-safety rules; a hotel may be treated differently from a conventional dwelling in some contexts. Do not infer that the new 2025 dwelling-unit statute automatically exempts motel rooms from health/fire requirements.

## Finance: start from observed affordability, not $110/night

Compare **40 continuously paid private worker room-months** against **40 rooms, 50% shared**, where the first case houses 40 workers and the second houses 60. Sharing expands bed coverage **without automatically doubling room revenue**: if the operator charges employers a flat $1,650/room/month, **40 rooms × $1,650 × 12 = $792,000/year gross** irrespective of whether rooms are private or shared.

| Room-block scenario | 40 rooms annual gross lodging |
|---|---:|
| User's observed historical $1,300/month, full payment | $624,000 |
| Proposed $1,650/month negotiated contractor rate, full payment | $792,000 |
| Proposed $2,000/month serviced rate, full payment | $960,000 |
| $1,650/month, **90% paid room-months**, e.g. uncertain demand | **$712,800** |

**Counterparties need genuine year-round room-block guarantees**, not speculative plant staffing. If all 58 rooms were occupied continuously at the user's historical $1,300 monthly rate, annual lodging gross would be **$904,800**, which is not property NOI, nor enough on its own to demonstrate an attractive $7M purchase. Corporate rate must reflect independent competitively procured catering, cleaning, utilities, staff and transportation, not arbitrary markups or worker wage extraction.

**Dining feasibility screen:** assuming 60 residents (optional shared case), 70% buy one meal at $13 on 22 workdays/month => **60×70%×22×12×$13 = $144,144/year meal sales**. A hypothetical 25% meal contribution is **$36,036 before overhead, food permits, buildout, licensing and shared services**. A food-service contract for Harbor Island worksite meals could raise demand but **cannot be assumed**. Contractor shifts may run nights and weekends, and the ferry may have queues; meals and shuttle schedules need shift-specific logistics.

**Tourist/executive retained rooms:** if 18 rooms yield $125 average daily rate ×55% occupancy ×365 days => **$451,688/year gross room sales**, before visitor marketing, room costs and tourism seasonality. Combined $712,800 worker plus ~$451,688 visitors plus $144,144 meals = **~$1.309M/year gross sales**, not profit. Retail rent, existing owner residence, parking capacity, owner labor and actual historic guest reservations are not added without contracts and data.

**Illustrative $7M purchase + $850k modernization allowance** = **$7.85M before taxes/transaction costs/working capital**. At 7% financing for 20 years, *if the entire acquisition and renovation were financed*, annual annuity **~$741k**, which leaves a substantial need for actual net operating earnings, not gross revenue. Do not assume external grant money will purchase a private hotel. Seller equity, debt service, insurance, repairs, hurricane/wind exposure, year-round seasonality and corporate rate contract govern ROI. Construction and operations groups could instead lease the property without buying it; lease-first is a strong way to test real demand.

## Renovation and amenity program — staged, no assumed entitlements

**Phase 1:** clean, modernize operations (digital reservation/PMS, smart/keypad access compatible with fire code and cyber/accessibility rules), high-speed WiFi, safe lighting, routine grounds care, housekeeping training and workforce privacy; refresh rooms for long stays. Verify actual utility consumption and install PV/BESS only if life-cycle economics and storm safety support it.

**Phase 2:** inspect existing kitchen/lounge for food-service equipment, ventilation, health permit, sanitation and fire suppression. Start breakfast, shift-ready boxed meals and healthy dinner service with fair service charges. Pet-friendly units, laundry, shuttle/ferry timetable and accessible accommodations. Check court geometry/surfacing/lighting, offer safe recreational basketball, native/low-water-demand coastal botanical gardens, shaded wellness spaces, and off-season community access if owners and city approve.

**Phase 3:** optional executive/business-visit experience and licensed retreat program for maritime trades, water/energy apprenticeships and regional resilience projects, without implying military affiliation or making unsupported claims about criminal activity. A purported 'NBA training-camp quality' court requires dimensions, NBA requirements and capital engineering assessment; do not use that phrase as an achieved fact.
