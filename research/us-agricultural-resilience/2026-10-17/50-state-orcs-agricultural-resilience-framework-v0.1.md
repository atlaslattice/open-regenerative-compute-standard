# ORCS National Agricultural Resilience & Pre-Planting Exchange — 50-State Framework v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-17  
**Status:** ILLUSTRATIVE / PROPOSED / NON-CANON / ZERO REALIZED CREDIT  
**Purpose:** Generalize the Hawaiʻi farm-first circular resources + food demand + verified offtake architecture into a reproducible 50-state feasibility and scenario engine **before** calibrating Texas. Not a forecast, program mandate, negotiated contract or state-level economic output claim.

## Executive proposition

Build a state/county/crop dashboard and machine-readable agricultural intelligence interface that coordinates local and interstate food resilience *without centrally directing farmers' private production decisions*. Combine nutrient and water circularity, soil health, regenerative practices, demand signals, voluntary pre-planting purchase commitments, fair-price market access, disaster reserves and transparent external dependency accounting.

**Primary outcomes:** marketable nutritious domestic food, resident affordability, farmer net margin, verified soil/water gains, less preventable spoilage, domestic supply continuity, lower imported inputs, and transparent after-reserve exportable surplus.

See Hawaiʻi pilot: [Hawaiʻi farmer-first exchange v0.1](../../agricultural-resilience/2026-10-17/hawaii-farmer-first-agricultural-resilience-exchange-v0.1.md). This framework intentionally does **not** project Hawaiʻi 15% fertilizer discounts, water tariffs, wastewater flows or acreage changes across the U.S.

## 1. National evidence envelope — avoid denominator errors

- **2022 USDA Census:** **$543.1B** U.S. farm agricultural products sold, **1.9M** farms, **880.1M** farmland acres. This includes livestock, nonfood products, and crops unsuitable for pre-planting specialty-produce exchange. It is NOT total marketplace GMV. Source: https://data.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Highlights/2024/Census22_HL_FarmEconomics_FINAL.pdf .
- **2024 revised USDA Food Dollar:** U.S. farm establishments' share **11.8¢ per dollar** of domestically produced consumer food; **88.2¢ marketing share** includes processing, labor, transport, restaurants and retail. It does NOT mean brokers collect 88.2% commission. Source: https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar/summary-findings .
- **EPA 2022 Clean Watersheds Needs Survey:** state facility and infrastructure capital-needs data, NOT a verified sum of available wastewater nutrients. Source: https://www.epa.gov/cwns/clean-watersheds-needs-survey-cwns-2022-report-and-data .
- **ORCS wastewater v0.3:** national scenario 50% qualified recoveries of ~**300,000 t elemental P**, **610,700 t ammonium-N**, **424,413 t K2O eq.** with gross dated commodity-equivalent value ~$2.38B (not product sales or net profit). Recoveries and analytical inventories are assumptions, not state allocations. Link: [wastewater v0.3](../../us-wastewater-resources/v0.3-2026-10-16/report.md).
- **PACA:** licensing and trust/contract protections applicable to covered fresh/frozen produce businesses and brokers. Source: https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/paca/licensing .
- Additional principal data families: USDA NASS Census/Quick Stats, USDA AMS Market News, USDA ERS, USDA Ag Transportation Open Data, USDA NRCS soils and conservation, USGS water use, NOAA/NWS weather and drought, EPA ECHO/DMR/CWNS, EPA food recovery estimates, state water and agricultural agencies, Census import/export (not sufficient alone for intranational commerce), DOE/EIA/utility rate data, state manure facility permits, voluntary processor/retailer procurement.

## 2. State/county × crop × nutrient × water × time accounting

For every state s, county c, crop k, period t, nutrient n:
- **Food deficit:** projected qualified local supply vs nutrition/demand/food-institution procurement, season, suitable climate, logistics, and consumer affordability. National domestic supply can move interstate without being treated as imports from abroad. Preserve geographic boundaries.
- **Agronomic nutrient demand:** crop removal and agronomic losses plus safe soil-stock restoration minus biological N fixation, retained organic nutrients and sustainable internal cycling.
- **Additional qualified nutrient supply:** separately measured wastewater, food-waste digestate, collectable confined manure, crop residues, permitted biosolids, tested ash/minerals and desal/brackish residuals; do not count residues twice or count pasture nutrients removed as new production.
- **Physical import dependence:** use N, P2O5, K2O, Ca, Mg, S, product-grade inventory and domestic vs international origin, not dollar proxies. State-to-state input movement is redistribution, not necessarily U.S. external reliance.
- **Water balance:** existing withdrawals vs qualified treated reuse, tailwater recovery, evapotranspiration and seasonal scarcity. Reuse does not create new water. Preserve ecosystems and water rights.
- **Supply security:** domestic priority, minimum reserve thresholds, resilient alternate routes and price/stock conditions; proposed export policy scenarios must respect existing law and contractual rights.
- **Cost/benefit:** producer gross margins, labor and capital, spoilage, fuel, water, nutrient input, harvest processing, transportation, broker fee, real consumer prices, public externalities; mark gross receipts vs disjoint avoided expenditure vs incremental net benefit.
- **Market:** projected supply, verified buyer intentions, enforceable offtake and actually paid shipments. Separate demand 'interest' from legally binding orders; never imply guarantee without security for buyer payment and grower performance.

A national model must guard cross-state double counting: a state importing food from another state is not a national shortage; a state's wastewater nutrient inventories must net biosolids, food and manure material streams already reported elsewhere.

## 3. Full-stack model architecture

Layer A: sensors, open data and provenance (unit normalization, dataset vintage, geographic/seasonal coverage, MEASURED/SIMULATED/PROPOSED/QUARANTINED/RECEIPTS).
Layer B: national food requirements and county crop suitability/water/soil constraint engine; farm user contributions only with explicit permission.
Layer C: distributed waste → certified nutrient, biogas, soil amendment, irrigation water and avoided treatment/disposal ledger.
Layer D: soil science optimizer (nutrient removal, legumes, mixed cropping, pH, organic carbon, infiltration, disease, yield, labor). Evaluate economic and ecological interaction terms, not additive percentage stacking.
Layer E: verified forward buyer procurements and pre-planting offtake contracts; high- and low-confidence supply/price forecast bands.
Layer F: routing, cold chain, grade, storage, delivery and financing; constrained resilience and disaster plans.
Layer G: multimodal/multilingual voice front-end + human agronomist; common API and user-granted private data; right to decline automated recommendations.
Layer H: audit, access equity, security, fairness, antitrust and conflict-of-interest review.

## 4. A *scenario*, not a national $ forecast

Illustrative matching service at **1.0%, 1.5%, 2.0%** of qualifying fulfilled, paid matched transactions:

| Annual matched paid transaction volume (hypothesis) | 1.0% gross fee | 1.5% gross fee | 2.0% gross fee |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $100M | $1.0M | $1.5M | $2.0M |
| $1B | $10M | $15M | $20M |
| $10B | $100M | $150M | $200M |

These are pure multiplications, not assertions of market share, net profit or brokerage displacement; the $543.1B farm-sales denominator is not eligible marketplace volume. Payment security, actual counterparty fulfillment and logistics are potentially material costs. The exchange must demonstrate improved actual grower net receipts *and* resident affordability.

**Integrated five-year sensitivity targets, not evidence-based national projections:** test adoption bands 1/5/15% of eligible farm sales, incremental crop contribution margins and net input requirements with field-trial calibration by geography. Do not publish one U.S.-wide '20% fertilizer reduction + 10% yield gain' as a reliable forecast. Example only: 20% less purchased fertilizer/acre and 10% more marketable production/acre implies 27.3% less purchased fertilizer per unit of output if both are independently measured and comparable. No-till is not universally labor- or cost-saving and may have tradeoffs with weeds, herbicides and local soils.

Required ranked target lanes based on *evidence*, not presupposed winners: (a) strong seasonal food import substitution; (b) nutrient recovery co-located with high existing disposal costs; (c) affordable reclaimed irrigation where water rights permit; (d) verified institutional off-take; (e) small-holder market access and short shelf-life spoilage; (f) disaster and fuel vulnerability; (g) soil restoration ROI.

Build a national state matrix with separate subtotals for international imports, interstate shipments, in-state production and qualified saleable circular products. The full 50 states and counties belong in the simulator; this whitepaper does not invent 50 rows of missing state data.

## 8. Required safeguards and failure tests

No marketplace coordination of competing farmers' prices, output restrictions or market allocations; publish privacy-protected aggregate forecasts with suppression thresholds and lag as necessary. Independent farmers determine their production and prices. PACA applicability and state licensing/antitrust law review before brokering; privacy/consent and consumer protection tests. Avoid exposing individual confidential grower intentions to competitors or buyers without explicit rights. Protect farmworkers, existing water users, community land rights and local procurement legality. Farm-product origin and real buyer demand auditable. Regulatory approval needed for wastewater-derived soil products.

Falsification checks: input and output mass conservation; independent nutrient/product grades; no yield claims without crop-matched controls; cost of distribution vs brokerage fee; harvested crop nutrient export replenishment; count existing reuse only once; accurate freshwater and hydropower energy balance; wet and dry year stress tests; loan coverage after O&M; demand forecasting error under abrupt price shocks; real spoilage and price outcomes.
