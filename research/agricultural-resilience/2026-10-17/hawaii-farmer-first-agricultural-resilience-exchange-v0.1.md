# ORCS Hawaiʻi Agricultural Resilience & Farmer-First Exchange — v0.1

**Date:** 2026-10-17  
**Status:** PROPOSED / NON-CANON / SCREENING RESEARCH / ZERO REALIZED CREDIT  
**Scope:** Hawaiʻi statewide (Oʻahu, Maui, Hawaiʻi Island, Kauaʻi and other relevant islands); Lake Wilson–North Oʻahu as an illustrative water/agronomy node  
**Intent:** Open, reproducible research; no procurement, investment, water-rights, permits, farmers' consent or signed offtake claimed.

## Executive proposition

Build a voluntary, farmer-first agricultural resilience network that couples (1) monitored, fit-for-purpose treated/reclaimed irrigation water; (2) qualified, locally recovered nutrients and soil amendments; (3) included soil analysis and human-plus-AI agronomy; (4) regenerative cropping and appropriately designed closed-loop water reuse; (5) machine-readable statewide production, price, soil, import, logistics and demand data; and (6) **verified pre-planting purchase commitments and contract administration**, with a proposed **1–2% exchange transaction fee** where lawful and commercially supported.

The service is accessible by multilingual voice, phone, extension staff or simple messages: farmers should not have to operate a complex dashboard. The dashboard is infrastructure for agents and experts, not a mandatory user interface.

**Mandate:** Hawaiʻi residents' food security and affordability; thriving farms; soil and watershed health; resilient in-state supply; lower external input dependence; transparent economics; local retention of useful economic value. Economic goals are joint, not an instruction to manipulate prices or restrict competition.

## 1. Farmer offering (proposed, not guaranteed)

- **Water:** Measured clean/fit-for-crop irrigation supply, verified against relevant pathogens, salinity/EC, pH, nutrients and contaminants; defend aquifers, ecological flow and dam flood-safety obligations. Lake Wilson inflow treatment is not a claim that all storm peaks can be routed through a finite plant. Distinguish treated water and different legal quality classifications from potable water.
- **Circular nutrient supply:** Target **15% discount to verified landed local comparable products** initially; sensitivity at 20% and 25%; certified and independently tested products. No claim this discount is already feasible for all N/P/K, gypsum or recovered organics.
- **Included analytics:** Baseline/repeat soil tests and human-plus-AI interpretation, nutrient budgets, crop/water advice and regenerative education included within the proposed water service rather than an additional fee; price and test frequency must be contractually specified.
- **Crops and market access:** Optional, privacy-protected planting/harvest forecasts; real buyer specifications, buyer identity and credit checks, purchase commitments and standardized supply contracts **before planting** where feasible. Forecast requests, letters of intent, purchase orders and legally enforceable contracts must be labeled differently. No promise every harvest will be purchased.
- **Transaction pricing:** test **1–2% of completed, paid, qualifying matched sales** for a broker-like service; paid freight, cold-chain, grading, insurance, legal/payment guarantees and crop-financing are transparently separate where incurred. Fees and conflicts of interest disclosed. Avoid charging on self-sourced existing relationships unless value was added and farmer opted in.
- **Interface:** Multilingual voice/text, accessible low-bandwidth mode, advisor handoff, human override and farmers' own data access/export. Machine-readable APIs with provenance and evidence status.
- **Voluntary entry and exit:** Respect water entitlements, competition, indigenous/community stewardship, privacy and farmers declining the package.

## 3. Nutrient sovereignty replaces headline commodity dollars

For each county/island, crop, season, and nutrient n in **N / P2O5 / K2O / Ca / Mg / S**, establish:

- **Demand** = expected crop removal + unavoidable losses + measured soil-stock repair + safe reserve adjustments − verified biological inputs (e.g. nitrogen fixation) − nutrient stock drawdown consistent with long-run soil health.
- **Qualifying local sources** = separately collected wastewater fractions, sidestream products, certified biosolids/digestate, inedible food waste, collected confined-animal manure, qualified plant residues, sustainable mineral/brine products, and soil nutrient cycling where these actually displace purchases.
- **Net external requirement** = max(0, agronomic demand minus *safely available* local supply); track long-run soil nutrient budgets and hazardous residuals. Do not treat potassium, phosphorus, gypsum, and N as interchangeable.
- **Import displacement** uses physical product-grade records from invoices, shipper bills, ports, distributors and farms, not just the value of USDA fertilizer spending or international trade custom districts.
- **Local priority:** satisfy island demand and necessary reserves before shipping qualified nutrient surplus between islands; freight and energy must pass a delivered-cost and emissions test.
- **Brine:** distinguish actual operating seawater/brackish desal from proposed projects. Chemistry may support magnesium and calcium, but do not assume economically marketable potassium or gypsum, and do not count gross seawater minerals as recovered product.

**Recovery rates are stream-specific, stage-specific and grade-specific.** 50% is a national screening case, not a technical ceiling; 90% applies only as an ambition requiring validated extraction pathways and collection coverage, especially for dilute potassium.

## 4. Farmer productivity: integrated field trial, not additive miracle gains

Soil practices: locally suitable legume cover crops and biological N fixation; diversified crop combinations; reduced tillage when agronomically appropriate; permanent cover/mulch; agroforestry and windbreaks; compost and targeted nutrient amendments; soil-specific pH management; tailwater capture and salinity management; monitored irrigation schedules. Crop export removes nutrients even in polycultures. Gypsum supplies Ca and S but does not normally raise pH and is not universally needed in Hawaiʻi.

**Five-year illustrative outcome targets, not predictions:**

| Metric | Year 1 baseline/pilot | Year 3 pilot scaling | Year 5 hypothesis to validate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purchased fertilizer per comparable acre | Soil/plant tissue baseline | 10% less | 20% less |
| External water per comparable acre | Metered baseline | 15% less | 25% less |
| Marketable yield, crop-mix adjusted | Baseline | +5% | +10% |
| Soil carbon, infiltration, pH and exchangeable nutrient stocks | Baseline per depth | Statistically assessed trend | Sustained improvement without nutrient mining |
| Per-output nutrient intensity | Measured | Recalculate | At 20% less input and 10% more marketable yield: **27.3%** lower purchased fertilizer/tonne |

Targets need control/comparison plots and locally valid seasons. Shared mechanisms must not be double counted: input and yield **ratios** compound mathematically, but causal effects of mulching, no-till, cover crops and precision irrigation cannot simply be added. Some companion species compete for light, water and nutrients; no universal mandated crop mix.

Measure net margin after labor, equipment, service charges, water, crop establishment, grading and sale. Count conserved native habitat, watershed runoff and resident affordability separately.

## 5. Farmer-first pre-planting exchange

1. Buyers (schools, hospitals, grocers, restaurants, processors) submit specifications, delivery windows, demand volumes, price offers, creditworthiness and whether commitment is binding.
2. Farmers privately supply optional crop plans and availability, with granular permission and secure retention.
3. Shared AI aggregates independently sourced production forecasts, local demand, external shipments, price distributions, weather, soil suitability, water and expected crop maturity. Record timestamp, forecast revision, source and error history.
4. Advisor presents **alternative crop plans** with expected *contribution margin* and confidence bands, not instructions to suppress supply or collectively set prices.
5. Farmers and buyers voluntarily negotiate independently. Standardized contracts include identity, grades, pricing method, force majeure, crop failure clauses, delivery, payment and dispute resolution.
6. Verify fulfillment and payment; charge transparent commission only where contract allows. Credit/insurance/logistics billed separately.
7. Outcome audit: contracted before planting %, farmgate margin, spoilage %, price volatility, food basket affordability, buyer default, farmer complaints and data misuse.

**Compliance boundary:** U.S. Perishable Agricultural Commodities Act licensing may apply from first negotiated produce sale for a broker; check counsel on actual operations. Prevent antitrust coordination: no individual competitor production plans disclosed, no platform-set floor prices, no market allocation, no coerced crop quotas, fair platform access. No promise 'guaranteed sale' except actually secured enforceable offtake with payment/counterparty risk addressed. Source: https://www.ams.usda.gov/rules-regulations/paca/licensing .

## 6. Service accounting and affordability

Three separate ledgers: **(A) water/treatment/soil advisory utility; (B) nutrient/organics recovery and qualified inputs enterprise; (C) digital market matching/offtake service**. A fourth farm-level ledger measures incremental benefit.

At 20MGD fully billed and $2.50/kgal, the water-service surcharge grosses $18.25M/y, unverified. Water conservation could reduce volumetric receipts; examine equitable two-part tariffs or capacity contracts, rather than rewarding wasted water. Soil testing and ordinary agronomy consultation **included** if supported by cost model.

Fertilizer: a 15% discount is a goal against real **Hawaiʻi delivered comparable grade** prices, not automatically 15% off national retail proxies; verify processing, contaminant testing, logistics, energy, renewal and supply before selling. **Debt service ≤40% of fertilizer gross receipts** is only a cap and does not prove solvency; stricter cash-flow coverage after O&M and reserves governs. Biogas, digestate and avoided disposal may contribute if separately realized and not double-counted.

Marketplace example: annual **$100M qualifying paid sales × 1.5% = $1.5M gross fees**. Not an adoption forecast or net profit. Compare against actual alternative commissions and full-service wholesaler economics, not USDA food-dollar marketing share (transport, processing and retail are not a broker's profit).

## 7. Multi-island roll-out, 2027–2031 (proposed)

| Year | Verification gate | Public deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 | Obtain island-by-island fertilizer invoices/product masses, farm soil sampling permissions, treatment assays, manure actually confined/collectible, grocery organics contracts, user/buyer discovery | Audited baseline, pilot cohort, minimum machine-readable dataset |
| 2028 | Demonstrate qualified wastewater/food/manure recovery, certify safe products, pilot multilingual advisor and buyer purchase orders | Water and input-saving trials, completed sales records, supplier qualification |
| 2029 | Expand opt-in forecast coverage; connect verified purchasing institutions, live shipment and soil signals; fund only bankable hardware | First island-county NPK closure ledger, forecast calibration and unit economics |
| 2030 | Optimize verified interisland surplus shipping and shared cold-chain; continue multi-season trials | Farm margin distribution, spoilage and affordability comparisons |
| 2031 | Independent ecological, social, financial, data-governance and nutrient audits | Verified import displacement, farm income and soil stocks; scale/stop decision |

**Kill criteria:** no verified buyers; persistent negative farm net margin; fertilizer fails contaminants/product rules; untreated hazardous tailwater; ecological damage; confidential farm-data leakage; unaffordable tariffs; financing lacks coverage. Farmers and Indigenous/local communities are participants and rights holders, not assumptions of consent.

## 8. Dashboard / API contract (design target)

Entities: `region`, `farm_private`, `field_private`, `soil_sample`, `water_source`, `water_quality_observation`, `crop`, `crop_cycle`, `planting_intent_private`, `aggregated_forward_supply`, `buyer_demand`, `buyer_commitment`, `contract`, `shipment`, `market_price`, `fertilizer_product`, `qualified_nutrient_lot`, `waste_stream`, `resource_recovery_plant`, `permit`, `farmer_outcome`, `evidence_receipt`.

Each record: id, geospatial/scope granularity, timestamp and valid interval, units, confidence/uncertainty, source, source revision, measured/simulated/proposed/quarantined status, owner/consent, public/private aggregation classification, revision history, API schema version. Voice-advice responses include plain-language recommendation, expected profit interval, assumptions, safety restrictions, underlying source receipts and optional human handoff. No untreated raw livestock waste or unverified nutrient product offered as food-safe certified fertilizer.
