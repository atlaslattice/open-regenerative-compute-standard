## II. Nutrient crystal / desal-salt symbiosis: production side
**Struvite:** MgNH4PO4·6H2O. Molar mass ~245.41 g/mol; ~12.62% elemental P, ~5.71% N, ~9.90% Mg by mass. Source nutrient stream must actually have recoverable *dissolved* NH4 and orthophosphate, often digester sidestreams; historic coastal discharge P is not necessarily same as influent sidestream P.
**Gypsum:** CaSO4·2H2O, molar mass ~172.17 g/mol; ~23.28% Ca and ~18.63% S, **no N and no P**. Recover from seawater salt-processing streams with measured separation, purity and crystallization yield.
**Mechanistic integration:** upstream Mg fraction isolated from desal concentrate can supplement controlled struvite crystallization on nutrient-rich wastewater sidestream, with precipitation and washing to avoid carry-over salinity. Ca and sulfate can be processed separately into tested gypsum products. Keep seawater brine and raw sewage physically separate until appropriately purified reagents are introduced to controlled reaction.
Peer-reviewed actual demonstration of brine as Mg donor to struvite: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1944398624151788 ; experiments with seawater demonstrated 80–90% P precipitation and ~30–50% reduction in reagent expense *under specific conditions*: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0043135420301081 ; pilot-scale seawater vs MgCl2: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30954828/ .
**Stoichiometric illustrative cap**: Historical *residual discharged* elemental P at three Maui facilities totaled ~29.3 tonnes/year in an earlier ORCS baseline (not re-verified in this addendum). At assumed 65% recovery of THAT residual P, 19.05 t P/y could form **~151 t/yr pure struvite**, consuming ~8.62 t N and ~14.96 t Mg if both available in sidestreams. Not forecast of physical recovery, clean fertilizer yield or guaranteed market value; P is limiting, and coastal residual discharge figures aren't digester-side nutrient assays. A different feed-stream mass balance may produce much more or less.
**Struvite nutrient equivalent**: 151 t clean struvite/y ~19.1 t P, 8.6 t N, 15.0 t Mg. Gypsum yield unknown; the prior theoretical calcium inventory was not commercial gypsum production. Do not pretend mass of salt recovered = product.
**Environmental controls**: screen PFAS, trace metals, pharmaceuticals where relevant, pathogens, plastics and residual saline contaminant; verify crop fertilizer regulatory compliance. No unqualified sewage sludge for field spreading.

## III. Long-horizon soil restoration: complementary functions and tradeoffs
- Struvite releases P more gradually than common soluble phosphate products and supplies N and Mg, potentially reducing runoff and purchased phosphate. Availability highly soil-pH dependent; 2026 soil comparison: acidic and neutral soil favorable, calcareous conditions inhibit struvite-P diffusion and availability. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41930670/
- Gypsum provides Ca and sulfate S; may help **sodic** soil aggregate/flocculation and reclamation WHEN sodium displacement is followed by a drainage/leaching plan; useful for diagnosed sulfur/calcium deficiency. NRCS gypsum Conservation Practice Standard (333): https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/resources/guides-and-instructions/amending-soil-properties-with-gypsum-products-ac-333-conservation
- NOT universally beneficial; higher Ca can precipitate Ca-phosphates and **reduce available P** in alkaline/gypsiferous soils: https://www.fao.org/4/t0323e/t0323e04.htm ; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016706105001333 .
- Add clean audited compost/biochar/cover crops, soil microbiome management, field shade and mulch, diverse crop rotation, precision fertigation and crop-specific irrigation. Biochar agronomic claims require feedstock/pyrolysis/soil tests and particulate controls. Microplastic-bearing mixed sludge not accepted as clean amendment.
- Pilot experiment **factorial design** 4 treatments: control; struvite alone; gypsum alone; both (plus separate organic-matter standardized subplots). Establish P-balanced comparisons and gypsum-rate ranges from baseline agronomic soil tests, never arbitrary “tons per acre.” Track 0/1/3/5/10-year outcomes: pH, EC, SAR/ESP, available P/N/S/Mg/Ca, organic carbon, aggregate stability, infiltration, yields, irrigation m3/kg crops, N2O, P runoff, soil biology and contamination. Disclose interactions and failure modes. 
- Compare **sequential timing** and localized placement to avoid immediate struvite-P fixation by high-calcium amendments, with field agronomists designing safe rates. 
- Net claims: avoided imported nutrient spending is only replacement-equivalent after commercial-grade purity and crop demand; productivity/water benefits measured against identical controls, without double counting.
