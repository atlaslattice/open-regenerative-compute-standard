## Executive directive

Reframe the Maui water project as a **community-led regenerative infrastructure and circular-construction program**, with human quality of life, Hawaiian cultural priorities, clean accessible water, healthy reefs and whales, scenic beauty, local housing, disaster resilience and affordable island livelihoods as primary outcomes. Financial return and import displacement are critical *constraints and enablers*, not substitutes for ecological or community performance.

Add complementary subsystems:

1. **Surfbrick / Surf Block**: evaluate the **existing** Surf Block Maui recycled-surfboard-EPS insulated composite concrete form (ICCF) product for code-compliant construction; investigate the broader **Surfbrick** design idea separately. Surf Block uses cast-in-place reinforced concrete and is *not* automatically a 3D-printable feedstock.
2. **3D-printed buildings**: housing and commercial structures, potentially including Front Street-compatible reconstruction subject to *2026 commercial architectural design guidelines*, parcel-by-parcel coastal, cultural, structural, historic, fire and flood reviews. Prioritize appropriate nonstructural printed elements and certified structural printable mixtures.
3. **Circular materials**: recover and sort end-of-life cars, aluminum, ferrous metals, PET/HDPE, EPS, glass, clean construction rubble and other segregated feedstocks. Keep fire ash/hazardous wildfire debris out of building feedstocks unless independently cleared under applicable law and testing; default **quarantine**.
4. **Water and oceans first**: treat source pollution, remove microplastics upstream, extend reclaimed-water use and assess phased *minimal/zero liquid discharge (MLD/ZLD)* with full energy, solid-waste and marine impact accounting.
5. **Full mineral portfolio**: Na/Cl, Mg, Ca, SO4, K, Br, Sr, B, Li, trace elements and deuterium screened as measurable mass **inventories**, not booked revenue; include wastewater N/P/K, manure, compost, agricultural runoff and clean organic wastes.
6. **Biosolar, agriculture and optional edge inference**: beautiful native landscape integration, distributed resilient generation and storage, tested agrivoltaics, reclamation of degraded land with soil-specific remedies, verified optimization computing only where it creates net value.
7. **International logistics**: preserve Maui's inbound/outbound freight, landed input costs, exported recyclable value, landfill costs and priority local-reserve ledger before counting commercial exports.

## 2. Quality of life is the priority, not a cosmetic co-benefit

Use **lexicographic** or constrained multi-objective optimization, rather than monetizing every outcome:

**Nonnegotiable gates:** cultural and community consent; drinking-water safety; reef and whale protection; net pollution prevention; fire, wind, flood and tsunami safety; legal construction and worker safety; affordability and equitable access.

**Tier 1 resident/environment scorecard:** safely usable homes, local housing affordability, chronic water shortages and interruptions, drinking-water quality, coastal nutrient and microplastic loads, reef indicators, emergency water-days and power-days, access to shade/green space, runoff/flood exposure, viable local employment and cultural integrity.

**Tier 2 economic scorecard:** annualized net resource cost, local labor value, delivered energy cost, freight/import offset, landfill volume avoided, project operating budget, affordability to ratepayers, verified local demand and reserve sufficiency.

Tourism and scenic appeal count as measurable outputs (visitor satisfaction and locally retained value), but must **not** outrank resident housing access, cultural stewardship, or reef health. Avoid claims that any architecture has already received local approval.

## 3. Circular-construction manufacturing campus

**Location hypothesis:** non-coastal industrially appropriate Central Maui parcel near existing freight and recycling logistics; assess siting, ownership, potable/reclaimed water, wildfire/flood constraints, power and environmental permits. Do **not** designate a specific available parcel without evidence.

**Feedstock-to-product routing:**

| Intake | Preprocess | First-pass realistic products | Key exclusion/gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Existing Surf Block EPS/surfboard offcuts | Proper sorting, contamination control | Existing recycled EPS insulated composite concrete forms; prototype insulation components | Do not assume independent 3D printing compatibility; fire tests and encapsulation to prevent shedding |
| Sorted PET/HDPE | Wash, dry, extrude, filtration | Nonstructural panels, shaded furniture, temporary formwork, reusable molding, limited printed components | UV/salt weathering, polymer chemistry, flame/smoke/toxicity, microplastic shedding |
| Clean aluminum scrap | Alloy assay, de-coat, melt or cold reprocess; viable export comparison | Noncritical architectural fittings, shading, enclosures or locally certified extrusions | Very high melt energy; alloy mismatch; no structural approval absent code-compliant testing |
| Ferrous metals and dismantled cars | Depollution, separate batteries, refrigerants, oils, tires, metals | Rebar only if standardized/certified; otherwise remanufacturing or optimized scrap exports | Do not put mixed metal fragments into cement expecting greater strength |
| Glass | Contaminant cleanup, grading | Selected tile, aggregate or tested pozzolanic additions | Alkali-silica reaction and printability test |
| Clean concrete/C&D | Screening, quality control, crushing, grading | Recycled aggregate in lab-verified printable cementitious mixtures | Reject contaminated wildfire ash/debris and incompatible gypsum/drywall impurities |
| Recovered gypsum | Purify, dry, product testing | Approved soil amendments where agronomically useful; certified building gypsum where standards met | Agricultural-grade != construction-grade; saline residues and contaminants |

### 3.1 Three distinct building systems—do not collapse them

**System A — Surf Block Maui ICCF:** assembled composite form remains in place; reinforced concrete is placed inside. Evaluate manufacturer's tests, certified performance, insurance and permitting before any housing use.

**System B — 3D-printed cementitious walls:** printable rheology, reinforcement strategy, interlayer bonding, compressive/tensile performance, salt/chloride corrosion, fire/embers, high wind, seismic, moisture and durability. Compare recycled glass/aggregate and *very low* plastic fractions in controlled mixes. Published reviews warn that excessive plastic aggregate tends to degrade concrete strength.

**System C — Modular printed parts:** shading lattice, landscape elements, planters that capture runoff, acoustic screens, rain gardens, kiosks, accessible outdoor furniture. These can be a low-risk first demonstration **only where exposure and fire risks are addressed**.

The 2026 commercial architectural design rules, National Historic Landmark District, coastal setbacks, cultural surveys, permitted height, water and sewer capacity determine Front Street eligibility. Start with **a facade/design-conforming schematic and a nonstructural prototype**, not promises of mass building approvals. Housing should prioritize resident needs and living costs over novelty.

**Product certification:** sample/source provenance, ASTM/ICC or equivalent applicable acceptance route, licensed Hawaii structural engineer, third-party performance tests and code official review. Specifically test water absorption, wind/impact, fire/combustion and smoke, seismic, corrosion, VOCs and microplastic shedding over years of tropical exposure. Never assert recycled-plastic composite printing is inherently stronger or safer than conventional construction.

## 4. Car upcycling and abandoned vehicle remediation

County already provides a **no-cost program** for qualifying residents with titled conventional vehicles, up to two vehicles annually, subject to its conditions. Local metal drop-off also exists, sometimes with fees. Local scrap economics are therefore a **service/access, end-market and processing-capacity** problem, not a total absence of infrastructure. The February 2026 hybrid/EV exclusion is especially relevant.

Pilot sequence:

1. Work with county Tow & Scrap and permitted recyclers; collect documented intake by drivetrain, vehicle size and VIN/title eligibility. Nothing moved without legal authority; photo geotag abandoned vehicles for authorities, not self-help towing.
2. Set up safe removal of oils, fuels, antifreeze, refrigerants and lead-acid/high-voltage batteries through properly permitted specialists. Keep them separate from construction feedstock.
3. Weigh steel, aluminum, copper, plastic, glass, rubber, battery types and unrecoverable residue **per actual vehicle**.
4. Compare (A) current collection/export/landfill baseline, (B) better sorting and island reuse of selected components, (C) local intermediate aluminum/plastic production, (D) fully integrated materials campus.
5. Price net avoided hauling, illegal dumping and remediation against towing routes, equipment, disposal, product QA, freight, avoided imported products and collection volumes.

**No revenue credit from illegal vehicle burning.** Design safe removal and diversion as the intervention, with community-based reporting and a legitimate low-friction end-of-life route.

## 5. Desalination & entire mineral portfolio

### 5.1 Explicit reference case (not the candidate actual Maui plant feed)

Reference **open seawater** case: 5 MGD finished water, 45% RO recovery, 3.5 kWh/m³ finished water, 35 g/L feed TDS, negligible product TDS for elemental accounting.

- Finished water = **18,927 m³/d**
- Feed water = **42,060 m³/d**
- RO concentrate = **23,133 m³/d**
- RO electrical demand = **66.2 MWh/d** = **24.18 GWh/yr**
- Inlet salt inventory = **~537,300 tonnes/yr** (35 g/L × annual feed).
- Concentrate salt inventory = **~534,000 tonnes/yr** if finished water TDS around 0.5 g/L; no claim of commercial recovery.

**Typical feed elemental inventory, rounded, not simultaneous saleable products** (representative ocean chemistry only):

| Species | Assumed feed concentration g/L | Theoretical mass passing intake per year |
|---|---:|---:|
| Sodium (Na) | 10.8 | 165,800 t |
| Chloride (Cl) | 19.4 | 297,800 t |
| Magnesium (Mg) | 1.29 | 19,800 t |
| Calcium (Ca) | 0.412 | 6,325 t |
| Potassium (K) | 0.399 | 6,125 t |
| Sulfate (SO4) | 2.71 | 41,600 t |
| Bromide / Br | 0.067 | 1,029 t |
| Lithium (Li) | 0.00018 | 2.76 t |
| Strontium (Sr), boron, silicon, rare elements | **sample** | No credited output |
| Deuterium (D), primarily in the WATER, not salts | Approx. natural ocean D/H ratio 155.76 ppm of hydrogen atoms | **~530 tonnes/year elemental-D-equivalent in TOTAL incoming water**: isotope inventory only; it does not accumulate in the RO salt as a product and separation is highly specialized. Prior ~2.4 t/year estimate is superseded. |

**Double-counting controls:** molar product constraints, competing ion balances, impurities, moisture, actual process recovery, and mutually exclusive product slates. Sodium plus chloride quantities cannot be *added* to putative NaCl product tonnage. All seawater elemental feed inventories must partition to product freshwater, brine, wastes and net extraction. Membrane rejection factors differ by species.

### 5.2 Gypsum only one branch

From feed Ca max 6,325 t/year and molar mass ratio CaSO4·2H2O/Ca ≈ 172.17/40.08, theoretical **~27,200 tonnes/year gypsum** (about **74 tonnes/day**), *if* 100% of Ca can be converted with matching sulfate. Actual production potentially much lower; recovered Ca and sulfate are no longer available to other revenue branches. Agriculture-appropriate gypsum additionally requires soil need, safe chloride/metals/boron content, certified grade, agronomic field trials, delivered-cost advantage and measured infiltration/crop response.

### 5.3 Mineral screening / go-no-go economics

**Bulk market candidates:** purified NaCl, Mg hydroxide/carbonate, calcium sulfate/other calcium products, K salts. **Specialty screening:** Br, Sr and boron where concentration and demand justify. **Research only:** Li, deuterium and other trace materials. Recoverable price is not equal to spot price of a high-purity commodity; no revenue without product specification and buyer.

Per material j:

**net local material value(j)** = min(saleable amount(j), verified local demand(j)) × (landed import cost(j) − delivered local production and QA cost(j)) + legitimate net export sales − incremental processing costs − waste/byproduct disposal costs − allocated capital costs.

Evaluate 0%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75% **chemical recovery efficiencies** as sensitivities only, not engineering yield predictions. Also evaluate **source variations**: seawater, actual saline wells, brackish aquifers, treatment reject, wastewater liquor, agricultural drainage and manure digestion rejects. Do not apply seawater mineral grades to all streams.

## 6. MLD/ZLD and ocean cleanup: highest-priority ecological branch

Protect watersheds and seas **at source**, not by assuming a desalination plant itself cleans the ocean. Microplastic interception pilot boundaries:

- Urban storm drain trash and sediment interceptors; maintain them to avoid flooding.
- Wastewater tertiary filtration/membranes where justified; sample influent, effluent, sludge and residual solids using consistent particle-size and polymer-specific methods.
- Commercial laundry microfiber controls and source-reduction programs.
- Harbor fishing-gear recovery and legally permitted reef cleanup.
- Industrial pellet loss prevention and printed-polymer lifecycle shedding tests.
- Watershed runoff controls, infiltration/bioswales and Hāna culvert/drainage capacity maintenance, without changing rights-protected streamflow.
- Passive whale and reef monitoring; avoid acoustic disturbance, coastal intake injury and concentrated discharge habitat effects.

**ZLD policy:** investigate MLD, solid-salt crystallization, or full ZLD as ecological alternatives. Do not require ZLD regardless of cost/energy: ZLD avoids liquid concentrate discharge *but can create significant electricity use, emissions and solid residual management*. Compare to beneficial reuse and lower-impact permitted concentrate routes using peer-reviewed lifecycle assessment.

For **23,133 m³/day reference concentrate**, an illustrative **additional electricity intensity** of 5, 10 or 20 kWh/m³ of concentrate processed implies **42.2, 84.4 or 168.9 GWh/year** incremental beyond baseline RO, respectively. **These are user-adjustable stress tests, not claims about actual Maui ZLD equipment performance.** At an assumed $0.20/kWh, incremental electricity-only spend is **$8.44M, $16.89M or $33.78M/year**. Heat integration and renewable contracting can change costs but not erase material/energy balances.

**Marine outcome indicators:** net kilograms microplastics captured upstream and leak-to-environment reduction (both uncertainty-bounded), effluent nitrogen/phosphorus loads, outfall plume salinity, beach bacteria, benthic/coral coverage, turbidity after storm events, nuisance algal blooms, public beach access, and whale-disturbance incidents.

## 7. Agricultural nutrient recovery: wastewater + manure + runoff + organics

**West Maui water co-product:** Based on the November 2025 Lahaina WWRF **4.2 MGD** reclaimed water figure, an upgrade to an additional **2.5 MGD R-1 capability** implies up to **912.5 million gallons/year** at year-round operation. This is a *capacity differential*, not existing unused R-1 water or guaranteed irrigation sales. Phase 25/50/75/100% of this delta yields **228 / 456 / 684 / 913 million gallons/year** potential increased R-1 output; constrain it by verified reuse demand, pipes, storage, seasonality and regulation.

**Effluent nitrogen conservation check:** Maui County FAQ reports typical treated-plant nitrogen **4–10 mg/L**, which at 4.2 MGD yields a hypothetical effluent nitrogen mass of **23–58 metric tonnes nitrogen/year**. **This is not an observed Lahaina lab measurement, nor total upstream available N, nor a fertilizer recovery prediction.** Actual plant-specific N/P, inflows, variability and irrigation needs are required.

**Manure:** 2022 USDA **Maui County** inventory includes **13,442 cattle**. Convert to collectable manure only after measuring confined vs pastured fraction, housing, collection, bedding, moisture, competing land use and transport. Add cattle, pigs, poultry, green waste, food scraps and relevant crop by-products *once* to a nutrient ledger.

**Candidate products:** struvite (MgNH4PO4·6H2O), ammonium sulfate, recovered nutrient concentrates, compost, pathogen-controlled digestate, clean biochar, and soil-appropriate gypsum. Recovery must account for nutrients left in treated water, atmospheric losses, process reagents and residual pollutants; do not over-apply manure or use saline concentrate for land application.

**Stoichiometric controls:** For struvite, moles recovered ≤ min(moles available Mg, NH4-N, orthophosphate-P) before process loss; for ammonium sulfate, required sulfate balance and nonduplicated nitrogen accounting. Nitrogen recovered for fertilizer cannot simultaneously be counted as removed from discharge *and* as avoided purchased fertilizer without properly explaining both distinct outcome categories; financial totals must avoid duplicate avoided costs.

**Island fertilizer import ledger:** gather customs/freight or distributor landed purchases, SKU nutrient analysis, island-specific annual demand, crop/soil needs and transport/disposal costs. **No defensible Maui fertilizer import total verified yet**, so descriptions such as "massive" remain a testable hypothesis, not a quantified number.

**Soil-health gates:** pH, EC, ESP, SAR, CEC, chloride, boron, organic carbon, infiltration, heavy metals, nutrient budget, crop and soil biology; compare gypsum only where soil chemistry warrants it against compost, cover crops, mulching, agroforestry, regenerative grazing, biochar, restored drainage, and controls. Coastal waters benefit only if nutrient and sediment losses are actually reduced.

## 8. Biosolar + AI co-location

Design flexible solar PV as **shaded roofs, carports, agricultural canopies where crops benefit, native landscaping and visually screened industrial facilities**, with fire-safe defensible space. Local housing needs backup power, filtration, cooling, water storage and passive survivability.

Edge inference supports leak/overflow alerts, process control, crop irrigation, materials QA, coastal pollution telemetry and wildfire/drainage warnings. Do not add compute as a fictitious thermal energy source; evaluate without-compute baseline. Use data-center waste heat only when a demonstrated heat sink and heat-exchanger thermodynamics justify it; reverse-osmosis energy recovery is **reduced consumption**, not exported power.

Reference 250-kW average IT node at assumed PUE 1.2: 2.628 GWh/year facility demand; fully charged to the program and balanced against traceable service/inference revenue and useful avoided operating costs.

## 9. Island-scale import/export and local-reserve ledger

For each input/output define:
**island / county boundary; source; destination; HS/SCTG category; annual gross physical tonnage; kg dry/wet; local demand; import CIF/landed price; net export realization; storage days; quality; available collection %; actual processing yield; incremental energy; CAPEX; OPEX; permits; residue; ecological load; jobs; buyer; provenance.**

Initial priority categories: crude/refined petroleum/fuels, fertilizers/gypsum, food/feed, construction binders and metals, plastics/EPS, glass, imported lumber and aggregate, wastewater chemicals, salt, equipment, electrical/solar hardware, wastewater nutrients, recovered scrap, landfill residuals. Distinguish **Hawaiʻi statewide** from **Maui County** from **island of Maui**. Do not turn Honolulu port traffic into Maui receipts.

For products with scarce local markets, evaluate *avoided exported low-value scrap plus reduced imported finished products*, not fabricated simultaneous revenue. Compare opportunities for certified local materials with continued exporting of scrap that cannot be economically upgraded locally.

## 10. Three project scenarios

**A — Resident-first water, runoff and waste services:** Extend safe R-1 reuse and stormwater filtration, curb vehicle-dumping bottlenecks, establish ecological baseline, improve zero/low waste sorting. No new 3D structural material or ZLD promise.

**B — Integrated biosolar and building-material pilot:** Waste segregation/vehicle depollution, Surf Block ICCF evaluation, 3D-print nonstructural elements, pilot clean recycled aggregates, 50–250 kW optional inference only where valuable, R-1 upgrade, agronomic nutrient recovery and **lab-scale** mineral separation.

**C — Circular island resource infrastructure:** Commercial scale only after buyer and feedstock proofs, staged mineral product slate, possible selective/full ZLD after environmental-economic assessment, code-certified 3D housing/commercial systems, scaled local remanufacturing and on-farm nutrient substitutions, backup-power and water reserves.

**Decision gate hierarchy**: ecological protections and community/cultural approval → safety/permitting → source and product QA → actual local demand and import avoidance → net lifecycle cost → island expansion. A technically interesting process with high ocean risk or unaffordable water fails the gate even if commodity revenue appears attractive.

## 11. Critical quantitative corrections, scope warnings, and research queue

1. **Correct salt accounting:** 5 MGD 45% recovery seawater scenario feed contains ~537,300 t/year dissolved salts; concentrate around 534,000 t/year if product water salinity 0.5 g/L. Do not confuse inventory with extracted output.
2. **Correct deuterium:** a representative 155.76 ppm D/H atomic ratio implies ~530 t/year elemental deuterium distributed in the 5-MGD case's **total feedwater**, not 2.4 t/year in brine. Deuterium recovery remains **research-only, zero revenue**.
3. **No double credits:** source water and final reclaimed water, salt/product ions, nutrients and fertilizer, scrap/export and import substitution, electricity/avoided electricity/reserves.
4. **No blanket claims:** 3D-printed composite fire resistance, reinforced composite structural advantage, gypsum as universal soil cure, zero ocean impacts from ZLD, available Front Street building approvals, stranded acreage free for conversion, or commercial mineral revenue.
5. **Urgent evidence gathering:**
   - Lahaina/Maui county 2025–26 recycled water R-1, injections, total N/P, microplastics, seasonal irrigation, pipeline capacity;
   - historical and current Lahaina coastal contaminant indicators, reef and sediment monitoring;
   - Maui Island port cargo imports/exports by product, landed commercial building materials and fertilizer invoices;
   - licensed scrap dealer tonnages, tow program accepted/rejected vehicles, battery handling gaps, actual paid disposal fees;
   - full Surf Block manufacturer testing, certifications, real recycled EPS feedstock and current output;
   - safely sortable (non-fire-ash) C&D, polymer resin assays, 3D printable feedstock trial results;
   - feed brine complete ion analyses, mineral slates, energy load and buyer contracts;
   - farm-level soil chemical tests, on-farm use of manure, fertilizer purchase ledgers;
   - Native Hawaiian/local landowners, Lahaina town stakeholders and agencies engaged by formal documented consultation.
