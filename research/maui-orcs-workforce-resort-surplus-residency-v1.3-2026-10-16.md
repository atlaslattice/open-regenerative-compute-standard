# Maui ORCS v1.3 — Verified Surplus Resort Housing, Skilled Trades Recruitment & Permanent Residency
**October 16, 2026 | PROPOSED / NON-CANON where unverified | No contracts executed, no rooms reserved, no tax enacted.**
**Project:** Atlas Lattice / Open Regenerative Compute Standard (ORCS), Maui Island circular-water and public-resilience enterprise.

## Executive policy
Maui residents, Lahaina rebuilding and current displaced households come first. Recruit available qualified Maui residents and train Maui apprentices before importing supplemental skilled workers. Pursue **100% compliance with applicable Hawaii resident-workforce, prevailing wage, licensing, procurement and immigration rules on each covered contract**. The planning objective is for outside recruits to **genuinely relocate, establish lawful Hawaii residency and contribute to the island long term**, rather than create nominal residency to evade legal requirements. A 3–5 year job offer and housing do not automatically confer residency or satisfy any statutory test. Obtain counsel/contracting-agency rulings and maintain individual records of genuine domicile and qualifying work hours. Prioritize recruitment from elsewhere in Hawaii where feasible; nonresident recruitment only within applicable law or approved exemptions.

Recruitment package to test: 3–5 year guaranteed employment subject to lawful termination terms; licensed-trade recognition, relocation, affordable family-suitable housing at below comparable market rents, occasional meals/credits, benefits, local mentorship, long-term job placement and optional transition to ownership or rental housing. Target 100% **legal compliance**, not guaranteed 100% worker retention. **50% staying permanently** is the user-selected illustrative planning rate, not a surveyed preference; sensitivity 25/50/75%.

## 2025 lodging supply: distinguish vacancy from verified available blocks
2025 Hawaii DBEDT Visitor Plant Inventory: **21,911 Maui Island accommodation units**, including **3,978 timeshare units**; hotels ~34.9% (~7,650). https://files.hawaii.gov/dbedt/economic/tourism/VPI/2025VPI.pdf
Previously retrieved full-year Maui County hotel occupancy ~64.4% and timeshare ~89.8%. Annual average mathematical unused-room equivalents: hotel ~7,650 ×35.6% ≈ **2,723**; timeshare 3,978×10.2%≈**406**. These figures mix Maui Island inventory and Maui County occupancy, so are rough. They are **NOT 3,129 guaranteed continuously empty rooms**, and timeshare units may be reserved/subject to owner rights. Never treat occupied, reserved, contracted, disaster-recovery or unavailable units as surplus. Room-night vacancy cannot simply be converted to continuous 3–5-year room leases.
Only count units after operator/owner legally authorizes **specific multi-year blocks** with independent seasonal utilization verification, minimum availability, no displacement, negotiated price and safety/health standards. Prefer hotels and vacant units in purpose-built visitor accommodation; do not divert long-term residential housing or violate timeshare owner rights.

## Safeguards and milestones
1. Survey Maui contractors/trades and existing Lahaina recovery projects for genuinely available local staff; establish training/apprenticeship wage and placement targets.
2. Obtain Hawaii HRS 103B legal determination for covered public works, including hour-counting, domicile documentation, exemptions and agency audits. Do not represent relocation offers as automatic qualification. https://labor.hawaii.gov/rs/research-statistics/state_resident_workforce_requirement/
3. Solicit **verified vacant and continuously contractable** room-block bids from resorts/timeshare associations without displacing owners, residents or disaster survivors; evaluate long-stay operating rules, fire code, family safety, transportation and emergency occupancy releases.
4. Price 500/1,000/1,500/2,000 verified rooms, occupancy guarantees and minimum 3–5 year terms; compare temporary housing capital.
5. Produce phased craft-hour and housing Gantt, pre-build permanent housing, protect residents' affordability and prioritize Lahaina restoration.
6. Track long-term employee retention 25/50/75%, permanent licensed trade supply, wage levels, apprentice graduations, housing cost burden, housing vacancies and net local value retained.
7. Reconcile bridge housing, permanent housing, water-utility debt and hotel-resort taxes in ONE auditable 20-year cashflow without double-counting.
**Interpretation:** This is a recruitment and housing design hypothesis; no evidence yet that a majority of recruits would permanently remain or that unused timeshare inventory can be leased continuously.
