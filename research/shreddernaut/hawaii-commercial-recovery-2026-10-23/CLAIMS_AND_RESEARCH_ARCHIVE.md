## Executive synopsis

Shreddernaut has shifted from designing a custom 20-metric-tonne/day machine toward deploying commercially available mobile sorting and screening equipment. A Desen equipment package reported by DeepSeek at approximately US$300,000 is the leading purchase candidate, conditional on a model-specific quotation, delivered configuration, performance acceptance and support. It is not an authenticated landed-price quotation in the evidence reviewed here. Container-shippable skids, trailers and mobile machinery are acceptable; operation inside ISO containers is optional.

The public decision should compare recovery and diversion against the full lifecycle costs of additional disposal capacity, including land acquisition, construction, closure and long-term care. Potential benefits include land preservation, accepted material recovery and net airspace recovery. Neither complete remediation nor elimination of residual landfill requirements has been demonstrated.

Current preferred direction: purchase one suitable commercial unit for a defined campaign after supplier validation; retain rental and existing-island-contractor bids as comparators. A paid demonstration at the supplier may resolve machine-specific questions more efficiently than renting a different machine. No equipment purchase or outreach has been authorized or executed here.

## Corrected processing scenarios

All quantities below are metric tonnes. Rated throughput is assumed sustained during available operating hours. Availability is applied once: throughput × hours/day × 300 days/year × 0.80. Whole-line throughput must include excavation, hazard inspection, separation and residual handling.

| Rated throughput | One 8-hour shift: tonnes/year | Two 8-hour shifts: tonnes/year | Years for hypothetical 11 million metric tonnes, one shift |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 t/h | 57,600 | 115,200 | 191.0 |
| 75 t/h | 144,000 | 288,000 | 76.4 |
| 150 t/h | 288,000 | 576,000 | 38.2 |
| 200 t/h | 384,000 | 768,000 | 28.6 |

Four 200-t/h units on two shifts would nominally screen 3.072 million tonnes/year, or the hypothetical inventory in about 3.58 years. At an assumed $300,000 per base unit, four machines cost $1.2 million before freight, adaptation, supporting equipment, infrastructure and working capital. Screening all feed does not equal completing environmental remediation.

The 11-million-tonne inventory is a sensitivity case, not a verified Waimānalo reserve. Recover annual disposal records, cell inventories and surveys; distinguish MSW, ash, cover and C&D, and convert U.S. short tons to metric tonnes (1 short ton = 0.90718474 metric tonnes). Do not mix those units.

## Financial and public-value framework

- **Purchase versus rental:** At $16,697/month, three months cost $50,091; twelve months $200,364; eighteen months $300,546. An assumed $300,000 purchase equals about 18 rental months in base equipment expense only. This is not project payback. Compare equivalent equipment scope, delivered cost, operating hours, service, maintenance, financing and resale value.
- **Production versus first-year ramp:** DeepSeek introduced 20,000–60,000 tonnes for a calendar-year pilot, $800,000–$1.5 million supporting/operating costs and near-zero RDF/fines revenue. These are illustrative assumptions, not site-derived forecasts. Define whether the year starts before permits or at commissioning. Do not suppress output merely by labeling a campaign a pilot.
- **Withdraw the unsupported $28–54 million headline from the publication's financial case.** RDF at an asserted global positive sale price and fines at retail compost prices do not establish Hawaiʻi realizable revenue. Landfill fines are not equivalent to retail compost. Allow treatment or disposal costs. Nonferrous valuation also needs correction: at $0.50/lb, 1,200 metric tonnes of fully accepted aluminum alone would be about $1.32 million, revealing a mismatch with the earlier $0.2 million low estimate. Purity, recovery and acceptance must be explicit.
- **Mass balance:** Do not combine the upper bounds of independently reported RDF, fines and metal fractions; they exceed 100%. Separate contained material, captured material, accepted product, rejected material and remaining disposal. The process must balance to feed mass, allowing measured water losses/inputs.
- **Public value:** Compare discounted total public cost per cubic yard of legal disposal capacity avoided or recovered. Track land preservation and verified environmental benefits separately when monetization is uncertain. Avoid double-counting service payments as additional societal benefits or both tipping fees and identical underlying avoided costs.
- **Residual capacity:** Resource recovery does not automatically authorize post-2028 disposal at Waimānalo or remove the need for safe residual containment. Incinerator ash should be assessed as its own potentially recoverable feedstream where justified, not assumed universally valueless; no ash recovery is included in the base campaign.
- **RDF:** H-POWER is a candidate technical discussion, not an established paying buyer for excavated fuel. Obtain written acceptance specifications, spare capacity, gate fees or payments and transport costs. Proposed facilities are not committed offtake. Do not claim RDF has no possible local destination, nor assume positive sale revenue.
- **Domestic equipment:** U.S.-available equipment is not necessarily U.S.-manufactured. Historical EPA system-level capital ratios cannot establish current like-for-like U.S. screen prices. The new DeepSeek rental prices and closure costs remain unverified leads unless independently sourced below.

## Appendix: English translation of the latest DeepSeek assessment

Translator's note: This preserves the supplied assessment, including its recommendations and unverified figures. Claims in this appendix are not adopted as findings; the audit above governs publication use.

Your judgment is directionally correct, but one important data correction comes first: comparable U.S. equipment is not a similarly priced alternative to Desen's approximately $300,000 unit. It represents a significantly higher capital threshold. This makes the comparison between a pilot and acquiring land through eminent domain for a new landfill clearer, because the latter's true cost extends far beyond land compensation.

### U.S. equipment pricing: rental fits the pilot budget more closely than purchase

An older EPA technical assessment gave capital-cost ranges expressed per ton of daily capacity: approximately $33,100 per daily ton at 25 tons/day, and $21,400 per daily ton at 200 tons/day. This implies roughly $1 million–$1.6 million in capital cost for a 50-ton/day U.S.-manufactured system, substantially above Desen's $300,000 reference price. U.S. manufacturers such as Tuffman offer customized sorting stations, trommels and magnetic equipment, but require quotations; customization means there is no standard catalog price.

Rental is more consistent with a pilot approach. Public monthly rental benchmarks include:

| Equipment | Claimed capacity | Monthly rental |
|---|---:|---:|
| Komplet K-TS30 | 60 t/h | $6,616 |
| McCloskey 512A | 200 t/h | $9,500 |
| McCloskey 516RE | 200 t/h | $11,000 |
| MDS Scrap Pro 400 | 330 t/h | $16,426 |

Source cited by DeepSeek: public US Equipment Sales rental prices. EPA landfill-mining assessments also recorded typical processing-equipment rental costs of $16,000–$19,000/month, a major operating-cost component. One year renting a McCloskey 516RE-class machine would therefore cost about $132,000. With excavation, internal transport, staffing and compliance, total first-year spending is consistent with the earlier $800,000–$1.5 million operating range. Rental converts sunk capital into operating expenditure, a more reasonable structure for a pilot intended to generate data.

### A new landfill costs far more than compensation for land

Your intuition that taking beautiful land for a new landfill is economically unattractive has supporting data, and the cost is substantially underestimated. According to University of Florida research, landfill closure alone costs $110,000–$155,000/acre, plus annual long-term maintenance of $2,000–$13,000. This excludes land acquisition, permitting, liners and community or political costs.

Early EPA demonstration projects imply landfill-mining operating costs of about $115/ton, or approximately $250/ton adjusted to today's dollars. Although high, these processing costs should be compared with a new landfill's full lifecycle costs: acquisition, construction, closure, long-term monitoring and community opposition—not with free disposal.

Academic assessments have also shown positive benefit-cost results. A Denton study found a 2.20 benefit-cost ratio over ten years, with approximately $37.4 million net benefit and $34.1 million NPV. That is the same order of magnitude as the earlier theoretical full-production revenues, but the distinction is that Denton's study was based on operating-project data rather than theoretical extrapolation.

### RDF destinations: real potential outlets, but still an additional benefit

Your judgment that RDF should not be relied on is correct, but Hawaiʻi's potential destinations are more concrete than you might think. H-POWER is Oʻahu's only waste-to-energy plant, with two RDF boilers rated at 854 tons/day each. Historically, as much as 90% of MSW volume was diverted from Waimānalo Gulch through this facility. Because H-POWER generates power by burning RDF, a technical basis for a local RDF outlet already exists.

Maui also has a proposed waste-to-fuel facility, E&K Aloha Aina, intended to gasify MSW into compressed natural gas for shipment to Oʻahu. Oʻahu's Aloha SMRFF is also under development, targeting energy recovery from organic components of C&D waste. These facilities indicate that RDF and combustible material are not necessarily waste streams without an outlet in Hawaiʻi.

But a facility is not a paying purchase contract. H-POWER's RDF boilers were designed for preprocessed MSW. Whether aged, wet and mixed landfill-derived combustibles meet heat-value, chlorine and moisture specifications requires separate trials. The Aloha SMRFF and E&K projects remain proposed or under development and cannot be counted as dependable first-year income.

The soundest approach is to treat RDF as an option that could significantly improve economics if a local acceptance route is established, while ensuring the pilot's financial plan remains viable if it is not.

### Comparing the pilot with the alternatives

| Route | Estimated first-year cash spending | Described first-year dependable revenue | Main output | Strategic value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rent U.S. mobile equipment | $500,000–$1 million, rental plus operation | Limited ferrous/nonferrous sales, $50,000–$300,000 | Measured Hawaiʻi waste data | Strong evidence for later scale-up decisions |
| Buy Desen unit | $300,000 equipment plus $800,000–$1.5 million operation | Same as above | Same data plus an owned asset | Asset retained, with electrical-adaptation and cross-border support risks |
| Acquire land through eminent domain and build a landfill | Millions to tens of millions for acquisition, permitting, construction and closure | None; a pure cost | Additional permanent environmental burden and community/political conflict | Directly conflicts with ecological priorities |

The first-year cost of renting equipment is approximately comparable to early permitting and site-assessment expenses for a new landfill. The pilot produces reusable data and a reversible commitment; landfill development produces irreversible land occupation. For a pilot intended to generate data and validate markets, the rental logic is internally consistent: buy decision information through operating expenditure rather than commit capital to a route that later data might reject.

U.S. equipment performs comparable functions—trommel screening, magnetic separation and conveying—with an established rental market, compatible 60 Hz configurations and domestic service, eliminating cross-border adaptation risks. Its higher capacity-adjusted purchase or rental cost is manageable for a 20–50-ton/day pilot and offset by avoiding sunk capital. There is no need for an ideological choice between cheaper Desen equipment and expensive U.S. equipment: rental can make U.S. equipment more affordable for a pilot than purchasing a Desen unit requiring cross-border adaptation.

你直觉中“征用美丽土地建新填埋场”在经济上不划算，这个判断有硬数据支撑，而且成本被显著低估了。根据佛罗里达大学的研究，**填埋场的 closing（封场）成本本身就在$110,000–$155,000/英亩**，外加每年**$2,000–$13,000**的长期维护费。这还只是封场，不包括征地、许可、 liner 建设和社区政治成本。而填埋场开采的**运营成本**，早期EPA演示项目折算下来约**$115/吨**，按今天美元调整后约**$250/吨**——这看起来很高，但它是**处理成本**，其对比对象不是“免费填埋”，而是**新填埋场每吨的全生命周期成本（征地+建设+封场+长期监测+社区阻力）**。

### 🔥 RDF出口：有真实承购方，但仍应作为“锦上添花”

你关于RDF“不能依赖”的判断是正确的，但夏威夷本地的承购图景比你想象的要具体一些。**H-POWER是Oahu岛上唯一的垃圾发电设施**，拥有**两台854吨/天的RDF锅炉**，历史上**高达90%的MSW体积**被从Waimānalo Gulch转移至此。H-POWER本身就是通过RDF燃烧发电的，这意味着**技术上存在一个现成的RDF承购逻辑**。此外，毛伊岛有一个拟议的**垃圾制燃料设施（E&K Aloha Aina）**，计划将MSW气化制成压缩天然气，然后运往Oahu；Oahu的**Aloha SMRFF**设施也在开发中，目标是将C&D废物中的有机组分转化为能源。这些设施的存在说明，RDF/可燃物在夏威夷**不是一个完全无出路的废物流**。
