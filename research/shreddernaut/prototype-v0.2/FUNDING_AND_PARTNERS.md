# Shreddernaut initiative, partner and funding map

Checked 2026-10-20 (America/Chicago). Public-source desk research; no outreach, application, award or partnership established. Program fit is an analyst judgment, not agency eligibility approval. Recheck the actual solicitation before applying.

## Priority connections to existing initiatives

### 1. West Maui collection centers — strong mission match, prospective host

EPA lists County of Maui as selected for $3,607,065 under SWIFR, for two West Maui collection centers serving green waste, recyclables and large/bulky items. Named streams include scrap metal, batteries, appliances and electronics. This provides a specific collection-and-sorting initiative to investigate. It does not establish that the centers are built, that money is uncommitted or that a legacy landfill excavation line is eligible.

Proposed role: source-separated trial, grading/aggregation and verified downstream shipments; all legacy excavation stays a separate scope. County public sponsor/operator must assess procurement and grant terms. Island freight and mainland acceptance can dominate economics; bulk metals need lot aggregation. Do not assume local refining.

Source: [EPA Maui project fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/swifr-ps-fact-sheets_maui_508adc.pdf).

### 2. Austin Resource Recovery — reuse linkage, distinct project

EPA lists a $4 million Austin Reuse Warehouse project accepting furniture and eventually building materials. The relevant connection is salvage before size reduction and a potential operational learning partner. This is not a metals refinery or authorization to divert its grant to Shreddernaut.

Source: [EPA Austin project fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-09/City_of_Austin_SWIFR.pdf).

### 3. Texas councils of governments — public sponsor and closed-site inventory

TCEQ allocates regional solid-waste funds through 24 COGs, which also maintain closed landfill inventories and regional plans. Eligible pass-through recipients include cities, counties and specified public bodies. Private/nonprofit entities cannot directly receive these pass-through grants, but may provide contracted services. Projects must fit regional plans and not create prohibited competitive advantage over existing services. Pair with existing recyclers rather than propose subsidized displacement.

CAPCOG's public page states 2026–27 funds have been awarded. Treat this as a next-cycle/contracting/technical coordination path; exact remaining funds and procurement opportunities are unverified.

Sources: [TCEQ program and eligibility](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/waste_permits/waste_planning/wp_grants.html); [CAPCOG solid waste funding](https://www.capcog.org/services/funding-grants/solid-waste/).

## Recommended financing sequence

1. Public host or existing waste operator commissions a bounded feasibility/trial scope; leases equipment and uses existing qualified staff where actually available. Establish legal entity, contracts and permits before spending.
2. Host/public program pays eligible public-benefit work; operator/buyer may contribute agreed trial equipment, sampling or logistics. None of these contributions is committed.
3. Prototype report provides accepted-product yield, measured power and a real service-fee requirement. Compare to baseline site management and remediation alternatives; historic waste generally has no new incoming tipping revenue unless a service contract pays for its processing.
4. Contracted service revenue plus net commodity sales supports operations. Reserve grants for eligible incremental development and capital; avoid counting transfers as additional system benefits.
5. After two representative sites, seek equipment finance or portfolio-scale circular infrastructure lending. Do not extrapolate municipal collection performance into profitable national landfill remediation.
