# Shreddernaut v0.5 — commercial, resilience and environmental recalculation

2026-10-20 | EXECUTED SCREENING MODEL / SYNTHETIC FEED AND COMMERCIAL INPUTS / NOT A CERTIFIED LCA OR SOCIAL ROI

## Main result

The operator return is unchanged by benefits that nobody has contracted to pay for. But the public-benefit case is substantially larger than the operator cash account alone in the selected metal-rich scenario.

For the illustrative Maui case, five full operating years at 20 metric tonnes/day and 300 days/year give:

- $600,000 initial equipment/development investment, excluding working cash.
- $894,917 operator net cash after recovering that investment, with no service payment: 149% cumulative financial ROI.
- Approximately 6,757–14,168 tCO2e of recycling benefit proxy after modeled extra site and island transport emissions, under 50–100% additional recovery/virgin displacement assumptions.
- At an explicitly hypothetical $100/tCO2e shadow value, $675,741–$1,416,810 additional climate public value.
- Expanded net-value proxy $1,570,658–$2,311,727, equivalent to 262–385% of the initial $600k investment. This is NOT cash ROI, a grant value, a carbon-credit offer or an official valuation.

The model still assumes 14% contained metals, including 2% copper, in the selected stream. Neither the feed supply nor its composition nor downstream acceptance is established. Baseline recovery matters: if material was already going to be recycled, additional public benefit falls; it cannot simply be claimed twice.

## Comparison of accounts

At $100/tCO2e, 50–100% additionality/virgin displacement and ZERO assigned resilience dollar premium:

| Feed / location | Operator net cash after initial investment, no service fee | Climate public-value proxy | Expanded net-value proxy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Selected metal-rich / mainland | $2.143m | $0.721–$1.462m | $2.863–$3.605m |
| Selected metal-rich / illustrative Maui | $0.895m | $0.676–$1.417m | $1.571–$2.312m |
| Legacy / mainland | -$3.561m | $0.121–$0.263m | -$3.439m to -$3.298m |
| Legacy / illustrative Maui | -$4.127m | $0.103–$0.245m | -$4.023m to -$3.882m |

A negative result is a modeled funding gap, not a prediction that an operator can run without funding. Legacy feed can have additional remediation, land, pollution and resilience benefits; these are unpriced rather than declared zero. This model does not establish that a simple metals line completes remediation. Compare actual alternatives with equal environmental outcomes before awarding a cleanup payment.

For the illustrative Maui legacy case, after the $100/tCO2e climate sensitivity, a further $129–$134 per incoming tonne of justified, non-overlapping public benefit would be needed to close this five-year expanded-value gap. That is a break-even requirement, NOT evidence those benefits exist at that value. A public service fee is a transfer between payer and operator: it may finance a useful project, but is not itself another social benefit.

## Avoided virgin-production equivalents

At 100% additional virgin displacement:

- Aluminum: approximately 3,899–5,848 tonnes of bauxite input equivalent. [International Aluminium Institute](https://international-aluminium.org/landing/aluminium-carbon-footprint-faqs/) describes 4–6 tonnes of bauxite per tonne of primary aluminum.
- Ferrous: approximately 2,873 tonnes of iron ore input equivalent, using accepted steel scrap input and worldsteel's [1.4 tonnes iron ore per tonne scrap](https://worldsteel.org/steel-topics/raw-materials/) relationship. Not all ferrous grades are identical to this industry proxy.
- Copper: approximately 110,580 tonnes of ore equivalent under an explicitly assumed 0.5% ore grade and 90% mine recovery. This ore grade is an analyst scenario, not a measured displaced mine. At 1% grade the ore equivalent halves; at 0.25% it doubles.

These are equivalents of primary production, not evidence a named mine stops extracting that amount. Overburden, ore mineralogy, tailings characteristics, water, biodiversity and land footprints need route-specific inventories. No generic dollar credit is assigned per tonne of ore.

## Climate and energy method

EPA WARM v16 is a comparative waste-management screening framework. We use its steel-can, aluminum-ingot and copper-wire recycling factors as proxies for accepted metal input; landfill-origin mixed metals need dedicated testing and lifecycle assessment.

| Accepted input proxy | Recycling credit, tCO2e per U.S. short ton | Upstream energy credit, million Btu per U.S. short ton |
|---|---:|---:|
| Steel cans / ferrous proxy | 1.83 | 19.97 |
| Aluminum ingot | 7.20 | 113.85 |
| Copper wire / copper proxy | 4.49 | 82.59 |

Sources: [EPA WARM metals documentation, Exhibit 2-17](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-12/warm_containers_packaging_and_non-durable_goods_materials_v16_dec.pdf); [WARM management practices, Exhibit 7-7](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P101982A.txt). Metric tonnes are divided by 0.90718474 to obtain short tons. Those factors already incorporate recycling/manufacturing and yield assumptions; apply them to accepted recycling input and do NOT multiply them by our independent downstream yield again. Finished-output figures remain separate. We do not add a separate baseline landfill credit.

Formula: GHG benefit proxy = sum(accepted recycling tonnes / 0.90718474 × material credit) × additionality/virgin-displacement fraction − extra modeled project emissions.

Project deductions use synthetic 203.6 kWh/day sorting power, 20 L/day mobile-equipment diesel, 0.4 kgCO2e/kWh grid intensity and 2.68 kgCO2e/L diesel. Maui adds an assumed 0.3 kg/kWh grid-intensity difference and incremental 5,000 km shipping at 0.02 kgCO2e/t-km on accepted metal shipments. These are scenario inputs, NOT measured Maui electricity or quoted shipping emissions. They do not account for all capital manufacture, methane releases, truck legs, water treatment or contaminants. Some front-end deductions may overlap WARM collection/processing assumptions; this conservative deduction is not a fully reconciled LCA. Unknown methane disturbance could materially change results.

Selected feed upstream energy-credit proxy is 32–65 GWh equivalent over five years at 50–100% displacement, BEFORE additional site energy and Maui transport adjustments. It is lifecycle energy equivalent across fuels/electricity, NOT electricity produced on Maui or a reduction on the site's utility meter. It is NOT monetized a second time because production costs and climate effects are already represented elsewhere.

## Explicit valuation sensitivities

Carbon shadow values of $0/$50/$100/$150 per tCO2e are user-editable appraisal cases, not a verified carbon-credit price or agency-approved social cost. The headline illustration uses $100 solely to make the environmental contribution visible.

For resilience, the model tests $0/$250/$1,000 per ADDITIONAL finished copper-plus-aluminum tonne. These are arbitrary willingness-to-pay sensitivities, not estimated disruption losses, not metal prices, and not premiums offered by Vault. Their legitimacy depends on documented reserve needs, compatible product grades, lead times and downstream interruption costs, without overlap with climate/product values.

At 100% additionality the five-year selected stream produces 1,472.31 tonnes of finished nonferrous equivalents. Thus a hypothetical $250/t resilience benefit adds $368,078 to the expanded account; $1,000/t adds $1.472m. Neither changes operator receipts without an actual payment. At 50% additionality those benefits halve.

For the Maui selected case at $100/tCO2e and 100% additionality:

| Assigned resilience value | Expanded net-value proxy | Ratio to initial $600k investment |
|---|---:|---:|
| $0/t | $2.312m | 385% |
| $250/t | $2.680m | 447% |
| $1,000/t | $3.784m | 631% |

This is a screening market-value-minus-cost account plus selected externalities, not a formal social cost-benefit appraisal or monetized ecosystem model. Commodity prices, taxes, subsidies, foreign versus domestic perspectives, alternative uses of capital, risk and discounting need treatment in a formal appraisal. We have not manufactured a precise dollar value for resilience where evidence is missing.

## Measurements still needed

Representative assays and existing recovery baseline; domestic product acceptance; refinery route and losses; incremental excavation/processing/freight emissions; methane disturbance; measured water/treatment and contaminant balances; true alternative remediation costs; buyer shortage exposure; reserve coverage and delivery readiness. Availability and all economic stress tests in v0.4 still apply. The larger benefit account does not validate uptime or erase a negative operating cash balance.

## Reproduce and checks

`python3 public_value.py --output public-value-results.json`

The script executes 96 combinations across two locations, two feeds, two additionality factors, four carbon values and three resilience values. Assertions check the expanded-account identity and finished mass bounds. Operator cash is read from the no-service-fee v0.4 calculation so inter-party transfers cannot inflate system value. Gross import purchase equivalence is reported but never added. Original source files remain unchanged.
