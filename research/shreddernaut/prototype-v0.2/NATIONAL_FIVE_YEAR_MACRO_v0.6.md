# Shreddernaut v0.6 — United States five-year macro scenarios

2026-10-20 | EXECUTED CONDITIONAL SCREENING MODEL | NOT A NATIONAL RESOURCE ASSAY, PROCUREMENT PLAN OR INVESTMENT FORECAST

## Purpose and scope

Apply the v0.3-v0.5 operator, resource and environmental accounting to a 50-state network while accounting for a staged fleet rollout. National arithmetic does not prove feed supply, manufacturing capacity, site permissions, staffing, product acceptance or domestic refinery capacity. No autonomous deployments, contracts or public spending are authorized by this model.

Two feed types stay separate: a synthetic selected stream with 14% contained metals (including 2% copper) and a synthetic legacy sensitivity with 4% metals. The richer stream is NOT a national landfill average or a reserve estimate. It may represent targeted cells, source-separated bulky goods, industrial or demolition streams only after assays establish composition, additionality and legal access.

## Five-year rollout assumption

Central illustration: target 100 units per state, 5,000 nationally by year five. Equal allocation is an initial arithmetic convention; final siting follows feed, transport, permits, buyers and utilities.

| Operating year | Average active units | Equal average/state | Annual gross intake |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 500 | 10 | 3.0m t |
| 2 | 1,250 | 25 | 7.5m t |
| 3 | 2,500 | 50 | 15.0m t |
| 4 | 3,750 | 75 | 22.5m t |
| 5 | 5,000 | 100 | 30.0m t |
| Total | 13,000 unit-years | — | **78.0m t** |

Each operating unit achieves 20 t/day over 300 days/year, including the earlier within-shift assumptions. These average active counts are a schedule sensitivity, not evidence of delivery dates. Construction/deployment within a year, early trial failures and actual procurement lead times can lower achieved exposure. A requirement of four crew positions per active unit gives 20,000 crew positions at full deployment, not a verified net job-creation estimate.

## Financial assumptions

- Unit installed equipment/development: $500,000, unchanged from the synthetic single-unit model.
- Shared initial engineering/integration allowance: +10%, giving **$550,000 per target unit**.
- Shared operating oversight/training allowance: +5% on local cash operating costs.
- No assumed volume discounts, 3D-printing savings, free host assets or automatic financing.
- At 5,000 units: **$2.75bn total equipment/development/program investment**, plus **$0.80bn working-cash allowance** at full fleet. Combined $3.55bn is a gross program allocation envelope, not the amount required upfront or a computed maximum funding draw.
- Borrowing, taxes, inflation, commodity price changes and major new refinery construction are excluded. Some sites will exceed the host-site scope. Costs/fees must be replaced with actual bids. Working cash may be insufficient for loss-making feed without committed compensation.
- Operator ROI is cumulative unlevered five-year cash after initial investment divided by $2.75bn. Reserve cash is separate; no salvage is credited. Different unit vintages have different operating years; no assumption that all 5,000 units run for five years.

This is the commercial upper case for selecting higher-value feed, not a demonstrated national inventory. No service fees are included.

Climate ranges use 50–100% additional recovery/virgin displacement. EPA WARM proxies and modeled site emissions are unchanged from v0.5. No resilience dollar premium is assigned in the headline case. The financial result is substantially lower than the single-unit five-full-year ROI because of the ramp and additional shared costs. Financial profitability also retains all original synthetic feed, price and toll-processing assumptions. No national commodity-price equilibrium or feedback from increased supply is calculated.

The last difference is modeled downstream processing loss; it is not missing mass or automatically harmless waste. Toll fee assumptions include downstream handling, pending contracts. Only metals are sold in this conservative boundary; some other fractions may ultimately have value but require separate qualification. **78m tonnes processed is not 78m tonnes remediated, recycled, diverted or eliminated.** No reduction in legacy stock is booked in this all-selected scenario until the source is actually identified as legacy waste.

### Capacity and feed requirements — NOT verified

At year-five target throughput, this case needs:

- 30m tonnes/year of qualified enriched feed, with a modeled 600,000 tonnes/year of contained copper BEFORE capture/acceptance/refining losses.
- Approximately **497,610 t/year finished copper capacity**, **974,700 t/year aluminum**, and **1,949,400 t/year ferrous products** from downstream partners.
- Acceptance of 513,000 t/year copper input, 1,026,000 t/year aluminum input and 2,052,000 t/year ferrous input to downstream processing.
- Approximately 26.4m t/year of front-end residual management.

Those are procurement/qualification requirements, not assertions of spare domestic capacity. National results are conditional until feed and capacity constraints can be set to measured limits. Current configuration leaves those limits null and flags them UNKNOWN. If a supplied limit is smaller, the model flags FAIL; reduce deployment/feed allocation or explicitly fund capacity expansion before treating results as achievable. More collecting/sorting units do not create smelting/refining capacity.

### Import exposure and virgin-production equivalents

At 50–100% additionality and an explicitly assumed 100% domestic delivery/import replacement of attributable nonferrous output, the physical import-replacement ceiling is approximately:

- Copper: **0.647–1.294m tonnes over five years**.
- Aluminum: **1.267–2.534m tonnes over five years**.
- Gross copper/aluminum purchase equivalent: **$9.64–$19.27bn**, using synthetic unchanged $10,000/t copper and $2,500/t aluminum.

This is potential trade exposure replaced, NOT extra net profit. Metal value already appears in product receipts and is never added a second time. Actual import reductions need matched grades, domestic delivery and buyer counterfactuals. No percentage of national imports is claimed because an appropriate verified product-specific baseline and market response are not incorporated here. Stockpile additions and industrial consumption have separate destinations and cannot both claim the same tonne.

At full virgin displacement, the same output corresponds to about **10.14–15.21m tonnes of bauxite input equivalent**, **7.47m tonnes of iron ore equivalent**, and **287.5m tonnes of copper ore equivalent** at the assumed 0.5% copper grade and 90% mine recovery. These are mining-route equivalents, not evidence of cancelled mining. Overburden, tailings toxicity, water, biodiversity and land effects are unpriced. At 50% displacement these quantities halve. Upstream energy credit is roughly 84–168 TWh equivalent before added project-specific energy adjustments; it is lifecycle fuel/electricity equivalent, not power generated for the grid.

## National legacy-remediation program — distinct economics

A mixed sensitivity dedicates 80% of units to ordinary legacy feed and 20% to the selected richer stream. It handles 78m tonnes total, of which **62.4m tonnes is legacy input** and 15.6m tonnes is selected feed. Against the rounded 10bn-tonne legacy-stock assumption, that is **0.624% of stock processed once**, not fully remediated. Actual waste-in-place and site distribution require updated inventory. The [2016 Waste Informatics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27651028/) provides the historical 8.5bn-tonne estimate; 10bn remains an analyst planning assumption.

| Five-year account, mixed case | No service contract | $150/legacy-input-t contract sensitivity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Operator net cash after investment | -$8.259bn | **$1.101bn** |
| Operator ROI | -300% | **40%** |
| Service payments | $0 | **$9.360bn** |
| Expanded system net value after climate proxy | **-$7.632bn to -$6.953bn** | **Same** |

Payments improve operator funding but are a cost to the payer, so the system account subtracts them. A service payment is not itself proof of remediation benefit. The remaining system gap requires approximately **$111–$122 per legacy-input tonne** of independently supported cleanup, land, environmental or resilience benefit under the modeled climate sensitivity. These benefits are unpriced, not proven nonexistent. Compare the cost of actual baseline closure, containment or alternative remediation with equivalent outcomes; do not assume all legacy waste must be excavated.

Mixed-case finished output: approximately 389,498 t copper, 805,615 t aluminum and 2,294,136 t ferrous over five years. Do not apply metal-rich yields to every landfill to make a national cleanup case profitable.

## Reproduce and audit

Run `python3 national_five_year.py --output national-results.json` beside the earlier model files. The script executes 32 cases across four fleet sizes, four feed/funding structures and two additionality levels. Checks cover mass reconciliation, rollout investment, legacy inventory limit and invariance of system value when service-payment transfers change. All earlier files remain unchanged. The public national-summary.json contains headline 5,000-unit cases; full results are reproducible.

Next upgrades: state/site waste inventories, representative assays, buyer-constrained routes, agreed domestic refining capacities, deployment lead times, regional freight, environmental baselines, local staffing/wages and qualified modular-maintenance costs. National financial and environmental results remain conditional until those receipts replace synthetic inputs.
