# Shreddernaut 20 t/day — Executive Synopsis v0.1
**2026-10-20 | PROPOSED / NON-CANON**

## Decision
Retain a 20 metric tonnes/day **presorted intake** research goal. Do **not** route all material through wash, dry and pelletize. Start with battery/hazard exclusion, coarse screening, preservation of whole valuable items, ferrous/nonferrous separation, selective shred and sale of certified graded outputs. Treat energy-intensive polymer processing as optional/downstream.

## Where the original broke
Original 1,800 kWh/day = 75 kW mean; incompatible with full-chain operating-plant comparators at 20 t/day. Published 20-tonne/day screens range **5.1–21.5 MWh/day** for distinct polymer-processing boundaries (these use differing denominators and are *not* a measured Shreddernaut estimate). Deficits relative to 1.8 MWh/day range **3.3–19.7 MWh/day**. Dry metals-first presorting energy is not established and must be separately metered.

## Top options
1. Cut washing/drying/pelletizing for fractions not justified by offtake; presort BEFORE landfill.
2. Metals-first dry line, separate e-waste and battery recovery.
3. Grid power + optional solar canopies + emergency backup; not container-roof solar as exclusive supply.
4. Mechanical dewatering, VFDs, idle control, right-sized equipment, verified heat sources when available.
5. Ecomaine's successful ashfill **metal** recovery is a precedent for segregated metals, not unassayed aged MSW polymer mining.

## Metals and materials inventory
Steel, iron, stainless, aluminum, copper, brass, zinc, tin, lead, nickel, manganese, high-grade precious-metal electronics (gold/silver/palladium), rare-earth magnets, battery materials (lithium, nickel, cobalt etc.), glass, mineral aggregates, brick/concrete, plastics by resin, timber/textiles/paper and source-separated organics. Contamination/leachate/product qualification precedes sale or reuse. Lithium batteries and hazardous items must NOT enter a bulk shredder.

## Economics and proof
20 t/day × 300 days = 6,000 t/year **gross incoming**, not saleable output. Need stage-specific meter readings, real feedstock assay, contamination profile, quoted vendor costs, qualified products and buyers, alternative disposal baselines, permits, ashfill versus MSW controls. Separate operator profit from avoided disposal and national import-displacement benefits.

## November 1, 2026 — e-waste solar and oilfield-brine boundary (new provenance)

[Penn State / Intel-E-Waste's NSF-stage prototype program](https://iee.psu.edu/news/startup-penn-state-research-scale-domestic-critical-mineral-recovery) describes recovery of strategic metals from complex e-waste and end-of-life solar panels, with up to $2M initial 10-month support; this is **E3 research award/prototype development**, *not* measured Shreddernaut extraction performance. Pre-sort dedicated e-waste and PV intake; exclude uncontrolled lithium batteries/charged devices and hazardous lead/cadmium sources from a generic 20 t/day shredder feed. Audit dismantling, environmental permits, qualified hazardous transport/refiner handling, actual product purity and reject streams before any revenue estimate.

[ORNL's October 7 oilfield-brine lithium update](https://www.ornl.gov/news/national-lab-partnership-helps-launch-u-s-lithium-supply-chain/) concerns a **chemically separate aqueous separation line**, with the underlying Element3 facility milestone in February 2026. Do not model brine as dry solids throughput in the 20 t/day Shreddernaut and do not add unmeasured lithium to the machine's output ledger. Brine processing and PV recycling go into connected specialized factories subject to actual feedstock assay and independent cost/energy/environmental analysis.
