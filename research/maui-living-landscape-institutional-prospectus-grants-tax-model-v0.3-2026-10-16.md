## 2. Proven precedent, without extrapolation

- The US Forest Service's **AI Puaʻa** in September 2026 is a locally adapted Hawaiʻi camera-trap classifier built on ~1.64 million images from 5,417 cameras; locally adapted model **97.3%** versus globally trained comparison **66.3%** species classification on the stated reference test. Inference can run locally/offline. This shows the practical rationale for **place-based ecological inference**, *not* projected accuracy on plant health in Olowalu.
- A March 2026 UH announcement confirms **$2 million** from Jeff Bezos and Lauren Sánchez Bezos for **Maui fire-prone grasslands and vegetation management research**; this is an existing funded project, **not** money offered to ORCS. Separately, philanthropic gifts for Lahainaluna student support are not fungible construction funds. The research includes evaluating land-use transitions, agroforestry, forests and wildfire fuel options.
- Hawaiʻi's CARE-oriented Indigenous-data scholarship and practice require collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility and ethics. Indigenous environmental data are not unrestricted scraped training data. Use consent, data categories, local review and removal rules, not a blanket open-data requirement.
- Hawaiian Electric BYOD Plus is an **existing** battery grid-services program. Its published indicative incentive **$400 per kW committed** (plus another **$400/kW** for specifically eligible low/moderate income accounts), with underlying contract requirements, applies to suitable **new household batteries attached to solar**, not to an unspecified commercial-scale botanic data center. Incentive recipient may be a leasing company. The VPP model remains a separate residential phase.
- A private landowner's acreage, name recognition or philanthropic interests never establish participation. **An attractive PR/ESG story is a possible benefit only if independently measured community benefit and genuine local authority exist.**

## 3. Pilot project boundary: start small and make it beautiful

**Phase 0: consent + site diligence.** Prioritize legally permitted, **already disturbed or compatible educational/agricultural facilities**, without trespass or claim to public land. The historic **613-acre Olowalu Town** envelope in v0.1 is only a **hypothetical spatial denominator**; not a current developable single parcel. The restored **19.4-acre debris-staging quarry is separate public land** with independent restrictions. Any Native Hawaiian cultural inventory, flood, sea-level-rise, wildfire and archaeological no-build layer precedes siting. Existing community uses cannot be displaced by 'regeneration.'

**Phase 1: Living Landscape Field Lab (first fundable unit, not a resort).**
- **Small, scenic botanical and native stewardship site**, drawn from preexisting lawful use. Integrate native dry forest/restoration and traditional useful plants chosen by local stewards; place **ornamental, noninvasive rainforest display** in a contained water-budgeted zone only if locally desired and permitted. Never plant fire-prone exotics solely for visuals.
- **10 kW average IT inference**, PUE **1.2** assumption, and a further **3 kW average combined critical monitoring/pump/communications load**; the 3 kW is a simplification, not a real landscape irrigation system design.
- **100 kW BioSolar PV** over compatible gardens and structures, **450 kWh rated battery** emergency sizing hypothesis, grid connected; weatherproof and fire-safe utility rooms, independent safety controllers, architecture screened by vegetation. **Not** a hyperscale or AI-training campus.
- Sensor grid: local cameras only in rights-cleared ecological zones; soil moisture and evapotranspiration, canopy and leaf temperature, wildland fire fuel and invasive detection, wetland/reef runoff indicators, native seedling survival, PV temperature/soiling/shade, inverter/battery SOC and historical generation. GPS obfuscation and controls for sensitive cultural and endangered-species locations.
- AI only proposes bounded noncritical choices, e.g. irrigation allocation, survey routes, local plant-health triage, PV cleaning, botanical pest review, battery SOC reserve. Electrical relays and life-safety, potable-water sanitation and chemical dosing stay under code-certified deterministic systems and human licensed oversight.
- Clean, locally produced educational and scientific services and workforce apprenticeships; no sale or export of culturally protected training data without explicit authority.

**Phase 2, separately financed and permissioned:** resident-first affordable housing, garden public amenities, six hypothetical licensed food-truck pads, agroforestry, microplastic interceptors outside unsafe surf lanes, and **beautiful West Maui distributed desal + ZLD/mineral recovery** where independently approved. Deuterium remains a separate potential isotope-assay **research** stream in water molecules; zero base revenue. **Do not add water/ZLD, residential housing, marine devices or land acquisition to Phase 1 costs and label them financed.** Use the v0.1 613-acre allocation only for long-range scenario comparisons.

## 4. Transparent USD economic model — first 100 kW / 10 kW IT pilot

Companion runnable Python: [`maui_living_landscape_pilot_finance_v03.py`](maui_living_landscape_pilot_finance_v03.py). **All dollar figures are non-bid stress-test assumptions.** Nothing in this model depends on already secured tax credits, grant awards, land, data center clients or botanical ticket sales.

### 4.1 Upfront capital screen

| Independent scope | USD assumption |
|---|---:|
| 100 kW BioSolar PV, $3,000/kW installed | $300,000 |
| 450 kWh nameplate battery at $500/kWh | $225,000 |
| 10 kW inference IT, edge network, monitoring computers | $180,000 |
| AI/software integration and environmental sensors | $160,000 |
| Native nursery, landscape restoration and plant care establishment | $280,000 |
| Education, training, public teaching exhibit | $180,000 |
| Access, utility interconnect, small buildings, engineering and fire measures | $350,000 |
| **Direct capital** | **$1,675,000** |
| **25% contingency** | **$418,750** |
| **Illustrative Phase 1 total** | **$2,093,750** |

Excluded: land/easement purchase, legal/zoning changes, geotechnical contingencies above 25%, significant water source construction, 1MGD RO/ZLD, full homes, new district sewer infrastructure, hazardous waste, financing during construction and later expansion. Some civil/utility costs could exceed assumed scope.

### 4.2 Annual energy balance is possible, *not* 24/7 self-sufficiency

10 kW IT ×1.2 PUE + 3 kW other averaged load = **15 kW total average** = **131.4 MWh/yr**. 100 kW PV ×1,600 kWh/kW-year = 160 MWh nominal; assume **+1.5% BioSolar uplift**, **162.4 MWh/yr** hypothesis. With a coarse **70% of annual solar generation self-consumed**, **113.68 MWh** avoids purchases, **48.72 MWh** exported, **17.72 MWh** imported annually. The 70% rule is algebraic only; actual economics require 8,760-hour profile, batteries, inverter and utility constraints.

At illustrative avoided purchase value **$0.28/kWh** and export value **$0.08/kWh**: avoided purchases ~$31,830/year **are already reflected in the low import bill**, not a separate additional cash inflow. Purchased energy ~$4,962/year; exports ~$3,898/year. Unverified grid fees, demand charges and tariffs could change this materially.

Rated battery 450 kWh ×80% deliverability /15kW constant critical load = **24 hours** sunless autonomy, **not 72 hours**. A prior 450kWh example claiming 72 hours at ~5kW assumes a *distinct, deeply downshifted emergency load*, which needs engineering and operator validation. The initial pilot should have selectable subcritical load-shed modes and may retain the utility as its main firm supply.

Proposed sources with explicit contract tests: stewardship service agreements; research/remote sensing service procurement; academic cost-share; garden admission/donation *if community-approved*; nursery product sales; horticulture training contracts; utility performance payments *if actually qualified*. **No dollar of speculative funding is booked in base case.** Public grants and charitable underwriting may be appropriate because the project produces valuable public goods not captured by electricity revenues alone.

## 6. Institutional funding architecture — do not double count

Propose legally distinct accounting **work packages**:
**WP1** rights-cleared restoration, native nursery and habitat MRV (potentially foundation/donor/public restoration contract);
**WP2** resident/Indigenous-governed ecology research, training, dataset controls and field IT (university research/civic tech partnership, not assumed tuition funding);
**WP3** built BioSolar and battery assets (owner capital, utility-approved VPP if residential and eligible, tax counsel for state/federal dates, no automatic grants);
**WP4** affordable housing and water infrastructure (distinct later capital program, not covered by $2.094M pilot);
**WP5** commercial nursery operations/public botanical experience (resident-governed, lawful concessions and open public access);
**WP6** coastal catchment and permitted ocean cleanup pilots (marine regulator review, not unpermitted floating devices).

**Suggested stakeholder outreach sequencing:**
1. Begin with Native Hawaiian stewards and local residents, environmental rights and actual use of land. No off-island sponsor outreach bypassing local decision-making.
2. Obtain recorded owner/land manager consent and land-use planner's written feasibility screen.
3. Offer University of Hawaiʻi and existing state/forest management researchers a small **independent data protocol + use-case** pilot; cite AI Puaʻa as precedent without claiming staff relationships.
4. Coordinate with DOFAW/Maui County Agriculture/HCF on **their specific application rules**, no unsolicited retroactive grant attribution.
5. Only then approach philanthropic sponsors, island agriculture landholders and technology firms for **specific separately scoped purchases**. Community governance, access and measured benefits are the pitch; donor publicity is optional and consent-based.

## 7. Monitoring, open science and AI rights

Use locally hosted inference to recognize invasive fauna/plants, monitor native plant survival, predict understory PV microclimate, minimize irrigation, estimate wildfire fuels, identify watershed sediment events, and schedule maintenance. Train or fine-tune limited models only on **fully permissioned, public or specifically licensed ecological data**; compute purpose **inference first**, not speculative hyperscale training. Public dashboards report aggregate verified energy, water, plant survival, revegetation, county restoration jobs and resident costs. Exact protected locations and Indigenous traditional knowledge remain governed by locally determined permissions, potentially non-public indefinitely.

Measurement units and standards:
- Energy: utility-metered hourly kWh, inverter power MW/kW, battery AC delivered kWh, SOC and cycle loss, rated outage hours with weather scenarios.
- Plants: survival and biomass growth, species richness, invasive cover, plant root-zone EC/salinity, water use per accepted plant, fire fuel load.
- Water and land: measured legal reclaimed irrigation gallons, freshwater displacement **only with actual baseline proof**, sediment/nutrient mass avoided, soil contamination, coral/reef impacts.
- Socioeconomics: permanent local jobs, stable housing support, employee retention, resident access, affordable produce, local supplier spend, actual outside funds vs internal transfer, audited reserve costs.
- Climate/ecology credits: no carbon/water/biodiversity credits asserted until independently verifiable; separate social benefits from bankable cash flows.

**Go/no-go conditions:** host site's existing zoning allows demonstration; culture/heritage and seawater/reef protection rules verified; local governance and owner invitation documented; solar/battery model passes hourly safety and permit checks; grant match and fiscal sponsorship validated; sponsor cannot own/sell restricted data; recurring service contracts or an endowment cover modeled operating gaps; private funds never commandeer housing or traditional water rights.
