# Gulf-to-East-Coast Domestic Fuel First — Product-Matched Import Displacement, Strategic Reserves and DPA Logistics
**Atlas Lattice / Open Regenerative Compute Standard (ORCS)**  
**v0.1 · October 16, 2026**  
**Status: PROPOSED / CITED / SCENARIO / NON-CANON / ZERO REALIZED CREDIT**  
**Scope:** U.S. Gulf Coast (PADD 3) finished fuels to the East Coast (PADD 1), especially Northeast ultra-low sulfur diesel/heating oil and jet fuel, seasonal shortages and domestic shipping infrastructure. An independent companion to existing national self-sufficiency and maritime/water research. **No claim that a DPA export ban exists.**

## 1. Executive policy recommendation

Establish a **Domestic Deliverability and Strategic Fuel Reserve Ledger** before imposing broad export restrictions. Verify which **specific grade of export-bound U.S. Gulf refined product** can be moved at acceptable delivered cost to a U.S. shortage region without disrupting domestic refineries, statutory contracts, defense or allied supply. During defined regional scarcity, acquire or incentivize volume to cover verified household, emergency, critical industrial and strategic reserve needs, using commercial offtakes and legal, time-bound federal authorities. Finance long-lead refinery flexibility, terminal, storage and qualified maritime/pipeline investments under existing Defense Production Act (DPA) authorities. Publish monthly grade-matched PADD inventories, winter reserve floor and transparent before/after price, emissions and ton-mile results.

**Principle:** domestic essential demand and demonstrable strategic buffers should be evaluated before discretionary exports **when logistics and chemistry really allow substitution**, not an unqualified ban on all crude, gasoline or LNG exports. International counterparties, refinery profitability, maritime labor, pipeline limits and legal rights matter. A well-defined program also avoids accidental harm to U.S. Gulf refineries that need export markets for surplus grades.

## 2. New and authoritative 2026 signals

1. **EIA August 17:** Emergency Jones Act waiver beginning March 17 allowed new coastwise movements. Waterborne Gulf->East shipments reached **1.2 million b/d April 2026**, **11% above prior record**. Finished gasoline blend components **620k b/d** and distillate **220k b/d** had record April waterborne movements on this corridor. Most Gulf->East waterborne supply historically goes to Florida, so these numbers are **not** proof that Northeast import demand was displaced. Gulf->West shipments also changed dramatically. Source https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=67964 .
2. **EIA October 2026 STEO:** East Coast distillate imports **124,000 b/d in 2025**, ~**10%** of regional demand; mostly Canadian product delivered to Northeast, with winter months approaching **15%** import share. EIA forecasts seasonal inventories **20–30% below** five-year average through winter. This import base is **historical**, not unfilled need in October 2026. Source https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/ .
3. **EIA Oct 15 weekly data:** East Coast (PADD 1) distillate imports **79k b/d for week ending October 9, 2026**; do not confuse a weekly reading with 2025 annual 124k/d. Source https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/pet_move_wkly_a_epd0_im0_mbblpd_4.htm .
4. **April 20 DPA §303 Presidential Determination:** domestic petroleum production, refining, transmission pipelines, storage and marine terminals declared defense-essential and authorized for financial instruments, purchase commitments and related capacity. This is **not** a blanket export diversion, Jones Act repeal or entitlement to appropriate private inventory. Source https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/04/presidential-determination-pursuant-to-section-303-of-the-defense-production-act-of-1950-as-amended-on-domestic-petroleum-production-refining-and-logistics-capacity/ .
5. **Delek Tyler Oct 13 press report:** executive proposal to leverage DPA for refinery expansion; 74k b/d current processing cited, but added capacity, costs, lead time and federal financial terms **not yet established**. Source https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/trump-administration-expand-delek-texas-refinery-using-defense-production-act-2026-10-13/ .
6. **NBER prior structural research:** An empirically informed Jones Act counterfactual found that, absent cabotage limitations in its model, Gulf exports could replace **nearly all East Coast jet fuel and ULSD imports**, while replacing only **36% of conventional gasoline and light crude imports**. Estimated consumer benefits ~$769M/year, producer profit reduction ~$367M/year under studied period assumptions; **not** 2026 savings guarantees, and does not mean free transport or surplus guaranteed. Source https://www.nber.org/digest/202402/jones-act-and-energy-prices .
7. **Global October 2026 constraint:** IEA warnings on diesel scarcity to 2027 and Reuters coverage of trade disruptions show why producer/freight price, refinery utilization, international supply and allied obligations must be separately modeled. https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/diesel-shortages-could-persist-well-into-2027-iea-says-2026-10-14/

## 3. Quantitative import displacement screen: East Coast distillate

Baseline **124,000 barrels/day 2025 annual average**. Annualization uses 365 days. These are **counterfactual displaced foreign import volumes** if identical-grade, appropriately timed, deliverable additional Gulf supply exists; no automatic economic gain or export-curtailment benefit is assumed.

| Share of 2025 annual imports displaced | barrels/day | million barrels/year |
|---|---:|---:|
| 25% | 31,000 | 11.315 |
| 50% | 62,000 | 22.630 |
| 75% | 93,000 | 33.945 |
| 100% | 124,000 | 45.260 |

**2026 risk case:** model monthly winter imports separately because 2025 seasonal imports were much higher than annual average. Northeast end-use diesel/heating oil demand, PADD 1c Florida marine supply, grade specifications, Colonial pipeline receipt obligations, and inventory minimums must not be combined as if fungible. Do not apply an optimistic NBER counterfactual to 2026 without updating prices, fleet rules, pipelines, export tonnage and counterparty trade.

### Avoided imports are not identical to net U.S. economic gains

Dollar value of displaced purchases (e.g. 62k b/d × unknown delivered price) is a **gross trade-flow figure**; U.S. refiner shipment revenues, lost export revenues, alternate foreign suppliers, marine freight, refinery margins, tax, strategic costs, product blending, worker income and changes in consumer prices must be counted separately. Do not call every displaced dollar an incremental U.S. net benefit. No fabricated shipping quote or oil price in base case.

Ledger per grade g, region r, month m:
- domestic refined supply physically offered to PADD 1 = exportable merchant surplus + additional legally contracted output − unmet Gulf/inland priority;
- effective delivery = min(surplus, available pipeline + actual qualified marine capacity + usable terminal/port capacity, grade-equivalent deficit, emergency inventory targets);
- net import displacement = min(delivered incremental grade-equivalent supply, verified actual imports avoidable), **subject to documented attribution**;
- reserve buildup separately from current consumption; if one barrel goes to storage it does not also replace current imports;
- export opportunity cost = export netback foregone vs East Coast netback, plus breach/contract cost and downstream allied impact.
- emergency floor follows days of cover on projected **peak essential demand** with hurricane, refinery outage, winter stress and recovery-lag scenarios, not a single national headline reserve figure.

### Three policy cases
**A. Baseline trade** — current shipping and commercial routing including waivers where valid; no new domestic-first rule.  
**B. Voluntary/contracted domestic-priority procurement** — targeted purchases and logistics rewards for matching Gulf finished product to Northeast needs; DPA-backed terminal/pipeline/tanker modernization where eligible.  
**C. Emergency trigger** — conditional narrow product/region/time prioritization only after statutory legal review, documented severe shortage and proportional, compensated available measures. **Never assume DPA §303 alone automatically permits unilateral export prohibition, indefinite Jones Act suspension or seizure.**

Test 25/50/75/100% import-displacement scenarios with no guarantee all are achievable. Compare consumer price incidence, wage and supply-chain savings, avoided foreign import exposure, Gulf refiner export revenue/costs, allied import disruption, carbon/freight, PADD stock floor and service reliability. Distinguish permanent Jones Act reform from lawful time-limited waivers; include shipyard/maritime safety/workforce externalities.

## 4. DPA infrastructure candidate slate

Eligible-for-analysis categories (not awarded projects):
- debottleneck U.S.-built pipeline pumping, scheduling and terminal tank capacity; Colonial/other operators must consent and preserve safety;
- qualified coastwise vessel availability, chartering and emergency waiver procedure with statutory scrutiny;
- secondary Northeast storage and blending facilities with code, coastal hazard, tank-fire and neighborhood safety checks;
- refinery unit flexibility for winter ULSD/heating fuel specs and jet fuel, including military use where justified;
- granular commodity tracking and reserve digital twin (ORCS inference/forecasting, **not autonomous market control**), with separate auditable supplier and operator decision systems;
- rail/truck only for specific emergency grades/volumes where safety, costs and capacity justify, not default for high-volume marine substitution.

Use **preapproved legal purchase/offtake contracts**, time-limited provisions, public benefits reporting, contractor labor, private-sector procurement and independent auditors. DPA authority and federal solicitations **must be confirmed for each project**.

6. Model product-quality constraints (ULSD sulfur, winter biodiesel blending, jet fuel ASTM/spec, heating oil and propane as independent streams).
7. Report *actual* relative consumer cost and outages prevented; publish positive and negative results. Do not double count consumer benefits vs transfer from refiners, or savings from already-existing waivers.

## 6. Institutional decision

Recommend DOE/EIA/DOT/MARAD/DHS, PADD emergency planning authorities and accredited academic researchers **examine a public domestic-deliverability ledger and time-bound matched-fuel priority procurement demonstration** against the present East Coast winter stock shortfall. DPA capital authority already exists in principle; actual project execution must be justified and lawful. Federal policy should respect coastwise workforce, tribal and coastal communities, refinery economics, trading partners and consumer needs.
