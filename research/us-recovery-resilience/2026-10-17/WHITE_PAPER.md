# American Recovery and Resilience Network
## County guarantees, regional construction, and three 30-year financing scenarios

**October 17, 2026 · Working white paper v0.1 · Prototype v0.8**  
**PROPOSED / SIMULATED / NON-CANON / NO REALIZED CREDIT**

This is an open research proposal. No government adoption, appropriation, manufacturer approval, independently verified public benefit, or financial underwriting is implied. It develops the New Deal 2.0 v0.2 proposal [1] and the preceding national waste-network screen. The downloadable prototype is included in this directory's release archive.

## Findings

A coordinated recovery network is a plausible civilian resilience investment, but its present financial case depends on explicit public-service funding. The new A/B/C simulations place **partial installed capital at approximately $38.6B, $96.7B, and $196.5B**, including assumed planning allowances. These are scenario budgets, not bids or confidence limits. They do not cover all national waste, water, desalination, plastics, or construction recovery infrastructure.

The corresponding illustrative **county-module commissioning endpoints are programme years 5, 6, and 9**. Slower wastewater work extends last commissioning across the modeled portfolio to **years 6, 7, and 10**. A 2027 authorized start is assumed. Individual sites could finish earlier; national coverage requires sustained parallel delivery. No scenario treats national average completion as proof that every county has received its required asset and services.

All three scenarios retain an uncovered annual cash requirement. At steady operation while all tranche loans remain active, the requirement is approximately **$2.64B, $8.33B, and $21.22B/year**. These are financing gaps, not calculated social losses. Avoided disruption, soil and water improvements, workforce development and quality of life could justify public expenditure; their incremental value has not yet been established by this model.

An immediate policy connection is now verified: the **June 29, 2026 phosphate-fertilizer emergency proclamation** [2]. It strengthens the rationale for a qualified agricultural-phosphorus recovery pilot. It does not establish how much of the emergency need the network can satisfy, nor authorize or fund this proposal. A separate February order addresses elemental phosphorus [3]; recovered agricultural phosphate is not automatically a substitute for elemental phosphorus.

The most actionable engineering gap is **construction sequencing**. A new regional calculator separates permits/site readiness, civil work, equipment delivery, and installation/commissioning. Hypothetical qualified 3D printing can reduce the civil-work path and selected costs; it cannot shorten an unrelated equipment bottleneck. No printing saving is credited to the A/B/C capital budgets.

## 1. Scope and inherited evidence

The financial base consists of county food/yard/urban-wood modules, local manure modules, state wastewater nutrient recovery, an industrial-food capacity floor, and a three-plant full-ZLD option. National physical recovery quantities were screened previously at 25%, 50%, and 75% capture. County municipal quantities are population allocations, not measured local feedstock. County manure, agricultural residues, forestry and brine inventories remain incomplete.

The midpoint prior screen contains roughly $77.4B of partial capital and a $7.07B annual funding gap at 5%/30-year financing. Scenario B adds a **25% capital planning allowance** and staged execution; its steady gap therefore differs from that earlier figure. The additional allowance changes capital and debt, not the inherited operating cost assumptions. It is not an independently derived estimate of every missing scope item.

Plastics and construction recovery have separate physical screens using EPA's 2018 baselines [4–5]. Their capital and comprehensive operating costs are not added to the programme financial totals. Full wastewater reuse conveyance, a complete national desal inventory, contaminated-site remediation, major workforce/QOL packages, and conditional crop/forest expansions are also not fully costed. Some modeled energy output has broader physical scope than the capital budget. Do not call these figures the all-inclusive national network price.

## 2. County benefits are a non-substitutable delivery requirement

The prototype now carries **3,144 individual county-equivalent requirement records**, matching its inherited geographic register. Each record requires a local appropriately scaled bioreactor and access to the state wastewater recovery system. A regional or national surplus cannot waive those requirements.

This is a universal-service design choice with a budget consequence. Small or sparse counties may require continuing cross-subsidy or availability payments. These obligations must be shown, not removed to improve a portfolio return. Actual reactor sizing must use collectible local sources, existing uses, contamination and seasonal availability. Population alone is insufficient.

For each county require:

1. The named local asset and operator, source commitments, and usable service capacity.
2. A baseline and target for a relevant local service, with units, beneficiaries and delivery date.
3. A funded operating, maintenance and replacement plan, including public support where needed.
4. Access to paid training and jobs, with places and outcomes recorded rather than promised by a multiplier.
5. Environmental and community acceptance, applicable consultation, complaint resolution and safe residual handling.
6. Evidence of delivery and independent review before an actual benefit is credited.

All current registry records are **MISSING_EVIDENCE**. None is reported as a verified delivered county benefit. The implemented check confirms the geographic requirements and missing evidence; it is not an authenticated approval system. National capacity percentages are capital-weighted and cannot override individual county gates. A project outside a county may provide additional regional benefits but does not silently fulfill the county's onsite-reactor requirement.

## 3. A/B/C assumptions

| Assumption | A: lower capture / lower budget | B: midpoint / coordinated | C: higher capture / stress |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Capture scenario | 25% | 50% | 75% |
| County cost case | Lower assumed budget | Middle assumed budget | Higher assumed budget |
| Extra capital planning allowance | 15% | 25% | 40% |
| Fixed loan rate / term per draw | 3% / 30 years | 5% / 30 years | 7% / 30 years |
| Main portfolio draw years | 1–4 | 1–5 | 1–7 |
| Main annual draw shares | 15/25/30/30% | 10/20/25/25/20% | 5/10/15/20/20/15/15% |
| County/manure/industrial commissioning lag | 1 year | 1 year | 2 years |
| Wastewater commissioning lag | 2 years | 2 years | 3 years |
| Three-plant ZLD commissioning lag | 3 years | 3 years | 4 years |
| ZLD capital draw | Year 1 | Year 1 | Year 1 |

Lag means commissioning occurs in the indicated later programme year, with half-year output in that commissioning year and full output thereafter. For example, a year-1 draw with lag 1 operates at half rate in year 2. These are execution hypotheses, not evidence that every site can achieve them. They simplify spending into tranche draws rather than engineering progress payments. Scenario C tests greater recovery and difficult execution simultaneously; it is not the same project at a higher interest rate.

All dollars use a **flat nominal planning convention with zero inflation**, not a claim that prices will stay fixed. No price escalation, taxes, grants, refinancing, demand growth, productivity learning, carbon credits, or monetized social benefits are assumed. Future analysis must add independently varied escalation and demand stress. Both operating receipts and costs/reserves scale with each commissioned component. Additional construction carry equals 1%/year of drawn uncommissioned capital. This is a declared placeholder for pre-operating expenditure, not a quoted national cost.

The inherited operating-cost budget is unchanged by the capital allowance. A full cost revision must separately estimate labor, energy, maintenance, testing, rejects and replacement reserves. Brine is represented by its net operating deficit; the model does not invent a gross mineral sales forecast to decompose that deficit.

## 4. Thirty-year results and debt tail

| Screening output | A | B | C |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Base partial capital | $33.6B | $77.4B | $140.4B |
| Capital including allowance | **$38.6B** | **$96.7B** | **$196.5B** |
| Last county-module commissioning | Year 5 | Year 6 | Year 9 |
| Last modeled portfolio commissioning | Year 6 | Year 7 | Year 10 |
| Steady annual uncovered cash | **$2.64B** | **$8.33B** | **$21.22B** |
| Uncovered cash, first 30 programme years | $74.0B | $229.4B | $554.7B |
| PV of that funding at 5% | $35.8B | $109.5B | $255.0B |
| Loan principal remaining after year 30 | $3.22B | $12.77B | $45.69B |
| Loan payments after year 30 | $3.39B | $14.05B | $54.41B |
| Final loan payment | Year 33 | Year 34 | Year 36 |
| New food/manure net electricity at steady state | 2.94 TWh/y | 5.87 TWh/y | 8.81 TWh/y |

The 30-year programme window is not the lifetime of the last loan. Each later capital cohort receives its own 30-year amortization. The annual output file extends through the final debt payment and discloses remaining principal at year 30. Future operating support also continues where revenues do not cover operating cost; the debt tail alone is not the remaining total public obligation.

Uncovered cash is the amount needed after modeled operator receipts, costs, reserves and debt. It is not necessarily a federal appropriation: a lawful service-purchase plan could distribute it among national, state, county and other payers. No such payer commitments exist here. Borrowed capital is repaid through debt service already included in the funding calculation; adding the entire installed capital again would double count a financing burden. Public economic benefit-cost analysis must use real resource costs and incremental benefits instead of summing financing cash flows.

These scenarios should inform a pilot and budget debate, not establish that $1.5T of bankable New Deal projects exists. The proposed $180B bridge/risk window cannot be treated as both a permanent operating subsidy and unused loan-loss protection. Its complete credit exposure still needs scoring.

## 5. Regional construction and the role of 3D printing

The regional screen reserves one county-node slot per county equivalent, allows an explicit assumed number of existing qualifying nodes, and tests a user-entered rate of new starts per month. It uses equal module durations and parallel starts; neither manufacturer capacity nor permitting capacity has been verified.

The simplified critical path is:

**Project months = max(permit/site-readiness months + civil-work months, equipment-delivery months) + installation/commissioning months.**

Equipment procurement is assumed to begin in parallel from project start; actual procurement rights and readiness must be checked. Last completion equals the project duration plus the interval between the first and last start. A capacity-constrained start rate is therefore as important as shell-printing speed.

Consider an explicitly hypothetical example: permits/site readiness 6 months, civil works 9, equipment delivery 18, and installation/commissioning 6. Baseline completion is 24 months. If half the civil stage can be printed and that part is 50% faster, civil works fall to 6.75 months. Completion remains **24 months**, because equipment governs. With equipment available in 10 months instead, baseline completion is 21 months and the printing case is 18.75 months. That 2.25-month gain is a result of the entered dependencies, not a vendor performance claim.

At 10 starts/month, 254 Texas county slots and zero assumed existing qualifying nodes imply roughly **49.3 months** from first start to last completion under the 24-month example. It requires about 240 projects concurrently at peak, which is a delivery requirement to test, not proof the workforce or suppliers can meet it. If all 3,144 national county slots were new at 100 starts/month, the analogous screen is roughly **55.4 months**. These figures cover county modules only and exclude slower wastewater and desal construction. The user can change region, start rate, durations, existing nodes and module cost in the prototype.

Hypothetical cost savings are limited to the eligible share of capital. A 15% eligible civil share and 20% saving reduce gross project capital by 3% before printer, testing, mobilization and related premiums. At a purely illustrative $5M/module, that is $150,000 gross; a $100,000 premium leaves $50,000. The default qualification checkbox is off, so no saving is credited. A/B/C budgets remain unchanged by this separate screen.

ICON's current Titan page advertises deliveries beginning in 2027 [6]. The materials/program page describes a proprietary integrated system [7]. This is not evidence of a deployed national Titan fleet or approval to use our recovered aggregate. Recycled-aggregate printing research [8] establishes a research direction, not an approved local formulation. Printer time and price are not whole-facility completion time and cost.

Required next evidence: real site bills of quantities; appropriate printable applications; separate tank/liner/reinforcement requirements; certified mix chemistry and grading; geotechnical and structural design; printer/mixer availability; utility interfaces; actual equipment quotes and manufacturing slots; installation and operator-training capacity. Prefabricated process skids, standardized designs, existing-plant retrofits, and coordinated equipment procurement should be compared alongside printing.

## 6. Phosphorus: turn a national resource estimate into a timely fertilizer service

The June 29 proclamation identifies threatened phosphate-fertilizer availability and provides temporary relief on certain Moroccan fertilizer duties, for up to eight months or earlier termination [2]. We verified issuance and the described relief; this paper does not certify the current status of every implementing tariff measure or establish entitlement to funding.

The modeled new food/biomass/manure first-season phosphorus quantities are approximately **9,533, 19,066 and 28,599 tonnes P/year** for A/B/C. These are additional to the separate prior wastewater recovery screen and must not be mistaken for the entire network's phosphorus inventory. The prior midpoint combined inventory context contains about 323,832 tonnes P/year, but it mixes the inherited wastewater screen with new first-season quantities; it is not an agronomically harmonized saleable-fertilizer forecast. This release deliberately avoids using that combined number as a proven supply offset.

To address the emergency, recoverable nutrient must become a qualified product, accepted by an actual grower, in the correct season, delivered at a workable price. Match soil/crop requirements and nutrient availability; avoid nutrient overapplication. Record P and P2O5 consistently, maintain contaminant controls, identify whether a displaced purchase was imported, and preserve existing manure nutrient cycling in the counterfactual.

The separate elemental-phosphorus order [3] identifies an industrial supply concern. We grant no elemental-phosphorus substitution credit. Processing recovered phosphate into industrial products would require its own qualified technology, energy, safety, buyer and cost case. This proposal does not depend on expanding glyphosate use or military applications.

Immediate pilot priority: pair an existing wastewater recovery opportunity and a nearby agricultural buyer with source-specific assays, a seasonal purchase agreement, a delivered-price comparison and a farm nutrient plan. Determine commissioning lead time from actual retrofit scope. Recovery is a medium-term addition; emergency imports, lawful reserves, efficient nutrient use and existing production should not be assumed unnecessary before it operates.

## 7. Employment, education and quality of life

Employment is a core delivery objective, but job-years, permanent staffing, indirect jobs and net new employment require separate reporting. BLS input-output employment requirements are sector-specific and price-year-specific [9]; applying one coefficient to all infrastructure would obscure industry mix and displacement.

As a transparent budget capacity calculation—not an employment forecast—allocating 20–35% of capital to US construction labor at $90,000–$140,000 fully loaded per labor-year gives approximately:

| Hypothetical labor-budget capacity | A | B | C |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Construction job-years across build | 55,000–150,000 | 138,000–376,000 | 281,000–764,000 |

These assumptions are not contractor staffing plans. They do not establish worker availability, domestic sourcing, additional national employment, or permanent jobs. Labor is already inside capital; wages are not added again as an independent economic benefit. Ongoing staffing needs plant-specific rosters and coverage for leave, maintenance and emergency operations. A small node still requires reliable access to qualified staff; AI cannot be used to make safe staffing disappear on paper.

BLS describes long-term on-the-job training and typically state licensing for water/wastewater operators [10]. Start paid operator pathways before commissioning. Partner with existing operators, community colleges, registered apprenticeship providers and relevant licensing bodies. Teach material accounting, sampling, process safety, electrical/mechanical work, controls, cybersecurity and emergency response. Measure completion, qualification, placement, wage progression and retention. Include incumbent workers rather than only new recruits.

Recommended workforce/QOL contract terms include health coverage, paid training and leave, predictable rosters, safe shift lengths, clean facilities, water/heat protection, accessible transport, and housing or childcare assistance where local conditions require it. Ask workers and residents about actual conditions. These are proposed service standards; their complete cost is not separately priced in the inherited budgets, so no claim is made that the current O&M allowance fully funds them. No numerical productivity or health dividend is credited.

## 8. Environmental acceptance is an investment gate

Recovery must improve the full disposal and treatment counterfactual. Track methane leakage and net energy, residual transport and disposal, nutrient losses, salinity and soil compatibility, water use, noise, odors, truck routes, exposures and cumulative neighborhood effects. Assess contamination before crediting agricultural use. Capture in a residue is not proven destruction.

EPA's current biosolids page discusses its draft PFOA/PFOS risk assessment and management work [11]. Do not interpret pathogen treatment or a generic biosolids classification as blanket assurance against persistent contaminants. Recovery routes need product-specific evidence and lawful application limits. No default assumption is made that pyrolysis or printing safely eliminates PFAS, microplastics, metals or other hazards.

Recovered construction materials require appropriate application qualification. Plastics need polymer/additive sorting and buyer acceptance. Trex and ByFusion offer commercial pathway examples [12–13], not acceptance of arbitrary mixed waste. Track service life, fire performance, weathering, emissions, shedding, repairability and end-of-life recovery. Reusing an unsafe product to avoid a disposal cost is not a public benefit.

## 9. Self-sufficiency, domestic output and the trade aspiration

Use separate targets for critical supply continuity, delivered input affordability, domestic productive capacity, actual import dependence and the overall trade balance. A nation can improve a specific supply chain while continuing to import efficiently elsewhere. Avoid eliminating an import on paper by omitting a required grade, upstream chemical or imported machine.

The A/B/C priced product-and-energy receipt proxies, excluding gate fees and brine gross sales, are roughly **$1.25B, $2.51B and $3.76B/year** at steady state. They reflect assumed sales or savings in the prior model, not measured new national output. They are neither GDP value added nor net benefits: recovered inputs can be intermediate purchases elsewhere, and their use can displace existing domestic output. Gross domestic output and import reduction cannot be added together without tracing the overlap.

For scale, the dashboard's historical February 19, 2026 release reports a 2025 goods-and-services deficit of $901.5B, with a $1,240.9B goods deficit and a $339.5B services surplus [14]. That release has been superseded; it is retained as an explicitly dated comparison, not the latest estimate. The recovery sales proxy does not establish a comparable trade improvement. This partial waste programme cannot substantiate elimination of the overall deficit.

Move toward the broader aspiration through an expanded portfolio: crop/season demand matching and farmer purchase commitments; reliable water reuse; soil testing and nutrient-use efficiency; domestic material processing that fills specific remaining gaps; diversified equipment/reagent suppliers; affordable firm power; maintenance and repair; selective reserves; cold-chain and freight improvements; and competitive domestic production in other major import categories. Model imported content of every replacement and effects on exports. A target is not a forecast.

## 10. AI and human audit flows

The prototype implements deterministic screening and downloadable research records. No live AI service, authenticated approval authority, autonomous dispatch, or plant control is connected. AI can later reconcile records, examine alternatives and flag anomalies. Material conservation, financing and eligibility checks must remain reproducible. Human authorities approve consequential commitments; independent reviewers assess resource/buyers, engineering/environment, and finance/public value.

Every decision record should connect source snapshots, units and grade, constraints, a counterfactual, alternatives, modeled outcomes, uncertainty, responsible approver, execution receipts and measured results. Data age must match decision use. Annual surveys cannot become real-time observations by refreshing the screen. Multiple AI summaries of one source do not constitute independent verification.

## 11. Immediate gaps filled and next work

This release adds staged capital cohorts, debt-tail disclosure, component-specific commissioning, a regional critical-path/printing sensitivity, and an explicit per-county requirements registry. It verifies the fertilizer emergency connection and separates agricultural and elemental phosphorus. The public package contains assumptions, annual results, reproducible code, tests and the downloadable prototype.

Next priorities are: (1) a small set of local assay/buyer and retrofit dossiers; (2) actual vendor and civil-work bid matrices; (3) source-by-source county agricultural/manure data; (4) harmonized fertilizer-grade import/demand accounts; (5) a costed worker/QOL package; (6) printing bills of quantities and application approvals; (7) source-specific pollution and residual controls; and (8) independent public-benefit appraisal against reserves, efficiency measures and conventional construction.

DOE's wastewater measure toolkit provides practical assessment and efficiency routes [15]. Such improvements may provide earlier benefits while larger recovery projects are designed. Evaluate them from each facility's actual baseline; do not multiply reported programme savings across every plant without validation.

## 12. Reproduction and limits

Run `python run_abc.py` beside `scenario-inputs.json` and `base-network-results.json` to recreate scenario JSON and CSV outputs. Run `python test_abc.py` for financing and conservation checks. The release archive contains prototype source, a standalone HTML dashboard and its additional calculation/UI-harness tests. Open the standalone HTML in a modern browser; bundled data and simulations work without a server. Source links and optional live feed refresh require connectivity.

Browser visual QA was unavailable in this environment. Prior user feedback reports the v0.7 interface worked; that does not substitute for visual/touch testing of new v0.8 features. Automated calculation and DOM-harness checks are reported separately. All current outputs remain screening results, and the county evidence gate remains incomplete.
