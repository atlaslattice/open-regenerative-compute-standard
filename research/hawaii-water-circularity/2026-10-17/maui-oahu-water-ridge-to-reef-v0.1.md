## Executive finding

An integrated program can assess **reclaimed municipal wastewater → qualified irrigation/industrial supply**, **farm return-flow recovery → treatment → reuse**, **stormwater and roof capture → permitted off-stream storage**, **desalinated potable water → local bottling where displaced imports can be verified**, and **wastewater/sludge/brine recovery → qualified products**. Unlike raw annual rainfall, recoverable stormwater must be quantified from actual event hydrographs, available site area, water rights, ecological flow obligations and storage operation. Restoration in Lahaina/Olowalu must retain flood detention capacity and prioritize reef protection.

**Three deliberately hypothetical incremental municipal reuse build-out cases:** Maui 0.50/1.50/3.45 MGD plus Oʻahu 5/15/30 MGD. They imply combined 5.5/16.5/33.45 MGD of added delivered water, or 2.008/6.023/12.209 billion gallons/year; over five years 10.038/30.113/61.046 billion gallons, **if and only if continuously delivered, qualified, accepted by buyers, and additional to existing reuse**. These are design sensitivities, not probability intervals or 2026 projects. No extrapolation of 2022 actual reuse to 2026.

### Critical newer flood-risk evidence

In March 2026 Maui issued flood advisories/warnings when Lahaina-area retention basins approached capacity and crews pumped excess water. **Retention capacity dedicated to flood safety cannot be permanently occupied by irrigation inventory.** Any combined basin must reserve flood-storage volume, operate tested spillways and safe release protocols, and model storm forecasts with independently certified civil infrastructure. Source: https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/18513 and https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/18452.

**Five-year tariff-proxy arithmetic:** conservative $26.02M; central $78.07M; high $156.57M. This range is not profit, and higher sales do not mean higher net benefits if conveyance cost exceeds the tariff.

Annual aquifer protection = Σ(volume delivered to a verified prior groundwater customer × actual substitution fraction). Cannot assume all gross reused water protects potable groundwater; many farms use surface water and other water sources.

**Pipeline versus trucking:** One 8,000-gallon truck carries just 0.008 MGD/day per trip; 1 MGD requires 125 loaded trips/day. At sustained large loads a permanent tested pipeline is the appropriate *candidate*, with CAPEX, easements, pressure and pump-power design compared to modular alternatives. No pipeline route is approved here.

## C. Rainfall / catchment / storage — keep ecology and storm safety intact

Hāna, Big Bog and East Maui stream systems are **excluded from additional diversion siting** in this proposal. Investigate disturbed/off-stream corridors in Lahaina/Kahoma/Wahikuli, Kīhei drainage, Waikapū and existing Central/West Maui detention/storage assets. Olowalu–Ukumehame is a community-led *restoration* area, not assumed to offer free appropriable streamflow.

Acre-inch = **27,154 gallons**. For illustration, 100 acres at 10/20/40 inches per year and effective additional collection 20%/40%/60% yields:
- low: 5.43 million gal/year (0.0149 MGD annualized)
- mid: 21.72 million gal/year (0.0595 MGD annualized)
- high: 65.17 million gal/year (0.1786 MGD annualized)

These are intentionally **unit-based sensitivities**, not rainfall predictions for specific land parcels or capturable runoff; effective collection fractions are not measured. Rainfall *already used by crops or recharging groundwater* is never added again as a resource. Flood-detention and water-retention reservoirs must be sized independently, accounting for design storms and freeboard. Rainfall Atlas datasets: https://rainfallatlas.ikewai.org/downloads .

**Agricultural return-flow:** document irrigation application, crop evapotranspiration, soil storage, beneficial recharge, drainage, captured runoff, reject brines, energy and contaminant mass. Do not extrapolate hydroponic water efficiency to sugarcane, pasture or all field crops. Audit farm-specific drainage first; filtration alone cannot remove dissolved salts or all pesticides.

## D. Recovery chemistry, desal, sludge and supply security

National wastewater v0.3 is a **screen** with 50% recovery/deployment assumptions: $2.38B/year gross nutrient replacement value; $1.283B/year wholesale diesel-equivalent HTL output **as an alternative sludge-carbon pathway**; approximately $3.663B/year arithmetic product-value ceiling before costs/existing reuse. Neither is national profit; additional phosphorus may be far below gross inventory, while existing pellets and digestion reduce incremental opportunities. Full report: https://github.com/atlaslattice/open-regenerative-compute-standard/blob/main/research/us-wastewater-resources/v0.3-2026-10-16/report.md .

Oʻahu FY2024 100.18 MGD scaled against the national paper's 34 billion gallons/day approximate national flow yields roughly **$10.8M/year**, or **$54M/five years**, in conditional gross nutrients + HTL diesel equivalent, **not ocean-discharge inventory nor local sales**. Existing biosolids nutrient reuse, actual wastewater assays, real sludge yields and qualified product routes must be netted. No revenue from mineral sales or wastewater reuse is added before confirming nonoverlap.

**Seawater desal brine:** planned Kalaeloa concentrate disposition requires site-specific chemistry, RO production and concentrate volumes. Magnesium recovery, calcium for stabilization, salt, bromide, potassium, boron and other co-products are separate assays/processes, not automatic sales. Potable remineralization requires water-treatment/food-grade quality; struvite Mg is not an additional saleable Mg commodity when used as reagent. No new full-fleet US desal economic totals are claimed.

**Imported bottled water:** quantify finished-bottle mainland arrivals versus on-island bottling, packaging, transport, reusable/refill alternatives and actual buyers. Bottle wholesale revenue is not GDP or an added import saving. Use DBEDT official 2022 Hawaiʻi inter-county input-output tables for local value added and retained earnings; avoid arbitrary economic multipliers: https://dbedt.hawaii.gov/economic/input-output-study/.

**Import offsets:** physical unit and grade aligned. ORCS wastewater v0.3's 67.4% headline is gross modeled recovered P versus assumed P in **phosphate-rock imports**, not total US phosphorus imports nor incremental import displacement. Its potash comparison is 7.6%; Oʻahu actual purchase replacement is presently UNKNOWN. Keep within-island, interisland, mainland and foreign flows separate.

## E. Program gates and no-double-counting controls

1. **Water:** every gallon has one origin, baseline destination, treatment grade and delivered end-use, plus meter timestamp. Reuse plus reinfiltration or stormwater capture cannot appear twice.
2. **Carbon/sludge:** existing pellet use and AD/CHP/RNG/HTL compete for some material. Hybrid requires explicit carbon, energy and contaminant balance.
3. **Nutrients:** struvite phosphate/nitrogen credited only once; existing beneficial biosolids use is baseline not new import replacement.
4. **Minerals:** Mg supplied to struvite cannot also be booked as exported Mg(OH)₂ sales. Deep-seawater intake does not automatically mean higher Mg concentrations.
5. **Economy:** local gross business sales, Hawaiian GDP, off-island invoice displacement, import avoidance, and avoided public costs are distinct ledgers. Do not add gross sales to GDP or count an avoided purchase twice.
6. **Ocean:** record nitrogen/phosphorus/sediment mass **actually prevented** from arriving in coastal waters, including subsurface migration; concentrate injection is not zero discharge. Capture is not destruction for PFAS or microplastics.
7. **Land rights:** verify Native Hawaiian customary water rights, stream standards, easements, watershed ecology, fish habitat and landowner/community participation before selecting sites.
8. **Operations:** AI may optimize scheduling, pumps, leak detection and water quality forecasts; certified treatment, regulatory holds, alarms, manual override and human operators retain control.

## F. Deliverables requested for facility/farm-grade confidence

- Twelve monthly flow, R-1 grade/QA, receiving outfall/injection, biosolids output and water-sales records for each wastewater facility.
- Crop-specific acreage, irrigation water-right/source ledger, actual deliveries, seasons, evapotranspiration and return-flow quality.
- GIS watershed rainfall, event streamflow, parcel/land-cover/slope, rights, reservoir condition, pipeline alignment, power and pump curves.
- Sediment, total N/P, pathogens, PFAS, dissolved solids, salinity, microscale plastics, marine receiving-water baseline and ocean-loading fate.
- End-use buyers, paid purchase orders, existing fertilizers/import origin, tanker/ship freight, distribution, CAPEX/OPEX and financing.
- Existing Olowalu grant scope and local consultation before expanding sediment basin concepts.

### Candidate project screening — no construction recommendation

**West Maui**: Lahaina reclamation, Kahoma/Wahikuli existing flood works, Olowalu–Ukumehame native dryland and sediment-control restoration, reef measurements.

**South/Central Maui**: Kīhei R-1 users; Wailuku–Kahului R-1 treatment upgrade; Waikapū agricultural conveyance; runoff and infiltration assessment.

**Oʻahu ʻEwa/South**: Honouliuli qualified wastewater; Kalaeloa future desal/brine chemistry; industrial demand and potable aquifer protection.

**Oʻahu North/Central**: small municipal/private local reuse, Wahiawā storage, tailwater recirculation, then carefully sized south-to-north conveyance for remaining *verified* demand.

**Outcome metric**: five-year **net public/private system cost per additional gallon of truly displaced aquifer withdrawal** and **per verified tonne of marine sediment or pollutant load avoided**, with Hawaiian value added and purchase imports independently measured.
