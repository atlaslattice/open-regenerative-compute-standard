**Date:** 2026-10-26  
**Status:** PUBLIC RESEARCH / PRELIMINARY ENGINEERING / NOT VALIDATED FOR MANUFACTURE, CLINICAL USE, OR SPACEFLIGHT  
**Application areas:** adult emergency food, disaster relief, food-system resilience, animal-free nutrition (future variant), and exploratory spaceflight applications.  
**Evidence convention:** [VERIFIED] linked primary or credible reporting; [REPORTED] attributed supplier or journalist claim; [MODELED] explicit arithmetic or assumed cost; [TARGET] not yet achieved; [UNVERIFIED] not supportable with currently identified evidence.

> **Executive finding:** Yeast-derived protein appears commercially promising as an affordable building block for nutritionally useful emergency foods. At a modeled **$8/kg landed food-grade ingredient purchase price**, the **93.52 g** provisional AngeoPro ingredient load costs about **$0.75** per 1,000-kcal XL pouch. Mature-volume *illustrative*, non-quoted variable cost is **$2.88/pouch** at 1 million units. A robust public-health case exists for affordable complete nutrition, but the product has not been formulated, validated, sterilized, or medically qualified. Fermentation's cost and environmental advantages require product-specific testing and life-cycle assessment.

## 1. The problem is not merely insufficient protein

The global problem comprises (a) inadequate food energy, (b) poor dietary quality, (c) inadequate or poorly absorbed macro- and micronutrients, (d) unequal food access and costly distribution, and (e) shocks to water, agricultural, and transportation systems. A high-protein product alone cannot solve these.

**Important correction to earlier third-party summaries:** 'One billion people are protein deficient' is *not* an established contemporaneous WHO/FAO headcount. A [2017 modeling study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5783645/) estimated 12.2% *at risk* of protein intake below estimated average requirements and 1.4 billion *projected at risk by 2050 under specified assumptions*. Risk modeled from food supply and requirements must not be reported as clinically demonstrated protein deficiency. Likewise a stated **250 million tonnes/year protein deficit in 2050** was not corroborated with a credible, comparable primary reference and is **excluded as a validated headline**.

A [2024 global dietary micronutrient inadequacy model](https://knowledge-for-policy.ec.europa.eu/publication/global-estimation-dietary-micronutrient-inadequacies-modelling-analysis_en) estimated more than five billion people with insufficient intake of some individual nutrients (such as iodine, calcium, vitamin E) *from food excluding fortification/supplements*. These separate estimates overlap: adding them together would be wrong. They nevertheless illustrate why a modular fortification platform addresses a much wider problem than isolated protein supply.

### 1.2 Appropriate use cases

1. **General adult disaster/emergency sustenance:** compact calories, acceptable taste, essential amino acids/fats, stable transport, controlled micronutrients.
2. **Supplementary adult community nutrition:** locally tailored flavors and needs; does not displace varied diets.
3. **Institutional field rations:** logistics, robustness and acceptance; procurement is hypothetical.
4. **Specialized humanitarian/therapeutic products:** *separate* development, requirements, clinical evidence and oversight, especially children, pregnant people and vulnerable clinical populations.
5. **Long-duration space food:** research objective, not NASA selection, endorsement, flight rating or existing qualification.

UNICEF's established Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) system has specific composition, safety and evidence rules under [Codex CXG 95-2022](https://www.unicef.org/supply/ready-use-therapeutic-food-rutf). Unity Gel is **not RUTF**, and the prototype cannot be used as a substitute for evidence-based medical treatment.

## 2. Why microbial and yeast protein matter

### 2.1 The technology distinction

* **Purified yeast protein:** food-grade concentrated protein from *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, potentially relatively neutral-tasting, fine powdered, and practical for fortification; this is the presently proposed Unity Gel ingredient.
* **Fungal mycoprotein:** commonly filamentous fungi such as Quorn's *Fusarium venenatum* biomass; notable texture and meat-alternative properties. It is **not interchangeable** with purified yeast protein.
* **Yeast extract / flavor yeast:** may deliver pronounced savory flavors and need not match high-protein purified-grade functionality.
* **Precision fermentation:** microbes producing a specified target protein; economics and downstream purification may differ sharply from yeast biomass processing.

Angel Yeast's [AngeoPro ingredient leaflet](https://en.angelyeast.com/upload/files/2023/9/Angeopro.pdf) describes a **>80% protein** yeast-derived ingredient, nine essential amino acids, neutral flavor, and manufacturer-reported **PDCAAS 1.0**. These are manufacturer's product-level representations, not independent qualification of the precise food grade and production lot. Current certificate of analysis (COA), technical data sheet (TDS), microbiological limits, lot variability, RNA/nucleic-acid content, allergen and regulatory statements are required.

**Neutral flavor is a hypothesis to test in Unity Gel's extreme fat/protein emulsion**, not guaranteed absence of yeast notes, bitterness, grittiness, foaming or separation.

This **confirms commercial interest**, not general proof that all whey products are being replaced or that Unity Gel's sensory and nutritional challenges have been resolved.

### 2.3 Producer cost versus usable food-grade purchase cost

The path from low-cost fermentation substrate to edible, safe, formulated ingredient includes utilities and labor, fermenter capital, strain performance, contamination controls, downstream concentration and drying, assay/QA, packaging, freight, distribution, manufacturer margin and import compliance. A **$1/kg fermentation production target** must not be substituted for the **$8/kg food ingredient purchase scenario**.

## 3. Environmental and animal-welfare opportunity — the comparison that can actually be substantiated

DeepSeek highlighted approximately **98.4% lower CO2e**, **97.6% lower land footprint**, and **97.0% lower water footprint** than beef. These exact percentages can be reconstructed from **Quorn Foods' 2024 Net Positive report, page 7**, whose published comparative data are:

| Impact per kg reported material | Quorn mycoprotein | Beef comparator | Computed reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Carbon footprint | 0.53 kg CO2e | 32.13 kg CO2e | **98.35%** |
| Land footprint | 1.67 m² | 68.3 m² | **97.55%** |
| Blue-water footprint | 31 L | 1,048 L | **97.04%** |

Source: [Quorn Foods Net Positive Report 2024, PDF page 7](https://www.quorn.us/files/content/Net-Positive-Report-2024.pdf). Calculations: 100 × (1 − microbial/beef). Numbers are per kilogram of the compared materials as presented by Quorn, **not necessarily per kg bioavailable protein, identical nutrition, or consumer-ready ration**. A separate [peer-reviewed Annual Review of Food Science and Technology](https://doi.org/10.1146/ANNUREV-FOOD-111523-121802) also documents broad mycoprotein benefits but shows that methodological choices and functional units matter.

**Do not copy Quorn's life-cycle results to Angel Yeast, AngeoPro, or Unity Gel.** Different organism, plant location, electricity/steam, sugar/feedstock, water reuse, drying energy, nitrogen sources, product purity, oils, flexible packaging and distribution may change impacts. The Quorn values are comparative **proof of possibility**, not a Unity Gel environmental product declaration.

**Animal welfare:** replacing eligible animal-sourced protein with microbial protein could reduce reliance on livestock and exposure to some animal-welfare harms. That impact depends on **actual displacement**, feedstocks and formulation (for example, choose algal omega-3 rather than fish oil for an animal-free claim). No avoided-animal count is claimed.

**Proposed Unity Gel LCA:** cradle-to-gate kg CO2e, land m², blue-water L and energy kWh per **1,000 kcal** and per **75 g digestible protein**, plus an end-to-end ration scenario including packaging, shipping, refrigeration avoidance and food waste. Use geographically explicit supplier electricity and substrate assumptions. Test substitution against existing emergency rations, not just beef.

## 4. Provisional formulation: XL v0.4 engineering basis

### 4.1 Nutrition targets — not verified nutrition facts

* **1,000 kcal** nominal per adult XL pouch (energy must be analytically closed).
* **75 g protein**, **65 g lipid**, **28.75 g digestible carbohydrate** nominal macro targets.
* Essential fatty-acid adequacy, useful fiber, and approximately **50% of selected adult Daily Values of key micronutrients per pouch**; the actual premix has **not** been designed or tested.
* No utensils or added water; squeezable, shear-thinning stable emulsion; multiple culturally adapted flavor systems.
* Proposed package mass **288–400 g**, flexible with supplier feedback. Original calculated formulation is **288.41 g**, prior to physical testing.
* Ambient **12–18 months** as an initial research target, eventually **1–5 years**; no achieved shelf-life result.

### 4.2 Draft bill of materials — NOT a production recipe

| Material | g / XL pouch | Clarification |
|---|---:|---|
| AngeoPro F84-equivalent | 93.52 | **80.2% protein assumed** from earlier provisional grade data; actual COA needed |
| MCT oil | 15.00 | GI tolerance requires study |
| High-oleic oil | 38.00 | Oxidative stability must be assessed |
| Essential fatty-acid oil blend | 3.64 | Composition/omega balance TBD |
| Protected fish/algal oil equivalent | 2.00 | Encapsulation carriers change mass and lipid accounting |
| Added digestible nonsugar carbohydrate | 27.25 | Specific ingredients TBD |
| Added fiber/gelling modifiers | 6.00 | Rheology testing |
| Micronutrient/carrier placeholder | 12.00 | **Allowance, not nutritional specification** |
| Emulsifiers | 3.00 | Thermal/process compatibility unknown |
| Flavor/sweetness | 3.00 | Variant-specific |
| Added water | 85.00 | Added water fraction ~29.5%; actual food water includes intrinsic moisture |
| **Total** | **288.41** | Before line-loss and process optimization |

The 4/9/4 calculation from target macros is **75×4 + 65×9 + 28.75×4 = 1,000 kcal**, excluding any energy from fiber and other contributors. Measured caloric value, available carbohydrate, oil encapsulation carrier and final ingredient composition need reconciliation. Also, two pouches yield **150 g protein/day**, above what many adults require: adult humanitarian variants with lower protein and carefully matched micronutrients should be developed separately.

A label statement such as **'complete nutrition'** cannot be made until age-group-appropriate micronutrient amounts, bioavailability, upper limits, adequate energy, tolerability and testing are established. Protein powder is not by itself complete food.

### 4.3 Technical gates

1. Obtain current AngeoPro grade COA/TDS and samples; measure protein, functional behavior, sensory profile and lot stability.
2. Build three rheology prototypes: silkier, creamy, dense; measure viscosity across defined shear rates, temperatures and post-process aging.
3. Check droplet size, emulsion separation, oil oxidation, oxygen permeability, spout and cap performance, fill accuracy and microbiological limits.
4. Design micronutrient systems for intended adults; consider calcium, potassium, magnesium, iron, zinc, iodine, selenium, fat-soluble vitamins and nutrient-nutrient interactions. Validate bioavailability and upper-limit safety.
5. Use a process authority and appropriately qualified factory to evaluate low-acid versus acidified processing, retort/aseptic choices, heat effects and sealed-package integrity.
6. Conduct sensory tests, GI tolerability work, accelerated stability screenings followed by real-time shelf-life validation and robust food-safety release criteria.

**Food safety non-negotiable:** nitrogen flushing inhibits oxidative deterioration but **does not sterilize** a high-moisture protein food. Refrigerated short-duration R&D samples are not shelf-stable rations. As applicable, [FDA acidified/low-acid food rules](https://www.fda.gov/food/guidance-documents-regulatory-information-topic-food-and-dietary-supplements/acidified-low-acid-canned-foods-guidance-documents-regulatory-information), scheduled process filings, [food facility registration, import and labeling rules](https://www.fda.gov/industry/importing-fda-regulated-products/importing-human-foods), hazard controls and U.S. importer obligations must be evaluated by qualified parties. FDA facility registration is **not product approval**.

### 4.4 Space-ration distinction

NASA's [civil-space shortfall description](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/civil-space-shortfall-descriptions-july-2024.pdf) identifies potential **five-year Mars food-system shelf-life** requirements and system-level nutrient, mass, water and acceptability challenges. Unity Gel's projected moisture, macros and shelf life are neither certified nor flight-rated. The provisional **85 g added water / 288.41 g** (~29.5%) does not establish that the **total** finished food is below any NASA mass target; all sources of water, nutritional needs, packaging and preservation methods must be accounted for. NASA, defense and humanitarian institutional purchases remain purely speculative scenarios.

## 5. Version 0.1 techno-economic model

### 5.1 Assumptions and reproducible formulas

Protein ingredient load = 93.52 g = 0.09352 kg per pouch; `C_yeast = 0.09352 × P_yeast_per_kg`. Baseline `P_yeast_per_kg = $8`, so `C_yeast = $0.74816 ≈ $0.75`.

These remaining costs are **constructed scenario inputs**, not verified factory quotes. Variables cover raw ingredients and finished-pouch production at different order sizes; no learning curve has yet been inferred from supplier evidence.

| USD per XL pouch | 100 units | 10,000 units | 100,000 units | 1,000,000 units |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Yeast protein ingredient ($8/kg) | 0.75 | 0.75 | 0.75 | 0.75 |
| Other ingredients | 2.10 | 1.50 | 1.25 | 1.12 |
| High-barrier pouch, cap, label | 1.20 | 0.55 | 0.38 | 0.27 |
| Mixing, filling, sealing | 12.00 | 1.80 | 0.75 | 0.38 |
| Routine QA allowance | 1.00 | 0.45 | 0.30 | 0.22 |
| Outbound logistics allowance | 1.50 | 0.40 | 0.22 | 0.14 |
| **Modeled variable subtotal** | **18.55** | **5.45** | **3.65** | **2.88** |

**Excluded:** formulation consulting and IP work, laboratory equipment, development samples, line setup minimums, validated kill steps, process-authority fees, specialized regulatory documentation, packaging tooling, accelerated *and real-time* shelf-life studies, ingredient import duties/taxes, factory scrap beyond allowance, warehousing, distribution to end user, organizational overhead and margins. Therefore these are **neither actionable consumer prices nor delivered humanitarian procurement prices**.

**Pilot realism:** extrapolating the $18.55 variable cost to a 50-pouch run gives **~$928 before fixed R&D and setup**, not a quoted $500 pilot offer. Professional development/food safety may cost materially more. A 50-pouch lab-development arrangement may have a completely different cost curve from a commercial co-packing plant.

### 5.2 Protein-price sensitivity

| Supplier ingredient price | Yeast portion / pouch | Total at 1M-unit scenario (holding other costs fixed) |
|---|---:|---:|
| $1/kg | $0.094 | $2.22 |
| $6/kg | $0.561 | $2.69 |
| **$8/kg** | **$0.748** | **$2.88** |
| $12/kg | $1.122 | $3.25 |
| $20/kg | $1.870 | $4.00 |

At $8/kg, yeast protein comprises ~**26%** of the $2.88 variable-cost scenario, so reducing protein price alone does not solve processing, packaging or complete-nutrition economics. Conversely, a commercially obtainable $8/kg ingredient could support early development without waiting for a <$1/kg hypothetical upstream fermentation process.

### 5.3 Two-pouch adult illustrative ration

At maturity, **2 × $2.88 = $5.76** variable cost per 2,000-kcal, 150-g-nominal-protein daily ration. This **does not establish** suitability or nutritional adequacy for any person.

| Illustrative deployment | Pouches | Variable subtotal |
|---|---:|---:|
| One person × 30 days | 60 | $172.80 |
| 1,000 people × 30 days | 60,000 | $172,800 |
| 100,000 people × 30 days | 6,000,000 | $17.28 million |
| 1,000,000 people × 30 days | 60,000,000 | $172.8 million |

At a rate of two pouches daily, a hypothetical continuous **1 million pouches/year** sustains **~1,370 person-years**. Sustaining one million people all year would require **730 million pouches/year**, before wastage. These are capacity arithmetic, not an estimate of unmet demand or available government orders.

**Actual procurement should benchmark existing adult humanitarian foods, UNICEF therapeutic-food contracts where applicable, shelf-stable rations, and in-country locally procured diets—per complete, appropriate nutrient delivered**, not simply per pouch.

## 7. Program architecture: shared nutritious base, locally appropriate experience

**Modular nutritional foundation:** standardize ingredient safety, product identity, macro and micronutrient design controls while varying approved food flavors and culinary profiles to local preferences. Possible modules: mild/savory, South Asian spice, Middle Eastern herb/spice, Latin American acidic/spicy, fruit-based. These are design ideas only; acid profile may change food safety classification and processing requirements, and spices/terpenes must meet food-grade safety and dose controls.

**Adult and pediatric branches must diverge.** A high-protein adult emergency pouch should not be treated as food for infants, children with severe wasting, pregnancy or kidney disease. Child therapeutic products require separate nutritional specs, evidence, manufacturing approvals and treatment integration.

**System performance indicators:** USD/1,000 kcal *safely delivered*; USD/usable digestible gram of protein; USD/day meeting population-specific nutrient requirements; product mass and volume/1,000 kcal; microbiological release rate; 12/18/36/60-month nutrient and sensory stability; manufacturing yield and sourcing reliability; end-to-end LCA; local acceptance and food waste; availability in humanitarian supply routes.

**Social hypothesis:** standardization of a validated base with culturally appropriate flavor options may lower supply complexity and improve acceptability. This must be studied, not presumed.

## 8. Risk register and falsification criteria

| Risk | Why it could defeat the thesis | Test / decision gate |
|---|---|---|
| Protein and fat fraction cannot be packaged as squeezable gel | Rheology, gritty texture, unpalatable viscosity | Bench batches and rheometer/spout trials |
| Heat/process destabilizes product | Separation, oxidized fats, destroyed vitamins | Process-authority trials and stability assay |
| High moisture leads to unsafe shelf-stable claim | Spores/pathogens and seal failure | Validated commercial sterility/process and microbial quality |
| Protein lot inconsistency | Flavor and viscosity variability | Multi-lot COA, small rheology study |
| Micronutrient premix fails requirements | Not nutritionally adequate even with calories | Full nutrient design, stability/bioavailability and upper-limit review |
| Unit costs differ sharply from scenario | Special packaging, low line speed, expensive validation | Bind assumptions to formal quotations, amortize CAPEX |
| Sustainability transfer invalid | Yeast substrate and energy materially different from Quorn LCA | Supplier-specific LCA with functional units |
| No humanitarian demand because alternatives are better | Cost, clinical suitability, acceptability, local markets | Comparator procurement and pilot program data |
| Institutional demand overstated | NASA/DoD are not customers | Treat procurement as *scenarios* only |

**Kill / pivot conditions:** failure of safe physical stability; inability to devise a valid ambient shelf-stable process without unacceptable nutrient loss or cost; unsafe micronutrient upper-limit profile; unacceptable sensory acceptance or worse total delivered nutrition cost than existing alternatives. Alternative outcomes include refrigeration-only prototypes, lower-protein formulas, powdered sachets, or food fortification using the same yeast protein.

## 9. Near-term deliverables and open questions

1. **AngeoPro supply:** get 2–3 grades' COAs, sample route and prices at lab, 1 tonne and commercial scale; verify ~80% protein in our precise food grade.
2. **Formulation mass balance:** design actual 100%-specified food-grade ingredient schedule, macros, minerals, vitamins, microbiological specs and flavor variants; close energy and moisture accounting.
3. **Process selection:** appoint competent food/process authority and compare refrigerated R&D, retort, aseptic and water-activity/acidification alternatives without assuming nitrogen makes food safe.
4. **Actual supplier quotes:** for R&D sampling and then 10k / 100k / 1M units, with tooling, loss, compliance and logistics disclosed.
5. **Adult use-case benchmark:** compare to existing shelf-stable ready-to-eat emergency rations and complete-nutrition products by end-to-end cost and validated nutrition.
6. **Humanitarian branch:** explore fortifying locally accepted foods and authorized clinical-research partners; do not market adult gel as RUTF.
7. **Environmental accounting:** request primary-specific energy/water/feedstock data; compute yeast-versus-livestock alternatives without assigning Quorn's footprint.
8. **Publication discipline:** publish results as observed, modeled, unverified or target; update numeric claims rather than repeating viral projections.
