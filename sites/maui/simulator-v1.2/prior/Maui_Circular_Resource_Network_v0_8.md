# Maui Circular Resource Network — v0.8 Research Handoff
**Date:** 2026-10-19  
**Status:** PROPOSED / NON-CANON; historical observations and modeled scenarios explicitly distinguished.  
**Scope:** Maui water, organics, recycling, micro-haul, fuel, and electricity integration.

## Architecture
Three proposed regional hubs: West Maui (Lahaina/Kā‘anapali), North Shore (agricultural), Central Maui (Kahului/Wailuku), plus existing South Maui/Kīhei wastewater infrastructure as a fourth water-resource node. These are planning areas, not approved facility sites.

Source-separate edible grocery surplus, spoiled grocery produce, restaurant/hotel food scraps, farm residues and manure, green waste, sewage/biosolids, used cooking oil, plastics, metals, glass, and residual waste. Preserve food rescue, animal feed and soil-retention priorities. Avoid double counting materials already diverted or present in landfill waste estimates.

Use neighborhood micro-haul collection, local consolidation, regional specialty processing and backhaul of finished products. Track route distances, collection frequency, contamination, tonnage, vehicle energy and labor.

## Treatment and products
- Sewage: existing treatment plus candidate vibrating membrane bioreactors; reclaimed R-1 water for approved nonpotable uses; controlled biosolids recovery.
- Food and compatible manure: anaerobic digestion for methane and nutrients; compare with hydrothermal liquefaction (HTL) for biocrude, not both yields from the same feedstock.
- Sewage sludge: screen moisture, dry solids, PFAS/metals/pathogens; evaluate HTL biocrude, digestion and permitted disposal. HTL upgrading, aqueous-phase treatment and fuel certification are additional costs.
- Green/woody residues: prioritize mulch/soil cover, compost, then appropriate thermochemical options.
- Used cooking oil: segregated fuel pathway.
- Plastics/metals/glass: sorting, repair, reuse, and commercially viable upcycling; test markets and contaminant standards.
- Seawater: modular desalination with pressure-energy recovery; study selective mineral recovery and brine management. Zero-liquid-discharge is an objective subject to lifecycle economics and permitting.

## Preliminary reference flows (not a current census)
- Historical 2019 Maui landfill organics: approximately 41,000 US short tons/year food waste and 32,000 short tons/year green waste. At hypothetical 70% capture: 28,700 and 22,400 short tons/year respectively, 51,100 total or 140 short tons/day.
- Maui County wastewater reference: ~14.6 MGD; actual current facility flows, R-1 volumes, treatment constraints and customers need reconciliation.
- Proposed desalination sizing retained only as scenario: West 6 MGD, North 5 MGD, Central 17 MGD = 28 MGD. Reoptimize after full water balance.
- Historic 24,000 tons/year biosolids project reference requires verification of wet/dry basis and project implementation before inclusion.
- HTL, digestion, nutrient and recycling yields require feedstock sampling and vendor pilot data; do not infer island fuel self-sufficiency.

## New membrane research implications
Vibrating membrane bioreactor study reports ~24% reduction in total treatment electricity, ~75% reduction in fouling-control electricity, and ~40% less phosphorus chemical dosing at the studied facility. Use as pilot comparator, not a Maui guarantee. FO–AD–RO desalination integration research reports configuration-specific modeled RO energy reductions; distinguish brackish from seawater and RO-stage from whole-plant energy.

## Energy baseline (2025, Maui County, not Maui island alone)
Hawaiian Electric generation: oil 58.5%; customer-sited renewables 20.3%; wind 13.3%; utility solar 7.9%. No reported geothermal, hydro, biofuel or biomass contribution. Maui Island's major oil stations: Mā‘alaea 212.1 MW, Kahului 37.6 MW (utility published capacity). Utility-scale Kuihelani solar 60 MW + 240 MWh storage; wind Kaheawa I 30 MW, II 21 MW, Auwahi 21 MW. Energy storage is not generation.
